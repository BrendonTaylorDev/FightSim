Her muzzle throbbed. Her nose was a hot, wet, numb crunch that made her eyes stream. Her chest had gone from an ache to a building, spreading pressure that made every breath a negotiation. Her back—the thick, muscled, resilient back that had shrugged off everything—had finally been hit properly, and it ached from shoulders to hips in a deep, bruised band. Her head had gone past building. It pounded now, sick and heavy, the cave tilting and doubling around her.

And her stomach.

Her stomach had crossed over. She'd felt it happen on the first slam—the sharpness thinning, then going quiet, becoming a place in the centre of her body where feeling simply stopped and something worse began. She curled around it, and her arms found nothing to hold that helped, and she made a small, lost, wheezing sound.

Nocturne let the tails drop. She stood over the Buizel with her head hanging and her neck throbbing from the whip, her flank a bell that would not stop ringing, and her sides heaving.

The Buizel was curled on her right side in the shallows with her back to the Absol, arms wrapped around her stomach, tails limp.

Her left side was up. Exposed. The first thing in this fight to be hurt—the Milotic's Dragon Tail in the opening minute—and she had been guarding it with an elbow ever since. Now the elbow was wrapped around her stomach instead, because the stomach was worse.

Nocturne looked at it for a long moment.

Then she put her left forepaw on the Buizel's shoulder to hold her still, drew her right paw back, and the claws lit with shadow—three curved blades of dark stretching past the claw-tips—and she brought them down across the side from armpit to hip with everything she had.

Ripples' scream broke the cave. Every drop of water on every stalactite shivered. Her body arched so hard her head and tails nearly met behind her, every limb flung rigid, her mouth stretched in a shape that had no sound left in it after the first half-second.

Then she fell back into the water and lay there, twitching, a thin, wheezing whistle coming out of her throat.

The Buizel went still. Not surrendered—thinking. Her arms were pinned. Her legs were pinned. Her chest couldn't expand. Her neck couldn't turn.

But the thing between her chest and her neck was hers.

The flotation sac. The thick yellow collar around her throat, the one part of her the lightning had barely touched. It sat directly under the Absol's jaw. And it could inflate.

She started drawing air—not into her lungs, she couldn't—but into the sac, tiny sips through her nose, each one a fight against the weight on her chest, each one swelling the collar a fraction.

Nocturne felt something shift under her jaw and pressed down harder, not understanding.

The sac vented. All of it, at once, in a hissing blast of air straight up into the Absol's face, and Nocturne's head jerked back on reflex, and her teeth came free of the neck, and the Buizel's arm—numb, heavy—swung up from below wrapped in crackling frost, straight into the Absol's flank.

The flank. The centre of Nocturne's world.

Nocturne got up.

It took her a long time. She rolled onto her left side and lay there. She got her left legs under her. She got her right foreleg under her. She did not try the right hind leg—it hung, curled, twitching, beneath a flank that had stopped being a flank and become a condition. She stood on three legs with her head hanging almost to the water and her whole body shaking so hard the ripples spread out from her across the pool.

She lifted her head. Slowly. The scythe caught the light.
