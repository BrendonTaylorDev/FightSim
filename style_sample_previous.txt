The Brine Hollow was a sea cave the tide had been carving for ten thousand years: a wide floor of grey stone veined with pink and orange coral fossil, ringed by ankle-deep pools that breathed in and out with the swell outside. Salt hung in the air. Somewhere water dripped in a slow, patient rhythm. The three of them entered from three different mouths of the cave, and for a long moment nobody moved.

Seraphina rose first—eleven feet of pearl-white scale and rippling cream, her long body sliding through the shallow perimeter pool with barely a sound, the red hair-fins framing her face drifting like silk in a current. Her tail fan opened and closed once behind her, blue-and-red eyespots catching the dim light. She lifted her head high on that long, elegant neck and let out a low, resonant note that filled the cave.

Ripples answered with a bark—sharp, cocky, bouncing on the balls of her feet at the edge of a pool, twin tails whipping behind her in tight little arcs, the yellow flotation sac around her neck already half-inflated with excitement. Orange fur bristling. She slapped one paw against the water and grinned with all her teeth.

Nocturne said nothing. The Absol stood on the dry centre stone, white fur stark against the grey, the black scythe of her horn tilted just slightly toward Seraphina. Her red eyes moved from the Milotic to the Buizel and back. Her sickle tail was perfectly still.

Then Seraphina's throat swelled.

The Milotic's mouth opened and the cave roared. A column of water as thick as Nocturne's body slammed across the twenty feet between them in the time it took to blink—Nocturne saw it coming and had a half-second to drop her head and set her front paws, and then it hit her.

It hit her everywhere at once. The jet caught her square in the chest and split around her, hammering both shoulders, blasting up under her jaw and across her throat. Her front paws skidded backward on the wet stone, claws shrieking as they scraped for purchase. Her ears flattened to her skull. The impact drove a grunt out of her—a short, hard hurf—and she squeezed her eyes shut against the spray, water forcing its way into her nose and mouth, her whole front end shuddering under the sustained pressure.

Three seconds. Four. The stream cut off and Nocturne staggered two steps sideways, coughing, shaking her head violently so that water flew off her fur in sheets. Her chest heaved. She rolled her shoulders once, feeling the deep stinging ache where the pressure had hammered the muscle against the bone—not agony, not yet, but a hot heavy soreness across the whole width of her front. Her neck felt bruised on the inside. She swallowed and it hurt a little.

She lifted her head. Her eyes found Seraphina's and narrowed.

Ripples didn't wait for the Absol to finish shaking herself off. The moment the Hydro Pump cut out she was already moving—a lunge into the perimeter pool, a surge of water sheathing her body, and then she was a streak of orange and white launching low and fast across the stone.

Nocturne heard the hiss of it and tried to turn. Too slow. Ripples' shoulder—the whole compact, water-wrapped mass of her—drove into the Absol's right flank, the soft stretch of body between the ribs and the hip where there was nothing but muscle and organ underneath.

The sound Nocturne made was a sharp, choked yelp. Her whole body bent around the impact like a bow, her right hind leg lifting off the ground involuntarily, her head whipping around. Ripples felt the flank give under her, felt the Absol's breath punch out across her fur, and then she was skidding past and rolling to her feet on the far side, tails lashing, chittering with delight.

Nocturne stumbled. Her right hind paw came down wrong and she nearly went over; she caught herself with three legs and stood there for a moment with her right side hunched inward, curled protectively over the flank. It didn't hurt badly—the fur and muscle had taken most of it, a heavy dull throb—but she could feel something else, something worse: the way that spot now felt thin. Exposed. The Buizel had knocked the toughness right out of it in a single shot, and the Absol knew with animal certainty that the next hit there would be a very different experience.

Her lip curled. Her eyes flicked to Ripples, then to the shallow pool the Buizel was still standing in, ankle-deep.

Nocturne's fur began to lift. Static crackled along the ridge of her spine.

Ripples saw the light before she understood it—a blue-white shimmer racing down the Absol's horn—and her ears went straight up. Her eyes went wide. She started to move.

The bolt hit the pool.

Every muscle in the Buizel's body locked at once. Her back arched so hard her tails and the back of her head nearly touched; her arms flung out rigid to the sides, fingers splayed and shaking; her mouth stretched open in a scream that had no sound because her diaphragm had seized. Electricity crawled across her wet fur in visible threads, jumping from the water up her legs, through her hips, through her chest, down her arms, up her neck, into her ears—

Her ears. The delicate, thin-skinned, barely-protected ears. The current found them like it had been looking for them, and the shriek that finally tore out of her when her lungs unlocked was high and ragged and scared. She clapped both paws over her ears even as she was still convulsing, even as her knees buckled and she dropped into the ankle-deep water, splashing, spasming, her tails thrashing in the pool and sending up spray.

Her muzzle burned. The tip of her nose stung like she'd been slapped. Her cheeks tingled. Every rib felt like it had been individually flicked. Her stomach clenched in a hard, sick knot. The only parts of her that felt nothing were the thick pads of her paws, the rubbery flotation sac, and the dense muscle of her back and thighs—those had barely registered the current at all.

But her ears. Ripples curled on her side in the pool, panting hard, both paws still clamped over the sides of her head, and whimpered. They throbbed with a hot, deep, vibrating ache, and worse—she could feel how fragile they'd become. Paper-thin. If anything touched them now...

Above her, the cave still smelled of ozone. Nocturne stood with sparks dying along her fur, breathing hard, watching the Buizel writhe.
