## opening
— Both —

The clearing sat at the bottom of the old forest like a bowl someone had set down in the trees and forgotten. Pines stood tall and straight round three sides of it, and between them broad oaks spread their branches low, their roots humped up through the grass wherever the shade began. Along the treeline a fallen trunk lay half sunk in moss, furred green and soft, warm to the touch where the light reached it. On the fourth side the grass thinned and paled into a beach of fine sand that ran down gently into the lake. The shallows were clear and warm over the sand; a few lengths out the water went dark all at once, cool and deep.

It was afternoon, and warm. Fat white clouds came over the far shore one after another, and their shadows slid across the clearing, dimming the grass and letting it go. A breeze came in off the water, never strong, just enough to turn the oak leaves pale side up and set the long grass at the edge of the sand nodding. A boulder sat where the grass gave way to the beach, grey and sun-warmed, the only hard thing on that side. Up in the trees the birds sang, and the lake lapped at the sand as if it had all day.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne came out from under the pines and stopped where the shade ended. The sun found her at once: white fur gone almost too bright to look at, the dark slate of her face, the curved blade of her horn throwing a thin line of light as she turned her head. Her red eyes went round the clearing one thing at a time. Trees. Trunk. Boulder. The long open stretch of grass. Last of all the lake, and the slim shape standing at the edge of it.

*Stay off the sand. Stay out of the water. Make her come to me.*

Her broad paws settled into the turf. The sickle of her tail lifted behind her, swung once, and went still.

— Ripples —

Ripples stood in the shallows up to her ankles with the lake warm round her webbed feet and watched the Absol come out of the shade. She had already been in the water. Her orange fur lay flat and dark against her body, and the bare cream of her throat showed pale in the sun; drops ran off the blue fins on her forearms and pattered into the shallows. Behind her the twin tails had started to turn, slowly, the cream tips drawing circles in the air.

She knew this one. Knew the horn, and the claws, and the way that tail came round when you weren't looking for it. She rolled her shoulders, one and then the other, and felt the water drip off her elbows.

*She'll want the grass. Fine. Let her have it. I'll bring the lake to her.*

The birds in the oaks went on singing. Neither of them moved, and then one of them did.

## beat 2
— Ripples —

Ripples dropped low, crouched almost flat to the shallows, and the water came up round her.

It did not splash. It climbed. It came up her legs and over her hips and wrapped her whole body in a skin of moving water, glassy and rushing, and then she kicked off the lake bottom and the skin took her with it. She went up the beach like something fired, low and straight, sand spraying away on either side of her, the twin tails spinning so fast behind her they blurred into one pale disc. The sheath of water roared round her ears. She kept her chin tucked and her eyes open and picked her spot: the broad white chest, square in the middle, where the ruff began.

The Absol saw her coming. Ripples saw her see it: the red eyes widening, the weight starting to shift onto the hind legs, the beginning of a twist away. Too late. Ripples was already there.

— Nocturne —

It hit her like a wave hits a rock, except that she was not a rock.

The Buizel's shoulder and the whole weight of the water behind it drove into the middle of her chest, and every bit of air in Nocturne's lungs went out of her in one ugly bark. Her forepaws skidded back across the grass. The force jarred out through the ribs on her right side and up into her left shoulder, a deep, shaking thud she felt all the way to her spine, and for a moment she could not get a breath in at all. Her mouth hung open. Nothing came.

Then a thin breath came, and another, shallow and careful, and she found she had kept her feet.

The water had not stopped when the Buizel did. It broke over her in a sheet: down her face, into her ruff, along her back and flanks, soaking the white fur through to the skin. When she shook her head it flew off her in a spray and her coat hung heavy and dark and dripping, clinging to her ribs, cold where the breeze found it.

— Ripples —

Ripples bounced off and landed on her feet a body-length back, panting, the water sliding off her and soaking into the grass.

She looked at the Absol. Drenched. Every white hair on her stuck down flat, water running off her horn in a thin stream, her sides heaving short and quick.

*Wet now. You're wet now.*

Ripples grinned.

## beat 3
— Nocturne —

Nocturne did not give her the grin for long.

She came forward fast, still dripping, still breathing short, and her head came up and round in a quick hard jab at the Buizel's face, the horn flashing, her jaws half open. It was meant to be seen. She watched the small black eyes fly to it, watched the orange arms jerk up to cover the face, the whole slim body leaning back from where the horn was going.

The horn was not going anywhere. It stopped short in the air.

Her right forepaw was already moving, low and wide. Dark came up along the three claws as they swung, a thin smoke of it clinging to each edge, and she raked them across the Buizel's right arm where it had come up high and left everything under it open.

— Ripples —

She had fallen for it. She knew it the instant she did it, arms up round her face for a blow that never came, and the knowing was no use at all.

The claws went across her upper arm, the right one, the meat of it just below the shoulder, and the pain was so big and so sudden that for a moment she did not even feel it as pain. It was a white flash. Then it was fire. It went into the arm and stayed there, a deep, tearing, unbelievable burn, and a sound came out of Ripples she had never heard herself make: a high, cracked shriek that went up into the oaks and sent a whole flock of small birds bursting out of the branches.

The rake carried across her chest and up over her right shoulder, two heavy thumps that rocked her, but she hardly noticed them. The arm. The arm. She clutched it to her body with her other paw and folded round it, eyes squeezed shut, the twin tails stopped dead behind her.

*Get up. Get your head up. She's still there.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne stood over her with the dark still curling off her claws and watched the Buizel curl round the arm.

The shriek was still going out across the water. The birds were still settling. It had gone in deeper than she had meant, and she felt a flicker of something that was almost surprise, and under it, harder and colder, the knowledge of exactly where to put the next one.

## beat 4
— Nocturne —

The Buizel was still bent round her arm. Nocturne didn't wait for her to straighten.

She dropped her head and went in low, a long driving lunge with all four legs behind it, and her shoulder took the Buizel across the middle. Both of them went over. Nocturne felt the slim body give and fold under her and then they were down together, rolling once on the sand at the edge of the grass, and when the roll stopped she was on top.

The Buizel was on her back. Nocturne planted both forepaws on her chest, square, and leaned. Her weight came down through her shoulders and into her paws and into the Buizel's ribs, and she felt the chest under her give and stop and push back. Her hind paw found the left thigh and pressed it down into the sand.

*Stay down. Stay there.*

The sand was warm under her. The Buizel's heart was going under her paws like something trapped.

— Ripples —

The sky. That was the first thing. She was looking at the sky, at a white cloud going slowly over, and she did not remember falling.

Then the weight. Two paws on her chest, big and hard and heavy, pressing her ribs down so that every breath had to shove up against them. Something pinning her left leg to the sand. And above her the Absol's face, upside down against the sky, wet and white and the horn hanging over her like a hook.

Ripples bared her teeth up at it.

She was not hurt enough for this. She was angry, and she was pinned, and the arm was still on fire, but she was a long way from done. She could feel the strength still in her legs. She could feel the tails under her, twisting against the sand.

*Not yet. Not on the first one. Not like this.*

## beat 5
— Nocturne —

Nocturne felt the Buizel gather herself under her paws. She could feel it coming the way you feel a storm in the air.

She struck first. Her head came down and her jaws closed on the side of the Buizel's neck, short and cramped, no room to swing, just a hard snapping bite from on top. Her teeth sank into the thick fur and the muscle under it and the jolt went through into the throat and the jaw beside it. She held it a heartbeat and let go.

Under her, one of the Buizel's arms had got itself trapped against her own side, folded wrong, a nerve pinched. Nocturne felt it go weak and useless.

*Good. Less to fight me with.*

— Ripples —

The bite hurt. It hurt badly, a deep clamping ache all down the side of her neck, and it jolted into her throat so that for a moment her breath caught on nothing. Her left arm had gone numb and tingling where it was crushed between her body and the Absol's paw.

But the paws were on her chest, and her chest was not her neck, and the weight was coming down from above, and she knew what to do with weight from above.

She planted both feet in the sand. She drove her hips up.

It was all of her, everything from her toes to the back of her skull, one long bridging heave that lifted her middle clean off the ground and the Absol with it. She felt the big body tilt over her. She felt the paws slide. And as they slid she brought her right knee up hard between them and drove it into the Absol's chest, right where the water had hit her, and felt it go in.

— Nocturne —

The knee went into her chest in exactly the place that was already hurt, and the hurt went from a deep dull ache to something white and roaring in the space of a heartbeat.

Nocturne made a sound like a cough with a scream inside it. She came off the Buizel sideways, staggering, her forelegs splaying, and only kept her feet because her hind legs did what her front ones couldn't. Her chest was a hot, crushed, bursting thing. Every breath went into it and found a wall. She stood with her head down and her mouth open and breathed in tiny, careful sips because anything bigger tore.

*Too much. That was too much. Breathe. Breathe.*

Her wet fur had stopped dripping. The sun and the breeze between them had taken the worst of it off her; her coat was only damp now, sticking in places, lifting in others.

— Ripples —

Ripples rolled clear and came up against the trunk of a tall pine. She caught it with her good paw and pushed straight up the bark in one hard move and stood, breathing rough, her right arm tucked against her body.

She shook her left arm out. The pins and needles went fizzing down to her fingers and the feeling came back.

The Absol was still bent over her own chest, a body-length away, breathing like something with a hole in it.

*That's yours now. Remember where.*

## beat 6
— Ripples —

Ripples did not go in. She stood under the pine and let her breath come back, and she lifted her face to the sky and called it.

She did not know how it worked. She had never known. She only knew that the lake was right there behind her, warm and huge and full of weather, and that when she reached for it, it came. She threw her good arm up and her tails began to spin and the air changed. The breeze dropped and came back cold. The fat white clouds over the far shore went grey at the bottoms, then darker, then all at once they were not drifting anymore but coming, piling up over the clearing, swallowing the sun.

The first drops hit the leaves above her with a sound like fingers tapping.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne felt the first drop land between her ears and looked up.

Then it came down properly. Rain, straight and warm and heavy, hissing in the grass and drumming on the sand and pocking the surface of the lake into a grey field of tiny craters. Within a breath her fur was soaked again. Water ran off her horn, off her nose, down her chest into the hurt. The clearing went dim and loud with it.

She blinked rain out of her eyes and looked at the Buizel standing under the pine with her face turned up, eyes half closed, drinking it in. The orange fur gleamed. The tails spun.

*She's happy. Of course she's happy.*

Nocturne lowered her head, and felt the hum start in the base of her horn.

## beat 7
— Nocturne —

The rain was a gift, though not to the one who'd asked for it.

She had been holding it since the fight began, a tight, prickling charge coiled in the bone of her horn, and now she let it build. It crawled up the curve of the blade and gathered at the point, white-blue, spitting, lighting the rain around her head so that every falling drop flashed for an instant as it passed. Her fur stood up along her spine. The air tasted of metal.

The Buizel was soaked to the skin, standing in a clearing full of water, under a sky full of water. Nocturne swung her head and let go.

— Ripples —

It did not hit her. It went through her.

There was a crack so loud it was not a sound but a blow to the inside of her skull, and then every part of her was lit up at once. Her whole body locked. Arms flung out stiff, legs rigid, back arched, tails standing straight out behind her like iron bars, and through all of it the white-blue fire crawled and snapped and tore. She could not breathe. She could not close her mouth. She could not even scream, because every muscle she would have screamed with was clenched solid.

Then it let go of her, and she dropped to her knees in the wet grass.

The worst of it had gone into her face. Her nose was burning, a deep raw throb as if it had been struck, and it felt wrong when she breathed through it, too soft, a give in it that had not been there before. Her throat ached. Both ears were ringing, a high thin whine that would not stop, and her cheeks and muzzle and the top of her head were all one sore, buzzing ache. Smoke rose off her fur in the rain.

She stayed on her knees and shook. Small twitches still went through her legs.

*Get up. Get up. She's coming.*

The numb weight had gone out of her left arm. That was something.

## beat 8
— Nocturne —

The Buizel was on her knees with smoke coming off her, and Nocturne did not need to be told twice.

She went round her. Not at her: round, quick and low through the rain, and came in from behind while the Buizel was still trying to lift her head. Her forepaws landed on the orange back, and her full weight came down after them. The Buizel's legs folded under her and she went flat on her front in the wet grass with a grunt, and Nocturne came down on top of her, chest on her upper back, pinning her flat.

The right arm was flung out to the side. The hurt one. Nocturne put her head down and closed her jaws round it, not biting, just holding, teeth set in the fur above the elbow.

*There. Now move.*

— Ripples —

Face-down. Grass in her mouth. Rain on the back of her neck.

The weight on her back was enormous. It pressed her into the ground, ribs against the soaked earth, and the breath she pulled in had to fight it. And her arm. Her right arm was in the Absol's jaws, and it was not being bitten, not yet, only held, and the holding alone was agony: the teeth resting on the place the claws had torn, the slightest pressure there lighting it up again.

She did not scream this time. She ground her teeth and pressed her face into the grass and breathed through her nose.

*Twice now. She's got me down twice.*

She was furious with herself. And she was a long way from done.

## beat 9
— Nocturne —

She could have just held her. She did not.

Nocturne lifted her chest off the Buizel's back just far enough to get her right forepaw free, and dark came up along the claws again, curling in the rain, and she raked it down across the side of the orange chest where it lay pressed into the grass. It was a short, cramped stroke, no swing in it. She did not need a swing. She felt the claws catch and tear, felt the slim body jerk under her, and the rake carried on up over the arm in her jaws and across the back of the neck, and she put her weight back down before the Buizel could use the space.

— Ripples —

The claws went into her chest, and her chest, which had been sore, was suddenly not sore at all but a ripping, scalding, howling thing that blotted out everything else.

She screamed into the grass. Her whole body jerked under the weight. Before the first scream had finished the rake carried on over her arm, still in the Absol's mouth, and the arm flared again, and then it caught the back of her neck where the bite had been and the jolt went down her spine and the scream went higher and cracked.

She did not fight. She could not. She lay there with her face in the wet earth and shook, every muscle in her body clenched round the pain, and waited for it to drop below the level where she could think again.

— Nocturne —

It took a long time. Nocturne felt every second of it through her chest, where it lay on the Buizel's back: the shaking, and the small hitching breaths, and the way the slim body would go still and then jerk again.

She kept her weight down. The rain ran off her horn onto the back of the orange head.

## beat 10
— Both —

The rain was the loudest thing in the clearing. It hissed in the grass, drummed on the fallen trunk, roared in the leaves of the oaks, and under it, close to the ground, were two other sounds: the Absol's breath, heavy and slow and even, and the Buizel's, short and ragged and pressed flat.

— Ripples —

She listened to the breathing above her and timed it.

In. Out. In. Out. The weight shifted, very slightly, with each one: heavier on the in, a fraction lighter on the out. Not much. Enough.

On the out, she twisted. She threw her hips to the left and dragged her shoulder after them and for a moment the Absol's chest slid off her back and she was half turned, face out of the grass, and her left arm came free and swung blind. It hit something. The Absol's shoulder, the right one, solid and round under wet fur, and she felt it land with a thud that went all the way up her own arm.

— Nocturne —

It stung. A hard, numbing knock on the point of her shoulder that made her grunt. And then she put her weight back where it belonged and shoved the Buizel down flat again, and the twisting body went still under her.

She tightened her jaws on the arm. Not much. Just enough to say: no.

The Buizel gave a thin, high sound into the grass.

Above them the rain was easing. It came down softer, then in scattered drops, then it was only the drip from the leaves, and the clouds were breaking up, tearing open in long blue rents, and the sun came down through them hot on Nocturne's wet back. Steam began to lift off the grass.

## beat 11
— Ripples —

The arm was the problem. As long as the arm was in those jaws, every move she made went through it.

She worked her left paw into the earth under her chest, fingers digging, testing. She drew one knee up an inch, then another, getting her foot under her. She felt the weight on her back and found where it sat heaviest, which was high, between her shoulder blades, and where it was lightest, which was her hips.

Then she bucked.

She threw her whole body up from the hips, all of it, kicking off that one bent leg, and the big white weight on her back pitched forward over her shoulders, and the jaws came open as the Absol's head jerked, and the arm was free. Ripples rolled. As she rolled she kicked back with both feet, blind, and both of them went into something soft.

— Nocturne —

Both the Buizel's feet hit her low in the belly at once, and it was as if something had reached inside her and torn.

She went over. She did not decide to, there was no deciding; her legs went out from under her and she was down on her front in the steaming grass, and her belly was a single huge burning wound that swallowed everything. A sound came out of her that was half howl and half gasp. She could not get her legs under her. She could not even think about getting her legs under her. She lay there and the pain went on and on.

*Down. I'm down. She put me down. Breathe.*

— Ripples —

Ripples crawled. Her legs were numb, both of them, pins and needles from the knees down, and the right arm hung off her shoulder like something that belonged to someone else. She got as far as the nearest oak and got her good paw on a root.

She hauled. She got halfway up the trunk and the world tilted, the whole clearing swinging sideways round her, and she sank back down on her knees with her forehead against the bark.

She breathed. She tried again, slower, dragging herself up the oak paw over paw, leaning her whole weight on it, and this time she stayed up. Her legs shook. Her side heaved. She held the bark and did not let go.

The Absol was still lying in the grass where she had fallen.

## beat 12
— Ripples —

She was lying face-down and Ripples did not stop to think about it.

She let go of the oak and lurched across the grass, numb legs and all, and dropped. Both knees came down in the middle of the Absol's back, one either side of the spine, and her weight followed them. The big body jerked under her. A forepaw, the left, came scrabbling out toward her and she caught it with both paws, the good one and the burning one, and pressed it flat into the grass.

*Got you. Got you this time.*

The Absol was bigger than her. A lot bigger. She could feel it through her knees, the size of the ribs, the slab of muscle along the back. It was like kneeling on a hillside. She leaned forward and put everything she had on it.

The pins and needles faded out of her legs. They felt like her own again.

— Nocturne —

Something landed on her back, two hard points that drove her chest and her burning belly into the ground, and then her left foreleg was caught and held.

She was pinned. Not once yet in this fight had anyone held her down, and the shock of it was almost worse than the weight: the Buizel, small and slim and hurt, kneeling on her.

Her lip lifted off her fangs. She turned her head, as far as it would go, and looked back at the orange shape over her with one red eye.

*You. Off.*

She was already working out how.

## beat 13
— Nocturne —

The foreleg. Everything else was pinned flat, but the foreleg was held only by two small paws, and one of them was weak. She could feel it: the right paw on her foreleg did not grip as hard as the left, and it flinched every time she moved.

She moved. Not with her body: just the foreleg. She twisted it at the elbow, turning it against the weak paw, and pulled, and pulled again, a hard, wrenching jerk, and on the third it came out. The moment it was free she swung it up and back, blind, all the force of her shoulder behind it, and felt it hit.

— Ripples —

The forepaw came round and caught her on the left shoulder, and it was not a slap. It was a club. It hit the joint square and knocked her sideways off the Absol's back, and the shoulder went bright and loud with pain, a deep grinding ache that sat in the socket and would not leave. She stumbled two steps and caught herself and stayed up. Barely.

She clutched the shoulder. Now both her arms hurt.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne rolled onto her front, gathered her legs under her, and pushed up off the grass against the boulder at the edge of the beach. She stood. Her belly burned. Her chest burned. Her right shoulder ached where the Buizel had hit it. But she stood, square, all four legs under her, and turned her head to look.

The Buizel was a body-length away with one paw clamped to her shoulder, breathing hard.

*That's twice I've had you down and once you've had me. Remember that.*

## beat 14
— Ripples —

She took her paw off her shoulder and set her feet.

She could not use the right arm for this. She knew that the moment she thought about it: it would not take the strain. So she lifted the left, despite the shoulder, despite everything it was telling her, and stiffened the paw into a blade, fingers flat and locked together, and stepped in.

The Absol's head was right there. Big and white and close, the horn rising off it, the eyes on hers. Ripples brought the paw down on top of it like an axe.

— Nocturne —

It came straight down onto the top of her skull, between her eyes and her horn, and the world flashed white.

Her legs buckled. Her chin hit the grass. The chop had come down through the bone and into her jaw, and the jolt had gone on down into the soft place at the front of her throat, and she could not tell which hurt worse, and then she could: her head. Her whole head. It was a bell that had been struck and would not stop ringing, a vast deep throb that filled her skull from the inside and pushed against her eyes.

She heard herself whine. A thin, high, frightened sound that did not belong to her.

*No. No. Get up. Don't you dare.*

She pushed. Her forelegs straightened, shaking. She got her chin off the grass. She stood there with her head hanging low and her eyes screwed half shut and swung it slowly, very slowly, toward the Buizel.

— Ripples —

Ripples stood with her paw still raised and watched the Absol come back up off the ground.

She had felt the skull through the edge of her paw. She had felt the head go down. And she had felt, too, how much it had cost: the right arm hanging, the left shoulder screaming at what she had made it do, her breath tearing in and out of her.

She lowered the paw.

*Still standing. Both of us. Still standing.*

## beat 15
— Nocturne —

Her head was still ringing. It did not matter. What mattered was that the Buizel was close, and had her weight back on her heels, and was tired.

Nocturne whipped round. The sickle tail came up off the ground in a long hard arc, steel-grey along its edge, and caught the Buizel under the jaw. The blow snapped the orange head back and lifted the whole slim body clean off its feet. For a moment the Buizel was in the air, rising, arms flung wide, the tails trailing, nothing under her at all.

Nocturne did not let her come down.

She swung her head back the other way and the horn flashed and something leapt off the blade of it: a thin curved shimmer of force, pale and bending the light, that cut through the air and took the Buizel across the belly and chest and thigh while she hung there helpless.

— Ripples —

She was flying. She was flying and she could not breathe and her jaw was on fire, and then something cut across her stomach and chest like a wire drawn tight and snapped, and she was not flying anymore. She was falling.

She came down on the grass from a height and she came down wrong.

Everything that was already hurt hit first. Her right arm, folded under her, took the ground with her whole weight on it. Her chest slammed down flat, the torn place where the claws had raked it grinding into the earth. Her neck, already bitten, snapped forward and her chin bounced. It was not three hurts. It was one, all at once, total, and it went through her like the lightning had, except that the lightning had stopped.

This did not stop.

She lay face-down and made a sound she had no control over. It was not a scream. It was lower and rawer and it came up out of her in long ragged pulls, again and again, with every breath. Her right arm would not move at all. Her chest was a wound the size of her whole body. Her neck would not lift her head. Her legs kicked feebly against the grass, on their own, as if trying to run from something that was inside her.

*Can't. Can't. Can't.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne stood over her and breathed.

She had felt the landing. She had heard the sound the Buizel made when she hit, and was still making now, low and broken and terrible, and something in her chest went tight at it. It was not pity. She did not know what it was.

She stepped back and gave it room.

## beat 16
— Ripples —

The sound stopped when she ran out of breath for it. She lay there, emptied, and slowly the clearing came back: the drip from the leaves, the warm steam off the grass, the shape of the Absol standing a body-length away.

She lifted her head. It was the hardest thing she had ever done. She looked at the Absol, and the Absol looked back, and Ripples opened her mouth and let the lake come out of her.

It was a thin jet, not much, all she had, and it went straight at the white chest.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne saw it come and turned her shoulder into it. The jet hit the round of her shoulder at an angle and glanced off, spraying away across the grass, and did nothing at all.

She shook the drops off and watched the Buizel try to get up.

— Ripples —

She got a paw on a root of the old oak and pushed, and her feet went out from under her on the wet grass and she went down again on her face.

She tried again. She got almost all the way up, hauling on the root with her good paw and pushing with her legs, and then her right arm swung against her side and her left shoulder buckled at the strain, and she dropped straight back down onto her knees, hard, with a gasp.

The third time she did it slowly. She crawled the last of the way to the oak and climbed it, paw over paw, leaning her whole body on the bark, and she stood. Her legs shook. Her sides heaved. Her right arm hung, and she held the left one close and did not use it for anything but holding on.

*Still standing. Still. Standing.*

## beat 17
— Nocturne —

Nocturne did not go after her. She had learned something about this Buizel already: hurt, she bit.

So she let her come. She set her feet wide on the steaming grass and sank her weight back onto her haunches, chest low, head level, every muscle wound and waiting. Her eyes did not leave the Buizel's face. Her ears were up. The sickle of her tail lay flat and still behind her.

*Come on, then. Come and get it. Whatever you send, you get back.*

The sun was out fully now, hot on her back, drying the last of the rain off her coat. Her head throbbed in time with her heart. Her belly burned when she breathed. She held still through all of it.

— Ripples —

Ripples leaned on the oak and looked at the Absol crouched and waiting, and did not like it.

It was too still. Everything about her said: try it.

## beat 18
— Ripples —

She tried it anyway. She had nothing else.

She let go of the oak and went in fast and low, mouth open, going for the right foreleg, the one nearest. Her teeth were bared. She meant to close them on bone and crush.

The Absol moved at the last instant: not away, but into her. A white shoulder came round and met her open jaws side-on, and her teeth skated off the round of it, and all her weight, everything she had thrown into the bite, went on past with nothing to stop it.

— Nocturne —

— Ripples —

Ripples squinted against the sudden glare. The heat came down on her wet fur and she could feel it drawing the water out of her.

## beat 42
— Nocturne —

She was still on the ground, but her head was up, and her horn worked.

— Nocturne —

## beat 43
— Nocturne —

The Buizel was close. Right there, a step away, where she had stepped in to kick.

— Ripples —

The first rake went across her stomach, and her stomach, which was already torn open inside, was ripped into something past pain. She screamed. It came out of her raw and cracking, and her whole body went rigid in the Absol's jaws, every limb locked for a heartbeat.

— Nocturne —

And then something happened inside her. A surge, from somewhere deep down, a hot rush that went through her whole body and lit it up. The pain did not go away. It just got smaller. Her legs firmed under her. Her breath came deeper. She could feel her heart going like a drum.

*Now. Now. While I have it.*

— Ripples —

She could not breathe. The rakes had knocked it out of her and she could not get it back; her chest heaved and heaved and nothing came. And she was folded over, bent double round her stomach, head down, and she could not straighten.

The Absol still had her arm. She hung from it.

## beat 44
— Ripples —

She had one thing she could do without breath. She closed her eyes and let a sheen come over her.

And she twisted. She wrenched her body against the jaws on her arm, turned and pulled and turned again, and felt the teeth slip a little, a fraction, and the grip was not as tight as it had been. She got a fuller breath. Her breath came back into her, ragged but real. She straightened, slowly, the hurt still there.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne felt the arm twist in her jaws and clamped down to hold it, and could not quite. It slipped. She still had it, but she did not have it the way she had.

The silvery sheen on the Buizel's fur caught the sun and threw it back into her eyes.

*What is that? What are you doing?*

## beat 45
— Nocturne —

She did not know what it was and she did not stop to find out. She had the surge still in her and the Buizel at point-blank, and she swung her head and let the horn go.

It was too much. Her body was tired under the surge and the swing was sloppy, badly timed; she threw it with everything and lost her footing on it, overbalanced, and had to catch herself. The thin shimmer of force came off her horn and went into the Buizel's head and chest and stomach at a range where it could not miss.

— Ripples —

It hit her head and her head rang. It hit her chest and her chest, which had been ruined since she had come down out of the air on it, burst into a pain so huge that a raw scream tore out of her and cracked in the middle, and her whole body jerked and clenched round it on its own. She could not stop it. She could not stop any of it now. Her legs kicked. Her free paw scrabbled at the air.

And then the sheen on her fur took the force, all of it, and threw it back.

— Nocturne —

It came back at her doubled. She saw it come off the Buizel's fur in a bright flash and then it was in her throat.

Her throat. Her ruined throat. The force went into it like a blade, and the pain was not a pain. It was the end of the world. She heard a sound she had never heard and realized it was her, a choked, high, splintering cry that went on and on. It caught her horn too, a hard knock she barely felt.

Her jaws came open. She did not decide to let go. She just did.

— Ripples —

She was free. And she could not breathe; the scream had taken everything. Her chest heaved on nothing.

She shoved the Absol away with her shoulder, as hard as she could, and stumbled back.

And then the surge came for her too. The same thing that had come for the Absol, she thought later: a hot rush from somewhere deep, flooding her whole body, lighting it up. The pain shrank. Her legs firmed. Her chest still heaved for air that would not come, but she was standing, and she was free.

Overhead, the white glare was going out of the sky. The fierce heat eased off and the ordinary sun came back, and the clouds began to drift in from the far shore again.

## beat 46
— Nocturne —

They were both on their feet, a body-length apart, and they were both done, or close to it, and Nocturne knew that whoever moved first had the next pin.

She moved first. She went in low and swept her tail through the Buizel's legs from the side, and the Buizel went down where she stood, hard, on her back. Nocturne heard her hit. Her upper back and her lower back, both, slammed into the ground.

And then Nocturne was on top of her. Both forepaws on the chest. Her hind paw on the left thigh. The same as the very first pin, a lifetime ago at the start of this.

*Again. Again. Hold her this time. Hold her.*

The surge was gone. She felt it go as she came down on the Buizel, draining out of her and leaving all the pain behind it at full size. Her balance was back. Her strength was not.

— Ripples —

She was on her back again. The paws were on her chest again.

Her chest. Her ruined chest, the place that had burst when the sheen threw back the blow, the place that had been torn and crushed and torn again. And now the Absol's whole weight was on it.

Ripples' breath came back to her, ragged, all at once. She pulled it in against the weight and let it out in a sound that was not a word. She had almost nothing left. She was barely holding on to herself.

*Not like this. Not now. Not after everything.*

## beat 47
— Ripples —

She tried. She twisted, hard, to one side, trying to turn under the paws and get her shoulder beneath the Absol's chest. It was everything she had left, and it was not much. It was slow. It was late. She felt the Absol feel it coming.

The paws pressed down on her chest.

It was not a blow. It did not need to be. Her chest was so ruined that the slightest touch there was agony, and this was not a touch; it was the Absol leaning, the whole white weight of her driving down through two forepaws into the place where Ripples hurt most. Her body went off on its own. It jerked under the paws, again and again, and a raw sound tore out of her with every breath, and her free paw clawed at the Absol's foreleg trying to pull it away, and she had not told it to do any of that. She could not have stopped it if she had tried.

Her left thigh, under the hind paw, had started to throb too, deep and heavy.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne held on. The Buizel bucked and jerked under her paws and made that sound with every breath, and Nocturne held on and did not lean off.

She was running out of breath herself. She could feel it. Her own chest burning, her legs shaking, her ruined shoulders screaming at the effort of holding the weight on her forepaws. The pin was slipping, not because the Buizel was strong but because Nocturne was weak. She pressed down harder and it held, for now. Less surely than it had.

*Hold. Hold. Just a little more. Just hold.*

## beat 48
— Ripples —

Her left leg was trapped under the Absol's hind paw. Her right was free. She found the ground with it, braced, and twisted the trapped leg, wrenched it against the weight, and it came loose.

And she drove both feet up into the Absol's belly.

— Nocturne —

The kick went into her belly where the first kick had gone, a whole fight ago, and something in there that had been hanging on by a thread let go.

She came off the Buizel and staggered back on her feet, all four legs splayed, and stood there with her head down. The pain in her belly was not pain. It was a void. *Still up. Still up. How am I still up.*

— Ripples —

She rolled onto her side and tried to get up. Her legs were numb, both of them, pins and needles, slow to answer. Her right arm and her left paw gave out under her and she dropped back down on the grass. She tried again and dropped again. The third try she hardly moved.

The surge had gone out of her. She felt it go, and felt everything it had been holding back come in after it: the chest, the stomach, the nose, the arms, all at once, full size.

She lay at the edge of the sand and breathed.

## beat 49
— Ripples —

She could not get up. She tried; her right arm and her crushed left paw would not take her weight, and she went down on her face. She tried again. Shorter. Weaker. The third time she barely lifted her head.

The numbness went out of her legs at last. They were her own again. It did not help.

— Nocturne —

She was ahead. Barely. She had made the last of the attacks; the Buizel was on the ground. But Nocturne was running out of fight, and she knew it, and she could feel the Buizel knowing it too.

— Both —

A long low swell came rolling in across the shallows, the kind of thing that comes from nowhere on a big lake on a calm day, and it reached the beach and kept coming. — Ripples —

Ripples lay in the wet sand with the lake running off her and laughed, once, a cracked little sound.

## beat 50
— Ripples —

She got her good foot onto the sand at the waterline and pushed, and it slid out from under her, and she went down.
