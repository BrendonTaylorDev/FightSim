— Nocturne —

Nocturne leaned on the boulder and breathed. That was all she did. She had nothing else to do it with.

Each breath went down her throat like swallowing glass. Her neck throbbed. Her head throbbed. Her chest and belly burned. She stood with her shoulder against the warm stone and her head hanging and let the air in and out, in and out, and listened to the blood thundering in her ears slowly quiet.

She was still ahead. She knew that, somewhere at the back of her head. The Buizel had taken more than she had. But the last few exchanges had all been the Buizel's, and Nocturne had been giving ground, and her body told her so with every breath.

— Ripples —

Ripples stood a little way off with her ruined paw clutched to her chest and did not go in. She could not. She was breathing as hard as the Absol was.

They stood and looked at each other across the grass in the hot sun, and neither moved.

*Sore. Cross. Not done. Not even close.*

— Nocturne —

She saw it coming at her neck and her neck was ruined, and she flinched before it landed, tucking her head down, curling her shoulder up, turning her whole body to cover her throat without ever deciding to. It left her back open. It did not save her neck.

The chop came down on the side of her neck and the world went white.

It was not as hard as the last one. The Buizel's arm could not put it in as hard. It did not matter. The neck was so hurt that anything would have been too much, and this was not anything, and the jolt of it went straight into her throat and she made a noise she had never made before, a choked, high, broken sound that came out of her crushed throat in pieces. It went into her left shoulder too, a heavy thump she hardly felt next to the rest.

Her neck felt thin now. That was the only word for it. As if there was very little left between the outside and everything that mattered.

And then, under all of it, breath. Real breath, all the way down. The last of the tightness from the coils let go of her and the blood came back into her head in a rush and her sight cleared.

She stood there with her head tucked down and her shoulder hunched up round her throat and breathed.
