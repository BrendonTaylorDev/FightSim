# Swift vs Gale: the clock tower

Two Swellows in the machine room of the clock tower (scene 10), pins on. Seed 4, 24 beats, played by
`drive.py` / `plan.py` through the real engine. Every hit, pain level, landing, get-up, roll and the ending
below is what the engine resolved (beat files `b01.txt`–`b24.txt` hold the full screen and the narrator's
notes). The prose is mine, written as the target for what the narrator should be able to do with those notes.

---

## Beat 1

The room was never quiet. Behind the great pale disc of the clock face the gears turned along the north wall,
tooth into tooth, a steady brass *clack, clack* that never hurried, and in the well at the room's heart the
pendulum swung: a weight the size of a boulder on its long iron rod, sweeping out of the shadow on one side,
across the light, and away into the shadow on the other. The whole tower ticked with it. You could feel the
tick in the boards.

Gale stood on the planking near the gear housing with her wings folded and her head tilted, watching the young
one with one dark eye. She did not move. She had the stillness of a bird that has done this before.

Swift could not have stood still if the tower had depended on it. She hopped once along the beam of the bell
frame, flicked her forked tail open and shut, and then she simply went: off the frame in a streak of glossy
blue, up past the hanging loops of chain, over at the top and down, the whole length of the room in a heartbeat,
and into the older bird's breast with a flat, hard *thock*.

It knocked a short breath out of Gale. The blow sank into the cream-white feathers of her chest and left a
deep, spreading soreness there, and a smaller jolt ran up into her right shoulder; she rocked on her feet,
claws scraping on the oak, but she did not give ground. Swift was already gone, climbing away with her wings
pumping, chirping, high and piercing and pleased with herself. Gale shook out her feathers once, slowly, and
watched her go.

## Beat 2

Gale let the young one have her moment. Then she dropped off the planking, opened her wings and was in the air
without seeming to have tried: one low turn under the sweep of the pendulum, close enough that the moving air
of it stirred her plumes, and she came round and up on Swift's left side as the young bird banked.

They crossed at speed. Gale's wing cut across Swift's left wing as they passed, a hard, slicing stroke that
caught the long flight feathers near the joint, and Swift felt it as a stinging line of pain that went right
through to the bone and settled there, throbbing. A lesser jolt caught her chest and her left shoulder in the
same instant. She lurched in the air, lost a wingbeat, found it again. Then they were past each other and
turning, two blue shapes wheeling in the brass-coloured light, and Swift's left wing ached every time she
brought it down.

## Beat 3

"Kek," said Gale, low, under the clack of the gears. It might have been a laugh.

Swift wheeled tight round the swinging weight, so close her wingtip nearly touched the iron rod, and came
straight back at her. The two of them closed with all of both their speeds in it, and Swift clubbed her wing
across Gale's head as they crossed.

It was a heavy, ringing blow. Gale's head snapped aside with it, the hurt flaring out across her crown and
settling in deep and hot behind her eyes, and the edge of it caught her beak too, a sharp knock that clicked it
shut. She kept her wings. Swift went screaming up toward the roof beams, tail streaming, and Gale turned her
head slowly back, blinking, and found her again.

## Beat 4

Gale did not chase her. She hung where she was, in the slow column of air under the clock face, and fanned her
wings once, hard.

The blade of air she sent after the young bird hummed as it went. It caught Swift across the breast and then
across the long scissor-feathers of her tail, and she felt it in both places at once: a stinging, spreading
bruise in her chest, already sore from the pass before, and a strange, wrenching jolt in her tail that threw
her flight off its line for a moment. Her forked tail flared and twisted, the two long feathers flexing
against the air. She dropped a body's length, caught it, and came level, panting with her beak open.

## Beat 5

Swift came over the top of her.

She had built her speed in two long passes around the room and she spent all of it now: wings drawn back and
gone hard as steel, grey light running along their edges, and as she crossed above Gale she raked one down
across the older bird's back. The impact was a brutal, metallic crack. It drove into the muscle between Gale's
shoulders, and a lesser shock went through the small of her back behind it.

Gale's whole body jolted forward in the air. The pain came up between her wings in a deep, burning band, and
for a stroke she flew crooked with it, one wing short. Then she straightened. She clicked her beak, once, the
small dry sound almost lost in the tick of the tower, and sleeked her feathers flat.

## Beat 6

They passed again at speed, and this time Gale turned her head as the young one came by.

It was the perfect moment, and she had been waiting for it. She snatched at the long feathers streaming back
from Swift's head and missed them by a hair, and her beak closed instead on Swift's own beak, side-on, with
every bit of both their speeds behind it: a snatching, wrenching strike that jerked the young bird's head round
and wrenched her whole body off its line.

Swift did not even cry out. The shock of it went straight back through her beak into her skull, a white,
ringing jolt that jarred her whole head, and her wings forgot what they were doing. The air let go of her. One
moment it was there under her, solid and familiar; the next she was dropping, wings grabbing at nothing, the
room wheeling past in a smear of brass and pale light. She struck the oak planking flat on her back, a hard,
jarring blow across her shoulders and her left thigh, and the force of it carried her on: she went skidding and
tumbling across the rough, splintery boards, wings flailing, rolling over a shoulder and a thigh and the small
of her back, until at last she came to a stop out in the open and lay there on her back, chest heaving, the
roof beams turning slowly above her.

Her beak throbbed. It was a deep, bone-heavy ache that ran back from the tip into her face, and every breath
she drew across it made her eyes water. Somewhere above her, in the tick and the clack, Gale was circling.

## Beat 7

For a moment Swift only lay and breathed.

The pendulum swept over the well beside her, slow and unstoppable, and its shadow crossed her and went on. She
could hear Gale up there, the soft, unhurried stroke of her wings, not coming down. Waiting. Of course she was
waiting.

Swift rolled onto her front. Her wings fanned out against the planking, braced, and she gathered her feet
under her and pushed up. She came up quickly, all at once, the way she did everything, and shook herself hard
from head to tail. Her breathing was rough. She held her head a little low, beak working open and shut as if
she could work the ache out of it, and she kept her weight off the thigh that had hit the boards first. But
she was up, and she lifted her eyes to find the older bird against the light of the clock face, and her forked
tail flicked open and shut, open and shut.

*All right,* she thought. *All right. Again.*

## Beat 8

She went straight up off the boards in a blur of blue and met Gale coming round.

They closed on each other head-on, and Swift's whole body hit the older bird's breast in the same place she had
hit it at the start, right where it was already sore. The pain went into Gale like a fist into a bruise. It
spread deep through her chest and drove the breath out of her in a hoarse, hissing whistle she could not keep
in, and a jolt went up her neck behind it.

Gale stayed in the air. She flew on for three strokes with her chest heaving, her breath coming short and
careful around the hurt, and when she turned her head to find the young one again there was something new in
the way she held herself, a stiffness through the breast, as if she were flying around the place that hurt.

## Beat 9

Gale went up after her and turned inside her.

Swift saw it coming and raked at her as they crossed, a short, vicious slash of her wing that caught the older
bird's right shoulder and jarred it; but Gale did not flinch from it. She came on through it with her wing
gone hard and grey as steel, all of her speed behind it, three passes of it, and she brought it down across
Swift's left wing, the wing she had already cut.

The impact went through it like a dropped weight through thin ice. Swift heard the feathers rattle against
each other and felt the wing jar back on its joint, and then the pain arrived: not a sting any more but a
great white burst of it, so much that her whole body seized around it and her breath came back to her in
pieces. A second, lesser shock thumped into her chest. Her left wing would not lie right. She held it out a
little from her body, the feathers along it standing awry, and when she drove it down to stay in the air the
pain flared up through the joint into her shoulder so sharply that she cried out, high and broken, and her
flight lurched and wavered and held.

She stayed up. Every stroke of that wing hurt, and she made every one of them anyway.

Gale came down to the boards behind her and stood there, breathing hard through her open beak, favouring the
shoulder the young one had raked.

## Beat 10

Swift climbed. She went all the way up to the roof beams, where the drive chains hung in loops in the dark, and
she folded there and dropped.

Gale saw her coming the whole way. As the young bird went past her she struck out with her wing and caught the
very tip of Swift's left wing, a sharp, stinging knock on the wing that already hurt worst; but the dive did
not waver. Swift came down in a blaze of blue light, her whole body thrown at the older bird like a stone, and
she hit her low and hard, in the thigh and the belly.

The blow was enormous. It drove into Gale's left thigh with a force that seemed to go right through the leg and
into the boards beneath it, and a sick, deep pain followed it up into her belly. Gale was shoved back a step by
it, claws skidding on the oak; she did not fall. She stood on the leg and it shook under her. When she tried to
shift her weight she could not quite make herself do it, and she held the leg half lifted, trembling, her beak
open, a thin whistle of pain hissing out through it.

The blow cost Swift too. The force of it came back into her as she struck: up through her neck in a hard,
wrenching jolt, and into her chest, already bruised, so that she came out of the dive gasping and landed on the
planking a few lengths away, stumbling, her head low and her wings half spread for balance. For a moment the
two of them stood on the boards and panted at each other across the sweep of the pendulum.

## Beat 11

Gale hopped back to the gear housing and settled on its edge, where the great wheels turned behind her and the
clack of their teeth filled the air. She stood there with her hurt leg drawn up under her feathers and her
beak open, breathing slowly, deliberately, and she clicked her beak once, a small dry sound.

She watched the young one. She tilted her head and watched her with one eye, the way she would watch an insect
she had not decided to take yet. Swift's left wing hung a little low. Swift's beak was working, open and shut,
open and shut, as if something in it would not settle. Swift could not keep still.

*Good,* Gale thought. *Spend it.*

## Beat 12

Swift did not wait for the wing to stop hurting. She was hurt and angry and showing it, and she went at the
older bird across the boards in a low, furious rush and threw herself at her head.

It was reckless, and it worked. Her body struck Gale's head with all the fury of being hurt behind it, right
where the club of her wing had landed before, and the pain of it went into Gale's skull like a nail. It was far
worse than the first time. Her head was knocked aside, the gears and the bell and the pale clock face lurching
past her in a smear, and the edge of the blow caught her right eye and filled it with stinging tears. She
staggered. Her claws skidded on the edge of the housing and she stumbled, swaying, her head ringing, the world swimming around her in slow, sick circles; she could not quite make it hold still.

Swift backed off a step, panting, tail flicking. She had heard the older bird cry out that time.

## Beat 13

Gale shook her head once, hard, and the room steadied.

Then she did something the young one did not expect. She went up off the boards into the air, still unsteady,
and turned at the top and came down on Swift with everything she had left: all of her hurt in it, all of it at
once.

It hit Swift full in the chest. There was a hard, flat sound, a terrible jolt that drove every bit of breath out
of her, and the pain came pouring in after it: through her whole breast and up into her throat, deep and
crushing, so much more than she had known could be in one place. It caught her left wing too, the ruined one,
and sent a white flare of agony through it. She was smashed down out of her stance and onto the planks, flat on
her back, and the landing slammed across her shoulders and drove her hurt wing into the boards beneath her.

She lay there. She could not, for a moment, get any air into her at all. And then something strange happened in
her left wing: the pain in it simply stopped, and in its place came a cold, wrong numbness, as if the wing had
gone away from her and left only a heavy, tingling weight behind. She could not feel it. She could not tell
where it was.

Gale climbed away above her, and the stagger was gone from her flight.

## Beat 14

Gale came down on her again from above, a quick, stooping dive, and struck at her head as she lay.

Swift twisted, and it saved her nothing: the beak meant for her head came down on her own beak instead, on the
place that already ached, and drove her head back into the oak. The pain was immense. It went back through her
face in a deep, sickening wave, far beyond the ache that had been there, and the jolt of her skull hitting the
planking rang through her whole head behind it. She heard herself make a sound, a thin, broken shriek.

Then Gale was gone again, climbing, and Swift had to get up.

She tried. She got her good wing under her and pushed, and her strength went out of her halfway and dropped her
back down hard on the boards, the jolt of it jarring her. She lay still for a breath. She tried again, and sank
back again, softer this time, her legs not ready. The third time she did not try to be quick. She pushed
straight up, slowly, every muscle in her trembling with it, and she stayed up. She stood swaying on the
planking with her numb wing hanging and her head held low, her beak half open, and she could not quite close it
without the pain flaring.

## Beat 15

Swift went up anyway.

It was the only thing she knew how to do. She drove herself off the boards on one good wing and one numb one,
lopsided and labouring, and caught Gale as the older bird came round, and hit her across the small of the back
on the pass, a hard blow where Swift's steel wing had raked her before. Gale grunted and flew crooked with it.

But staying in the air had cost Swift something terrible. Every stroke of her left wing was a stroke on a wing
she could not feel, and she drove it anyway, harder and harder to keep from falling, and somewhere in that
straining she could feel, through the numbness, a deep and growing wrongness in the joint. She flew in short,
jerking strokes, her whole body tilting with each one. She did not have much left up here, and both of them knew
it.

## Beat 16

Gale dropped on her from above and took hold.

She came down with her feet out and her hooked, black-tipped talons spread, and she closed them on the young
bird's neck. They sank in through the red feathers of the throat-side and locked there.

And then there were two of them in the air and neither one flying. Their wings hammered out of time, feathers
tangling and slapping against each other, Swift thrashing, Gale holding on, and the whole knot of them went
down through the air together, spinning, the room turning over around them, the pendulum sweeping past, until
the planking came up under them and they landed on it, hard, on their feet, still locked. Swift's legs nearly
folded under her with the jolt.

The grip on her neck tightened.

It was not a crush, not yet. It was a band drawn round her, front and sides, the talons pressing in on her
windpipe and on the great vessels of her neck at once, and she felt both of them go: her next breath had to
fight its way through, a narrow, whistling pull that brought her only half of what she needed, and at the same
time a slow, heavy pressure began to swell up into her head, a fullness behind her eyes, her own pulse
beginning to pound in her ears. She strained her neck against the grip and her eyes watered with the effort.

And her left wing came back to her. The numbness drained out of it all at once and the pain came flooding into
the space it left, worse than it had ever been, and a raw sound tore out of her that the grip on her throat cut
down to a gasp.

## Beat 17

Gale did not let her get used to it. She folded her wings, bore down, and drove the young bird down onto the
boards with the grip.

Swift hit the planking on her back and her head struck the oak behind her with a sharp, flat crack that filled
her vision with sparks. The landing jarred hard across her shoulders. The talons were wrenched loose from her neck by
the force of it, and the pressure in her head drained away all at once, and she lay on her back on the boards
dragging in air, great whooping, ragged breaths of it, the roof beams swinging above her.

Gale stood over her. Her chest heaved, her hurt leg trembled, her right eye was still half closed and weeping;
she did not look like a bird who was winning. But she was standing, and the young one was not.

## Beat 18

Gale struck down at her throat. Swift twisted her head, and the beak meant for her throat snatched instead at
the long feathers streaming back from her head and wrenched them, a sharp, tearing pull that dragged her head
round. Her ruined beak struck the boards with it, and a fresh wave of agony went through her face, and the
strike's edge caught her throat and her head as well, driving her skull back into the oak.

Swift tried to get up. She tried three times. The first time she got her good wing under her and her body
simply did not follow it. The second time she pushed and nothing happened at all. The third time she got as far
as lifting her head from the planks, trying to come up to sitting, and her strength left her there and laid her
back down.

And then, from somewhere very deep, something in her came up burning. It was not strength exactly. It was
fury, bright and hot and wordless, flooding through every muscle she had left, and her heart was suddenly
hammering and her eyes were suddenly clear, and she lay on her back on the boards with her beak open and her
good wing spread and glared up at the older bird with everything she had.

*Not you. Not from you.*

## Beat 19

Gale dropped onto her before she could rise.

One foot came down on the young bird's breast, right on the place that had taken Endeavor, and Gale put her
full weight on it; the other foot closed round her throat. Swift felt the talons settle in a ring around it and
lock, and the weight bore down on her chest, and the boards pressed back up from underneath, and every way she
might have moved was suddenly closed. Her breath went out of her in a hard, startled huff and only half of it
came back.

It hurt everywhere at once. The press on her chest was a deep, grinding agony on bruises that were already
beyond bearing, and the grip on her throat squeezed both ways: the front of her throat flattened under it so
that her breath whistled thin, and the sides of her neck held, so that the pressure began to climb in her head
again. She lay pinned on her back on the oak planking, her wings spread out on either side of her, with the
older bird standing on her, and above them the pendulum swept on, slow and unstoppable, and the tower ticked.

## Beat 20

Swift bucked. The surge was still in her, burning, and she threw her whole body up against the weight on her
chest in one great heave.

It went nowhere. Gale rode it out, sinking with it, pressing down as it passed, and when it was spent she
closed her foot on the young one's throat a little tighter, deliberately, as an answer. The talons drove in.
Swift felt her windpipe narrow to a thread under them; she felt the blood held back in her head, the fullness
swelling, a roaring beginning in her ears. Her breath came now in short, rasping sips that never filled her,
and her chest heaved for more than it was given.

Her struggles were weaker already. She knew it and hated it. The weight above her had not changed at all.

## Beat 21

Swift kicked. She drove her small grey feet up at the older bird's belly, raking with her claws, and they
scrabbled against feathers and found nothing to hold.

Gale bore down. The grip on the throat closed another degree, unhurried, and this time it took nearly all of
it: the air would barely come at all, a high, thin whistle squeezed through the hold, and the pressure in her
head had become a world of its own, the drumming in her ears, the heat in her face, the slow, frightening
narrowing of everything. Somewhere in the middle of it the fury that had held her up went out of her like a
lamp. She felt it go. What was left was much smaller.

"Stop," said Gale, very low, almost gently. "It's done."

## Beat 22

It was not done. Swift wrenched at the wing trapped under her, trying to drag it free and get it under her for
leverage, and the effort tore a fresh burst of agony out of her chest under Gale's weight, and the wing did not
come.

There was no air now. Her chest heaved against the weight and drew on nothing, again and again, the throat
crushed shut under the talons; and at the same time the blood to her head stayed held back, and the grey began.
It crept in at the edges of her sight, soft and heavy. The roar in her ears had grown so loud it swallowed the
clack of the gears, the hiss of the steam whistle by the far wall, the tick of the tower. She could still see
the clock face, a great pale disc of light, but it seemed very far away now, and it seemed to be getting
smaller.

Gale's leg shook under her. She held on.

## Beat 23

Swift bridged. She drove her head back against the boards and heaved her chest up against the foot on it with
the last of her strength, a long, shuddering arch of her whole body.

It lifted nothing. Gale leaned into it and let it spend itself, and closed her grip once more, and the talons
sank deeper round the throat, and the arch went out of Swift's body all at once and she sagged back flat onto
the oak.

She was very far down now. Her struggles had become small and slow, and between them she lay with her chest
jerking on empty air and the grey rolling in from every side. The pressure in her head had turned heavy and
warm. The pain was still there, all of it, the chest and the wing and the beak and the throat, but it had
begun to feel like something happening a long way off, to someone else.

## Beat 24

She kicked out once more. It was barely a twitch of her feet against the older bird's belly, and it ended
almost before it began.

Then the last of her air was gone, and the blood to her head had been held back too long, and the light
narrowed to a single point in the middle of the pale clock face, and the point went out. Her body went slack
under Gale's feet. Her good wing, which had been pressing against the boards, relaxed and lay flat. Her head
rolled a little to one side, her beak fell half open, and her eyes slid half closed and stayed there.

Gale felt it go through the grip: the throat under her talons going soft, the body under her foot going still.
She held a moment longer to be sure. Then she opened her foot.

Swift's chest jerked, and jerked again, and then the air went into her, a long, ragged, unconscious breath, and
another after it. She did not wake. She lay on her back on the oak planking with her ruined wing spread out
beside her, breathing slow and deep, out cold.

Gale stepped off her. She stood over the young one on her trembling leg, her own breath whistling through her
open beak, her right eye still weeping, and for a while she did nothing at all. Then she fanned her tail open,
slowly, all the way, and let it close.

Behind her the pendulum swept across the well, out of the light and into the shadow, and the tower ticked.
