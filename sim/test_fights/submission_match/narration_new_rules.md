# Ripples vs Nerine — submission match, the lakeside clearing

## Beat 1 — The terms, and the throat

They had agreed the terms on the walk down through the pines, in the short, clipped way two fighters agree anything.
No knockouts. Nobody would win by putting the other one to sleep with a lucky blow, however hard it landed. It ended
when one of them gave up, a paw slapping the ground, a tail beating the grass. Or it ended when one of them went
under in a hold and didn't come back up until it was over. Nothing else counted. Not who was bleeding, not who was
ahead. Only who quit.

That changed everything about how they looked at each other.

The clearing was warm and drowsy in the afternoon. The grass was long and soft underfoot, the lake lay flat and
bright beyond the sand, and the pines dropped their resin smell over everything. A dragonfly hung over the shallows.
Neither of them looked at any of it.

Nerine looked at Ripples' legs. She looked at the knees and the long muscle of the thighs, at the way Ripples set
her feet, the left a little ahead of the right. She looked at everything a leg could do and everything that could be
done to one.

Ripples looked at Nerine's throat: the bare cream fur of it, no sac, nothing to pad it, and the way Nerine held her
chin high and cocky, as if she'd never once had to protect it.

*She doesn't guard it,* Ripples thought. *She thinks she doesn't need to.*

Ripples moved first, which surprised them both. She was the patient one. She was supposed to wait.

She didn't wait. She stepped in on the diagonal, left foot planting, and swept her right forearm across Nerine's neck
in a flat, backhanded arc. The hard blue edge of the fin led the way, still wet from the lake. It caught Nerine's
throat with a raspy swish that parted the cream fur in a clean line, and the jolt of it carried on into the neck
behind. A sheet of water came off the fin with it and soaked Nerine's chest and shoulders.

Nerine huffed, a short, cross little *Hff*, more surprise than pain. For a moment she held her head very still, the
way you hold a cup you're afraid of spilling. Then she swallowed, carefully, and felt the line the fin had left: a
thin, cold sting across the front of her throat, already fading.

The water had done almost nothing. She was a river Buizel; water ran off her the way it ran off a stone. But that
wasn't what Ripples had wanted, and they both knew it. She'd wanted to touch the throat first. She'd wanted Nerine to
feel exactly where this was going to end.

Nerine shook herself hard, a full-body shake from ears to tail tips, and droplets flew off her in a bright spray. Her
cheek marks twitched as she grinned.

*You want my neck?* she thought. *Fine. You can try. I'm having your leg.*

She said nothing. She didn't have to. She let her eyes drop, very deliberately, to Ripples' left knee, and let them
stay there long enough to be noticed.

Ripples noticed. Something cold settled in her stomach and stayed.

## Beat 2 — Knee to the thigh

Nerine didn't bother with anything clever. She stepped straight into Ripples' guard, took a fistful of fur at her
shoulder to hold her still, and drove her knee up into the meat of the left thigh.

It landed with a solid whump, like a sack of wet sand dropped from a height. Ripples felt the knee hit the long
muscle on the outside of her leg and sink into it. For an instant the whole thigh went dead and heavy. Then it came
back as a dull, deep throb that spread out from the point of the knee like ripples from a stone. The jolt carried up
into her hip and rattled the joint in its socket.

She sucked a quick breath in through her nose and snatched the leg up off the ground on reflex, the way you lift a
foot off something hot. For one step she was standing on the right leg alone. Then she made herself put the left one
down again, carefully. It held. It ached, and it held.

Nerine let go of her shoulder and stepped back out of reach, light on the balls of her feet, and watched her put the
leg down.

It wasn't much. A bruise starting deep in the thigh, a little stiffness in the hip. A fighter could carry that all
day. But Nerine had felt it give under her knee, that soft, deep give of muscle that wasn't ready for it. She smiled
a narrow, private smile.

*Left one,* she thought. *I'll remember.*

Ripples remembered it too. She stood with her weight shifted a fraction to the right without quite deciding to, and
her eyes went to Nerine's knees instead of her paws, and stayed there.

*So that's how it's going to be,* she thought. *Fine. My leg against your throat. Let's see who runs out first.*

## Beat 3 — Thrown to the water, and the gust

Nerine came in low and fast, ducking under the fin before Ripples could bring it round again. She wrapped both arms
round Ripples' middle, planted her feet, and threw her.

It was a hip throw, quick and brutal, and the clearing turned over. For a moment Ripples saw the sky beneath her and
the grass above, the pines hanging upside down. Then the root-knotted ground came up and hit her across the upper
back and the left shoulder. A grunt was driven out of her, and the shoulder flared, and the roots dug into her spine.
She bounced. Her left hip struck the grass next, and the bruised thigh slapped the ground behind it with a jolt that
made her eyes water.

And she kept going. The throw had too much in it to stop. She rolled over and over down the slope toward the water, a
loose orange tumble of limbs and tails. Her right shoulder clipped a root, her thigh scraped across the ground again,
the beach went past in a blur of pale sand. Then she was in the shallows, and the roll stopped all at once in a great
fountain of spray. The warm water closed over her hip and the small of her back.

She lay on her back in the knee-deep water and stared up at the sky. Fat white clouds drifted across it. Water ran
into her ears. Every bruise she'd just collected announced itself one at a time: back, shoulder, hip, and the thigh,
the thigh, the thigh.

*Get up,* she thought. *Not yet. Breathe first.*

Nerine came down the slope after her at a jog, already reaching. And then the lake did something nobody had asked it
to.

A gust came off the water, hard, the kind that comes from nowhere on a still afternoon. It swept across the shallows
and up the beach. It flattened the long grass in a silver wave, turned the leaves of the oaks pale side up, and
threw a whole sheet of spray off the surface of the lake straight into Nerine's face. She flinched and threw up a paw,
half-blind, spluttering, and stopped where she was.

For a moment they both just looked at the lake.

*Even the water,* Ripples thought, lying in it, and a laugh came out of her that was half a cough. *Even the water's
on my side.*

Nerine wiped her eyes and glared at the lake as if it had cheated. Then she looked back at Ripples, lying there soaked
and grinning, and her own grin came back, sharper than before.

## Beat 4 — Out of the water

Ripples rolled onto her front in the shallows, got her paws flat on the sandy bottom, and pushed herself up. Water
poured off her in sheets, out of her fur, off her tails and down her legs. For a moment she just stood in the warm
water up to her knees and let her breath settle.

Then she walked out of the lake.

She did it slowly, and she did it without limping. She made very sure of that. Each step up the wet sand she put the
left foot down exactly as she put the right, though the thigh throbbed every time it took her weight, and the hip
ground faintly in its socket. She kept her face still. She kept her tails up.

The last of the gust's spray dried off Nerine's fur in the sun. She stood at the top of the beach and watched Ripples
come up out of the water, and she watched the left leg the whole way.

*She doesn't favour it,* Nerine thought. *Not yet. She's good at hiding it.*

A pause, and then the thought finished itself, colder.

*She will.*

## Beat 5 — The root trip, blocked

Nerine went for the leg again almost at once. She dropped low, hooked her foot behind Ripples' left heel, and drove
her shoulder forward to tip her backward over the humped root behind her. It was a dirty trip, the sort that put you
on your back with your leg twisted under you.

But Ripples had been watching for exactly that. She'd been watching for nothing else since the knee.

She didn't pull away from it, which was what Nerine expected. She turned into it, swung her right arm down across her
body, and caught the shoulder on her forearm before it reached her chest. The trip became a barge, the barge met the
arm, and they thumped together with a meaty smack. It was solid, but it was nothing like what it should have been.
Her forearm took all of it. Her heel stayed planted. Her leg stayed under her.

She ducked her head and barked once, *Bwah*, half pain and half sheer indignation. Then she shoved Nerine off her
arm.

They stood apart, both breathing a little harder. Ripples shook out her right arm and felt it tingle from shoulder
to paw, a bright, buzzing ache.

*No,* she thought, very clearly. *You don't get the leg for free. Not every time.*

Nerine rolled her shoulder, looked at the arm that had blocked her, and nodded slowly to herself. *She's reading me.
All right. Then I'll make her look somewhere else.*

## Beat 6 — The ear

So Nerine took the head instead.

She feinted low with a dip of her shoulder that made Ripples' eyes drop to the leg. Then she whipped her right paw up
and round, and the chop caught Ripples just above the right ear with a meaty smack.

It was a heavy blow, and Ripples heard it from the inside, a dull crack that went right through her skull. A groan was
dragged up out of her chest before she could stop it. Her paw went up toward the ear on its own and stopped halfway.
She didn't want to show it, and she wouldn't let her paw finish the job. The ear was ringing, a high, thin whine like
a mosquito trapped inside her head. The force of it had run on down into her skull and her neck. She coughed, once,
dry, and swallowed.

When she shook her head, the ringing came with it, and the world swung a little and settled.

*Clever,* she thought sourly. *Make me watch the leg, then hit everything else.*

The last of the lake dried out of her fur in the warm sun, leaving it fluffed and spiky. Nerine bounced on her toes a
few lengths away, grinning, flexing the paw that had done it.

*Two to one,* Nerine thought. *Keep score, little river otter. I am.*

## Beat 7 — The chop that missed the neck

Ripples hit back with the same chop, almost to spite her. She drove the edge of her paw straight at the side of
Nerine's neck.

Nerine tucked her chin hard at the last instant, the way she hadn't bothered to before, and the chop came down on top
of her head instead. A solid whump. The force jarred down into her nose and through her skull into the neck beneath.
Her eyes went blink-blink-blink, watering, and she exhaled sharply through her teeth, lips drawn back off them.

She shook it off. It hadn't been nothing, but it hadn't been the throat either.

Ripples stepped back and watched her, and something in her chest sank and steadied at the same time.

*She tucks it now,* she thought. *She learned that from one hit. She knows exactly what I'm after.* And then,
grudgingly: *So she's not stupid. Just cocky.*

Nerine rubbed the top of her head with the heel of her paw and looked at Ripples with new eyes. *Still going for the
neck,* she thought. *Even when it costs her. All right. I know where your eyes are, too.*

## Beat 8 — The water down her throat

Ripples came in again with the fin. This time she went for the throat directly, a short, upward rake meant to slip
under the tucked chin.

Nerine twisted her head away and the edge snicked across her jaw instead, then glanced on into her head and grazed
the throat beneath. The sound of it was sharp and small, like claws catching on hide and pulling free.

But this time the fin was dripping. Ripples had dragged it through the wet grass by the shore on the way in, and a
whole skin of lake water came off it with the slash. Nerine had her mouth open, breathing hard through it, and the
water went straight in. It went up her nose and down her throat at once, and she choked.

It was a horrible, ugly sound: a strangled cough, a spit, then a gasp that dragged in more water before it found any
air. She doubled over, coughing so hard her whole body jerked with it, water streaming out of her nose and mouth, her
eyes running. Her nose burned all the way up into her skull. She couldn't stop. Every time she tried to breathe in,
the cough came back up and took the breath away.

She was soaked to the skin again too, dripping and heavy.

Ripples saw her choking and didn't hesitate. *Now,* she thought. *Now, while she can't breathe. Get her on the
ground and get to that neck.* She closed the distance in two quick steps, reaching.

## Beat 9 — The slam

It was a mistake, and she knew it the instant Nerine's arms closed round her.

Nerine had stopped coughing just long enough. Water still running from her nose, eyes still streaming, she got under
Ripples' arm, drove her shoulder into her middle, and lifted her clean off her feet. For one long, dizzy moment
Ripples hung in the air with nothing under her, her tails thrashing for balance. Then Nerine brought her straight
down onto her back in the grass.

Her upper back hit first, then the small of her back, the whole length of her spine slapping the ground with a sound
like a door slamming. The breath went out of her in a short, startled squeak, *Ip*, and didn't come back for what
felt like a long time.

She lay there blinking up at the sky, her mouth open, waiting for her lungs to remember how to work.

*She was choking,* she thought, dazed. *She was choking and she still picked me up like a sack of fish.*

Nerine stood over her, chest heaving, still dripping, still coughing every few breaths. She spat a mouthful of lake
water onto the grass by Ripples' head.

*You want my throat?* she thought. *You'll have to get up first. Again.* And, a little more honestly: *That was
close. She nearly drowned me standing up. Don't let her get that fin near your face again.*

## Beat 10 — Up, again

Ripples got up in one ugly heave. She rolled half onto her side, got an elbow under her and then a paw, and shoved
herself up onto her feet. It wasn't graceful. Her back was stiff from the slam, a hot, bruised line all the way down
her spine, and when she straightened up the muscles along it complained.

She was standing before Nerine had decided what to do about her lying there.

Nerine's coughing had finally stopped. She breathed carefully through her nose now, in and out, testing it, the way
you test a sore tooth with your tongue. She wiped her muzzle with the back of her wrist and sniffed hard, and her eyes
were still red-rimmed and wet.

Neither of them said anything. There didn't seem to be anything to say.

Ripples' left thigh ached at every step, a deep, steady bruise where the knee had gone in. Her back throbbed. Her ear
still rang faintly. She stood very straight and very still and was careful not to let any of it show.

*Three hits, a throw, a slam,* she counted. *She's ahead.*

And then, because she was stubborn: *She's ahead now.*

## Beat 11 — Brick Break, low, blocked

Nerine went for the knee.

She didn't hide it this time. She dropped her right shoulder and brought the edge of her paw down and across in a
short, chopping arc, aimed at the side of Ripples' left knee, the joint just below the bruise. A chop like that
doesn't break a knee by itself, but it teaches it to buckle, and a knee that has learned to buckle remembers.

Ripples saw it coming because she'd been looking at nothing else. She didn't have time to move the leg. She bent
sideways at the waist instead and swung her left arm down like a gate closing. The chop met the meat of her upper
arm a hand's width above the elbow, with a hard, flat smack.

It hurt. It was meant for a knee and it hit an arm, and it still hurt. The arm hung there for a moment after it,
dumb and heavy, the fingers at the end of it slack. Then Ripples made it come back up. A short, startled squeak
got out of her, *Ip*, and she shut her mouth on the rest.

The force of it shoved her half a step sideways, and she had to plant her right foot to stop it turning into more.

*Not the leg,* she thought. *She doesn't get the leg every time she asks for it.*

Nerine stepped back and looked at the arm, and then, quite openly, at the knee behind it.

*You can't block forever with an arm,* she thought. *And every arm you lose to me is one less arm in front of that
knee.*

The last of the lake had gone out of Nerine's fur by now. She was dry and fluffed and warm in the sun again, and
the stinging line across her throat had faded to a faint itch she kept almost touching.

## Beat 12 — The ice blade

Ripples had been thinking about the lake ever since it threw spray in Nerine's face.

She didn't think about it in words. It was more of a feeling at the back of her paws, the knowledge that the water
was right there. The wet grass by the shore was still beaded with the gust's spray, a whole silver skin of it
clinging to every blade. And the cold she used for biting was sitting in her jaw doing nothing.

*What if,* she thought, and then didn't finish the thought. She just did it.

She swept her right forearm down and back through the wet grass, fin first, the way you'd drag a paw through a
stream. The water came up with it. It clung to the fin in a thin, shining sheet, and she reached for the cold. It
wasn't in her teeth this time. It was further back, in her chest and down her arm, and she pushed it out along the
fin into the water.

It hissed. A faint, crackling hiss, like frost forming on a window overnight, sped up. The water stiffened on her
arm. It went cloudy, then white at the edges, and it grew: along the fin, past the tip of it, drawing itself out
into a long, flat edge. When she brought her arm up again, a blade of ice ran from her elbow to a hand's length past
her paw, thin and pale and sharp enough to catch the sun.

It was cold. It was very cold against her own fur, a deep, aching cold that made her arm hum. She'd never done this
before. She didn't know how long it would last.

Nerine stared at it. For one moment the cockiness went out of her face completely, and she was just a young Buizel
looking at something she'd never seen.

*That's new,* she thought. *Where did she learn that?*

Ripples didn't give her time to wonder. She stepped in and slashed it across Nerine's left paw, which had come up
to guard her face on instinct. The ice edge scraped across the back of the paw with a dry, grating rasp. In the
same stroke it caught the forearm fin above it, and the blow jarred up through the whole arm. Nerine barked, sharp
and startled, *Bwah*, and snatched the arm in against her chest.

Frost bloomed white in the fur on the back of her paw, in feathery little crystals, and spread a finger's width up
the fin before it stopped.

It should have been worse. It was ice against a river Buizel, and her body knew cold water the way it knew air. The
cold spent most of itself on the outside of her and never got in. She felt it soak away, harmless, the way a
splash soaks into sand.

Most of it. A deep shiver went through her, once, hard, from the frosted paw all the way down her spine. When she
tried to close her fingers they were stiff and slow, as if she'd held them in snowmelt.

Ripples stood back with the blade still on her arm, breathing hard, and looked at it. There was a fierce, giddy
lightness in her chest.

*I made that,* she thought. *I just made that.*

Then, more soberly: *It didn't do much. She's water too. But it's an edge, and she's afraid of it. Look at her.*

Nerine was looking at the blade, and her eyes flicked between it and her own throat, and back.

## Beat 13 — The bite

Nerine answered the blade by going back to the leg. It was the only answer she had, and she knew it was a good one.

She ducked under the next sweep of ice, close enough that she felt the cold of it pass over her ears. Then she came
up inside Ripples' reach, too close for the blade to do anything, and bit down on the left thigh. She bit right on
the bruise her knee had left, as if she'd marked the spot.

The jaws closed with a crunching grind. Ripples felt every tooth. They went in through the fur and into the deep
bruised muscle of the thigh, and the whole leg buckled under her. It wasn't a slow buckle. It was a sudden lurch,
as if the ground had dropped away under that one foot. The jolt ran up into her hip and rocked it in the joint. A
quick, high chirrup of shock came out of her, *Prrt*, a sound she'd never in her life wanted an opponent to hear.

Then Nerine shook her. Once, hard, side to side, the way a hunter shakes something it means to keep. Ripples' leg
was wrenched one way and then the other, and the bite let go.

Ripples staggered. The bite pushed her back a stumbling step on the bad leg, and the leg nearly didn't hold.

And then she froze.

She didn't mean to. Her whole body jerked back from the bite on its own, a full-body flinch, and then locked there,
rigid. Her head was turned away and her shoulders hunched, the ice blade held out uselessly to one side. For a
heartbeat, and then another, she couldn't make herself come back. Her body had decided, without asking her, that
it had had enough for a moment.

She knew she'd frozen. She knew exactly how long it was lasting, and she hated every instant of it.

*Move,* she told herself. *Move, move, she's right there.*

The thigh had changed. Before, it had been a dull, deep bruise, the sort you carry and stop noticing. Now it was a
hot, living hurt, and she could feel the shape of the teeth in it, a crescent of separate little pains inside the
bigger one. The muscle twitched on its own around them.

*Same place,* she thought. *Twice. The knee, and now this. She's going to keep doing this until there's nothing
left of it.*

Nerine stepped back and licked her lips, tasting fur and a little salt. She'd seen the freeze, and she filed it
away with the others.

*She flinches now,* Nerine thought. *She's been put down twice and got up twice, but she flinches. Good. A body
that flinches is a body that's starting to listen.*

## Beat 14 — Breathing

They backed off from each other at the same moment, as if somebody had called a halt.

Ripples took one long step back on the right foot and brought the left one after it, careful, and stood there
dragging air in through her mouth. Her ribs heaved. The flinch went out of her slowly, the way a cramp lets go:
first her shoulders came down, then her head came round, and then she could feel her own paws again.

The ice blade was still on her arm. It had started to weep in the sun, a slow drip from its point, each drop
flashing as it fell into the grass.

Nerine shook out her left paw. The frost had melted out of the fur, leaving it wet and spiky, and she flexed the
fingers one at a time, wincing a little at each. The stiffness was going. It hadn't gone yet.

Neither of them took their eyes off the other.

A cloud shadow slid across the clearing between them, dark and cool, and went on over the beach and out across the
water. Out on the lake a fish rose with a small, neat plop, and the rings spread out from it.

*She's breathing harder than I am,* Nerine thought. *Not by much.*

*She keeps looking at my leg,* Ripples thought. *I keep looking at her neck. We both know. We both know.*

## Beat 15 — Slammed again

Nerine rushed her with no feint and no warning. She simply came, low and hard, and got under the blade before it
could come round.

Her arms locked round Ripples' middle. Her feet planted. And Ripples was lifted off the ground for the second time
in the fight. She hung there, kicking, the blade waving uselessly at the sky, and Nerine brought her straight down
on her back in the grass.

The upper back hit first, high between the shoulders. A tiny yip was knocked out of her, *Yp*, and her shoulders
hunched up round the spot. Then her lower back came down behind it, slapping the ground, and a short, startled
squeak followed, *Ip*, and then a hiss through her teeth as the bruise from the first slam lit up again beneath
the new one.

The ice blade hit the ground beside her and broke.

It didn't shatter. It was too far gone in the sun for that. It cracked across the middle with a soft, wet crunch,
and the whole thing came apart into slush. It slid off her fin in a cold, grey sludge and soaked away into the
grass, and there was nothing left of it on her arm but a wet patch of fur and a fading ache of cold.

She lay on her back and looked up at the sky, her arm empty, her back aching, and for a moment she wanted to cry
with sheer frustration. She'd made that. Nobody had shown her how. And it was gone, after one cut.

She didn't cry.

*It was a good blade,* she thought, absurdly, staring up at a passing cloud. *I'll make another one.*

Nerine stood over her and breathed. *Twice now,* she thought. *The same lift, the same slam. She doesn't see it
coming. Or she sees it and can't stop it, which is better.*

## Beat 16 — Up

Ripples got up straight away.

She didn't roll or crawl or make a production of it. She planted both paws flat in the grass behind her, tucked her
feet under, and pushed, and she was up. The back flared as she straightened. The thigh flared as it took her
weight. She let neither of them reach her face.

She stood with her paws loose at her sides and looked at Nerine. Her chest was rising and falling fast.

The thigh hurt, a hot, steady pulse in time with her heart, with the teeth marks pricking inside it. The back hurt
in a long bruised line where it had met the ground twice. Her right ear still rang, very faintly, a thin high note
under everything. Her left arm ached where it had stopped the chop.

None of it was bad. Not yet.

She knew exactly what *yet* meant, in a fight where nobody could be knocked out. It meant that nothing she carried
would ever be taken off her. It would only ever be added to.

Nerine watched her stand. She'd been ready to see her struggle up, to see her favour the leg, or try twice. She'd
been ready to see her not manage it. Instead Ripples had just stood up, as if the ground had been somewhere to
rest.

*Three times down,* Nerine thought, and there was something in it that was almost respect. *Three times up. Fine.
I've got all afternoon.*

## Beat 17 — The dodge and the trunk

Nerine came at her in a straight line, all speed, a Quick Attack. She went from standing to a blur across the grass
in the space of a breath, aimed dead at Ripples' middle, with the whole weight of her behind it.

Ripples didn't try to block it. There wasn't time for anything as considered as a block. She twisted. She turned
her body side-on and sucked her middle in, and Nerine went past her so close that her fur brushed Ripples' fur all
the way down her flank. Ripples felt the heat of her go past.

And as she went, Ripples hit her. It was only a short, hard paw thrown backhanded as Nerine flashed by. It caught
her across the left ear with a crack. Nerine's ear flattened and rang. She sucked a startled breath in through her
nose, a high little peep, *Pip*, and her head jerked away from the blow.

But she couldn't stop. A Quick Attack isn't built to stop. Her own speed carried her on across the clearing,
straight into the fallen trunk at the edge of the trees.

She hit it with her right arm and the side of her neck, wood and moss together, a deep, hollow *thock* that shook
the whole log. Bits of bark and dry moss flew. Her arm hung for a moment against the trunk before she could make it
lift. She choked on a cough as the blow to her neck caught her breath, and swallowed hard, twice.

She stayed on her feet. It was close. Her knees dipped and her paw caught the top of the trunk, and she hung there
against it for one long breath.

Ripples watched her bounce off the log, and something grim and pleased settled in her.

*Her neck again,* she thought. *Even when I don't do it myself. Everything goes there in the end.*

Nerine pushed herself off the trunk and turned round slowly. Her ear was still ringing, and she shook her head once
to clear it, and her right arm hung a little lower than her left. There was no grin on her face now. There was
nothing on her face at all.

*She baited that,* Nerine thought, though Ripples hadn't, not really. *She stood there and let me run into a tree.*

## Beat 18 — Missed

Ripples went after her. She saw the hanging arm and the shaken head and thought *now*, and she drove the edge of her
paw straight at the side of Nerine's neck, the same chop as before, harder.

Nerine twisted out of its line. It wasn't elegant. She simply threw her head and shoulders sideways and let her
body follow, and the chop went past her ear with an inch to spare. It whistled.

Ripples' momentum carried her a half-step on, and she caught herself on the right foot, and for a moment they were
very close. They were close enough that Ripples could hear Nerine's breathing, quick and shallow, and smell the moss
from the trunk on her fur.

Neither of them moved.

*Careful,* Ripples told herself. *She's slower now. She's still faster than you.*

*She came in on that left foot,* Nerine thought, her eyes going down. *She put her weight on it. She's stopped
thinking about it.*

## Beat 19 — The leg, caught

Ripples stepped in again, and her left leg came up off the ground to do it.

Nerine caught it.

It wasn't a grab so much as a gathering. She simply stepped forward into the step, to Ripples' left, and let the
leg come to her. She turned side-on as it came, and the leg slid in under her right arm. Her arm came down on the
thigh and clamped it hard against her own ribs. The knee ended up just in front of her chest, and Ripples' shin and
foot stuck out behind her back, kicking at nothing. Her right paw curled round the outside of the knee, her left paw
came across and took the inside, and she had it. She took it out of the air as easily as catching a thrown stick.

And then she twisted. She turned the knee the way it doesn't go, wringing it sideways between her paws, while her
arm kept the thigh pinned to her side so the leg above it couldn't turn with it.

Ripples was on one foot. That was the first thing she understood, and it went through her like ice water. Her
whole weight was balanced on her right leg, hopping to keep it under her, and her left leg was held out in front of
her under Nerine's arm, clamped at the thigh and the knee. Nerine's shoulder was right there in front of her face,
and her back, and the arm. Her own paws were free. They could reach all of that, and none of it would give her the
leg back.

The knee wasn't hurt yet. Nerine's paws were pressed into it firmly, the fur squashed flat under them, the flesh
bulging out a little at the edges of the grip, and she was turning it. It wasn't hard yet. It was slow and steady,
the way you'd turn a stiff lid.

The thigh was a different matter.

Nerine's arm was crushed into the bitten muscle, right across the bruise, right across the teeth marks. The fur
creased into a thick ridge round her forearm. The meat of the thigh was pressed so deep it bulged out round the
grip on both sides. And the hurt in it changed. It went from sore to something that was simply, plainly hurting, a
hot, insistent grip of pain that didn't throb any more but just stayed.

A whine slipped out of Ripples, high and thin through her teeth, before she knew it was coming.

She rolled her weight on her one standing foot, feeling for the edge of the grip, the place where an arm ends and
a gap begins. She couldn't find one. Nerine had done this before.

*No,* Ripples thought. *No, no. Not standing on one leg. Not this.*

She hopped. It was the only thing she could do. A small, undignified hop on the right foot, to keep her balance as
Nerine shifted. Nerine let her hop, and turned with her, and kept twisting.

Nerine's face was very close to hers now, over the trapped leg, and it was calm. She had stopped grinning a while
ago. She looked like someone doing a job.

*There,* Nerine thought. *Now we start.*

## Beat 20 — Brick Break, and the first refusal

Nerine kept the leg clamped under her right arm and freed her left paw.

Ripples saw it go back. She saw the paw draw back and stiffen, edge-on, and she knew exactly what it was. She got
her arms up. Both arms were free, and she brought them up in front of her face, crossed. She was too late. She was
standing on one leg with the other being twisted, and she could not step back, could not turn her shoulder, could
not do any of the hundred small things a body does to make a blow land softer.

Nerine aimed for the side of her head and missed it, just. Ripples' chin dipped at the last instant and the chop
came in under her guard instead and took her full on the nose.

It was a tremendous blow. She heard her own nose crunch, a dull, wet sound from the middle of her face. Her head
snapped back on her neck. The force of it went on up into her skull behind her eyes and down into her throat at the
same instant, so that she gulped and the gulp wouldn't go down. A tiny yip was smashed out of her, *Yp*, and then a
strangled sound, half a gag, as her body twisted away from the blow and found it couldn't go anywhere, because her
leg was held.

Her eyes streamed. Blink, blink, blink, and they kept streaming. A hot sting flooded up the inside of her nose and
spread out behind her eyes, and a thin warm thread ran down from one nostril over her lip. She tasted copper.

She screwed her face up tight and ducked her head, and for a moment the whole world was the nose.

And all the time, the grip on her leg never loosened.

That was the worst of it. The chop had happened, and it was huge, and it was in her face. But under it, steady as a
heartbeat, the leg was still being turned. Nerine's paws had sunk into the knee now, the fur and the flesh squeezed
out round them in a ridge. The knee still wasn't badly hurt, but her ears went flat at it, and she shoved at Nerine's
arm once, hard, and got nowhere. And the thigh. The arm across the thigh had dug a trench in her fur, and the muscle
under it quivered, a fine, constant trembling she couldn't stop. She tensed all over against it, held her breath,
and let it out in a hiss.

She squirmed. She fought it harder than before, twisting her hips one way and her shoulders the other, grunting,
her breath coming quicker. She tried to turn the knee with the twist instead of against it. She tried to drop her
weight. She tried to lift herself. She tried every angle she could think of, one after another, and none of them
found anything.

And then her paw went toward Nerine's shoulder.

She didn't send it. It went on its own. Her right paw dropped from her guard and reached for the shoulder in front of
her, the fingers already spreading flat to slap it. It was the tap, the one thing that would make all of this stop.
Her body had decided.

She caught it an inch from the fur and pulled it back.

It shook there in the air, her whole arm shaking, and she held it, and she made it come back up to her chest.

*No.* She was shaking all over. Blood was running into her mouth. *Not yet. Not to this. Not to a twisted knee and a
bloody nose. Not standing up.*

Nerine felt it. She felt the twitch go through the whole body she was holding, and felt it stop, and her ears went
forward.

*Close,* she thought. *She nearly did. She'll do it next time. Or the time after.*

She didn't let go.

## Beat 21 — Prying

Ripples went for the grip with her free paw.

It was the only thing left that she hadn't tried. She dug her claws in under Nerine's forearm where it crossed her
thigh and pulled, trying to lever the arm up even a finger's width. When that didn't work she went for the paws on
her knee. She worked her claws in between Nerine's fingers and tried to peel one of them back, just one, the
smallest, the way you'd start undoing a knot.

Nerine felt the claws in her arm and didn't even flinch. She leaned back and twisted harder.

The knee went over a little further than knees go. It was a small movement, no more than a finger's width, but the
pain that came with it was new. It was sharp and narrow and went straight into the joint like a nail driven
sideways. The muscle round the knee was flattened and pushed aside under Nerine's paws, a deep crease running out
from the grip. Ripples grimaced, all her teeth showing, and braced against it, and the knee went from aching to
sore and stayed there.

And the thigh crossed a line.

She felt it go. She didn't have a word for it at the time. Later she'd think of it as the moment the thigh stopped
being part of her leg and became the only thing in her world. Nerine's arm was crushed into it so hard that the
muscle went flat under the grip, the flesh bulging out round the edges of her forearm. The hurt that had been hot
and insistent became something with teeth of its own, very painful, a pain so big there was no room left around it
for anything else.

She strained against it until she shook. Every muscle she had locked up at once, pulling, heaving, and for a moment
she was all one trembling knot. Then it went out of her and she sagged, with a long, low groan that she couldn't keep
behind her teeth.

She thrashed. There was no thought in it. She kicked at Nerine's shins with the heel of her good foot, which was
stupid, because it was the only foot she was standing on, and it nearly put her down. She clawed at the arm. She
bucked, throwing her whole body back and to the side against the grip like a fish on a line. The sounds came out of
her louder and rougher with every heave: a grunt, a bark, a ragged, wordless snarl.

None of it moved the grip at all.

And everything in her wanted to tap. It wasn't close to what she could bear any more. It was past it. She was holding
on now with nothing but the stubborn, stupid part of her that had always refused to be told. Her tail twitched
toward the grass, both tails, flicking down, and she held them still by force. Her paw twitched toward Nerine's back and
she pulled it back against her chest and held it there with the other one.

*It's only pain,* she told herself, and it was the most useless thing she'd ever thought. *It's only pain. She can't
keep this up forever. Her arm has to get tired. Her arm has to get tired.*

Nerine's arm was tired. Her forearm ached from the strain and her shoulder burned. She didn't care in the slightest.

*Past it,* Nerine thought, watching the tails twitch down and stop. *She's past it and she's still holding. What is
she made of?*

## Beat 22 — Desperate

Ripples kept prying. Her claws were still in Nerine's arm, still working at the fingers, still trying to find the
one gap that would let her start.

Nerine bore down. The squeeze on the thigh came heavier now, not a press any more but a full, driving crush, as if
she'd decided to put her whole weight through her forearm and into that one bitten patch of muscle. Everything
round it was pushed out of its shape. Ripples' breath broke up under it into ragged little yelps, one with every
breath out, *Yip, yip, hah, yip*, that she couldn't stop and couldn't slow.

The knee was pressed in so far it changed the outline of her leg, the fur bunched up thick round Nerine's paws. She
squirmed against it, hissing.

And then she went for it. Everything she had.

She braced. She planted the right foot and set her hips and threw every scrap of strength in her body against the
grip, all at once, in a single wild heave. It was frantic and graceless and close to panic. Her whole body arched
back like a drawn bow. Her claws scrabbled at Nerine's arm, at her shoulder, at the fur of her chest, at anything
they could reach. The cry that came out of her cracked in the middle and went on in two pieces, high and then
higher.

It didn't work. The grip stayed exactly where it was. Nerine's paws on her knee and Nerine's arm across her thigh
didn't shift by so much as a hair, and the heave spent itself and left her hanging off her own trapped leg,
gasping, sobbing for air.

She looked down at the knee.

She hadn't meant to. It was just there, below her, with Nerine's paws wrapped round it, and she looked at it the way
you look at something that belongs to somebody else. It was sore. A deep, wrong soreness inside the joint itself,
not on the surface where bruises live. She tried to move her foot, to flex the toes, just to see. They moved. It
sent a sick little flash up through the knee when they did.

*That's mine,* she thought, very distantly. *That's my knee. I'm going to need that.*

Her paw went to Nerine's shoulder again.

It went further this time. It got there. Her palm came down on the fur, flat, and she could feel the muscle working
under it, holding her. She could feel how easy it would be, how small: a slap, two slaps, and then her leg would be
her own again and she could lie down and stop.

She held on.

She didn't know how. She didn't decide to. It was more that some deep, sullen part of her refused, without
explaining itself. Her paw shook on Nerine's shoulder and didn't slap it, and after a long moment she dragged it away
again.

Nerine felt the paw land on her shoulder and not tap. She felt it shake there. Her own breath caught in her throat.

*Give,* she thought. *Give. Give. Just give it up.* And then, unwillingly, from somewhere underneath the fight: *How
is she still holding on? How is she still holding on?*

## Beat 23 — The wrench free

Ripples squirmed, hissing through her teeth, a long, steady hiss like a kettle.

The squeeze on the knee came heavier now. Nerine's paws pressed so deep that the muscle round the joint went flat
and hard as a board under them, and the soreness inside it turned to something worse. It was a clear, bright
hurting now, a pain with edges. Her whole body twisted against it, trying to ease it, trying to give the knee
somewhere to go. The thigh was a solid block of very-painful that she had stopped being able to feel the edges of.
She bit down on a sound and it came out anyway, a high, strangled whimper behind her clenched teeth.

And Nerine was tired.

Ripples could feel it. Not much. The arm across her thigh trembled now, faintly, the way her own leg trembled, and
the paws on her knee had to keep readjusting, a little squeeze and settle, squeeze and settle. Five beats of this
had cost Nerine something too.

*Now,* Ripples thought. It was the first clear thought she'd had in a long time. *Now, while she's settling.*

She put her free paw flat against Nerine's shoulder. She braced her one good leg into the ground. And she shoved.

It wasn't a strike. It was a push, everything she had left behind it, straight out from the shoulder. Nerine had
her weight forward over the trapped leg and wasn't ready for it. She was rocked back half a step, her feet sliding
in the grass. The grip on the knee loosened for the space of a heartbeat as her paws moved to catch her balance, and
the arm round the thigh lifted a finger's width.

Ripples dragged the leg back out from under the arm.

It came free so suddenly that she nearly went over backward. She fell back two staggering steps, and the second one
was on the left foot, and the left foot wouldn't take it. The leg folded under her, a slow, sick buckling at the
knee. She caught herself just before she went down, threw her weight onto the right, and stood. She was swaying,
the bad foot only brushing the ground.

The leg was a mess. It was a single throbbing, burning column of pain from the hip to the knee, and it pulsed with
every beat of her heart. She was gasping through her mouth. Her whole body trembled, a fine shaking that started in
her stomach and went all the way out to her paws.

*Out,* she thought. *I got out. She can't do that again.*

She knew Nerine could.

Nerine had caught her balance. She stood there with her arms loose at her sides, flexing the tired forearm, and
looked at Ripples' leg. She didn't look angry. She looked like she was measuring it.

*She got out,* Nerine thought. *Fine. She got out on one leg. Let's see her get out on none.*

## Beat 24 — The heel kick, dodged

Nerine came straight back at the leg.

She didn't bother with the arms this time. She swung a low heel kick, an ugly, chopping thing, aimed at the side of
Ripples' left knee. She was trying to hit the joint she'd just been twisting, from the outside, while it was still
hot.

Ripples sprang back from it. It was more instinct than plan, and it wasn't much of a spring. It was a clumsy,
lurching hop off the right foot, her tails thrown out behind her for balance and her arms flung wide. But it took
her out of reach. The kick whistled past her shin close enough that she felt the wind of it in her fur.

She landed on the right foot. She landed hard. Then, very slowly, she put the left one down.

She did it the way you test thin ice: toes first, then the ball of the foot, then a little weight, then a little
more. Her face didn't change. Everything else about her did. Her shoulders were hunched, her tails had dropped, and
her whole body leaned away from the left side as if the leg were something she was carrying rather than standing on.

*Every time,* she thought. *She goes for it every single time now. She's not even pretending.*

Nerine saw the foot go down. She saw how careful it was and how long it took. She saw Ripples lean away from her
own leg.

*She's afraid of it now,* Nerine thought, and there was nothing at all triumphant in the thought. It was just true.
*I made her afraid of her own leg.*

## Beat 25 — Slammed a third time

Nerine got her round the middle.

It was the third time and it was no different from the first two, and Ripples couldn't stop it. She couldn't step
back and she couldn't sidestep, not on that leg. When Nerine's arms locked round her waist there was nothing to push
off with. She was lifted, she hung, and she was brought down on her back in the grass.

Her upper back hit first. A grunt was forced out of her by the jolt, and then a short, startled squeak, *Ip*, as her
lower back came down behind it. Her body arched away from the ground on reflex and then fell back flat.

And this time the back didn't just ache. This time it hurt. It had taken three slams in the same place now, and the
bruise along her spine had stopped being a bruise. It was a hot, spreading line from her shoulders to the base of
her tails, and when she breathed, it moved with the breath.

The breath went out of her in a sob.

She turned it into a cough. She coughed hard, twice, and lay there with her eyes screwed shut, and when she opened
them the clouds were going on drifting overhead, exactly as they had at the start, as if nothing at all had
happened.

She didn't try to get up. She couldn't, yet. She just lay and breathed, flat on her back in the grass, and felt
every part of her body report in.

*Get up,* she told herself. *Get up before she does the leg again.*

She didn't move.

Nerine stood over her, breathing hard. Her own forearm still ached from the hold. She looked down at Ripples lying
there, and then down the length of her, to the left leg, lying a little crooked in the grass.

## Beat 26 — The grab, and three tries

Nerine went for the ankle.

She bent down and caught it, the hurt left leg, by the ankle. She meant to drag her by it, across the roots, back
toward the trunk, anywhere, just to put her own weight on the end of that leg and pull. She got her paw round the
ankle and started to straighten.

Ripples kicked her off.

She didn't think about it. It came from somewhere below thinking. The moment the paw closed round her ankle, her
whole body convulsed. She yanked the leg back toward her chest, twisted onto her side, and lashed out with the right
foot at Nerine's wrist. She caught it with her heel, and Nerine's grip opened, and the ankle slid out of her paw.

Nerine stepped back, shaking her stung wrist. Ripples lay curled on her side with her bad leg drawn up, panting.

*Not that,* Ripples thought. *Not dragged. Never dragged.*

Then she tried to get up.

The first time, she planted both paws in the grass and pushed and got her knees under her, and then she put weight
on the left leg to stand. The thigh and the knee gave out together. It wasn't a slow buckle. They simply weren't
there any more, as if somebody had cut a string, and she dropped straight back down onto the leg. The drop jarred up
through the thigh like a hammer on a bell.

And the thigh broke.

Not the bone. Something else. Something in her. She felt the pain cross the last line it had, the line she hadn't
known was there. It went from very painful to a white, roaring, total thing that was bigger than her whole body,
excruciating, a pain that wasn't in the thigh any more but was the thigh. Her mouth opened and a sound came out of it
she had never made before. It was a high, raw shriek, ripped out of her chest, *Hhhhiiiiaaah*, and it went on for
longer than she could have stopped it if she'd tried.

She did try. She clamped her jaws shut on the end of it and it came out through her teeth anyway, thinned to a
whistle, and then it stopped.

She lay with her face in the grass, shaking from the ears to the tips of her tails. She breathed. She made herself
breathe. In through the nose, out through the mouth. She shoved the rest of it down, all the way down, somewhere
she could hold it shut, and held it there.

*Still here,* she thought. *Still here. I didn't tap. I'm still here.*

The second time, she got her paws under her and pushed, and got halfway, and the whole clearing tilted. The trees
leaned over. The ground slid sideways under her. She hung there on her paws and her good knee, and the lake swung
across the edge of her sight, and she sank slowly back down onto her side, because there wasn't anything else to do.

She lay there and waited for the world to come level again.

*One more,* she thought. *One more. Use the other leg. Forget the left one. It isn't there.*

The third time, she didn't use the left leg at all. She got her right foot flat under her, planted both paws on the
ground in front of it, and pushed straight up off the one good leg. She rose like an old Pokémon getting out of a
chair, slowly, in stages. Her arms took her weight, then her hip, then her shoulders, and then she was standing.

She stood swaying, her weight all on the right. The left foot barely touched the grass. The leg hung under her,
crooked, trembling, and every few heartbeats it twitched on its own.

She lifted her head and looked at Nerine.

Nerine had watched all of it: the kick, the scream, the fall, the long slow climb. She had her stung wrist cradled
against her chest, and her face was unreadable. Something had tightened in her own chest at the scream, and she
didn't like it, and she didn't know what to do with it.

*Stay down,* she thought. *Why won't you stay down?*

## Beat 27 — Caught again

Ripples couldn't step back fast enough. She knew she couldn't, and she tried anyway. And when she tried, her left
leg came up off the ground in front of her, because she couldn't put weight on it to push. It hung there in the air,
exactly where it had been before.

Nerine caught it.

It was the same hold, exactly, and this time there was nothing to work out. She stepped in on Ripples' left and
turned side-on, and the leg went in under her right arm as if it had been made to fit there. Her arm came down across
the thigh and clamped it against her ribs. The knee sat just in front of her chest. Ripples' shin and foot stuck out
behind Nerine's back. Nerine's right paw curled round the outside of the knee, her left came across and took the
inside, and she began to wring it, turning the lower leg out sideways against the joint while her arm held the thigh
still above it.

So they stood hip to hip. Ripples was hopping on her right foot, facing Nerine's shoulder, a foot from her ear. Her
arms were free. They could reach Nerine's shoulder, her back, the arm across her thigh, the side of her head. None of
that was the leg.

But this time the leg was already ruined.

The twist on the knee was solid, no harder than the first time had been. It hurt, steadily, the joint aching in its
new, wrong way. The muscle round it was pressed flat under Nerine's paws, the flesh bulging out at the edges, and
Ripples' toes, out behind Nerine's back, curled and uncurled in the air with every turn.

The thigh couldn't bear it at all.

It couldn't bear anything. It was excruciating before the arm ever came down on it, and every scrap of pressure on
it told: every movement, every breath Nerine took, every time her ribs swelled against it. The fur creased into a
thick ridge where her arm crossed it. Under the fur the muscle was hot and swollen and wrong.

The sound came out of Ripples before the grip had even finished closing. It started as a gasp and turned into a
cry, ragged, rising, climbing up through her chest and her throat, *Aaah—aaaaaah—*. It climbed until there was no
air behind it at all, and then it simply stopped, and she sagged sideways against Nerine's shoulder, panting, her
mouth open.

She gulped air. Her free paw beat at Nerine's back, frantic and aimless. It wasn't trying to pry now, just hitting,
flat-pawed, the way you'd beat at a door. Her whole body twisted on its one standing leg, back and forth, and the
trapped leg didn't move with it.

*Not again,* she thought. *Not again, not again, not again.*

Nerine held on. Her face was right there, turned toward her over her own shoulder, and it was set like stone. She
watched Ripples move, and saw the wince she couldn't hide any more every time she moved at all. She heard the breath
catch on the way in, as if it had hit something sharp.

*She can't hide it now,* Nerine thought. *She's stopped even trying.* And then, harder: *Good. This time I don't
let go. This time I don't get tired.*

She settled her weight, and set her feet, and leaned in.

## Beat 28 — The chop to the chest

Nerine took her left paw off the knee.

She didn't need it there. Her right arm still had the thigh crushed to her ribs, and her right paw still had the
outside of the knee. The leg wasn't going anywhere. She drew the free paw back across her own body.

Ripples saw it go back. She got her arms up. She was too late, the same as before: she couldn't step, she couldn't
turn, she couldn't do any of the things a body does to soften a blow when one leg is held out under somebody else's
arm. Nerine didn't go for her face this time. She swung the chop back across, hard, edge-on, straight into the
middle of her chest.

It landed with a hard crack, the sound of something solid being hit squarely. A sharp squeak was knocked out of
her, *Eek*. Her ribs heaved in short, shallow jerks, and her face tightened all over. Her body jerked backward with
the force of it, and the grip took it. The leg was held, so the rest of her had nowhere to go. She hung off her own
trapped knee for a moment, and the jerk ran down her leg into the joint.

And the knee went over a line.

Nerine's left paw came straight back to the inside of the knee, and she bore down with both paws, heavy, wringing
it further round, and the flesh between them squeezed out in a ridge. The ache in the joint swelled into something
bigger: very painful now. That was the same word the thigh had worn a few beats ago. A thin, wavering wail came up
out of her, *Iiiiiiiih—*, and wouldn't stop. It went on and on, wobbling, her whole body shaking under it.

The thigh was past that. The arm across it dug a trench in her fur, and the muscle under it quivered and jumped,
and when it jumped she screamed. It wasn't a yelp or a cry. It was a scream that went on and on, *Aaaaaaaaah—*,
and cracked at the top where her voice couldn't follow it any more.

She thrashed. She kicked at Nerine's ankle with the heel of her standing foot, nearly putting herself down. She
clawed at the arm across her thigh, tearing out tufts of fur. She bucked against the grip with her whole body. Every
heave was louder and rougher than the last, and none of them moved anything.

She looked down at the knee, once. It was in front of Nerine's chest, turned out a little the wrong way between her
paws, and she couldn't feel where the paws ended and the hurt began. She tried to move her toes behind Nerine's
back. They moved, and a white flash went up the leg into her hip, and she stopped trying.

*That's going to stay,* she thought, very clearly. *Whatever happens now, that's going to stay.*

Her paw went to Nerine's shoulder.

It went all the way there. Her fingers spread flat on the fur and lay there, shaking. All it had to do was lift and
fall.

She held it still.

Nerine felt it land: a small, hot, shaking weight on her shoulder, the fingers spread, the whole body behind it
straining toward that one small movement, and then stopping. She went still under it, waiting. She was breathing
hard. Her forearm ached. She felt Ripples' sides heave against her and felt her sway on the one leg she had left.

"Just tap," she said, low.

It wasn't a taunt. Ripples didn't answer.

## Beat 29 — The push

Ripples braced her good foot.

She planted it in the torn-up grass and drove against the grip with everything she had left. She shoved at
Nerine's shoulder with both paws and hauled her hips back, trying to drag the leg back out from under the arm, the way
she had before. The way that had worked.

Nerine felt it coming. She leaned into Ripples instead of away, kept her weight forward over the leg where it hadn't
been last time, and clamped her arm down. The leg didn't slide an inch.

And the grip came down harder than it had ever been.

It wasn't one sudden wrench. It was a slow, steady deepening, like a tide coming in. Every beat she'd been in it,
it had sunk a little further, and now it was a long way in. Nerine's paws had the knee turned so far out that the
joint was flattened between them. Long, gasping whimpers came out of Ripples one after another,
*hnn—hnnnn—hnnnnn*, each one a little higher than the last.

The arm across her thigh had squashed the muscle flat against Nerine's ribs, and the swollen flesh pushed out round it
above and below. When Nerine cranked the knee, the arm cinched with it, and a choking squeal was dragged up out of
Ripples, *Iiiiiiiih—hk*. It came again with the next crank, and the next. It went on until her breath ran out, and it
was there again the moment she found more.

## Beat 30 — The knee

Ripples pried at the grip with her free paw. It was a feeble, pawing thing now, her claws catching in the fur of
Nerine's arm and slipping out again, catching and slipping.

Nerine bore down.

The wrench on the knee was crushing now. Her paws had it turned so far out, and were driven in so hard, that the
joint gave between them, and the fur squeezed up between her fingers in ridges. And the knee went the way the thigh
had gone.

Ripples felt it cross. It was the same line, the same sudden opening-up of the floor under the pain, and she knew it
this time. She knew what it was as it happened, which was worse. The ache in the joint went from very painful to
excruciating in a single breath. It was a white, roaring heat inside the knee, as if the bones had been pulled apart
and something red-hot pushed in between them.

The sound that came out of her was a long, broken yelp. It started high and stayed high and stretched and stretched,
*Yiiiiiiiiiiiip—iiip—*, until there was no breath left to stretch it with. Then it broke off in a cough.

She hung there against Nerine's shoulder with her eyes squeezed shut, shaking from her ears to her tails. *Both,* she
thought. *Both now. The thigh and the knee. There's nothing left of that leg that doesn't hurt.*

She made herself look at it. She didn't know why. She opened her eyes and looked down, past her own heaving chest,
at the knee in front of Nerine's chest. It didn't look like her knee. It looked swollen and strange, and the shin
below it went off the wrong way, out behind Nerine's back. When she tried to feel her foot back there, there was only
a far-off, buzzing ache, as if the foot were somewhere else.

*I'm not going to walk home on that,* she thought, and it was so calm it frightened her.

The thigh was taking a tremendous squeeze under the arm now, crushed against Nerine's ribs, everything round it
pushed out of its shape. A thin, wavering wail came up out of her and wouldn't stop. It wobbled on and on under
everything else, like a sound she'd left running and forgotten how to turn off.

She heaved against it, desperate, and sobbed, and heaved again.

Her paw lay flat on Nerine's shoulder, and shook, and didn't tap. That was six times now. She had stopped counting.
She had stopped being able to count.

Nerine felt the knee go. She felt it through her paws, a sort of loosening deep inside the joint, the fight going out
of it. She heard the yelp, right beside her ear. Her ears went flat against her head on their own. And she felt the
paw on her shoulder, still there, still shaking, still not moving.

"Ripples," she said. It was the first time she'd said the name all afternoon. "Ripples. Tap."

Ripples' breath caught on the way in, as if it had hit something. A wince crossed her face and stayed there.

She didn't tap.

## Beat 31 — The thigh

Ripples pried at the grip again. She hardly knew she was doing it. Her paw was just moving at Nerine's arm, the way
a paw moves in a bad dream.

The hold came down tremendously on the knee now. It was wrung so far round that the muscle round the joint went flat
and hard as a board. A scream came out of her that went on and on, *Aaaaaaaaaaah—*, and cracked at the top. It
cracked again and kept going.

And then the thigh crossed the last line there was.

She hadn't known there was another one. She'd thought excruciating was the bottom of it, the place where pain
stopped getting worse because there was nowhere worse to go. It wasn't. Nerine's arm sank into the thigh, crushing it
flat against her ribs. The pain went past excruciating into something she had no word for at all. Devastated. It was
not a pain in a part of her any more. It was the whole world, and she was a small thing inside it.

What came out of her wasn't a scream. It was quieter than that, and much worse to hear. It was long, gasping
whimpers, one after another, each one higher than the last, *hnnnn—hnnnnnn—hiiiiiiinnn—*, as if something in her had
given up on screaming because screaming didn't help.

Her whole body had gone heavy. Her arms were heavy. Her standing leg trembled under her, and her head hung forward
until her brow rested against the back of Nerine's shoulder, and her breath came in short, shallow sips that didn't
seem to reach the bottom of her lungs. She was exhausted, all the way through. It was the deep, hollow tiredness of
a body that has been fighting for a long time and has nothing left to fight with.

She looked at the thigh. She couldn't see it properly. Nerine's arm was in the way, sunk in deep, and the fur round
it was flattened and wet. She didn't need to see it. She knew exactly where it was. It was the only thing she knew.

*It's gone,* she thought. *It's just gone. There's no leg there, there's only this.*

Her paw was on Nerine's shoulder. She couldn't remember putting it there. It lay flat and shook, and the hard little
knot under her breastbone said no, one more time, so faintly she could hardly hear it.

She didn't tap.

Nerine had stopped looking at her face. She couldn't any more. She stared at the grass in front of her own feet and
felt the paw instead: the weight of it, the heat of it, the shaking that came through her fur into her shoulder.
Ripples' sides rose and fell against her. Every few breaths the whole small body swayed, and Nerine had to lean into
it to keep her up.

*Seven,* Nerine thought. *Seven times.* Her own arms were shaking, and not only from the strain. *Please,* she
thought, and didn't know who she was asking either. *Please just tap.*

## Beat 32 — The tap

Ripples pried at the grip one last time, if it could still be called that. Her claws hooked in the fur of Nerine's
arm and didn't pull. They just stayed there, hooked, holding on to her.

The thigh went numb.

It happened all at once, between one breath and the next. The pain that had been the whole world suddenly drained
away from it, and left behind a strange, cold, empty heaviness. The leg was still there under Nerine's arm, crushed
flat against her ribs, the fur squeezed up round it in ridges. She just couldn't feel it any more. It was like a held
breath, or a door shut on a fire.

For one moment she thought, stupidly, *It's stopped. It's over. I held on and it stopped.*

Then the knee told her it wasn't.

With the thigh gone quiet there was nothing left to drown out the knee. Nerine's paws were wringing it round until
the joint spread flat between them, the fur splayed every way. All of it came through, every turn, every press,
clear and sharp and enormous. She wrenched against it, again and again, gasping, and her standing foot hopped and
scraped at the ground uselessly. A hoarse sound came out of her on every breath now, rough and cracked, her voice
worn down to nothing.

And something in her gave up before she did.

It wasn't her will. Her will was still there, still sullen, still saying no in its faint, stubborn voice. It was the
part of her under the will, the old animal part that doesn't care about matches or terms or who quits. It only
wanted the pain to end, and it had waited, and waited. It had sat quiet through the knee and the thigh and seven
refusals. And now it had stopped waiting.

Her paw came down on Nerine's shoulder.

Once. Twice. Again and again, flat and frantic, slapping it. Her other paw let go of Nerine's arm and hammered at
her back. Her tails beat at the ground behind her. Every part of her that could hit something hit something. And the
sound went on over all of it, a long, cracked wail that went out across the clearing and over the water. A flock of
small birds went up out of the oaks all at once, in a whirring cloud, and scattered.

Nerine let go at once. She had been waiting for it so long that her arms opened before she'd finished feeling it.

The leg dropped out from under her arm, and Ripples dropped with it.

She went down in a heap and curled up on her side in the grass, both paws wrapped round her left knee. The thigh was
still numb, and that was the strangest part: she could hold it and not feel herself holding it. She drew her good
knee up, wrapped her tails tight round herself, and shook. Tears she hadn't agreed to ran sideways across her face
and into the grass. Her breath came in long, shuddering sobs that she couldn't stop and didn't try to.

She was awake. She was entirely awake, and beaten, and she knew it. And the release was almost worse than the hold had
been. The knee went on throbbing in great slow waves now that nothing was holding it, and somewhere far off, under
the numbness, the thigh was starting to wake up again.

*I tapped,* she thought. *I held on so long. And I tapped.*

Nerine stood over her. Her chest was heaving. Both her forearms ached all the way to the shoulders, and they were
trembling, and she couldn't make them stop. Her neck still remembered the fin and the trunk. Her left paw was still
faintly stiff from the ice. Her right shoulder still felt the small hot shape of a paw, where it had lain so long. She
looked down at the curled-up shape, at the leg, swollen and wrong at the knee. She looked at the trench her own arm
had left across the thigh. She looked at the grass round where they had stood, torn into a ragged circle of bare
earth by one hopping foot, beat after beat after beat, rather than give in.

She had won. She waited to feel it.

What she felt, mostly, was tired, and something else that sat uncomfortably close to shame.

After a while she sat down in the grass beside her, careful of the leg, not touching, and waited for Ripples'
breathing to slow. The birds came back into the oaks one by one. Out on the lake, the fish rose again, and the rings
spread out over the still water.

"Seven times," she said at last, quietly. "You put your paw on me seven times and didn't tap. I've never seen anyone
do that."

Ripples didn't answer. She didn't open her eyes.

But after a while, one of her twin tails crept out across the grass and lay against Nerine's foot, and stayed there.
