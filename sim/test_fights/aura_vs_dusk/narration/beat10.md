— Aura —

She could feel the wolf through the ground. Not her weight: her breathing. Every heave of those ribs came up through the mud into the soles of Aura's feet, ragged and fast, a little too fast.

The wolf stood a body's length off, still blowing from the bites, and every time she swallowed she grimaced. The wet earth smelled of smoke from the split oak, and under it, faintly, of the wolf.

*She has nerve.* Aura kept the thought to herself and did not let it soften anything.

She raised her paws as if to step in and chop again, the same opening, the same angle as before, and saw Dusk's head drop to cover her neck, exactly as expected. Then she changed it late. She did not step in at all. She brought her paws together in front of her chest, wrists touching, and the aura came to them.

It gathered fast, a blue-white glow cupped between her palms, humming, the rain hissing to steam where it fell into it. In the grey light of the storm it lit the wolf's face from below: the pale eyes, the bared fangs, the stone of the mane.

She let it go from no distance at all.

— Dusk —

It hit her in the face with a sharp crack of air, and it hit everything at once: the dome of her skull, the length of her muzzle, all in one bright blow that went straight through the fur and the bone and settled behind them.

She felt it find the one place her body had never armoured, and settle there.

Her head snapped back. Her body followed, a single step backward along the line of the blast, her hind feet skidding in the mud, and for a moment she was not fighting at all, only trying not to fall. She lurched sideways and her balance chased after her. Her paws scrambled under her and caught up.

She was out of place. She knew it. Somewhere she had not chosen to be, half-turned, her guard a heartbeat late, and the jackal a step away.

*Feet. Feet now.*

A sharp hiss came out through her teeth, lips pulled back, ears pinned flat. She pressed her eyes shut for an instant, then glared. Her head had tightened hard to brace her muzzle, and the bracing hurt too; she bit down on a grunt and let her muzzle hurt.

Her ears rang faintly and then stopped. Her muzzle remembered the blow in a slow, spreading warmth that did not go away.

She knew that warmth. It was the warmth of being hit by the one thing she was not built to take.

She righted herself fast: she slapped her forepaws down square and set her shoulders and was angry, more than shaken. The mud under her pads was cold and slick and had nothing in it to hold on to.
