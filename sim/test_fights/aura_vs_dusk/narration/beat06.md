— Dusk —

The jackal's shadow lay across the mud between them, long and faint in the grey light, and its tip touched the shadow of the log where Dusk stood.

She was still turned away. Her face was still screwed up against the stones. Dusk watched her with an interest that had no worry in it at all.

*Now. While she's somewhere else.*

She came down off the log in a low, skimming rush, belly almost on the mud, on the side the jackal was guarding least. Every stretch of her stride pulled at her neck, and she shortened them to spare it, and was still fast. Mud sucked at a hind foot and let it go. There was pride rising in her, and she did not trust it; she folded it away.

The jackal's forearm was up in front of her face where she had thrown it against the stones, and it was the nearest thing Dusk could reach. She went for it with her jaws wide. A snarl rolled out of her as she snapped forward, her teeth closing on the black fur over the bone.

— Aura —

The snarl was what brought her back.

It cut through the freeze like a hand on her shoulder, and she was there again, all of her, and the jaws were already closing on her arm. She did not think. She twisted: hips and shoulders and spine all turning together out of the line of the bite, so that the teeth that should have met in her forearm met each other instead, a hand's breadth from her fur, with a hollow clack.

The wolf's own rush carried her on past. Aura felt the wet fur of the mane brush her elbow as it went.

Then the wolf was turning in the mud on the far side of her, and the two of them stood a length apart again, breathing.

Aura let her arm come down slowly. The freeze had let go of her completely now; she could feel her feet again, her balance, the weight of her own paws. Dusk's ears went back a fraction when she saw it. The jackal was back.

Lightning flickered somewhere behind the clouds without breaking through, and turned the rain silver for an instant. Then one rumble, very low, and nothing.
