— Dusk —

Blue. That was the colour in front of her: the jackal's forearm, blue fur and black, the paw at the end of it wound into her mane. Past it, the cream of the jackal's chest, gone grey with mud, and the dark, burned spike in the middle.

She blinked hard, waiting for her sight to settle. It settled enough.

The jackal's right knee barely touched the ground. She gasped whenever she had to twist, and she had stopped twisting. She was holding on, and holding herself up by holding on.

*One chance left. This is it.*

Dusk wanted it over. She did not wait for a better moment. There was not going to be one.

Everything the surge had given her, she put into her shoulders and her claws and her weight. She pressed her body in tight against the jackal and gave her no space to answer. She wrenched round in the grip and tore into the arm that held her, and then into the body behind it: a forepaw ripping across the forearm, her shoulder slamming into the belly, her skull driving up into the chest, short and compact, everything landing in the same place, everything she had.

She knew there would be nothing after it. She threw it anyway.

— Aura —

It came with a sharp crack of air, all of it at once, and it went into her the way only that kind of blow could.

The arm first. Claws across the forearm she was holding with, and pain shot up it and her fingers spasmed open in the stone mane. Then the stomach. She had known her body had no answer to this, and now her stomach proved it: the shoulder drove into it and it was already past bearing, and it went further, and the scream tore out of her before she could find it. It rocked her back on her heels, but her feet stayed planted, and that was the only thing she kept.

Then the chest, and the old burn in the spike woke under the new blow and the chest crossed over with it, and the scream caught and went higher.

The grip came off. The mane slid out of her open paw. She felt it go, and could do nothing about it.

When the scream stopped she was bent almost double round her stomach.

Every limb went rigid for a heartbeat, then she took a ragged gulp of breath. Breathing out was easy. Breathing in was a climb. A small, cracked sound escaped her every time her stomach so much as shifted, and it shifted with every breath. Her stomach trembled on its own, beyond her reach.

Then the sky broke.

The light came first, white, total, and in the same instant a crack so loud it was not a sound at all. Lightning hit the split oak behind them for the second time. Aura felt it come through the wet ground into her feet like a slap.

The wolf was nearer the oak. It went through her worse.

— Dusk —

Dusk's whole body jerked away from the white and the crack, on its own, and stayed jerked. Her head was turned. Her legs were locked. She could not make any of it come back.

She knew she had frozen. She hated every heartbeat of it.

Through the stinging after-image she could see the jackal: bent double, swaying and correcting, swaying and correcting, a little later each time. Her knees trembled. Dusk watched them and waited to see if they would hold. There was a tremor in the jackal that did not stop.

*That one went in. Look at her.*

There was pride in Dusk at that, and she did not trust it, and folded it away. She had nothing left to back it with. The surge was still in her blood, but she had spent everything it had brought.

A sound came out of her that she did not choose: a long, thin whine, under the rain.
