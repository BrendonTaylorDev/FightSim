— Aura —

A single raindrop hung from the tip of the wolf's ear, very clear, trembling. Then it fell.

Dusk was squinting as if against bright light, though the flash was long gone. When she looked at Aura, she turned her whole body to do it.

Aura counted what she had left. Breath: almost none, and every breath in was a climb. Legs: one. One good trick, maybe.

She stayed a step further away than she needed to. The wolf was still frozen from the lightning, and Aura still did not trust it.

Then she made herself go.

She could not time it to the wolf's breathing any more; breathing in hurt too much to time anything to. She struck on her own breath out instead. Her left paw came up and over, a looping chop coming down on the top of the wolf's head, and her breath went out in a hard huff behind it.

— Dusk —

The freeze let go of her as the paw came up.

She did not see it so much as feel the jackal's weight shift, and that was enough: she had felt that blow before, and it had hurt her worse than anything, and her body was already going before her mind had caught up. She sprang back, all four feet at once, and the chop came down through the place where her head had been and split nothing but rain.

She landed a length away.

And her head woke up.

It had gone numb at some point in the grip, quiet and far away, and she had been glad of it without noticing. Now the feeling came back into it all at once. Every blow the jackal had landed there, the blast, the steel fist, the chops in the grip, came flooding back in a single rush, as bad as it had ever been and worse for the moment it returned.

The sound was torn out of her raw. Her body seized round her head, her forelegs folding, her muzzle almost to the mud, and the shaking started all over again.

Nothing new had hit her. It did not matter.

The smell of wet earth and smoke came to her through her open mouth. She stayed bent low over her own forepaws, shaking, until the worst of it had passed through her and gone on, and left only the ordinary pain behind.

Water dripped from her muzzle into the mud. The echo of the thunder rolled away into the trees.
