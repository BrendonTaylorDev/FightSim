— Dusk —

Five, she thought, without meaning to. Five times she had got the jackal down or nearly down. Five times the jackal had come back up.

Every breath the jackal took showed on her face as it went past her stomach. She could not stand straight; her chest folded her. Her breathing had a wet catch in it now, at the bottom of each breath.

Dusk tilted her head, as far as her neck allowed, and looked at her with a cold kind of interest. That catch meant the jackal was close to the end.

So was she.

The jackal was still half-turned from the punch, off balance, one arm out to hold herself up. The arm was the nearest thing to the ground and the hardest to pull away.

Dusk did not stop to think. She went in low, a skimming rush, belly almost in the mud, and drove her head forward with her jaws already parting, and closed them on the arm.

It was like pushing on a door that opened the other way. She marked it, and filed the lesson away, and bit down anyway.

— Aura —

She saw it come and tried to pull the arm in, and could not; it was holding her up.

The teeth met in her right forearm with the hard click of fangs on bone, and raked down onto the back of her paw.

She felt it arrive, felt it weaken, and felt it die out against her. A bite. Only a bite. The surface of her arm noticed it; her steel underneath barely did.

But the forearm had been clawed already, and struck by stones, and it was not interested in what the rest of her could take. It crossed its line.

Her body jerked away from it on its own, every limb pulling in, the arm snatched tight against her chest, and a raw sound tore out of her and kept tearing with every breath. Her tail thrashed behind her in the mud. Her right paw buzzed, as if the bite had plucked a string between it and the arm. When she pushed against anything with that forearm, a hot jolt went up it into her chest.

She pressed herself small round the pain, every muscle quivering. Her mouth gaped and shut and gaped, like a fish on a bank.

— Aura —

She got her feet back under her somehow. Her guard came back up: one arm, the left, the right tucked useless against her body.

She checked herself the way she would have checked a house after a storm. This still worked. This didn't. This, she wouldn't try yet. Halfway through, she stopped counting. The count did not help.

Moving her stomach was past deciding; her body simply refused it for her. Her chest felt hollowed out and filled again with ache. Everything round it had gone tense and watchful, braced for the next one.

A scream had come out of her, and a gasp, and a second scream, smaller and hoarser. Now a broken little sound escaped every time she breathed out, and she had stopped trying to stop it.

Her breath shuddered in and fell out. She could not slow it. It had its own pace now, fast and shallow, and was not hers any more.

*How much more is there in me?*

She did not know. The surge was still holding her up. She did not know how long it would.

— Dusk —

Dusk had let go and backed off, and stood watching from where the bite had left her.

The jackal's movements had gone heavy and plain, all the tricks drained out of them. Her head turned to keep Dusk in view, slower than before. Rain drummed on the leaves above them.

Dusk stared at her for a long moment, and it took her a while to understand what she was staring at. It was the jackal's eyes: still open, still red, still on her.

*Any moment now.*

There was a small surprise in her at her own strength: that she was still here to think it.

Neither of them made a sound. In the silence there was only the rain, and two sets of breathing, and the thing they were both waiting for.
