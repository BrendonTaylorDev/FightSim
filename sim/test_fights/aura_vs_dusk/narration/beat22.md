— Aura —

Her mouth tasted of blood and smoke. She ran her tongue along her teeth and found them all still there, and one of them loose where the wolf's skull had come up into her muzzle.

The wolf's breathing had a thin wheeze in it, the whistle gone ragged at the edges. She held her head low rather than arch her back, and she had curled round her throat now, a shoulder drawn up over it without her knowing, which left her side open.

*Winning is the most dangerous place to stand,* Aura thought. *Don't stand there. Finish.*

She did not wait for the wolf to set herself. She turned half away, as if she had nothing left to worry about, and the contempt in it was real; then she came round out of the turn with her weight behind it. Her right knee buckled a little under the push. She rode it.

The chop came round low and hooked up under the guard, the edge of her paw into the wolf's ribs on the left side, her shoulder rolling in behind it, her whole body following through. She felt the blow land square on a body with no answer for it.

It found something in Dusk that had never learned to defend itself.

— Dusk —

It landed with a solid whump, like a sack dropped, and it did not spread across her: it went straight to her centre. The ribs on her left side took it, and her chest behind them, and then the whole of her was moving.

Her body followed the blow, several steps sideways to the right, her paws scraping and skidding in the mud for a grip that kept not being there. Her hindquarters dropped. She was going down. She was nearly down. A forepaw found a root under the slime and held, and she stopped, crooked, half-crouched, a breath from the ground.

The ribs crossed over.

A sound came out of her she had never made before, high and thin, and she heard it as if it came from outside her, from some other animal at the edge of the clearing. Every twitch of her left side set off a burst that rang through all of her. Her chest hummed with it.

Tears poured down her muzzle. She was not crying. Her eyes simply would not stop.

She stumbled back, hunched round her middle as if against cold, guarding the ribs with everything she had, with her leg, her shoulder, the curve of her whole body. Her left side jerked with every heartbeat, and she cried out with the worst of them.

— Dusk —

She held still. She noticed she was holding perfectly still, and that she did not dare stop.

Her throat was ruined for this fight and told her so on every heartbeat. Her back was one long, stiff ache from her neck to her hips. There was a cold sweat under her fur, though nothing about her was cold.

She looked down, past her own chest, and the ground seemed further away than it should have been.

The whine came out of her aloud. It did not stop when the worst of the pain faded; it went on, thin and steady, under her breath. Then a hoarse noise, and she kept that one from rising.

Her breathing was a rough sawing in and out, louder than anything else she could hear. She breathed through her open mouth, harsh and loud in her own ears. The wind changed its mind and blew the rain into her face, and she let it.

*Ow. Ow. Keep moving.*

Her feet were still finding the ground. She was out of place, slow, her guard nowhere. She knew it. She needed them back.

She looked at the jackal across the mud and her mouth opened, as if there were something she meant to say to her, and closed again on it.
