— Dusk —

The jackal's weight shifted in the mud before anything else moved. A hip turning. A shoulder starting to come up.

Dusk did not wait to see what it would become.

She tried to roar as she came. What came out of her ruined throat was a rasp, and she let that be enough. A white flash lit the clearing and printed the trees on her eyes, and in the white she was already standing over the jackal, all four feet planted in the mud on either side of her.

The jackal's limbs were drawn in. Ready, or simply hurt; it was hard to say. It did not matter. She was on her back with nowhere to roll, and her belly was open to the sky.

Dusk took the invitation.

She bit down into the stomach, short and hard, and let go, and bit again before the jackal's arms could come down to cover it, lower, into the right hip. Again into the hip. The jackal was twisting now, trying to draw her legs up, and the knee came up into Dusk's face and Dusk bit it. Bit it again. Each bite came down on top of the last, quick and ugly, no wind-up, her jaws working like something separate from her, and when she ran short of knee she took the foot.

Somewhere around the fourth bite she stopped choosing them. Something had gone cold and wild in her and her jaws kept going, and it took her a long moment to notice that her lungs were burning and her throat was whistling and the last bite had come slower than the first.

*Stop admiring it. Move.*

She stood over the jackal with her sides heaving, breathing through her nose in short, ragged pulls.

— Aura —

The first bite took her in the stomach with the wet clack of teeth closing, and her whole body curled round it, too late, her arms coming down a heartbeat after the jaws had already let go. *Rrh.* A short snarl, more anger than anything.

Then the hip. Then the hip again, and she bit off a rumble low in her throat.

Then the knee.

That was where they changed. The first bite on the knee squeezed her eyes shut for a heartbeat. The second wrenched the whole leg, and her body went with the jaws as far as it could, which in the mud was no distance at all: the ground gave her nowhere to go, and the bite had all of her. Her breath went out of her in a rush. *Hrrgh.* A grunt that ended in a growl. Then the foot, and her mouth hung open and nothing came out.

She almost smiled, through all of it. This was the one kind of hurt she had always been good at taking. Teeth were teeth; they bit and let go and her body closed up behind them. Even in a flurry like this, her steel and her training took the worst of it and handed back only the bruising.

Only the knee did not close up. The knee stayed hot: a steady ache that did not go away when the jaws did, and it leaned on her right foot until that ached too.

The wolf was panting above her. The bites had stopped.

Aura rolled onto her front in the mud, got her knees under her, planted her paws flat in the slime, and pushed herself up. One push, and she was on her feet, breathing rough, the right leg held a little off the ground. She set it down and her stride hitched when it took her weight.

She breathed out slowly through pursed lips. The sound she made under it was very small and very furious.

Her head went down, as if ducking a second blow that was not coming, and both her ears were turned toward the wolf.
