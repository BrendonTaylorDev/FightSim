— Dusk —

Her breathing told her she had a little time. It was short and whistling and it hurt on every pull, but it was steady, and steady meant she could do this before she had to stop.

The jackal lay at the foot of the oak with smoke drifting over her. She had rolled a little onto her left side, and the moment she stopped moving her weight came off her right knee, as if the knee had asked for it.

Her eyes were open and steady. That was the worrying part.

*She's not as hurt as she looks. Don't believe it.*

But the knee was real. Dusk had felt it go soft under her teeth. A hard, bright eagerness came up in her, and her weight came forward onto her toes.

She came in on the jackal's breath out, when the body was slackest. The jackal saw her and dragged the leg in to cover it, and Dusk went where the guard had been a moment before. Her breath hissed in through bared teeth, and then she lunged.

The fire came back into her mouth as she came, and this time it found its way all the way out, wreathed round her fangs, hissing in the rain. It hurt her throat to open her jaws that wide. She opened them anyway.

The right blow at last, after all that hard work to get here.

— Aura —

The jaws closed on her right knee with the wet clack of teeth closing, and the fire went in with them.

She had seen it come. She had dragged the knee in toward her chest, and it had not been fast enough, and there was nowhere to roll: the oak was at her back and the mud under her, and the bite had all of her. The jaws wrenched at her and her whole body went with them as far as it could, which was nowhere. Her head rolled with it, her cheek into the mud. The teeth raked down over her shin into her right foot and up across her hip as the wolf tore free.

The knee had been hurt already, chewed and bruised. The new pain found the old one waiting and went past it.

It climbed past anything she had a name for and kept climbing.

For a heartbeat every muscle she owned jumped at once, and then hung slack. The cry came out of her before she could stop it, a real cry, high and raw, and she heard it go out across the clearing over the rain drumming on the leaves. Her body recoiled from the fire as if it had touched the one thing it could not stand.

Her ears drooped sideways. Her head dropped and hung, mouth open, strings of spit running into the mud.

Then the wolf was gone, and she was alone with the knee.

Her whole frame trembled, and the trembling ran back through her right knee every time. Her right foot answered the bite late, with a hot, dull pulse.

She would not lie here.

She reached back over her head and found the charred bark of the split oak with both paws. It was warm under the rain, almost hot. She took hold and pulled. She got partway up, her back scraping the trunk, and then the clearing tilted around her and slid, the trees leaning, and she sank back down into the mud.

She waited. She breathed. She took hold of the oak again.

This time she did not try to rise smoothly. She heaved: one ugly lurch, all arms and good leg, nearly overbalancing forward as she came up, and then she was standing, her back against the oak and her shoulder braced on it, the right leg held just off the ground.

She was on her feet. Winded, wincing, her sides heaving.

She let her breath out slowly, and it shook all the way out.
