— Dusk —

The thunder was still going when she went.

There was no gathering, no crouch for the jackal to read. One moment Dusk was standing at the far end of the clearing with the rain running off her mane; the next her claws had bitten into the mud and the whole length of her had thrown itself forward, low and flat, and the clearing was coming at her in a blur of brown and grey.

*Close the gap before she breathes.*

Mud sprayed up behind her in long fans. A root came up under her forepaws and she was over it before she had decided to be. The jackal grew in front of her: blue and black and cream, the pale chest with the steel spike set in the middle of it, the red eyes that had already found her and had not widened at all. That was the part that put a hungry narrowness in Dusk's own eyes. She did not look startled. She looked like she had known.

Dusk kept her gaze on the shoulders, not the face. The face could lie. The shoulders would tell her which way the jackal meant to go, and the shoulders did not move.

So Dusk went straight through the middle of her.

She dropped her head at the last stride and put everything she had into the last push of her hind legs: chest and shoulder and the hard dome of her skull, all of it driving into the cream fur over the jackal's breastbone.

— Aura —

It landed with a deep, slapping thud, and it landed all at once: the wolf's head and shoulder into the middle of her chest, the steel spike there jarring back against its own root, the top of the wolf's skull clipping up under her chin and snapping her neck back a finger's width.

*Rrh.*

The snarl came out short and irritated, more temper than hurt. She rode the weight of it back half a step, her feet skating in the mud, and the mud took the slide for her and held. Her paw went to her chest of its own accord and pressed flat over the place, and under it she felt the bruise begin: a small, warm ache, no bigger than the wolf's skull. The spike had taken the worst of the force and given almost none of it back to her. It was what the spike was for.

That was all. For a blow that had crossed the clearing faster than the thunder, that was nearly nothing, and both of them knew it.

The plain blow had struck her hard body and stopped dead.

The wolf was already sliding past her and wheeling round in the mud, and Aura saw the moment it reached Dusk too: the tightening at the corner of the long jaw, the quick, reassessing flick of the pale eyes from her chest to her face. She had hit steel with nothing but speed, and speed was not enough.

Aura lowered her chin to cover her throat and let out one breath through her nose. Her neck ached faintly where the skull had clipped it. She let it ache.

Rain ran into her eyes. She blinked it away, and the cold of it reached her now that she had time to feel it, down the back of her neck and into the flattened fur along her spine.
