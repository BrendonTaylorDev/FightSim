— Aura —

She made herself quiet. Not still: still could be read. Quiet, the way water is quiet before it moves, everything loose and everything ready.

The wolf's breath whistled. Anyone could have heard it, even through the rain: a thin, high note on every inhale, where the chop had crushed something in that neck that had not yet uncrushed itself. Aura listened to it the way she would have listened to footsteps in the dark. In. Out. In.

*Keep it short. Nothing wild.*

On the out-breath she went.

Her right paw closed, and the aura came to it unasked, a hard, bright shine over the black fur of her fist, the steel spike on the back of it gleaming like a struck coin. Her weight went from her back foot to her front as the fist travelled, a straight, short punch with no wind-up, aimed at the long jaw that had just burned her. Water ran off the steel in a thin line behind it. She committed fully, sure her body would follow wherever she sent it.

Under the snarl of the storm she felt something close to enjoyment, and hid it from herself at once.

— Dusk —

She saw it in the hips before the fist had left the shoulder, and she was already going backward.

All four legs at once, a hard spring from the haunches that threw her clear, her forepaws leaving the mud last. The punch went through the place where her jaw had been and kept going, a gleaming streak a finger's width from her whiskers, and the wind of it was cold on her wet muzzle.

She landed light, and the jackal was still in front of her, still extended, overcommitted by half a heartbeat, her face open behind the fist.

Dusk did not think about it. She went back in on the same breath, a short, flat snap of her jaws that was not a real bite, only teeth raked fast and hard across the black mask of the jackal's muzzle, and then she was out again before the jackal could close on her.

It landed. She felt it land: the hard ridge of bone under fur, the jolt of it through her own skull.

— Aura —

*Tss.*

The hiss came out between her teeth before she could stop it. Her muzzle wrinkled hard against the sting, lips pulling back off her teeth, and for a heartbeat her eyes watered. Then she breathed out, and it was a sting and nothing more, a line of heat along the bridge of her nose.

She drew her fist back and set herself again. Her balance was her own. Her breath was a little shorter than she liked.

The wolf stood two lengths off in the rain, head low and stiff on that ruined neck, and watched her. The mud where she had landed was still settling round her paws, the brown water creeping back into her prints.
