— Dusk —

The tremor started the moment the need for her legs ended. It came up from the mud through her paws and into her shoulders and her hips, a fine, steady shaking she could not stop and did not try to.

She stayed on her feet. She did not quite know how.

Now that the fight was over her hurts began to speak, one at a time, as if they had been waiting their turn. Her throat first: a ring of fire round it that tightened every time she swallowed, so she tried not to swallow. Then her head, heavy and ringing, the ringing with a heartbeat in it. Her back, one long bruise from her neck to her hips, the spine stiff as a post. Her ribs, both sides now, so that there was no way to breathe that did not cost her. The thigh. The foreleg. One by one they spoke, and she listened, because there was nothing else to do.

*Water,* she thought. The creek was right there behind the log, loud and brown. *Lie down.* Somewhere under the trees where the rain did not reach.

She did none of it. She stood in the mud with her head low and her legs braced wide and shaking, and breathed in short, rasping sips through her open mouth, and did not move.

The rain hammered on the leaves overhead and ran down her mane in streams.

— Aura —

She lay where she had fallen, on her back in the churned mud, with the rain falling straight down into her face.

A flash of lightning, further off now, lit her for an instant and picked out every mark on her: the burned, blackened fur round the spike in her chest, the swelling down one side of her muzzle where the teeth and the branch and the wolf's skull had all found the same place, the bite marks on her neck and forearm, the right knee lying at an angle that was not quite right, swollen fat under the matted fur.

Her paws lay open at her sides, palms up, filling slowly with rain. The four black appendages at the back of her head lay flat in the mud like spilled ink. Her ears had gone slack.

Her breathing was slow and steady. Her chest rose, and fell, and rose.

Without the fight in it, her face had gone smooth. She looked almost peaceful, if it were not for the marks.

The thunder rolled away eastward over the trees, softer each time.
