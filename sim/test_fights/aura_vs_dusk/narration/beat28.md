— Aura —

The smallest movement she could make was to open her right paw. She made it.

The wolf's teeth had let go of her shoulder, and the jaws had dragged her in close as they went, close enough to feel the wolf's breath on her neck. Dusk was still there, a stride off, turning away to get clear. She moved stiffly, all in one piece, as if her back were a plank. Her chin was tucked low over her throat.

*Don't wait for her to set.*

A hunter's stillness had come over Aura, nothing wasted between one move and the next. She drove off the right knee because it was the one under her, and it buckled and threw her line wide, and she let the stagger carry her the rest of the way. Her open paw went into the stone mane and closed.

She clamped on with everything she had and braced to be dragged.

The wolf did not drag her. The wolf stopped, held there at no distance at all, the stone points of the mane digging into Aura's palm, the wet fur bunched in her fist. Dusk's muscles bunched against the grip and went nowhere.

Then Aura brought her left paw over the top and down onto the side of the wolf's head. And again. Short chops with no room behind them, the aura cracking off the edge of her paw.

She felt no resistance at all; it simply went where she sent it.

— Dusk —

The first chop came down on top of everything that had already been done to her head, and her head could not hold any more of it.

Her body flinched before her mind did, as if it remembered first.

A raw scream came out of her and cracked in the middle. The second came down on the same place before the first had faded and the scream caught and went higher. Then the paw moved, and found her right ear, and the ear crossed its line under the third blow and the fourth came down on it while she was still screaming. She was held. She could not turn away. Her body had no give to spread the blows, so her head took all of them at once.

A heavy pressure filled her skull, as if it were stuffed with wet sand. Her own pulse was loud in her ears, louder than the storm. The hurt seeped from her head into the ear like cold through wet fur. A forepaw scrabbled at the mud. She was jerking against the grip, curling round her head, and none of it was hers to choose any more.

The wail rose, hitched, and rose again.

And then something happened that was not pain.

It came up from the very bottom of her, from under all of it. A heat. A roaring in her blood that drowned out the roaring in her head. Her heart, which had been labouring, began to hammer. Her legs, which had been shaking, locked. The pain did not go away. It fell back. It went somewhere behind her, still there, and in front of it there was only the jackal, and the paw in her mane, and her own will, enormous.

Her head was beyond her now. She spent nothing on it.

— Dusk —

She glanced down, and the movement made the whole clearing swing.

Her head bobbed low and came up slowly. Her eyes struggled to focus and found the jackal's face, very close, black mask and red eyes, and lost it, and found it again. Her whimpering kept time with her breathing, and she breathed through her mouth, carefully, keeping her throat still.

Branches thrashed overhead.

She looked at her own head the way she would have looked at someone else's: someone she felt sorry for.

And the jackal was looking at her with something new in her face. The jackal had felt the change come into the body she was holding. The surge. The legs that had stopped shaking.

*Still standing,* Dusk thought. *She noticed.*

The clearing had got very small: one paw in her mane, one face, a breath of space between them.
