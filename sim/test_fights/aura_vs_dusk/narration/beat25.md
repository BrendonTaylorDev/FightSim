— Dusk —

She had been counting without meaning to. Three times the jackal had put her own body on the ground or nearly. Three times she had got up.

The jackal's right foot dragged a mark through the mud when she moved now. Her gaze kept sliding off Dusk and having to be dragged back. And she had turned her hurt side away, her stomach curled in behind her arm, so that what Dusk could see was her left flank and her shoulder.

*You reached too far last time,* Dusk scolded herself, in her own impatient voice. *Don't reach. Go round.*

She went round. Two steps to her left through the mud, keeping her eyes on the jackal's shoulders, not her face, and the shoulders turned to follow her, slowly, too slowly, and the stomach came round with them.

The fire came up into her mouth again. It hurt her throat. Thunder walked round the edge of the sky and she paid it no attention.

She was frustrated, and tired, and it made her bite harder than she needed to. She ducked low under the jackal's arm and closed her burning jaws on the belly.

That hard body held every bit of heat she gave it, so she gave it more. She saw it sink in and stay, and a hard satisfaction went through her.

— Aura —

She saw it coming and curled in over her stomach, and in doing it left the rest of her open, and the bite went in under the arm anyway.

The jaws closed in her stomach with a muffled crunch, deep and close, and the fire went in with them.

The scream came before she even knew she had been touched there.

Her stomach had been hurt and hurt again, by the stones and the wolf's claws, and it had been the worst thing in her. Now it went past that. The fire found everything that was already there and made it something else. Then the teeth wrenched upward across her chest, as the wolf pulled free, and her chest crossed its own line with it, and the scream went higher.

A cry broke from her before she could stop it; her body had no way to brace for this kind.

The force of it turned her and carried her with it, dragged by the jaws, several steps toward the wolf and round, her feet scrambling under her, and she was going down. She put her left paw down flat in the mud to stop herself. She stopped. A thigh burned where a fang had scored it on the way.

She could not straighten at all.

— Aura —

She stayed half-bent with one paw on the ground and one pressed into her stomach.

She looked down at herself and barely recognised the shape she was holding. Her legs folded and unfolded under her as if they were arguing. She had stopped trying to use her right knee at all; even the thought of it hurt.

She bowed her head to breathe, as if the air were heavier down there and easier to reach.

She brought everything else to bear, everything she had left, and left her stomach to shake on its own.

The wolf stood a length off, flanks heaving, head low. She was swaying.

*She's tired too,* Aura thought. *She's hiding it better, that's all.*

Dusk watched her, and the rain ran off the stone of her mane. Under all of it, her own heart was going far too fast, and refused to slow.
