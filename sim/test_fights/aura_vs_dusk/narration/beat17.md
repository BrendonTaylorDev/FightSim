— Aura —

The four black appendages at the back of her head had lifted on their own and were quivering. She had not asked them to. They were reading the wolf without her: the heat, the fear, the steady, stubborn will under it.

Dusk lay face-down at the foot of the log and blinked at her, slowly, as if each blink might turn into something longer.

*Easy. Don't telegraph it.*

A steadiness settled over Aura like a hand laid on her back. She went across the mud more in hops than steps, sparing the right knee, the rhythm of it all wrong, and the wolf saw her coming and could do nothing with it. Somewhere out of sight a branch came down in the forest.

The back was open. Long and tan and wet, the spine a ridge down the middle of it, nothing over it at all. Dusk's eyes widened and Aura saw the alarm in them: the look of a body meeting the thing it feared most.

She did not aim. She went where the guard wasn't.

Her paw came up and came down, and came up, and came down. Quick and ugly, no wind-up, the edge of her paw into the same place on the wolf's back again and again, like someone beating a rug.

— Dusk —

The first one went into her back and drove her down into the mud, and the mud under her pushed back into her left ribs, and that was the shape of every blow after.

The second was worse. The third was something else.

Some old instinct in her had always warned her off this, and now she understood why. It did not hit the stone of her; it went in at the seams, along her spine, into the long muscles either side of it, and every blow came down on top of the one before and drove her down again into the ground, ribs and chest into the mud, nowhere to go, nowhere at all.

On the third there was a stunned half-second of silence, her eyes huge, and then the scream.

It came out of her whole. She could not keep it in; she did not try. It went up through the rain and cracked and went on, and the fourth blow came down in the middle of it and the scream caught and went higher. Her head knocked down into the mud with it and the world flashed white.

Then it stopped. The jackal had stopped.

Dusk lay with her back on fire. She had forgotten what her back had felt like before; she knew only this. Tears ran down her muzzle into the mud and she did not even try to hide them now. A dull ache crept from her back into her left ribs over the next few breaths, finding the old bruises from the jackal's paws there and settling in beside them.

She would not lie here to be beaten.

She got her forelegs under her and pushed, leaning her shoulder against the log. She got partway up, and the clearing tilted round her and swung, and she sank back down into the mud.

She did not try her back again for a long moment; the last try was still shaking through her.

Then she rolled onto her front and gathered her legs under her, all four, and pushed up, slowly, her weight braced on the rotten log, her back bowed, her shoulders shaking. And she stood. Winded, wincing, her sides heaving, both shoulders hanging a little, but on her feet.

— Aura —

Aura had stepped back. She stood and watched the wolf rise.

The pale eyes kept losing their focus and finding it again, a little slower every time. The fur along Dusk's back was still raised, prickled stiff with alarm, all down the length of the bruised spine. Her guard had dropped a hand's width and she had not noticed.

*Look at her try. You have to give her that.*

Something like respect had come into Aura, reluctant, and it showed in the distance she kept now: a little more than she needed.

She swallowed. It cost her more than she expected; the muscles of her own throat were tired.
