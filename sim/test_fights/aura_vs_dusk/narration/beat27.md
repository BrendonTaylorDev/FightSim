— Dusk —

Something had changed in the jackal since the miss. Her stance had gone narrower, her elbows clamped in over her stomach, and the guard that had been in front of her face was gone because of it. Her whole body tilted away from the right knee. She was half a fighter on that side.

And her left was open.

Dusk breathed in short pulls through her nose, sparing her throat, and made a plan in three steps. *In. Bite. Out.*

She chose the bite with care, because she could not afford to waste it. Not the stomach this time. The jackal was guarding the stomach and the knee and nothing else, and the left shoulder stood up above all that guarding like a stone above water.

She darted in, low, under the clamped elbows, and her jaws closed on the shoulder. She worried at it: one short pull, head jerking sideways, and as she let go her teeth raked on across the neck.

Then she was back out of reach before the jackal had finished flinching.

Even as her teeth closed she felt the strength go out of it. Bite against that body was like bite against a fence post: it went in, but not far. Frustration tightened her jaw. That should have hurt far more.

— Aura —

The fangs met in her left shoulder with the hard click of teeth on muscle and bone. The jaws dragged her toward the wolf before they let go, and her weight rocked onto her heels and back.

She felt the strength in it falter as soon as it touched her. A bite, only a bite. Her body knew what to do with a bite.

But the teeth raked on as they left, across the side of her neck, and her neck had already been bitten and shocked, and it was not interested in what her body knew. It rang through her left shoulder into her neck like a struck pipe. A short cry was jolted out of her, high, and she shut her mouth on the end of it.

There was a small hot spot on her left shoulder that she kept almost noticing. Her neck was too hot, too tight and too loud to ignore.

She folded a little round it, and had to make herself straighten.

— Aura —

Afterward she could not tell any more where her right knee ended and the hurt began. It twitched, and each twitch was its own separate agony. Her stomach kept sending up little jolts long after the bite, each one a surprise, and seemed to drag the rest of her down toward it like an anchor.

A groan came up from somewhere deep and did not stop at once. She buried the rest of it in a cough, not very well.

She panted shallowly, afraid of the deep breath. Her breathing hitched and caught like a sob, though she was not crying.

There was fear in her. Real fear, for the first time in the fight. She looked at it squarely, for one breath, and put it down.

Her face was hot under the black mask. She had been caught so cleanly, from the side she had stopped watching, and she was embarrassed by it, which was absurd, and she was anyway.

— Dusk —

Dusk did not follow it up. She stood out of reach and watched.

The jackal licked dry lips and swallowed, and Dusk heard the swallow. Her tail hung low and still behind her, a weight now rather than a balance. Dusk watched the mud between them as much as she watched the jackal: every step either of them took would have to cross it.

*Don't rush it. She's coming apart on her own.*

A little of the tightness went out of her shoulders. It had landed. It had landed at all.

Somewhere out of sight a branch came down with a long crash.

The jackal's four black appendages hung limp at the back of her head now, plastered to her neck by the rain. Earlier in the fight they had lifted and quivered every time Dusk moved. They did not lift now. Dusk saw that, and knew she would remember it.
