— Dusk —

She was already moving when the surge began to go.

She felt it start to drain out of her mid-stride, like water out of a cracked bowl, and she did not stop for it. She had seen the jackal's guard drop: the chop had missed and the jackal was still bent over her own stomach, her arms down round it, her neck long and open above her shoulders.

The surge had one more thing in it. Dusk spent it.

She did not slow for the impact; she sped up into it. The breath went out of her in one push as she launched, and her jaws opened for the neck.

The jackal's chin came down. Some last reflex, tucked in to cover the throat, and the neck went with it, and there in front of Dusk's teeth was the spike again, the burned steel in the middle of that cream chest, and the jackal was leaning forward, falling into her.

It went where she had sent it, near enough. Just not quite where she meant.

She closed her jaws anyway, with everything that was left.

And it was perfect. She knew it as her teeth met: the angle, the instant, the jackal's own weight coming down into the bite. Nothing glanced away. All of it went in.

*She felt that,* Dusk thought, as the last of the surge went out of her. *All of it.*

Then every hurt she had came back at full size, all at once.

— Aura —

The jaws closed on the spike with a crunching grind, and the whole of the bite went into her.

She had been leaning into it; she had not been able to straighten, and her own weight came down onto the teeth and drove them home. There was no glancing off steel this time. Nothing slid away. The spike took it and passed every bit of it straight back through its root into her chest, and the old burn there woke with it, and her chest and her throat took the rest as the jaws dragged her toward the wolf, further than any bite should have, and let go.

It landed a hand's width from where it had been sent, and it did not matter.

She heard the scream from somewhere far away, as if it were someone else's. It was a long, breaking cry and she did not stop it.

She folded in half round her chest and stomach and stayed folded, head down, breathing at the mud, for one heartbeat, two. She could not straighten. She knew exactly how it looked.

Her chest would not breathe for her; she breathed with her shoulders. Her throat ached in a narrow line where the teeth had skidded across it.

*Stay awake. Stay here.*

And then, from somewhere under the floor of her, something came up to meet her.

— Aura —

It came the way a tide comes into a dry channel: from below, all at once, filling everything.

Her heart, which had been fluttering, began to pound. The cold went out of her hands. The pain did not leave. It stepped back. It stood just behind her shoulder, every hurt she had, and in front of it there was room again, and her own will, very large and very calm.

A cry still came out of her with every breath, small and helpless. She let it. Holding it in cost breath she did not have.

Every breath out dragged one eye shut, and it was slower each time to open. Every breath out ended in a small hitch, like a stair she kept missing. For a few heartbeats her stomach was the whole world, and the clearing was somewhere far off. She shifted once to ease it, and the shift made it worse, and she went still again.

Her stomach had gone past answering; it only lay there and hurt. There was no position for her chest that hurt less. She had tried them all.

She watched her own sides heave as if she were watching someone else breathe.

*All right,* she thought. *Now I know her reach.*

— Dusk —

Dusk stood two lengths off, emptied, and watched.

The jackal's guard came up again, a little lower than before, a little slower. Her weight rocked forward and back, looking for somewhere to settle that did not hurt, and not finding it. Her eyes closed on every breath out and came open again by effort.

*Don't stare. Just see.*

A bough shook above them and let its drops go all together.

For a breath there was guilt in Dusk, at a hit that clean on someone already that hurt. Then it was gone.

The jackal's shape blurred in front of her, and steadied, and blurred.
