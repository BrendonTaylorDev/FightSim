— Dusk —

Her body wanted to lie down in the mud and breathe. She made it stand instead, and then made it go forward.

She counted what she had left. Breath: not much. Legs: all four, still. One good trick.

The jackal stood with her right knee barely touching the ground, and when she turned her head she swayed on her feet. She was still bent a little round her stomach. Her neck was long and bare above it.

Dusk held her own head stiffly, turning her whole body instead of her ruined throat, and the hope that came into her was a wary thing. She held her breath a moment too long and let it go.

Drops shook loose from a bough above them and pattered on the mud.

Her teeth prickled. The charge came up into them from somewhere in her chest and crackled across her fangs in thin blue threads, the rain spitting where it touched them.

Her jaws parted just enough, and she lunged for the place she had been watching.

— Aura —

The fangs met in the side of her neck with the snap of teeth meeting through fur, and the lightning went in with them.

It was a hot wire drawn through her neck, and the wire stayed in. The jaws dragged her toward the wolf, and her body swayed like something hung on a line, and her feet did not move; she was too staggered to do anything with them but stand. A smaller, meaner jolt went sideways into her throat and lodged there.

The scream came in pieces, each one cut off by the next spasm of the current, and then the jaws let go and the current with them.

Both her paws flew to her neck and hovered there, afraid to touch it. Her whole stance tilted away from it. When she tried to turn her head she could barely move it. Her neck throbbed so hard she could feel it in her teeth.

*I've been hit before. I'm still here.*

Her feet caught up with her. She planted them, square, her guard back where it belonged. There was a stubborn calm in her now, slow and deliberate, and she made every movement after it with care.

— Dusk —

Dusk did not follow it. She stood a length away and watched.

She kept her eyes on the jackal's neck, where the muscles were still jumping, settling round the hurt. The ears had pinned flat and stayed there. When the jackal moved again, she took a breath first. Before, she had simply moved.

*She's shaking it off. Don't let her.*

Dusk wanted this over. She could feel it in how her weight was already coming forward again, too soon.

Her throat she could still feel. Her back she could still feel. Her hind feet, in the cold mud, she had stopped feeling some time ago.
