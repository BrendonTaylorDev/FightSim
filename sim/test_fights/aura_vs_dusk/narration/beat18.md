— Dusk —

Her breath caught at the top. She heard it catch, a small, ugly sound in her own throat, and then there was a silence in her chest before the next breath would come.

She stood against the log with her spine held straight because bending it was not possible, and it made her stiff and easy to read, and she knew it.

The jackal's right knee shook when it took weight. She took it off again at once. When she turned, she turned the long way round rather than pivot on that foot.

*Press now, or let her come?*

Dusk would not let her come. She had felt what came.

A grim patience settled over her, shoulders low, in no hurry at all. She did not need to reach the jackal. She needed the ground to.

She threw her head back and howled, ragged, and the stones answered. They came up out of the mud round the jackal's legs: first a few, then many, lifting, hanging there for a heartbeat in the rain, and then closing.

She aimed for the place the jackal was guarding most carefully.

She knew, even as she did it, that rock would not finish this. She would have to find another way in. But the stones would hold her for a while.

— Aura —

She saw them come for the knee and her whole body flinched before they landed, the leg snatched in against her, too late.

They closed on her with a roaring rush that drowned the rain: a ring of rock round her legs, a heavy edge into the right knee, a glancing one against the left, a slab across the right foot. The knee buckled a little and her weight shifted off it, but it held, and she was square again at once.

The cry was out before she could stop it, short and high. She shut her mouth on the end of it.

It was only rock. Rock spent itself on her; her body had always known that. But it sank into her right knee and lost itself there, in the place that was already wrecked, and that place did not care what had hit it.

The knee felt hammered flat and still being hammered. Her right foot flinched before the hurt from the knee had even reached it, an old sore place under the new one, a hot point with a cold, numb ring round it. A long groan came out of her that she could not stop halfway.

— Aura —

The stones did not fall away. They stayed round her legs, packed against her shins and knees, cold and heavy, wet mud setting round them. She tried to shift her feet and they would not shift. Her legs had gone stiff and slow, locked to the ground.

She could feel every heartbeat arrive in the right knee and spread outward from it.

She looked down at it. It had gone still and quiet, the trembling gone out of it, and she did not like the quiet. It felt as if it were being wrung out like a cloth.

A yelp came up and she swallowed it, and it showed only as a jump in her throat. Her jaw clicked shut and the next sound stayed behind it.

*Don't give her the sound. Don't.*

She took a deep breath through her nose and let it out slow. Then she breathed out hard through her nose, twice, as if she could push the hurt out with it. She held herself up very straight among the stones with a stiff, offended dignity, as if nothing had happened at all.

Branches thrashed overhead. The rain came down between them, and the clearing waited.
