— Aura —

Her next breath had a long way to go. She sent it all the way down anyway, past the burned spike and the bruised chest, and felt the stones round her legs loosen as she did. The cold stiffness went out of her shins. The rock was settling into the mud, nothing more now than rock.

Every stride on the right knee would be a short stab she would have to breathe through. So she would not stride.

The wolf stood against the log with her back held rigid. She would not bend it; when she needed to go lower, she lowered herself with her legs instead. She had curled round it without knowing, one shoulder turned in to cover it, and the whole of her front was open: the chest, the throat, the pale fur under the jaw. When she swallowed, she grimaced.

*Hit, then gone. Hit, then gone.*

Lightning went white across the clearing and printed the trees on her eyes, and in the white Aura brought her paws together. She had shifted her weight before she meant to; eagerness was running ahead of her. She let it run.

The aura came hard and fast this time, blue-white between her palms, the rain boiling off it, and she did not wait for the wolf to give ground. She already could feel it: once this started working, it kept working.

She fired it into the open front of the wolf.

— Dusk —

It struck her chest and her throat at once with a booming impact that rolled away through the trees.

It went into her throat as though nothing in her had been built to stop it.

There was a moment, a short one, where the hurt in her throat had been as bad as a hurt could be. Then it was worse. Something in her let go of the edge of it. The scream came up out of her with the blast still in her and went on longer than the blast did; it went on until there was no breath left to make it, and then her mouth stayed open on nothing.

Her head whipped back. Her weight went with it before she could stop it, several steps backward through the mud, her hindquarters dropping, her forelegs skidding, close, very close to going down. Her tail swung wide for balance. She caught herself on a back foot with the log's mossy end against her hip.

She did not fall. She did not know how she did not.

She tasted copper at the back of her throat and swallowed it, and the swallow brought a strangled cry out of her. What had begun as a point in her throat opened out into an ache across her chest, finding the bruises from the jackal's paws and the mud that had been driven into her there, and settled in among them. Her chest cramped once, as a warning, and let go.

She let her head hang rather than hold it up.

She was out of place, half-turned and slow, her guard nowhere. She knew she needed her feet back now. She curled her tail tight round her own hind leg and held on to it, as if that would hold her up.

She moved, when she moved, the way one carries an overfull load, slow and lopsided, her throat the heaviest part of it.

Then she stopped moving. She made herself stand there, completely still, and get her breath, and wait. It was the one thing in her body she could still choose.
