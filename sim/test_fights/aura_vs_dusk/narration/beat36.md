— Dusk —

*Go.*

Her body caught up with the word a heartbeat later.

She should have been down. She knew it. Everything in her said so: her ribs, her throat, her skull, the legs that had stopped answering properly a long time ago. Her feet found the mud anyway and planted, square, and the stagger went out of them.

The jackal was in front of her, bent nearly double over her stomach, swaying. Dusk felt her brace, felt the whole of that battered body gather itself round her neck, the soft, bitten neck, and pull her chin down to cover it.

She put the bite where the bracing was.

The last of the lightning came up into her teeth on its own, a thin blue crackle across her fangs, the rain hissing off it. She kept her ruined left side turned away. She went for the neck with her head low and her own neck stretched long, every hurt in her screaming at the stretch.

— Aura —

Everything in her tried to pull her neck out of the way, and nothing in her could.

The fangs closed in the side of her neck with a muffled crunch, deep and close, and the lightning went in with them. Her throat took the rest. Her neck crossed its line, and the scream cracked in the middle, and her whole body clenched round it, her good paw scrabbling at the wolf's mane, at the mud, at nothing.

And then the sound went.

It did not stop. It drew away from her, as if down a long tunnel: the rain first, then the thunder, then her own scream, then the wolf's breathing right against her ear. The light greyed at the edges. The clearing narrowed to the wolf's pale eye, very close, and then to a small bright place in the middle of everything, and the bright place shrank.

Her legs did not answer. She asked them, and they did not answer.

Her arms dropped to her sides and stayed there, palms open.

The ground came up to meet her slowly. The wolf's jaws let go. She went over backward into the mud and lay there on her back with the rain falling into her open face. Her ears went slack against her head as she fell. Every muscle she owned let go at once.

Mud sucked at a foot as the wolf took a step back.

— Dusk —

She stood over the jackal with her legs shaking and watched her go.

Nothing came up off the ground. The red eyes had closed. The black appendages lay flat in the mud. The chest rose, slow, and fell.

A slow wave of something like relief went through Dusk, and after it, the tiredness, all of it at once.

*Done.* *It's... done.*

She let her breath go out, slow, and it did not come back in quite yet.
