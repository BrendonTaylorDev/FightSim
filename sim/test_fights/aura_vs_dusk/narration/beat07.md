— Aura —

The clack of the wolf's teeth meeting nothing was still in her ears: a small, hollow sound, the sound of a door closing on an empty room.

And the wolf was right there.

Dusk had turned to face her, but she had turned her whole body to do it, not her head. Her neck was locked. Her chin was tucked down over her throat and she was guarding that, only that, her front low and her side left open to the rain.

*She's guarding the wrong side.*

Aura timed it to the wolf's breath. She watched the ribs swell under the wet tan fur, and on the in-breath, when the ribs were full and the body was at its least ready to be hit, she stepped inside.

There was no room to wind up and she did not need any. Her paws went to work at no distance at all. A short straight blow into the chest under the mane. A hook round into the left ribs, where the breath was. A chop down onto the point of the left shoulder. And as the head came round toward her, the heel of her paw up into the hinge of the jaw. Four blows, so close together they were nearly one, the aura cracking off her paws on every one of them like a whip.

She felt the first land and something in her blinked: a small surprise at her own strength, there and gone.

She felt the wolf give under it like soft ground.

— Dusk —

It came with a sharp crack of air, and it came everywhere.

Chest. Ribs. Shoulder. Jaw. She could not have said which came first; they arrived as one storm of small, hard blows, and every one of them went in. Not like a claw, which skids on stone. Not like a bite. These found the seams. She was stone, and she knew in her bones what a well-placed strike did to stone: it did not break the surface, it went in at the cracks and broke what was behind it.

*Prrt.* A stupid, startled chirrup jumped out of her at the first blow to her chest. Then the ribs. Her breath went out of her in a rush and her mouth hung open, and her body twisted away to the right with it, round the blow, her shoulders giving and then setting again. The shoulder took its own blow with a sharp squeak she would never have let anyone hear on purpose. And then the jaw, and her eyes squeezed shut and her teeth gritted against a groan that came up anyway from the bottom of her chest. *Tch-tch.* Indignant. She heard herself chitter like a pup and hated it.

A line of fire ran from her left ribs to her jaw, drawn in one stroke.

She took another breath. It caught at the very top, a short sharp twinge on the left side, and she let it out with a small *hff* and took another, shallower.

The hurt bit in and held, refusing to fade the way a plain knock would.

A full-body shudder went through her, tail to ears, and she had to stand through it with her four feet planted in the mud, while the jackal stood a stride away and watched her do it. Somewhere out of sight a branch came down in the forest with a long, splintering crash.

Dusk made a sound between a whine and a snarl and did not move. Neither did the jackal. Between them the rain fell, and whatever came next hung in it, unspent.
