— Dusk —

Both her ears were turned toward the jackal's right leg.

She had noticed it a moment ago and filed it away: the right knee was a beat late on every step. The jackal hid it well. She did not hide it from ears.

Dusk kept her chin down over her burning throat and looked up from under her brow. Her ears were set flat and hard with anger. She had been made to work for every breath since the thunder, and the jackal was still standing straight.

She could not bite. Her throat would not let her open her jaws wide enough to want to. She could not howl. What she had left was what she had started with: speed, and weight, and the ground.

The jackal shifted onto her good leg, and Dusk went into the space it left.

No gathering. Her claws tore into the mud and she was across the gap in a blur, head down, the hard top of her skull and the stone of her mane driving into the jackal's chest, and she did not stop when she hit her. She kept going.

She saw it break on the jackal and knew this kind of blow would never be enough by itself. So she gave it something to break her against.

The split oak was behind the jackal. Dusk drove her toward it.

— Aura —

The skull hit her chest and the spike there jarred, and the wolf's shoulder clipped her left arm, and none of it was much. A low *hnh* came out of her, more surprise than hurt.

Then her feet were gone.

The wolf did not stop. Aura's heels skidded backward through the mud, a dozen strides, her paws scrabbling at a mane full of stone, and the clearing went past in a smear of grey, and then the split oak came up behind her and stopped her dead.

Her back hit the charred wood with a solid whump, like a sack dropped. Her right shoulder took it, and her upper back, and the back of her right thigh, and the heat of the smouldering heartwood was right there through the crack, close enough to feel on her fur.

And the wolf kept pushing.

For a long moment she was pressed flat between them, the oak behind and the wolf's whole weight in front, the steel spike ground into her own chest from outside, her head snapped back against the trunk. Her mouth opened on a squeal that had no air behind it, only the shape of one. The breath and the spit were forced out of her together. The squeeze went all the way through her, front to back.

She gritted her teeth and held. She refused to be flattened.

Was that really all the wolf had?

The spike had taken the worst of the crush and let the rest go by, and the old burn in it woke under the pressure and throbbed. Her right shoulder pulsed against the trunk; she could feel her heartbeat in it for a few beats, and then she could not. The hit spread from her shoulder into her upper back like a ripple from a stone.

Then the weight came off her. Dusk staggered back out of the crush, her feet catching up under her at last, square again, her guard back where it belonged.

Aura's legs did not hold.

The tree was holding her up, and then it wasn't. She slid down the charred bark with her fur dragging on it, slowly, all the way down, and came to rest on her back in the mud at the oak's foot, looking up into the rain. Her eyes were watering. She blinked them clear, angry at them.

The wind changed its mind, and a thread of smoke from the oak drifted across her face.

The first thing she saw clearly again was the wolf, standing over the clearing with her head low, breathing hard.
