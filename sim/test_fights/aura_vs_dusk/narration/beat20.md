— Dusk —

She heard the jackal's breathing before she looked: harder now, a catch in it at the top.

Then she looked. The jackal lurched rather than walked, the right knee dragging behind each step, and her stance had gone crooked, set to spare the right foot. She was faster on her left side now. Everything good she had was on the left.

*Then go right.*

Dusk's feet had caught up with her. She planted them, square again, her guard back where it belonged. She tried to roar. It came out a rasp, and she did not care.

She came off the log low, dropping her whole body under the line of the jackal's paws, and then rose up into her at no distance at all.

She was a wolf and she fought like one: claws, shoulders, the weight of her chest, all of it thrown at once. A forepaw raked up into the belly. Her shoulder drove into the chest. A hind leg kicked out at the bad knee as she twisted. And as the jackal's head came down, the hard top of Dusk's own head came up into her muzzle.

It was not stone. It was not fire. It was the one thing the jackal had been doing to her all afternoon, done back, and she felt it work.

A small, fierce joy went through her, at a blow that did exactly what it should.

— Aura —

It landed everywhere at once with a booming impact that rolled away through the trees, and it got in.

Her muscles locked in alarm, and the locking only let it in deeper. Steel did not help her here; steel was the very thing it broke. The claws in her belly went in behind the muscle and stayed. The shoulder in her chest drove the breath out of her. The kick to her knee took the leg out from under her and the ground tilted and she went with it, several steps, two quick ones that weren't hers and then more, staggering out to the left, very nearly down, her good leg scraping and catching and holding her up by nothing. She ended half-turned, her side to the wolf.

Her knee had been the worst thing in her for a long time. It went past worst. The pain in it took on a dull, endless roar, like water over a weir, and she could not hear anything over it.

Her stomach crossed its own line a breath later. A hot, sick cramp low in her middle that made her want to fold, and she did fold, hugging herself round it, rocking on the spot. A ragged shout of pain came out of her and was cut off when her teeth met.

Her muzzle had been raked and struck by a branch already. The head-butt found all of that and woke it.

She still had strength in her. Every bit of it now had to go around her right knee.

— Aura —

She stood bent over her own stomach with her paws pressed into it and her right leg held off the mud, and the rain ran off her muzzle in a thin stream.

Her right knee felt hollowed out and filled again with ache. A cramp tightened round it and would not let go. She looked down at it as if it belonged to someone else, and watched it tremble.

Her muzzle jumped and shuddered under the fur with a will of its own. The ache filled it slowly from the middle out, like water filling a cup.

A grunt came up and she turned it into a cough, as if that would fool anyone. The next one she kept behind her teeth: a grunt through closed teeth, and nothing more.

Her chest lifted her shoulders with each pull of air. She let one breath go in a hiss, and the next came a little easier.

*Pain is only telling you something,* she thought. *Take what it tells you and leave the rest.* The knee was telling her she could not move. The stomach was telling her she could not take another of those. She took both, and left the rest.

The air smelled of wet earth and smoke. Her feet found the mud again, one and then the other.

She looked up. She measured the gap between them once more.
