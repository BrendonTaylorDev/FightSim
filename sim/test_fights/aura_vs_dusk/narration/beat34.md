— Aura —

Her heartbeat was very loud. It was the surge, still pounding in her, and under it, she could feel, something tired that was waiting for the surge to end.

The wolf carried her head tilted, the hurt side turned away. Her voice, when it came, had gone to a rasp. She was hunched round her left side, and when Aura shifted her weight the wolf flinched and snatched that side further away.

*That's where it hurts. Right there.*

*Make her turn,* Aura thought. *She hates turning.*

She did not try to make her turn. She went to her instead, short, from the chest, no wind-up to give it away, her one good arm swinging round the outside of the wolf's guard in a hook. The bitten right arm stayed tucked against her, useless, and she let it. She drove from her hips, and the shoulder snapped through after.

That was the moment, and she knew it.

The edge of her paw went into the ribs the wolf was guarding, and through them into the chest, and up into the neck, all one stroke. It hit far harder than the force she had put behind it, and she saw why in the way Dusk buckled.

— Dusk —

She tried to twist away, but the hurt made her slow.

It came in with a deep, slapping thud, ribs and chest and neck together, and it went straight into the place she had been guarding all along. She had always had to be careful of this. She had not been careful enough.

Her ribs went past anything she had felt yet. The sound came up out of her with the pain, raw, cracking in the middle, and then another after it, cracked and helpless. Her chest crossed its line with them. All of her clenched round it at once and a forepaw scrabbled at the mud and the shaking took her.

Her body followed the blow, several steps to the right, her paws sliding, her guard swinging wide as she tried to right herself. She was going down. Her shoulder was almost in the mud. She did not go down.

Then she tried to breathe, and nothing went in.

Her mouth opened. Her chest heaved. Nothing. The air was right there, all round her, falling out of the sky, and she could not get any of it. She stood with her mouth open on air that would not come, and every movement she made was a movement with nothing behind it.

She leaned away from her ribs as if they were trying to pull her down. They lay still because they could do nothing else, and the stillness ached. She guarded them, and her neck paid for the guarding: the hurt in it was so large now it seemed to stand outside her, and she was inside it.

A hoarse sound came out of her on every half-breath, quiet and constant. Water ran in the ruts round her feet.

— Aura —

And then, above them, the wind tore a branch out of the canopy.

Aura heard the crack and could not move. It came down turning, a dead limb thick with wet moss, and the heavy end caught her full across the muzzle that had already been raked and struck and battered, and slid down across her right shoulder.

The scream cracked in the middle. Her whole body clenched round her face, her good paw scrabbling at the bark of the branch as it rolled off her, and the shaking that came after would not stop.

And in the middle of the shaking, she felt the surge go.

It drained out of her the way it had come, from below, all at once. Every hurt she had came back at full size. The knee. The stomach. The chest. The arm. The face. All of them stepped back in front of her and stood there.

From the wolf, a drop of rain ran off the tip of her muzzle and fell into the mud between her forepaws.
