— Aura —

She would not look at her own chest. She knew what she would see there, and it would not help.

She made herself straighten, slowly, the hurt still there, until she was nearly upright. The surge held her up from inside like a hand under her ribs.

The wolf could not keep both eyes open at once any more. One squinted half shut, the other stared. She swallowed often, and each swallow was a visible effort.

*You always throw one too many,* Aura told herself drily. *And here you go again.*

There was a flicker of doubt at how much the wolf still had. She pushed it down.

She tucked her elbow tight to her side so that none of the force would leak away, and the aura came to her fist one more time, and she drove it. The twist that powered it pulled at her stomach and she grunted. Her weight went from her back foot to her front as the fist travelled.

It was wrong from the start. Her timing was gone, her feet were wrong under her, and the punch came out sloppy and late, half of what it should have been. Halfway through it her own weight ran ahead of her and she was falling after her fist. The wolf had her head down and covered, and the punch went low, past the head, past the chest, and down.

— Dusk —

It landed on her right thigh with a solid whump, like a sack dropped, and skidded up across her hip and down along her tail.

It was half a punch. It did more to her than its weight could explain, and she knew precisely why. Her whole nature was wrong for steel. The sting did not fade; it settled in, and then it grew.

The leg buckled a little under her and her weight shifted off it, more than a blow that size should have managed. It held. Her feet stayed where they were.

A yelp came out of her and cracked in the middle. Her ears pinned back. The pain forked from her thigh: one branch stayed, the other ran down into her tail and set it twitching.

When she planted the leg again, it prickled, and then it held.

The jackal had overbalanced past her, staggering, catching herself.

— Dusk —

Dusk turned to keep her in view, and her sight doubled, slid together, and doubled again.

*She wants me to look at her eyes,* Dusk thought, dimly. *Watch the shoulders.*

She looked down at her own thigh. The fur there was flattened and wet and dark, and under it the muscle twitched by itself. She stared at it as if it belonged to someone else.

Her breathing stopped for a heartbeat, and started again with a gasp.

The wind changed its mind and blew the rain sideways across the clearing.

The jackal had caught herself. She stood half-turned in the mud with one paw out for balance, bent at the middle, her right leg held off the ground, and for a moment neither of them moved at all, and she was held there like a picture.
