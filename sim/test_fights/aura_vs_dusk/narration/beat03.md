— Dusk —

She wanted the throat. It was as plain as hunger and needed no words.

Dusk kept her chin tucked down over her own neck and looked up from under her brow. Every movement of her head woke the ache there, so she did not move it; she moved everything else. The jackal's guard was loose, confident, her paws low. Nothing on her was being protected yet. She had been hit once and called it nothing.

*Then I'll give her something.*

Heat gathered in Dusk's jaws. It came up from somewhere behind her ribs and pooled in her mouth, and when she bared her teeth the rain hissed off them and turned to steam. Fire licked along her gums and wreathed her fangs in a flickering orange that lit the wet fur of her own muzzle from below.

She timed the bite to the instant Aura looked away.

The jackal's eyes flicked to the side, only for a heartbeat, to the smoke curling off the split oak, and Dusk came in under them with her burning jaws wide for the pale fur under the chin.

She saw the opening, and by the time she got there it had closed. The chin came down. The shoulders turned. And there was something else there instead, right in front of her teeth, something cream-furred and hard and gleaming wet, and her jaws were already closing.

She took what was there.

— Aura —

The fangs closed on the spike in the middle of her chest with the hard click of teeth on steel, and the fire came with them.

She had tucked her chin in time. That was the only thing she had done in time. The bite that had been meant for her throat found the spike instead, and the jaws locked round it and the heat went straight into the metal and through it, into the root of it, into her. Her chest and her right shoulder took the rest: the wolf's upper teeth raking the fur over her breastbone, the weight of the long skull slamming into her shoulder.

The jaws dragged her a half-step toward the wolf before they let go. Then she was standing square again, and the wolf was gone out of reach, and the spike was still burning.

*Hff.*

The grunt came through her closed teeth. Steel took a punch; steel took a stone. Steel took fire the way dry grass did. She had known that her whole life, the way she knew the shape of her own paws, and she knew it now from inside: the heat soaking into the spike and staying there, spreading outward through her chest like a hand laid flat against an oven.

She coughed once and pushed the breath back into place. Her ribs heaved in two short, shallow jerks and then steadied. She hunched a little over the burn, and straightened, and made herself straighten all the way.

The spike protested at her first movement and was quiet at her second. Her chest twinged where the teeth had raked it, and was quiet when she did not move. The shoulder only throbbed.

She did not touch any of it. She kept her paws up now, higher than before, the right one held across her chest where it could cover what had just been found, and she watched the wolf over the top of it.

A small muscle at the corner of her black mask worked, and worked, and would not stop.
