— Aura —

Her shadow and the wolf's lay faint on the mud between them, and the gap between the two shadows was very small.

Dusk had come away from the bite and turned, and was coming back. She was swallowing often, and every swallow was work Aura could see: the throat clenching under the fur, the chin tucked low to keep the neck covered. The wolf thought the neck was what Aura wanted.

Aura could not turn properly. Her right knee would not pivot, so she did not turn; she let the wolf come round to her. Water ran in the ruts round her feet.

Her paw curled into a hard knot, and the steel on the back of it took the aura and began to shine.

*The head. Not the neck. The head.*

She shoved off the oak with her shoulder, put all her weight on the good leg and drove the fist out from the hip and down, over the wolf's guard, at the broad dome of her skull between the ears.

— Dusk —

It landed with a solid whump, like a sack dropped, square on the top of her head, and the jaw took the rest of it as her head was driven down.

She had dreaded exactly this, and it was worse than the dread.

Steel. She had hoped the jackal would not think of it. The fist went into her skull like a stone into wet clay and the hurt went in after it and did not stop: deep, flat, crushing, a weight laid on the inside of her head. The ache already there from the blast found the new one and the two became a single thing.

Her head lolled before she could drag it up. It would not answer her properly. Every muscle near it locked, and the locking hurt as much as the blow had. Her jaw wrenched under it, trying to take the load her head had dropped.

A keening whine came out through her clenched teeth. Her eyes were streaming and blinking only stung them. Her face went through everything in one moment: lost, then found, then furious.

— Dusk —

The pain went quiet for a breath, and she did not trust the quiet.

She was right not to. It came back sharper the moment she let her shoulders loosen. She made herself unclench one muscle at a time, and every one she let go of brought the head back in.

She blinked and watched the clearing slide sideways and come back, and checked it again, and again. It came back each time. A little slower each time.

Her breath came loud through her open mouth. Her throat would not let her take it whole: she breathed in sips, because a full breath hurt going past. She tried to count the sips to keep them even, and lost count at three.

A small sound came up in her and she bit it off before it was all the way out. The next one got as far as her throat and turned back.

*How many more of those can I take?*

She did not know. That was the first time she had not known.

— Aura —

*Still sharp. Don't get greedy.*

Aura stayed where she was, her back to the oak, and watched with her eyes narrowed. The wolf's weight had come off her right side, the side the fist had driven her head down toward, and it stayed off. Once, Dusk's eyes flicked up toward the top of her own head, as if she could see it, and came back too late.

Aura kept a little more distance than she needed. Something hungry and focused had come into her and she let it stay.

Then a groan in the canopy, and a crack.

She looked up. A branch as thick as a leg was already coming down out of the rain, turning, and there was no room to move with the oak behind her and the knee under her. It caught her across both shoulders, the left and then the right, the right taking the heavy end on top of where the oak had already bruised it, and she hissed and hunched under it and it slid off her back into the mud.

The wind went on through the clearing, stirring the rain, and neither of them felt it.
