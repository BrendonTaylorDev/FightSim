— Aura —

The rain had turned colder. She could feel it on her skin through the soaked fur, and under it her own skin was hot, too hot, as if the fight were burning in her.

The wolf's voice had gone to a rasp. Her back was arched a little, held away from its own hurt, and the arch made her look smaller than she was.

*Stop being careful.*

Aura stepped in. She stepped short with the right knee and the whole move came a beat late for it, and she let it be late. She turned her hip into the punch so that the paw was only the end of it, and the aura came over her fist, steel-bright, and she threw it straight, without flourish, aimed through the middle of the wolf's chest.

The wolf made a small sound as it came, and a sting of guilt went through Aura at the sound. It was gone before it could slow her.

— Dusk —

She saw it coming. She knew this sort of blow by instinct, the way prey knew a shadow, and she flinched before it even landed: the right foreleg came up off the mud and across her chest, turned into the fist, and the steel crashed into it instead of into her.

It landed with a meaty smack on the thick of her upper foreleg. It was a fraction of what it would have been in her chest, and it was still steel. Her leg had nothing as hard as that to meet it with. She knew hard metal would always be harder than she was.

The blow knocked her back a full step, her feet skidding. She staggered, caught it, and the catching hurt too. Her claws scraped the mud for a grip she had not needed a moment before.

A real cry came out of her, and her knees dipped under it, and then she was standing.

The block had numbed the leg for a moment. She shook it out once, and flexed the paw open and closed, testing it, and did not take her eyes off the jackal.

She did not have a plan. She had stubbornness, pure, and nothing attached to it.

— Dusk —

It was only the foreleg. A tender patch, nothing worse, as long as nothing touched it twice.

It was everything else that came back.

Every breath scraped past the hurt in her throat, a dry rasp she could feel more than hear. Her throat burned and froze by turns, and she could not bear either. A slow ache ran out from her back along her ribs, wrapping round to the front. The hurt in her back had outlasted her flinching; now she only shook.

She looked down at her foreleg and watched the fur over it ruffle and darken where the rain had driven into the bruise.

*That's going to bruise.* She nearly laughed. There was no room for one.

A cry came out of her and she only half caught it, ragged at the end. She held her breath to stop the next one, and it came out anyway in a shaking rush that was half a groan.

There were gaps between her breaths now. She seemed to forget, and then remember. When she did breathe, she dragged the air in as if through cloth, slow and heavy and never quite full.

— Aura —

The twitches in the wolf had slowed. The breathing had not.

Dusk was trying to hide a limp by standing still. Aura saw through it at once: the right foreleg was set down too carefully, the weight already leaning off it. Between movements, the wolf's whole body shook.

*Every breath, she's checking it.*

There was a cold pleasure in Aura at having got it right, and she kept it behind a still face. The rain ran in the ruts round her feet, carrying the churned mud away toward the creek.

Her own knee trembled under her, and the tremor ran out through her and away into the ground.
