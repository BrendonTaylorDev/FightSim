The storm had been building over the forest all afternoon, and by the time the two of them came into the clearing it had stopped building and simply arrived.

Rain came down in sheets. It hammered the leaves overhead into a roar and ran off them in ropes, and the clearing under them had long since given up being ground and become mud: brown, churned, laced with roots as thick as a forearm that rose and sank in it like the backs of sleeping things. On the north side the old oak stood split down its middle where lightning had found it, and even in this downpour its heartwood smouldered, a sullen orange glow deep in the crack and a ribbon of smoke that the rain beat down and the wind tore away. A wall of brambles closed the east side. To the south a ring of pale toadstools stood in the grass like spilled teeth. A rotten log lay along the west edge, hollow and furred with moss, and beyond it the creek ran brown and loud and fast.

Aura stood at the south end with her feet set in the mud and her paws loose at her sides. The rain had flattened her blue fur and turned the cream of her chest dark, and the steel spike in the middle of it ran with water. The four black appendages at the back of her head lifted, very slightly, and quivered, the way they did when she was reading something nobody else could see.

What she was reading stood at the far end of the clearing, on four long legs, head low.

Dusk did not look like she minded the rain. It plastered her tan fur to the long muscles of her shoulders and darkened the cream of her underside, but the thick mane around her neck held its shape, and the stone spikes jutting from it shed the water like a roof. Her pale, blue-green eyes did not move from Aura. Her ears were pinned forward. Her tail hung straight and still behind her.

Lightning went white across the sky, and for an instant every tree in the forest stood printed on the eye, and the two of them with them.

*Fast,* Aura thought, reading the coiled stillness in the wolf. *She'll come straight. She'll come first.*

The thunder came a heartbeat later, and rolled, and kept rolling.
