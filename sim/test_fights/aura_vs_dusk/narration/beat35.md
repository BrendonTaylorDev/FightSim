— Aura —

She moved her good foot a finger's width further out. Nobody else would have seen it. It was the only thing holding her up.

Behind the wolf the ground dipped toward the creek, where the rain had cut a gully. She noted it the way she had been noting everything all afternoon, and knew she would not get the chance to use it.

The wolf was breathing in tiny, careful sips so as not to move her left side. One eye had swollen half shut.

Thunder walked round the edge of the sky.

*No need to be clever now,* Aura thought. *Just do it again.*

Her weight shifted before she meant it to. She held her breath through the effort, sparing her stomach, and went: a feint at the guarded left, the wolf curling further round it, and then her one good arm came round to the open right and drove into the ribs there. She aimed past the surface, at a point somewhere behind it, and stepped through with her weight following the blow.

The breath came out of her in a gasp.

— Dusk —

It landed with a meaty smack in the ribs on her right side, the side she had stopped guarding, and drove on through into her chest.

It got under her guard and under her skin, and stayed there working.

She had wanted to hide that side from her. Too late. It had been found.

Every part of her clenched round the ribs at once. Her chest, already past bearing, spasmed under the blow, and the scream that came out of it cracked in the middle and then the shaking started and would not stop. For a moment the world went quiet, as if someone had shut a door on it.

She was knocked off her line, several steps to the left, her legs going every way, her guard swinging wide. Her hindquarters went down and she caught herself on a back foot, her tail swinging out for balance, and somehow she came up out of it still standing.

And then the air came back.

It came into her ragged, thin and high, never enough to reach the bottom, but it came. She dragged at it. Each breath ended in a cry.

— Dusk —

Her heart seemed to have moved closer to her ribs; it was banging right under them. The world tilted a little to one side, and settled, and she was not sure it had settled all the way.

She looked down at her own chest and watched a tremor start there and climb, and braced against it as if it were another blow.

A strangled snarl came out of her and broke into a whine. The sound went up, and broke, and she did not try to mend it.

The pain in her left side had a dull, endless roar to it, like water over a weir. Her skull rang, and the ringing had a heartbeat in it. Her tail had curled tight round her hind legs and was quivering.

She made a small, stubborn count of the things that did not hurt yet. It was very short. Her left ear. One hind foot. The tip of her tail.

She panted with her eyes half closed, saving them. Each breath was a small climb, and she rested at the top of every one before she let it go.

The cry she had made was gone into the rain. Where it had been, there was only the sound of water.
