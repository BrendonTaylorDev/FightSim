— Aura —

The wolf was turning in the mud, and for one stride, as she turned, the back of her neck was the nearest thing in the world.

Aura saw the gap the way she saw everything in a fight: before it was quite there. The mane was thick and studded with stone, but behind it, where the neck met the skull, the fur lay flat and wet and there was no stone at all. The wolf's guard had dropped to carry the turn. Aura replayed that one moment, coldly, even as her body moved into it: the head swinging, the shoulders going with it, the neck stretched long and bare.

She did not step back to make room. She stepped in, so close she could smell the wet stone of the mane, and her right paw came up to her ear with the edge of it turned outward like a blade.

It came down with her hips behind it.

She felt the whole strike go into the wolf, all of it, with no give at all.

— Dusk —

It landed with a dull, heavy thud on the back of her neck, and her throat and her right ear went with it in the same instant: the chop, the crush of her windpipe against her own collar of stone, the side of the paw slapping across the ear so it rang.

The blow snapped her head back and her shoulders after it. Her feet never moved.

The scream went up and up and cracked at the top. *Waaah.* She hated it as soon as she heard it. She bit down on the second one and it came out as a short, offended bark instead, *Hah*, and then for a long moment no sound would come at all.

She knew. That was the worst of it. Her body had been made for stone and claws and the weight of other wolves, and it had never been made for this: the edge of a paw that found the place where her hardness was thinnest and split it open, as if the jackal had been handed the right key. It did not stop at the fur. It went in, down through the muscle of her neck to the root of it, and stayed there, throbbing, hot.

She swallowed, and the swallow would not go down. Air came through a space that felt no wider than a straw.

Her paw half lifted toward her ringing ear and stopped. She shook her head once, hard, as if to clear water from her ears, and winced at the shake. Then she squared up, keeping her neck stiff, her weight off it as much as a neck can be spared, and looked at the jackal from under her brows.

— Aura —

Aura did not follow it up. She stood where she was, with the rain running off her paw, and watched.

She studied the neck the way she might study a lock: where it was weak now, how it would give. The wolf was holding it too still. A neck that has been hit badly forgets how to turn; this one already had. When Dusk looked at her, she turned her whole body to do it.

Dusk set her feet, and then reset them, unhappy with both.

*Make her remember this one.*

There was a cold focus in Aura now, narrowed down to that one strip of wet fur behind the wolf's skull, and the rest of the clearing went quiet around it: the rain, the smoke from the split oak, the thrashing branches. The wind changed its mind overhead and threw the rain sideways for a moment, and still she watched.

The wolf swallowed again. Aura heard it this time, a small, effortful click under the noise of the storm.

Thunder rolled away over the trees, and the last of it faded among the trunks.
