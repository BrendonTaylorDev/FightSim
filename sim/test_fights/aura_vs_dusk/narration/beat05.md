— Dusk —

The ache in her neck had gone quiet while she moved. Now that she stood still, it came back, and brought the throat with it.

Dusk held her head stiffly and turned her whole body instead of her neck to keep the jackal square in front of her. Those red eyes were clear and quick and missed nothing. Every time Dusk had come in close, she had paid for it. Close was where the jackal lived.

*So don't go close.*

Behind her the rotten log lay along the edge of the clearing, dark with rain. She backed onto it in two long bounds, the soft wood giving under her paws, and from up there she could see all of the jackal at once: the paws held high, the chest she guarded, the muzzle with the fresh line across it. What the jackal was protecting was her front. What that left open was everything above it.

Thunder walked round the edge of the sky.

Dusk lifted her head, as much as her neck would let her, and howled.

It came out ragged, with the whistle of her crushed throat running through it, but the stone heard it. The spikes in her mane rattled against each other and then tore loose, one after another, and others came up out of the mud round the clearing to join them, rising, turning slowly in the rain, a slope of them hanging in the air over the jackal's head.

She brought them down.

She watched them go, and she saw the force go out of them before they landed. They were stone. The jackal was steel and bone and something harder than either, and stone was what her kind of body was built to shrug off.

— Aura —

They came down on her all at once with a hiss and a thud, a rattling, clattering wave of stone over every part of her: shoulders and back and arms and head, her ears, her face, a sharp edge against her throat and another into her stomach, a heavy one on her left foot. For an instant her whole body seized under the weight of it.

Then it was over, and it had been mostly weight.

She felt the push of it far more than the hurt. The stones rang off her like hail off a roof and slid down her into the mud, and almost none of them left anything behind. An old lesson paid off without her having to think it: let that kind of hit come, it cannot reach far.

But her head had snapped away on its own when the first stone hit her face, and it stayed turned.

Her eyes were screwed shut. Her ears had clamped flat. The stones had stopped falling, she knew they had stopped, and her head would not come back round. One heartbeat. Two. Her muzzle stung where the wolf's teeth had raked it and a stone had found the same line, and that was what had done it, she understood: a small sting on a small hurt, and her body had flinched from it like a pup's.

*Come back. Come back.*

She hated every heartbeat of it.

On the log, Dusk saw the flinch. Her ears came forward. That was an opening, and she meant to use it.

Aura's chest burned faintly under the cooling spike, the old bite finding the new bruise and settling into it. She breathed out sharply. The last of the stones settled into the mud around her feet with small, sucking sounds.
