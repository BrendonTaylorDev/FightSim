— Aura —

Grey. Everything was grey: the light, the rain, the mud, the wolf's wet fur gone dark as wet stone. Only the pale underside of her throat showed light against it, under the edge of the mane, where her head had been knocked back.

Aura's knee hurt when she stepped. She stepped anyway. She took a breath, and had to take it again, because the first one had caught on her nerves.

*One more like that and she'll start to doubt.*

The wolf's breathing had a thin wheeze in it now. Her head was still up from the blast. Her feet were still finding the ground. And the throat was right there, bare, pale, the one place on the whole wolf that did not have stone over it.

Aura stepped in, set her feet, and threw from the ground up.

She did not aim at the throat. She aimed a hand's width behind it, through it, the edge of her paw turned and her whole body behind the chop: hips, shoulder, the good leg driving into the mud. She felt the wolf's stone take the whole of the strike with no give at all.

— Dusk —

It landed with a dull, heavy thud across her throat and the side of her neck and the bridge of her nose, all in the same instant, and her head whipped back with it.

Her weight went with the hit before she could stop it: one step back, two, three, her paws skating and splaying in the mud, one forepaw sliding out wide before it found purchase. She did not fall. Her legs were shaking and she did not fall.

The cry came out and was gone, and she was panting hard through her nose after it.

The old hurt in her throat had been waiting. The new one found it and went straight in on top of it, and that was when the pain crossed over.

It was not a bone. Nothing broke. But for one long moment her throat was a hard ring of fire and it closed round everything: her breath, her voice, her thoughts. Her back arched off the blow and her mouth opened on a cry with no air behind it. Her neck locked, trembling. She could not keep it still and she could not bear to move it.

She bit down so hard on a sound that her whole head trembled with the effort.

Her body knew this one for an enemy and fought it from the inside, and lost.

— Dusk —

Then the moment went past, and she was still standing.

A hiss through her teeth, short, and then her jaw was clamped shut on everything else. She pulled air in through her teeth, hissing a little on each pull, in short, counted breaths, the way she would have breathed through a stitch. Her throat was fire to the touch and ice a finger's width under the skin. The ache in her neck filled it slowly from the middle out, like water filling a cup.

She felt small and slow and very aware of her own body, all at once: every leg, every breath, every place the jackal had touched her.

For a heartbeat there was fear, brief and honest, in the white round her eyes.

*So she got one in.* She made herself think it. *She'll get careless now.*

Her feet caught up with her. She growled through bared fangs and held her ground.

— Aura —

Aura stood where the chop had left her and watched.

The muscles round the wolf's throat jumped and twitched under the wet fur, and something in Aura went very calm. She saw the knees lock, the way legs lock when they cannot be trusted bent. She saw the mane hang lower, the stone points no longer bristling.

A bough above them shook, and drops came down out of it all together, pattering on the mud.

*She took that better than I thought.*

There was a flicker of doubt, at how much the wolf still had, and she pushed it down at once.

A small sound came up in her own throat. She swallowed it.
