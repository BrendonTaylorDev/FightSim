— Dusk —

She tried her right foreleg. Carefully. She lifted it a finger's width off the mud and the bruise in it told her exactly what it would and would not do now; she set it down again.

She tried to turn her head, and her neck would not let her. She turned her whole body instead, the way she had been doing all afternoon, so that she could see the jackal.

She could not stop watching her. Every slow breath the cream chest took, Dusk's eyes went to it, as if it might be the one that brought her up out of the mud again, the way she had come back every time but this one. It wasn't. The chest went up and down, slow and even, and the jackal did not move.

The pride came at last. It came late, and smaller than she had expected, and it was mostly surprise.

*I won.* She turned the word over. *Won.*

Her breathing came down, slowly, to something like her own. The shaking did not stop, but it got smaller.

She stayed standing in the rain on three and a half legs, her head low, her ribs heaving, and kept watch over the one she had beaten, because she could not think of anything else to do with herself.

— Aura —

She lay as she had fallen, on her back in the mud at the middle of the clearing.

The storm had begun to pass. The rain had thinned from sheets to a steady fall, and the light under the trees had gone from green to grey to a pale, washed silver. It lay across her and showed every bruise.

A faint frown had stayed on her face, a crease between her brows under the black mask, as if somewhere far down under it all she were still thinking about the fight.

Her chest rose and fell. Her paws lay open. She did not stir.

Behind her, in the split oak, the heartwood still glowed through the crack, sullen and patient, and the smoke went up from it straighter now that the wind had dropped. The creek ran loud beyond the log. Water dripped from every leaf in the clearing.

Somewhere off in the forest, a bird began, very tentatively, to sing.
