— Aura —

She could taste the oak's smoke at the back of her throat, bitter and wet, and under it the iron of her own bitten lip.

The wolf turned her whole body to keep Aura in view, not her head. Her breath whistled. Anyone could have heard it.

Aura tilted her head and looked at her with a cold kind of interest. The neck could not turn. The head had been hit hard. The legs were good, but legs alone did not make a fight.

*Finish it clean.*

She made a long pause of it. A long look. Long enough for the wolf to start to wonder what she was waiting for.

Then everything at once. She pushed off the good leg and the right knee only followed, and the aura came up round her like a wind, and she was simply there: across the gap faster than the wolf's eyes could track, shoulder-first into the wolf's right shoulder and chest. And she did not stop.

She felt it go wrong in the first instant. Her strike stopped short against the wolf the way a fist stops against a wall, and stung her more than it stung the wolf: plain speed, against stone. She felt Dusk absorb it like a full sack of sand and give almost nothing back.

So she did not rely on the blow. She kept driving. Her good leg pumped in the mud and the wolf's paws skidded backward in front of her, all the way across the clearing, toward the rotten log.

— Dusk —

The first hit was nothing. She had been built to shrug off that sort of thing, and she shrugged.

Then the log hit her.

It came up behind her out of nowhere, soft and wet and still hard enough, and she went into it back-first with a dull, heavy thud: both shoulders, the right and then the left, and the length of her back across the mossy top. The left took it worst. Something in it went heavy and warm, as if it had been packed full.

And the jackal was still on her.

For a long moment she was crushed between them, the rotten log behind and the jackal's whole weight in front, the steel of that chest spike pressed into her chest, her right shoulder ground into the wood. Her head snapped back against the moss. Breath and spit were forced out of her together, a squeal with no air behind it. She clamped her jaw and took it, eyes squeezed shut, every muscle locked against the pressure, then twisted her head sideways to find air and dragged it in through her teeth.

The blow wrenched a cough out of her and she nearly bit her tongue on it.

The surface of her right shoulder burned, and the burn was already thinning. The left did not thin. The left answered a heartbeat after the right and went on answering.

Then the weight came off her, and the jackal limped back a stride, and Dusk's legs, which had been held up by nothing but the press of her, did not hold her up on their own.

She slid down the side of the log. Slowly. Her shoulder dragged on the moss. She came to rest in the mud at the foot of it, face-down, chin and chest and belly in the cold slime, her hind legs sprawled behind her.

She hunched round the hurt there, mouth open, eyes squeezed to slits. Mud sucked at a foot when she tried to draw it in. She did not try to rise. Not yet.

She lifted her eyes instead, the only part of her that would lift, and locked them on the jackal, and held them there.
