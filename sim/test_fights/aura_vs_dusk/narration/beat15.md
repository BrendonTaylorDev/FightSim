— Dusk —

The ground under her was all wrong now. It tilted when she moved her head, so she did not move her head; she moved her feet, and the ground stayed where it was.

The jackal was still at the oak. Her right knee trembled under her even standing still, and when she shifted she tested the foot first, a quick dab at the mud before she trusted it with anything.

*She won't leave the tree. Keep her at the tree.*

Dusk held her head oddly still, as if balancing something on it, and came in. Every lunge pulled at her throat, so she shortened it. She went for the shoulder, the right one, the one the branch had just found: jaws open to seize it and lock on and hold the jackal against the burning trunk, where she could not get away from anything.

— Aura —

She twisted.

Her good leg did it all: one hard turn from the hip, her chest swinging out of the line of the jaws and her shoulder going with it, and the teeth closed a hair from her fur and found nothing. She felt the wolf's breath on her arm. The spin brought her round with her paws already up.

And there was the wolf's head, right beside her, still extended.

Aura chopped down at it. She turned her shoulder into it so that the whole of her weight would land, and the edge of her paw came down where the wolf's neck should have been.

It was not there.

Dusk was already moving before the chop started. She had felt that blow before, and she flinched away from it early now, a step to the side through the mud, and let it go past her. The chop cut the rain where her neck had been and Aura's own weight carried her half a step after it, onto the bad knee. She caught herself on the oak.

Thunder walked round the edge of the sky.

They stood a length apart again, the wolf with her head held carefully level and the jackal with one paw on the charred bark. Neither of them had touched the other.

Through the rain, Aura could just hear the wolf breathing: a thin, effortful whistle, in and out.
