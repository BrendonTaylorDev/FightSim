— Dusk —

Her neck hurt most. Then her ribs, every breath. Then her jaw. She took stock of them in that order and set them all aside.

She breathed in short pulls through her nose, sparing her throat, and looked at what she had done so far. The charge: nothing. The stones: nothing. The fire on the spike had landed. That was what it told her. The jackal's steel ate stone and shrugged off speed, and if she wanted to put her down, it would not be by hitting her harder in the same places.

It would be by putting her on the ground.

The jackal was light on her feet still. She came up onto her toes between movements, bouncing very slightly, her weight high. Her legs were wet to the knee with mud.

Dusk let a forepaw slip in the mud, as if by accident, and used the slip. Her claws raked down and dragged through the churned earth in one long stroke toward the jackal's feet, and the ground under them answered.

Stone came up out of it like a blade, a jagged edge of rock bursting out of the mud and slanting up between the jackal's legs.

Even as it rose, Dusk saw the jackal almost smile. She knew why. Stone again.

— Aura —

It took her across the left thigh and hip with a hollow thump that carried across the clearing, and the blow itself was almost nothing.

She took it squarely, the way she had always been able to take this kind. The edge rang off the muscle of her thigh and skidded up over her hip and she felt it the way she would have felt a shove. *Uff.* More surprise than pain. Her body said, quite calmly, that stone could not reach her.

Then the mud went out from under her.

The spike had come up between her feet and taken her standing leg with it, and the ground she had been bouncing on so lightly was slick as grease. Her feet went one way and the rest of her went the other, and she came down flat on her back in the mud with a slap that drove the breath out of her nose. Her upper back hit, then her left hip, harder, jolting the leg up off the ground, and her tail was underneath her and took the rest. She hissed at that, a short *Tss*, and her tail curled tight against her side, then loosened, then lay still.

She lay looking up at the canopy. Branches thrashed overhead. Rain fell straight down into her open eyes.

*Next time she does that, I'm ready.*

She did not try to rise yet. Her breath was coming back to her in short, irritated pulls, and her left hip had gone stiff with the shock, and she lay in the cold mud with the water soaking through her fur and let her body tell her it was all still there.

Then the wind came.

She heard it before she saw it: a groan high in the canopy, then a crack like a bone, then the hiss of leaves tearing through leaves. A dead branch as thick as her arm came turning down through the rain, end over end, and she saw it a heartbeat too late to roll.

It caught her across the face. The heavy end struck her muzzle where the wolf's teeth had raked it and skidded down along her jaw, and her whole face screwed up tight around the blow. *Hnh.* The breath went out through her nose. Her lips peeled back off her teeth and stayed there.

The branch rolled off her into the mud and lay there, dripping.

Nobody had thrown it. The clearing had simply decided to.

Aura turned her head, carefully, and looked across the mud for the wolf. Her ears were already turned that way.
