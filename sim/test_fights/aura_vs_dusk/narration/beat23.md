— Dusk —

The light had gone darker since the start. The clouds had come down closer over the clearing, and everything had taken on the dim, greenish colour of the underside of a leaf.

Her feet caught up with her. She planted them, square again, and felt the guard come back into her shoulders.

The jackal had put the oak on the side of her bad knee: something solid to lean on, there if she needed it. She was blinking too often, and shaking her head now and then as if to clear water from it. The branch had found that muzzle; then Dusk's own skull had.

*I was right about the knee,* Dusk thought, with a small, cold satisfaction. *I was right about the fire.*

The rain drummed on the leaves. Dusk gathered on her haunches, still as a drawn bow. Every lunge pulled at her throat, so she would make this one short, and make it count.

She let go.

She did not have the breath to aim properly. She did not need to. She turned her head sideways to get her jaws round whatever she found, and what she found first was an ear: the jackal's right ear, up and turned toward her.

Her teeth snapped at it.

— Aura —

She read it in the wolf's shoulders before it started, and she was going backward as the haunches let go.

It was not her legs that did it. Her legs had nothing left to do it with. It was her whole body throwing itself back off the good foot, a short, ugly spring that got her clear by the width of a hair. The jaws clacked shut where her ear had been, and the snap of them was loud enough to make her flinch after the fact. It clipped a few hairs and nothing else.

She landed on the good leg and the bad knee came down after it and she nearly screamed.

She did not. She stood swaying with her teeth locked and the right leg trembling under her, and let the scream go nowhere.

The wolf had overreached and come down heavily, and was getting her feet back under her, two lengths away, her head low and her breath sawing.

*If she'd had the breath to aim that—* Aura thought, and did not finish it.
