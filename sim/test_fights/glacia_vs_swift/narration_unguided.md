# Glacia vs Swift — the mountain shelf (narrated from the events only, without the engine's narrator notes)

## Beat 1 — First blood, of a sort

Up here the wind never quite stopped. It came over the lip of the shelf in long cold pulls and went back down into
the cloud, and between gusts the silence was so complete that Swift could hear her own heart.

The Articuno hadn't moved since she landed. She stood near the cliff with her wings folded and her long tail laid
out behind her on the rock like spilled water, and she was looking at the sky. Not at Swift. At the sky, as if the
swallow were weather she had already seen.

That decided it.

Swift went off the scree like a flicked stone, low and flat across the shelf, and hit her in the breast before the
red eyes had finished turning. It was a good hit, clean, the kind that usually ended things against bigger birds who
thought they were too big to bother dodging. The Articuno rocked back half a step. A wing jerked open, then closed
again, slowly, feather by feather, the way you might fold a letter you didn't intend to answer.

Swift was already gone, banking away across the rock, her forked tail snapping open and shut.

*Got you,* she thought. *First.*

Behind her, Glacia breathed out, and the frost under her talons crept a little further across the stone.

*Small,* Glacia thought, watching the dark shape flick across the rock. *Fast, and in love with being fast. She will keep doing that until something stops her.* The bruise on her breast was nothing, a coin of warmth in all that cold. She noted it the way she noted the wind: present, and not worth arguing with.

Over by the scree, Swift landed, hopped twice, and fluffed herself up, delighted. *Didn't even see me. Didn't even see me coming. This is going to be easy.* Some small, sensible part of her, the part that had kept her alive through a lot of bad ideas, said: *She didn't flinch, though. Did you notice that? She didn't flinch.*

## Beat 2 — Cold, meeting wind

Glacia rose the way snow falls: without any appearance of effort. Two long strokes and the shelf was below her.
Grit spun off the rock and went over the edge, and then she was turning in a slow, wide circle over Swift's head.

The beam came out of her beak white and thin as a wire.

Swift flung an air-blade up to meet it and the two of them collided with a sound like a frozen pond giving way.
Light everywhere, and needles of ice. For a heartbeat Swift thought she'd stopped it.

She hadn't. What was left of the beam came through the broken wind and laid itself along her left wing, and the cold
went in. Not on her: in. Down between the feathers, under the skin, into the long muscle she flew with. The wing
seized open on its own and burned and then, horribly, stopped burning, going dull and far away at the tip.

She shook it, hard, and a glitter of frost came off it.

*Okay,* she told herself. *Okay. That's what she does. So don't stand still under her.*

Overhead the Articuno finished her circle and didn't bother to look pleased about it.

Swift flexed the wing. It answered, slow and stiff, like a door in winter. She could still fly on it. She told herself that twice. *She's slow to start and she doesn't like to move. Make her move. Make her chase.*

Up in the thin air, Glacia watched a few loose blue feathers drift down past her own wingtip and fall away toward the cloud. *Ice gets in,* she thought. *That is all I need to know about her.*

## Beat 3 — Above her

So Swift went up.

Not in circles. She went up the way she did everything, straight and stupid and fast, a corkscrew that put her above
the big bird in the time it took Glacia to notice she'd left the ground. Then she folded and dropped.

She'd meant the shoulders. Glacia slid sideways in the air at the last instant and the claws caught her lower, across
the small of the back, and dragged. Pale feathers came away and spun off on the wind. The Articuno lost a body's
length of height all at once, as if someone had cut a string, and caught herself, and went on, a little slower on
that side than before.

They passed each other and turned, two shapes in all that empty sky.

Glacia felt the hurt line across her back pull every time she beat her wings. She filed it away the way she filed
everything: *There. She takes what she can reach and she leaves. She will come back for more.*

Swift, swinging wide on the far side of the shelf, was laughing out loud. *Feathers. Off a legend.* She wanted to shout it down the mountain. Her left wingtip was still stiff from the beam and she didn't care at all.

## Beat 4 — Snow

Glacia opened her wings and the sky filled up.

It wasn't a beam this time. It was the whole air, white and roaring and full of edges, and Swift was in the middle of
it with nowhere to go. It got into her eyes, which watered and froze and watered. It got into her throat, which
closed. It scoured her wingtips and her tail and the long plumes off her head, and it didn't stop. There was no one
place it hit. It was simply everywhere, and everywhere was cold.

When it thinned she came out of it lopsided, beating hard on her right side to make up for a left wingtip that had
gone stiff and raw. Her feathers stood out from her body. Her beak hung open.

*Can't see. Can't see. There. There she is.*

Glacia hung above the thinning snow and watched her come out of it, and felt nothing about it in particular except a
kind of confirmation. *Everything I have reaches her,* she thought. *That is the whole of it.*

Snow settled on the shelf below in a thin white skin, and the stunted pines in the cracks wore it like sugar. Swift's breath smoked. Her eyes kept watering and freezing at the corners, and she kept blinking the ice away. *Don't let her do that again. Whatever happens, don't be in front of her when she does that again.*

## Beat 5 — A miss, and a tail

Swift came out of the white with her wing gone grey and hard as a knife, and drove it at the big bird's left wing.

Steel was the answer to ice. Every bird knew that. If it had landed, the fight might have changed right there.

It didn't land. Glacia tilted, hardly at all, and the steel edge went through the place her wing had been. And then
her tail came round, that long blue streamer of a tail, and hit Swift across the belly like a thrown rope.

It folded the swallow in half in mid-air. She tumbled a turn before she found her wings again, and came up chirping
with laughter that had no breath behind it.

*Missed. Ow. Missed. She's not slow at all, is she.*

Something at the root of her tail had started to ache, a dull steady knock she hadn't noticed arrive.

Glacia watched her come out of the tumble, laughing, and felt something unfamiliar move under her calm: not anger, not quite. Interest. *She laughs when she is hurt,* she thought. *I have not fought one like that before.*

## Beat 6 — The tail again

Glacia didn't chase. She didn't need to. She flicked one wingtip and a sliver of cold air went after the swallow like
a thrown blade, and took her across the chest and the base of the tail together.

The chest stung. The tail screamed. Swift's two long tail feathers slammed shut like closing shears and stayed shut,
and suddenly every turn she made was a fraction wider, a fraction later, because the thing she steered with didn't
want to move.

She started flying with the hurt side turned away, as if the wind might not notice it.

*Fine,* she thought. *Fine. Fine.* It wasn't.

She tried a flick of her tail, the little steering flick she'd done ten thousand times without thinking, and it sent a hot bar of pain all the way up her spine. She stopped doing that. She'd have to turn with her wings alone now, like a fledgling. *Okay. Okay. Wings, then. I've got wings.*

## Beat 7 — Steel, and the answer to it

This time she didn't miss.

She came round faster than she had any right to, the speed of the last pass still in her, and the steel edge of her
wing went across the Articuno's left wing in a long grey rake.

For the first time, Glacia made a sound. It was high and clear and cracked down the middle, like a bell with a flaw
in it, and it went out over the cloud and didn't come back. The wing jerked away from the cut. A shower of loose frost
came off it and drifted down toward the shelf. Her whole body hitched in the air, lost height, found it.

And in the same instant she answered. Her tail lashed back as Swift went past and caught the swallow's tail, the same
tail, the same root, hard. Swift's shriek was higher than the bell, and broken.

They swung apart. Both of them were flying wrong now.

Glacia held the hurt wing out and flew on it anyway. She did it carefully, the way you walk on a bad ankle when you
have somewhere to be. Each stroke cost her something she didn't show. *That one will not take another,* she thought,
quite calmly, and kept flying.

Across the gap Swift was flying with her tail clamped shut and her eyes very bright. Her chest heaved. Her whole body hummed with the hit she'd landed. *I hurt her. I actually hurt her. Did you hear that? Did you hear the sound she made?* Under the joy, quieter: *And she hurt me back in the same breath. She didn't even have to think about it.*

## Beat 8 — Breath like smoke

Glacia breathed out, and what came out of her smoked.

It wasn't snow or a beam. It was a dry, terrible cold, the kind that cracks stone, and she laid it over the swallow's
right wing and down across her breast as though she were stroking it.

Swift's chest locked. For a second she couldn't make it open at all, and her legs jerked up under her as if she'd
landed on something hot. Then the breath came in short and tight and careful, because that was all there was room
for.

Frost crackled across her feathers.

And still: below her, the Articuno was flying lower on the left. Every stroke of that wing shed a little more glitter.
Swift saw it, and something bright and stubborn caught light in her chest under all the cold.

*There. I did that. I can do that again.*

Glacia felt her see it. She felt the swallow's attention settle on the wing like a hand. *She knows now,* she thought. *Good. Then she will come at it, and I will know where she is coming.*

## Beat 9 — The reckless thing

Swift folded her wings, wrapped herself in blue fire, and threw her entire body at Glacia's chest.

It was the most reckless thing she knew how to do and she did it with three passes' worth of speed behind her. The
impact was a deep flat crack that went off across the mountains like a gunshot. Glacia's breath went out of her in a
single choked note. Her chest, which nothing had really touched yet, was suddenly the loudest thing in her body.

The fire came back into Swift, the way it always did. Brave Bird hurt the bird who used it. Her throat filled with it,
raw and hot. Her right shoulder rang. And as she tumbled away, the Articuno, with no breath and a ruined chest, still
found the time to rake her across the right wingtip on the way past.

Then Glacia came down.

She didn't fall. She'd been on the wing a long time with a wound in it, and she simply stopped being able to justify
the effort. She settled onto the shelf in a soft gust of frost and stood there, the left wing hanging half-open
because it wouldn't quite fold, her chest going up and down in short careful breaths.

*She will break herself to break me,* Glacia thought. *I should remember that.*

The rock under her talons was warmer than the air. It felt like a kindness. She let herself have it for one breath, and then she looked up.

Swift was still in the sky, flying in a loose, uneven loop, coughing. Her throat felt like she had swallowed a coal. *Worth it,* she told herself. *Worth it, worth it. She's on the ground. I put a legend on the ground.*

## Beat 10 — Wind against wind

Glacia called the wind.

She didn't have the breath for it. Her chest wouldn't fill, and what came out of her was half a storm, badly aimed.
She overbalanced on it and had to catch herself with her talons.

Swift met it head-on with an air-blade, the same trick as before, and this time her wind was the stronger. The broken
Hurricane folded back on itself and slapped into Glacia's face. It caught her crest, her eye and the top of her
skull, and snapped her head round, and that was all. It wasn't much. But she'd felt the shape of it: her own weapon,
turned and sent home.

Swift came down onto the scree too. Her wings were done with holding her up for a while. She landed hard, panting,
beak wide, plumes plastered flat with rime.

*Turned it back,* she thought, and laughed, and coughed.

They were both down now, on the same shelf, a stone's throw apart. For the first time since it started they were looking at each other level, eye to eye, and neither of them liked what the other one's eyes said. Glacia's were very red and very calm. Swift's were black and wet and absolutely refusing to be afraid.

## Beat 11 — Down, up, round

Swift was up again before the coughing had finished.

The Articuno was still leaning wrong from the turned-back wind when the swallow came down on her from above. Swift
meant the head. Glacia twisted, and the claws went across her beak instead, a stinging rake that jolted her crest.
Then Swift's own speed carried her round in a tight loop and she came back across for a second, lighter cut, a flick
across the right wingtip, and was gone.

Glacia got her feet set. Her beak stung. Her crest throbbed. Her chest still caught at the top of every breath.

She watched the swallow climb and loop above her and, for the first time in the fight, she thought something that
was very nearly admiration. *She has found the rhythm of it. Down, and up, and round. Down, and up, and round.*

*And a rhythm,* she thought, *is a thing that can be waited for.*

## Beat 12 — Nothing

Glacia flicked a dart of ice at her. Swift flicked a blade of air back.

They met in the sky, cracked, and cancelled. Nothing got through to anyone. Powdered ice drifted down over the shelf
like flour off a baker's hands.

The two of them looked at each other through it, breathing hard. For a moment neither of them did anything at all.

*Even,* Swift thought. *That one was even.* She had wanted it to be more than even.

*She is learning,* Glacia thought. *So am I.*

## Beat 13 — The wing

Swift was tired, and it was a tired dive: early, too steep, too much weight at the front. It should have been
nothing.

But it was steel, and it came down on the wing she'd already cut twice, and it went into it like a spade into wet
ground.

Glacia had known it would come there. Knowing didn't help. The wing jerked away so hard it pulled her off her feet
sideways, several steps across the rock, and she only stayed up because she flung her good wing out against the
stone and dragged herself to a stop. The cry that came out of her was thin and short. She bit it off almost before it
started.

For a moment, there was nothing in her head but the wing. It wasn't a thought. It was a place, and she was in it.

Then she was back, standing, the ruined wing hanging off her like something borrowed, frost pouring off it in a slow
glittering stream.

Swift had overreached on the dive and almost come down with it, and caught herself, and climbed, wobbling.

*That wing will not carry me again today,* Glacia thought. *So I will not ask it to.*

Above her, Swift was shaking. Not from the cold. From the hit, from the sound it had made, from what she'd done. Part of her wanted to sing. Another part, smaller and older, looked at the big bird standing there with her wing hanging off her and thought: *That's going to hurt her for a long time. That was me.* She didn't know what to do with that, so she circled.

## Beat 14 — Breathing

Neither of them attacked.

Glacia stood with her bad wing held half open and breathed the thin air in slowly, one careful mouthful at a time,
each breath catching on the bruise in her chest. She let the stagger go out of her legs. She didn't take her eyes off
the swallow.

Above her, Swift circled, tight, close, quick, her tail clamped shut, her head turned always toward the white shape
below. She got her balance back. She thought about the next dive.

*She's waiting for me,* Swift thought. *Fine. I'll come to her.*

A gust went over the shelf and the stunted pines in the cracks bent and straightened and the wind went on into the
cloud.

Glacia let her eyes half close. Not to rest: to listen. The swallow's wingbeats were different now from the start, heavier on the right, a little hitch every third stroke where the tail should have helped and didn't. She could hear her coming. She always could have. She simply hadn't needed to, until now.

*Come, then,* she thought. *Come and finish your rhythm.*

## Beat 15 — The catch

Swift came down beak first, faster than she'd come down all day.

The beak hit the Articuno on the crown with a crack. Glacia's head went back. Every muscle in her clenched and let go.
She took one step backward, half-turned.

And then the thing Swift had been flying past for the whole fight finally reached up and took hold of her.

Glacia's talons closed on her as she struck. Not a rake: a grip. And the Articuno, still reeling from the peck,
simply pulled her out of the sky and brought her down onto the rock, hard, flat on her back with her wings splayed.

The air went out of Swift in a squeak. She lay there and looked up at the sky she'd been living in, and the great
white bird stood over her with one foot still on her chest.

Glacia let go and stepped back.

*There you are,* she thought.

Swift stared up at her. Her heart was going so fast it didn't feel like a heartbeat any more, just a hum. *She caught me. She caught me. Nobody catches me.* And then, absurdly, because some part of her was still the bird who had come up the mountain thinking legends were slow: *Okay. That was good. That was actually really good.*

## Beat 16 — Snow on the ground

Glacia spread her one good wing and the shelf went white.

There was nowhere for the swallow to go this time. The blizzard came down on her where she lay and got into all of
her. It closed her throat so that the cry that came out had no air in it. It hammered both wingtips and the right wing
that had taken the dry cold. And it found the root of her tail, the tail Glacia had already hit twice, and that was
the place it broke her open.

The scream was real. Her whole body clenched round her tail and rolled, half onto her side, back again. Her wings beat
uselessly against the stone. She shook and couldn't stop and her eyes streamed and froze.

When the snow passed she was curled in it, rimed white, trembling.

And then she got up. It took three tries. The right wing folded under her the first time and the second, and she went
down on it hard and cried out. The third time she got her feet under her and stood, swaying, sides heaving, her right
wing held out from her body like a thing she didn't trust.

Glacia watched her stand.

*She will not stop,* she thought. *Then neither will I.*

Swift stood with the snow on her and thought, as clearly as she had thought anything all day: *I can't win this.* And then, right behind it, just as clear: *So what.*

## Beat 17 — Everything she had

Swift dragged herself into the air.

There was no corkscrew this time, no snap. Every wingbeat was a separate decision. She climbed slowly, and Glacia
watched her go, and knew what was coming, and could do nothing about it but brace.

Brave Bird again. Blue fire, folded wings, at the ruined left wing.

Glacia flinched before it landed. She couldn't help it. The swallow saw the flinch and understood exactly what it
meant, and it didn't make any difference to either of them.

It hit. The bell-cry came out of Glacia cracked, and then something strange happened: the wing went numb. All at once,
the feeling just went out of it, like a candle pinched. She looked down at it, hanging there, and couldn't feel it at
all, and that was worse.

The fire came back into Swift, into her throat, into her beak. Her throat had been the worst of her already and now
it was everything. And as she went past, Glacia, staggering, got one talon into her throat on the way by.

Swift should have fallen. She was spent. She was past spent.

She didn't fall. Something came up from somewhere very far down, hot and bright and absurd, and her wingbeats
steadied.

*Not done,* she thought. *Not done not done not done.*

Below her, Glacia felt the change in the air, the swallow's wingbeats suddenly even and strong, and looked up at her with something that was almost dismay. *Still,* she thought. *Still. Where does she keep finding it?*

## Beat 18 — Out of the sky

Glacia looked up at her and sent a beam straight into her chest.

Swift was guarding her throat. Of course she was. So the chest was open, and the chest was already hurt, and the cold
went into it and took her over. She curled round it in mid-air, wings coming in to clutch it, and the moment her
wings came in she wasn't flying any more.

She fell. Upper back, shoulder, thigh: the rock took each of them, and she cried out at each, and then she lay still.

That was when the feeling came back into Glacia's wing.

It came back all at once, everything the numbness had been holding, and it was worse for the gap. She cried out. Not
loud, but she did, and she shook, and for a long moment the two of them simply lay and stood there on the shelf a few
lengths apart, both hurting, neither able to do one thing about it.

Swift, flat on the rock, turned her head and looked at the Articuno shaking over her wing, and for one strange moment didn't feel like an enemy at all. *That's what I did,* she thought. *That's what it feels like. She feels like this too.* Then the moment passed, and she started trying to work out how to stand up.

## Beat 19 — From the ground

Swift attacked lying down.

She couldn't get up yet, so she didn't try. When the Articuno came near she lashed one claw up at her, and the surge
was still in it. Glacia got her good wing in the way and the claws raked feathers instead of skin.

Only then did Swift try to rise. The first time the world tilted and she sank back. The second time the right wing
gave and she dropped on it, and it went from bad to worse, and she cried out. The third time she got as far as her
knees, rested one breath, and went the rest of the way.

She stood swaying, her head tucked down against her chest as if her beak could shield it.

Glacia stood opposite her with the left wing drawn in close, frost still pouring off it.

They looked at each other. Neither of them looked away.

*Why are you still standing,* Glacia wanted to ask her, and didn't, because she thought she knew the answer, and because she had no breath to spare for it.

## Beat 20 — The good wing

Glacia breathed the dry cold over the swallow's right wing and the root of her tail.

The right wing had been the wing she still trusted. It wasn't any more. The cold went into it and it went past
anything Swift could keep inside; the shriek that came out of her cracked down the middle and her legs buckled and
caught. Then the cold reached her tail and added itself to all of that, and the shriek went higher.

She tottered. She couldn't put her weight anywhere.

And the surge left her. It went out of her the way a wave goes back down the sand, all at once, and every single
thing that hurt came back at full size.

She stood on the shelf with nothing holding her up at all.

*Still here,* she thought, very quietly. *Still here.*

## Beat 21 — One more

She went at the Articuno's head one more time.

It wasn't really a Quick Attack. It was a lurch, a stagger forward across the rock with her wings half out, so
overcommitted that she nearly went down with it. But Glacia was guarding her left wing, curled round it without
thinking, and the right side of her head was open.

Swift's whole body hit it. It was a dull, ugly thump. Glacia's cry came out harsh and broken. Her eye watered and
blinked, and her neck went stiff, and for the first time the ache in her head was louder than the ache in her wing.

Swift tumbled off her and caught herself on the rock.

Glacia shook her head once, slowly, and felt the ache settle in behind her eyes like a stone in water.

*That was nearly nothing,* she told herself, *a stumble, a lurch.* It had still found her. Everything the swallow had thrown all day had found something. She was beginning to feel like a house in a storm that had lost a shutter, then a window, then a door.

## Beat 22 — The last of the swallow's breath

Glacia flicked a dart of ice at the swallow's chest.

Swift saw it coming at the place that was already ruined and flinched before it landed. She was still off balance
from the lurch. She couldn't get anything in the way.

It went in. Her chest locked, and then it wouldn't unlock. She curled round it, wings clamped over it, and opened her
beak, and nothing went in. Her chest heaved and heaved and no air came. The sound she made wasn't a scream. It was
smaller than that and much worse, a thin high keening with no breath under it at all.

Glacia watched her try to breathe and knew there was nothing behind the swallow's blows now, not for a while.

She didn't press it. She could have. She stood and watched the small body heave and thought, distantly, the first unkind thing she had thought all day, and it was about herself: *You are winning this by being bigger. That is all. Do not pretend it is anything else.*

## Beat 23 — Last stand

Swift beat one wing at the Articuno's side with everything she had left.

That was the whole of it. Every scrap of her, the last breath and the last of whatever was under the last breath,
went into one wingbeat. It was harder than anything she'd thrown in several beats.

Glacia caught it on her good wing, braced, and was driven back a step and had to plant herself not to go over. She
didn't go over.

Breath came back into Swift, ragged but real, and behind it there was nothing. Nothing at all. She stood swaying,
emptied out, and stared at the big white bird.

## Beat 24 — Nothing left on either side

Glacia had nothing left either.

She stood and dragged in air. The ruined wing hung, still shedding frost. Her chest caught at the top of every breath
and her head throbbed. She didn't attack. She couldn't, quite.

Two birds on a ledge in the sky, a few lengths apart, breathing at each other.

Swift's thoughts had gone very small. *Breathe. Okay. Breathe. Wing. Where's my wing. There. Okay.* She couldn't remember what she'd been planning. She couldn't remember the last dive. She remembered the sky at the very start, how big it had been, and how easy.

The wind came up off the drop and went over them both, and neither of them moved.

## Beat 25 — She falls on her own

Swift hopped in close one last time and jabbed at the crown.

Glacia turned her head and the beak landed on her shoulder instead, and the jolt of it went into the ruined wing, and
that was enough: she hugged it to herself and hissed and rocked with it, twisting away.

When she looked back down, the swallow had fallen over.

Nobody had touched her. Swift's legs had simply stopped holding her up, and she'd gone down on her face on the rock,
wings half spread, beak against the stone, her forked tail lying open and still for the first time all day.

She was breathing. She didn't try to rise.

Glacia stood over her.

*Stay down,* she thought. *Please.* It surprised her, how much she meant it.

## Beat 26 — The end

Glacia sent one last blade of air at her.

Swift saw it coming from where she lay and flinched round her chest, and it caught her there anyway, across the chest
and the back of the neck.

She went out slowly, from the inside. First the sound of the wind went away. Then the light greyed and narrowed to a
point. Her legs didn't answer when she told them to fold under her, and the rock came up against her breast and stayed
there. Then nothing. Every muscle let go at once, and she lay still, breathing, her feathers lifting in the wind.

The shelf went quiet. Snow-dust from the last blizzard was still drifting down out of the clear sky.

Glacia stood over the small blue body for a long time. Her left wing burned with every beat of her heart. Her chest
caught. Her head ached. She was cold, even for her, which was something she had never been before.

She looked at the red face, the open beak, the long plumes lying flat across the stone.

She found she was trying to remember the very first hit: the swallow coming off the scree like a thrown stone,
the jolt in her breast, how small and how fast. It felt like something that had happened to somebody else, a long time
ago.

*I thought you were weather,* she told the unconscious bird. *You were not weather.*

The wind came up off the drop, colder now that the afternoon was going. Glacia stepped round so that her body was
between it and the swallow, and lowered her one good wing over her, and stood there on the shelf, very still, the way
she had stood at the start, waiting. Only this time she was waiting for someone to wake up.

---

*Winner: Glacia, by knockout.*
