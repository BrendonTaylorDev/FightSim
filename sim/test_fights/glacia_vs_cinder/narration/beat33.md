— Cinder —

A single snowflake had caught on the bird's beak and was not melting.

Cinder saw it very clearly, the way things go clear before a blow: the flake, the open beak under it, the breath coming out in tiny, careful sips so as not to move that chest. The chest itself, dark and swollen under the clotted feathers, the bite marks and the claw marks crossing it. The right wing trembling against her side in a fine, fast shake that never stopped. In the shadow at the foot of the cliff behind her the frost lay thick and blue.

The bird saw her looking. Her body curled in round the chest, the left wing coming forward over it.

*Protecting it. Good. Now I know.*

Cinder stepped in. She kept the left wing tucked tight and fought one-sided, right shoulder first, the last of that white heat still running hot in her arms. She made a fist of her right hand, and the air round it began to crackle. Blue-white sparks crawled over her knuckles and between her claws and the smell of storm came up sharp in her nose. She knew what this did to the bird. She had seen it before.

*Bet she doesn't even get a sound out.*

She picked the spot she wanted, right in the middle of that bruised, bitten chest, through the wing held over it, and put the Thunder Punch there with all of her behind it.

— Glacia —

Her breath stopped. Her eyes closed. She simply waited for it.

It went through her guard as if her guard were not there. The fist struck the left wing she had held over her chest and drove it in against the chest itself, both at once, with a sharp hiss of sparks, and the lightning came after it.

It did not stay where it hit. It got in. It went into the swollen ruin of the chest and spread through her like cracks spreading through ice, out along every feather shaft, down her legs, into the roots of her wings. Her whole body jerked rigid, legs locked straight, wings flung out stiff, every muscle seized at once, and for a heartbeat she could see the bolt lighting her from inside. A scorched smell rose off her feathers. Then it let go of her and her feet were still on the stone.

The scream came after. It was raw, *aaah*, and her voice broke halfway through it and the rest was only air.

She knew this. She had known it from the first time those knuckles crackled: her body had no answer to this, none at all. It went into her as if she were made for it.

The chest was beyond anything now. The new pain sank into the place where the claws had raked her and the stone had struck her when she was thrown, and found all of it, and the old hurts and the new one became one huge, sick weight. She gasped, shallow and quick, and could not finish a single breath. A wheeze rode on every one. Her legs felt hollow, as if the throb in her chest were pulling the strength out of them from the inside.

The left wing hung off her shoulder with a hot wire drawn through it from root to tip, and the wire stayed in. She tried to fold it and it would not fold. Her long tail trembled along its whole length and she could not make it lie still.

— Cinder —

It had worked. She saw it work. A small, fierce joy flared in her and went out.

Because the heat was going. She felt it drain out of her arms and her chest and her legs like water out of cracked stone, and every hurt she had came back at once, full size: the pounding head, the burning neck, the left wing blazing against her back. Her knees dipped. Her tail flame sank low and spat. She swayed on her feet, barely able to hold herself up, and found she was gasping as hard as the bird.

— Glacia —

Under Glacia's talons the stone was cold, and it was still there, and she was still standing on it.
