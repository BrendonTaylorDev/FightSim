— Cinder —

The bird had not moved. She stood on the bare rock below with her talons spread and her head low and her wings folded close, the right one tucked in hard against her side, and every breath she took went out of her beak in a thin white plume that the wind tore away before it had finished.

She looked like something that would stand there until it was knocked over. That was what she promised. Nothing more.

Cinder could not get her own breath either. It came in short and hot through her teeth and went out of her nostrils as smoke, two grey threads that the gusts pulled sideways. Her chest burned with it. She leaned on the air and let herself turn in a slow, wide circle over the shelf, the cliff face wheeling past on one side and the white floor of cloud on the other, and somewhere below her a loosened pebble skittered off the edge of the rock and went down into the white and did not land.

Her right wing did the work. It had to. The left one opened only because she made it, and every stroke of it was a fresh insult: the frozen membrane dragging stiff and red against the cold, the swollen bones at the shoulder grinding a little as they lifted, then a long burning pull along the whole span as it came down. On the third stroke the burn went bright and deep, deeper than before, and a bellow climbed her throat and broke into a growl, *hrrgh*, before she could swallow it. Her tail flame sank low and spat.

*Fine. Hurt, then. You're still not down there with her.*

Her head ached in time with her heart, a cold, hard throb under the frosted scales between her horns, and she held it still and level and turned her whole body instead, so she could keep both blue eyes on the bird. She drew the heat up from her belly, slow, a smouldering ember she fed and did not spend. Not yet.

— Glacia —

From the rock the dragon was a ragged orange shape against the sky, and the shape was lopsided. Glacia watched it with her head held low and still, because the neck would not allow anything else. The right wing beat clean and wide; the left came up short and slow, half a stroke behind, and each time it faltered the whole dragon slid a little sideways on the wind and had to haul herself back.

*She is losing that wing. Slowly. So am I.*

Her own breath would not go deep. The chest was a heavy, swollen weight under the matted feathers, a dull deadened pressure where something sharper should have been, and every pull of air pressed against it from the inside and stopped short. Her neck was the same, thick and tight and strangely far away, the bite mark throbbing under the raised feathers like a second, slower heartbeat. The right wing lay against her side and burned along its whole length whenever the wind tugged at it. Her long tail she kept stiff and close to the rock, the scuffed feathers along it stinging in the cold.

She shifted her weight from one talon to the other. Frost had begun to creep, very faintly, along the edges of her folded wings.

The dragon came round again, lower, the tail flame a low orange sputter, and smoke trailed back from her nostrils on the wind.

Glacia's beak opened by itself. The breath went out of her in a thin, cracked whistle, *hhhee*, high in the throat and dragged out, and the wind took it over the edge and the silence after it was enormous.
