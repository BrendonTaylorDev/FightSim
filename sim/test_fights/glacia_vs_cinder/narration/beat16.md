— Cinder —

The bird wheezed. It was not meant for anyone, that sound: a thin whistle in and a thin rasp out, every breath, and the wind could not quite tear it away.

Cinder shook the last of the frost from her face. Ice cracked off the scales over her eye and fell in small bright flakes, and the cold that was left there throbbed deep in her skull. She ignored it.

Glacia had turned side-on to her, hiding the chest, and that only showed her exactly where the chest was. The right wing was pressed tight against the bird's body, and even so it shook every time she shifted her weight. And the neck. The bird held it like something carried on a tray, long and stiff and careful, the feathers along it frizzed and standing up where the fist had found it.

*Easy. Don't telegraph it.*

The thin air burned cold in Cinder's lungs. She took one step and stopped short. Waited. Watched the red eyes, the shoulders under the pale feathers, the slight forward lean.

She saw the bird see where she was looking. Glacia's head drew in, the long neck pulling back a fraction, the whole body bracing round it a heartbeat too late.

*There. That's the one.*

She went in.

Two strides, low, her bad wing dragging half-open behind her and her weight thrown forward to cover it. Her arms went round the bird beneath the wings, under the frost-cold feathers, her hands locking together behind the narrow back. She was lighter than Cinder had expected. Much lighter. Cinder felt a small blink of surprise at her own strength as she heaved the bird clean off her talons, the long tail swinging, and turned with her, once, round, the shelf and the cliff and the cloud all wheeling past, her forearm clamped hard across the base of that long neck.

She felt the neck take the whole of the spin.

Then she let go, and threw.

— Glacia —

A solid *whump* went through her, like a sack dropped, before she even understood she had left the ground.

The spin had wrenched her neck round against the dragon's forearm, and the pain in it crossed a line.

It had been very bad. Now it was something else. It flared from the base of her skull to her shoulders, white and enormous, swallowing the old electric sting that still lived there, and her throat closed round a choked cough and her left shoulder jarred against the dragon's arm as she tore loose. The cry came out of her clear and high and broke apart, *ahh*, a thin, cracking wail that the wind took out over the drop and nothing answered.

Then the stone.

She hit the bare rock high on the back and felt it as a sharp breath, *hk*, and nothing more; her right shoulder came down after with a hiss, *hss*, and she pulled it in tight against her; her left thigh jerked out to the side on the rock and she huffed, *hff*.

She did not stop.

The throw carried her on. She went over, end over end across the loose scree at the foot of the cliff, wings flung wide, the stones rattling and sliding under her and the sky and the shelf trading places. Her left shoulder skidded, a grunt through her nostrils, *hnh*. Her right thigh, briefly. Her lower back, a startled breath that straightened her spine.

The cliff face stopped her.

Her head cracked against it on the right side, just over the eye, and a low sound came out of her, *mmh*, and she shook her head once to clear the stars from that eye and wished she had not. Her chest hit the rock last. It should have been the worst of all. It was a dull, sick jolt, felt more as a lurch in her stomach than as pain, and then a wheeze on the next breath, and the next.

She slid down the stone and stopped, sitting, her back against the cliff and her legs out in front of her in the scree.

— Glacia —

She did not move.

Her neck was the whole of her. It sat in her throat like a stone and choked her, and every heartbeat drove another wave of it up into her skull. She could not turn her head. She could barely lift it. When she tried, a thin sound got as far as her throat and turned back, and her eyes squeezed shut.

*Breathe. Just that.*

Air rasped going in and wheezed coming out. She made herself slow it. A short gasp, mastered, then another, and slowly her breathing settled into something hard and steady.

She looked down, moving only her eyes.

Her chest lay under her beak, the feathers raked and singed and scattered with grit from the scree. There was a thin dark line of blood among the white of them, just one. She stared at it as if it belonged to someone else. She could not feel it. She could not feel anything there at all, only the dead heaviness and the wheeze, and somehow that was worse.

The very first fire had gone in there. It had all started there.

Her right wing lay half under her against the cliff, and when she shifted her weight even slightly it sent a flash of pain through her so bright the shelf went white at the edges. She held still until it faded.

Across the stone the dragon stood breathing hard, the left wing hanging crooked from her back.

*She is hurt too. That wing. Her head.*

Glacia leaned her own head back against the cold rock, very carefully, and let the ache in her neck settle deeper, quiet now, and waiting.
