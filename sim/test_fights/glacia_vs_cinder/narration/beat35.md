— Cinder —

Her own breath was the loudest thing on the shelf: ragged, catching, scraping in and out past her fangs.

The bird was drifting away from her over the edge, a pale shape tilted on the wind, her chest turned half away as if that could hide it. It only showed exactly where it was. The right wing stuck out from her body stiff and shuddering, beating short and late.

*No. You don't get to just leave.*

Cinder spread her wings. The right one opened wide and clean. The left she had to force, and it came open with a long, tearing burn from shoulder to tip that pulled a hiss through her teeth, and she used it anyway. She crouched, beat down, and heaved herself up off the stone. Grit sprayed out from under her. The first stroke barely lifted her; the second dragged her up past the lip of the shelf; on the third the wind off the cloud caught under her and she was flying, lopsided, the bad wing a hook dragged through her back on every downstroke.

*Breathe out on the strike. Not before.*

She came up under the bird, beneath her and closing, the gap shrinking fast with both of them moving. Her shoulder dropped. Her right hand swept up from below, claws spread like a fan, at the scorched chest.

The bird twisted, a half turn on the wind, and the chest went away from her and the wing came round in its place.

— Glacia —

She heard the rip before she felt it: a dry, tearing sound of claws through feathers.

Then the left wing. The claws went across it and out through the long feathers at its tip in the same stroke, and the pain that came up out of the wing was too big to stay in it. A raw scream tore out of her, *aaah*, cracking in the middle, and her whole body curled round the wing in the air, the wing itself jerking in against her side, and she lurched down a body's length on the wind before her wings caught her. The shelf and the cloud swung under her. She did not care. There was nothing in her for a long moment but that wing.

The dragon went past beneath her and was gone, and Glacia, with no thought at all, opened the wing again because she had to, and it held her, and she screamed again, thinner.

She could not find one quiet place anywhere in it now. It throbbed from the shoulder, where the claws had first raked it on the shelf and the lightning had gone through it after, down to the tip, which felt stiff and strange, as if it had cooled too fast. And the right one still had to beat. Each stroke of it was a small agony she had to choose, and she chose it, again and again, because the other choice was the cloud.

Her eyes were streaming. Far out over the drop a hawk turned, unconcerned.

She could not look away from the pain long enough to look at anything else.

— Cinder —

Cinder turned on the wind below and behind her, her left wing hanging half-folded between strokes and then dragged open again with a grunt that broke into a hoarse growl. Every downstroke on it went in deeper. Her tail flame streamed low and orange behind her. She hung there, panting, smoke tearing from her nostrils.

— Glacia —

Glacia found the dragon again through the blur, below her now and a little behind, labouring on the wind. Three wingbeats away. Perhaps four.
