— Cinder —

Her head hurt worst. It sat on her neck like a cracked stone, the side the bird's wing had struck a size bigger than the other, and every heartbeat thudded in it.

Cinder lay across the bird and did not move. Her full weight was on that scorched chest, and she could feel it under her belly, the thin frantic flutter of breath going in and out beneath the swollen, burned feathers. Her right forearm lay across the long neck, just under the crushing bite her own jaws had left there, and the neck was cold against her scales and trembling. Her claws held the left wing flat to the rock, sunk among the ragged feathers, and the wing under them twitched now and then on its own, like something dreaming.

To keep it all she had to spread herself wider than her body wanted. Her hips ached. Her heavy legs were braced out behind her on the stone, claws dug into the grit. The torn left wing she had let fall half open against the shelf, and the wind tugged at the membrane and made the whole root of it burn.

She listened to the breathing under her and changed nothing.

There was a tremor in the bird that did not stop. It ran under Cinder's belly and her forearm and her claws all at once.

*Finish it... clean.*

She settled her weight and felt for the first sign of resistance. Her own breath was going: short, wheezing, smoke threading out of her nostrils and torn away by the gusts.

— Glacia —

The face above her would not hold still. It slid one way and came back, two blue eyes and then one, and Glacia hauled her gaze back to it each time it drifted.

The neck was the loudest thing in the world. The chest was under it, cramping and twisting beneath the weight, and the left wing was a long line of fire where the claws sat in it. Her breath came in sips.

*Air.*

She gathered what there was. It was not much. She waited until the dragon's breath went out, and then she wrenched. Her shoulders twisted on the stone, the right wing beat once uselessly against the rock, and she tried to roll her body over onto her front, out from under.

She got nowhere. She had barely begun to turn when the dragon felt it and bore down, all her weight driving into the ruined chest.

It was a moment before anything arrived. Then all of it did.

The scream came out of her raw and hoarse and cracked in the middle: *Hhaaah-hh.* Her body curled up round the chest as far as the weight let it, which was hardly at all. Her talons scraped and scraped at the stone. Her eyes streamed, and the sky above the dragon's horns went white and swam.

When it eased she was lying exactly where she had been, flat on her back, the forearm still on her neck, the claws still in her wing.

Each breath under the weight came shorter and quieter.

— Cinder —

Cinder watched her.

The bird's head turned on the stone, toward the cliff, then back, then lay still a little further over than before. The red eyes were open but not quite on anything. The frost that had always drifted off her was thinner now; only a few flakes lifted from her feathers and were blown away.

Cinder watched for the moment the bird forgot the hurt and moved like herself. It did not come. She had stopped guarding anything. There was nothing left in her to guard with.

*Hold still. Let me see you.*

Cinder let out a slow breath, and it shook. That heave had cost her as well. Her arms burned from the shoulders down, the old frost-ache in her belly woke up and spread, and the smoke came out of her in small, ragged puffs.

Somewhere along the cliff a stunted pine creaked in the wind, and then that settled too.
