— Cinder —

Grit still hung in the air over them from the last heave, a thin haze the wind kept lifting off the stone and scattering over the drop.

Cinder held. Her full weight lay on the bird's chest and she could feel every breath under it, small and hitching. Her forearm lay across the long neck, and under the scales of her wrist a pulse was going, fast, much faster than her own. Her claws were still sunk in the left wing. She could feel it trying, now and then, to draw in against her hand, and failing.

Her right arm had begun to shake. She shifted it, just to rest it, and felt the neck start to slide out from under, and put it back.

*Quiet. Read her. Wait.*

The longer she held, the heavier she made herself. It was the only thing she had left that cost nothing. Everything else cost. The torn left wing lay half spread against the rock, and each time she leaned it took a little of the work and burned all the way to its root, and she winced every time. Her belly ached where the beam had gone in. Her breath was coming short and loud now, a dry rasp in her throat.

She watched the bird's face. Each movement in it started with a visible gathering, a stillness that came before the twitch, as though the bird had to collect herself for every small thing.

Cinder kept her fear out of her grip. She did not let herself think about how little was holding the grip up.

— Glacia —

Glacia lay under the weight and sank, though she had not moved at all. The dragon had only to breathe and the hurt in her chest surged up under it. Her neck was a hot, throbbing mass beneath the forearm. Air went in and out of her with no sound but a thin whistle, air with nowhere else to go.

Then the weight above her rose, a hand's width, on the dragon's own ragged breath.

She did not plan it. Her body moved before she did. She twisted her hips on the stone, got one thin grey leg drawn up between them, and kicked out, hard and wild, talons first, up into the soft cream scales of the dragon's throat.

They went in. The dragon's head jerked up and back, and a groan came out of her from deep in the chest, *Nnngh*, and broke off into a choked, wheezing cough. Her breath stopped. Glacia felt the forearm on her neck slacken for one heartbeat, felt the cold air come down into her chest all at once.

Then it was gone. The dragon came down on her again with everything she had, swallowing hard, coughing, her whole weight dropped back onto the ruined chest. The forearm ground back across the swollen neck, and the claws closed tighter in the wing. Glacia's head rocked against the stone with her mouth open, and a raw cry cracked out of her and broke.

Then she was lying exactly as before.

*Eyes... open.*

She kept them open, on the dragon's face. The face was not looking at her now. Its jaws hung open, wheezing, and a slow trickle of smoke and spit ran from the corner of its mouth, and its throat worked and worked as if it could not get a swallow down.

Under her back the bare rock was cold, and grit pressed into the feathers between her shoulders, and she could feel every small edge of it.
