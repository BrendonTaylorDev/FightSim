— Cinder —

Cinder's weight went forward before the rest of her did. She felt it go, down through her chest and into the front edges of her wings, and she tipped into it and let it carry her.

The bird was circling a little below her and to the left, wide and slow, holding her right wing just a fraction stiffer than the left. Every time it came down there was a hitch at the top of the stroke. A tiny thing. Cinder had seen it twice now and she was certain of it.

*She's guarding that side. So come at her from the other one.*

She beat hard. Three great strokes, the membranes cracking taut, the thin cold air rasping in and out of her lungs, and the gap between them closed at a speed that made her eyes water. The wind screamed past her horns. Below them the cloud rolled, white and far away, and the shelf was a small grey ledge stuck to the side of the mountain.

The bird saw her coming. The red eyes found her and the pale wings tilted to turn away, but she was turning into the wind and it held her back, and there was not enough time.

Cinder drew her right arm back, three claws spread and glowing a hard, dark blue-violet along their edges, the strange cold-hot energy of it humming up through her wrist.

She committed fully, sure her body would follow wherever she sent it.

They crossed.

— Glacia —

The claws came across her chest with a sound like cloth tearing.

All the speed of both of them was in it. It raked through the soft feathers over her breastbone, right where the fire had already been, and the force of it jarred up through her right shoulder in the same instant and spun her half round in the air. Her beak flew open on a strangled squawk, *kraa*, that the wind tore off short.

Then the dragon was past, a blur of orange and a gust of heat, already banking.

Glacia lost the air for a moment. Her wings beat wrong, out of time, and she lurched sideways before she caught the air again and steadied, tail streaming out behind her to hold her level.

The chest was bad. Not the flash of it; the after. Where the flame had left a dull warmth, the claws had laid a line of something sharper right on top, and the two had found each other and run together into one deep, hot bruise across the front of her. A low groan came up out of her, *nngh*, before she could close her beak on it. Her right shoulder throbbed along with it, a lesser ache, jarred more than hurt, already settling.

She tried a full breath. Her ribs pushed against the bruise from inside and a sharp twinge caught her halfway, and she let the breath go and took a shorter one instead. Then another, shorter still. A faint whistle came on the end of each.

Her chest throbbed in time with her heart, and every beat reminded her where it was.

Her scorched right wing stung on as it had since the fire, a hot, steady throb at every downstroke that would not leave her alone.

*She chose that. She saw the wing and went for the other side.* The thought was quiet and cold and almost admiring. *Remember it.*

She flew slowly now, deliberately, each stroke even, her crest lying flat, the snow still drifting off her into the wind. She brought her head round and found the dragon wheeling above her against the sky, and she held herself there, wings spread on the wind, chest drawn in, braced.
