— Cinder —

Cinder's good eye narrowed on the face beneath her. Up close it was all she could see.

The grey beak sagged open, and the breath rasped in and out of it slow and dry, with no frost on it any more. The crest of three dark feathers lay crushed and bent against the stone. The red eyes slid shut, dragged open, found her, and slid away again. A tremor ran across the burned feathers of the chest under her belly, stopped, and came back.

She could feel the bird through her hands: the pulse in the neck under her forearm, uneven now, and the long wing under her claws, where every so often a muscle bunched and thought about pulling.

*She'll... try. The wing.*

The thin cold air went into her lungs and did not seem to fill them. Her arms quaked. Her legs, braced out behind her on the rock, had stopped feeling like legs and felt like two posts driven into the ground. She held on by stubbornness alone, her eye squeezed half shut, her throat scraping on every breath where the talons had found it. She kept her face drawn back from the open beak, out of its reach.

A slow dread had crept into her, under the anger. Not of the bird. Of herself: that her own arms would let go first.

She panted hard, but her weight never wavered.

— Glacia —

Her face had gone slack. She could feel it, the fight smoothed out of it, and could not put it back.

The pain in her chest was all that was left of her; everything round it had gone grey. Under the forearm, the neck was the last clear thing she knew.

*The wing. Pull.*

She pulled. She wrenched at her trapped left wing, all at once and with everything, her shoulders lifting off the stone with it, the long feathers scraping under the dragon's claws. It came perhaps a feather's width.

The dragon bore down with her full weight, straight onto the chest.

A hoarse, broken wail came out of her, *Hhaaah-hh*, cracked and ragged, and all of her clenched round the chest, her talons jerking and scraping at the rock, her eyes streaming. For a long moment there was nothing in her head but that.

Then, while her shoulders were still curled up off the stone, heat poured in under them. The dragon had turned her head and breathed, short and close, one quick tongue of fire along the rock into the gap beneath the bird's back. It scorched across the feathers between her shoulders, the place that had struck the stone when she fell, and she arched away from it and came down onto the hot rock.

A thin, cracking shriek. Then the fire was gone, and the hold was exactly as it had been.

She lay with the smell of her own singed feathers in the cold air. Her eyes were nearly closed, and for a while they did not fight to open.

It felt wrong that the stone under her should be so plain. Grit and cold and a crack under one shoulder: the same ground it had been all along, as if nothing had happened on it.

She was alone under the weight with all of it, the chest and the neck and the wing and now the hot sting across her back, and nobody else in the whole wide sky.

Her legs shook against the stone and would not stop.
