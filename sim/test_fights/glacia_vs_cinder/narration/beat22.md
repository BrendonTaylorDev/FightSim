— Cinder —

She tasted her own smoke, thick and bitter at the back of her throat, and under it the clean, cold smell of the bird's frost.

Glacia stood hunched below her against the cliff, the good left wing wrapped across the front of her body like a cloak, her whole self curled round that chest. The right wing hung at her far side, turned away from the fight. She was guarding the chest so hard that the rest of her had been forgotten: the long neck stuck up out of the huddle of feathers, rigid, bare.

*She's open. Go.*

A dive was a bet. If she missed, she would be low and slow and in reach, with a wing that might not lift her out again.

She folded anyway.

The wind went from under her and the shelf came up fast. Her left wing screamed as she pressed it flat against her side, and she let it scream. Her jaws opened, and the fire came up into them, not as a stream but as a burning ring round her fangs that lit her own muzzle orange.

She picked her spot, the base of that neck where the feathers stood up frizzed and swollen, and she snapped forward from the neck with the rest of her following after.

She had nothing left to time it with. She knew it as she went. Her breath was gone and her body was a fraction behind her jaws.

The bird's head moved. The spot moved with it.

— Glacia —

She looked up too late. There was only a shape, and heat, and then a crunching grind of jaws.

She had tucked her neck down into her shoulders without knowing she was doing it, and the burning teeth went past it by a hand's width and closed on her chest instead.

The blow strayed, and her chest was the nearest thing to where it strayed to.

The fire went into the raw, swollen place and kept going. A shriek came out of her, *eeeh*, high and thin and shattering. In the same instant the jaws jarred against her left shoulder, and a startled breath was punched out of her, *hah*, her spine flinching straight, and the heat licked up the side of her neck where it pressed against the dragon's head. The shriek caught and went higher, a raw scream, *aaah*, that the wind took out over the drop.

The jaws let go. The weight of the dive did not. It carried her with it, dragged her off her line, her talons scrambling over the grit to catch up with where her body had gone, three steps, four, towards the dragon, her left wing flung wide for balance. She nearly went down. She did not. Her talons found the rock and held, and she was upright, swaying, a stride out of place with her guard gone.

Then two things went quiet.

Her chest first. All at once, the way it had before, the pain simply stopped, and in its place came that dead, heavy strangeness, a cold that was not hers spreading out from the middle of her. Then her neck. The burning down its whole length cut out like a snuffed flame, and her head sat on top of it as if on a stranger's body.

She did not trust it at all.

She tried to breathe. Her chest heaved and nothing went in. Her beak opened on air that would not come. Her ribs jerked in empty pulls, and her eyes watered, and every move she made cost her air she did not have.

— Glacia —

Above her, Cinder was climbing.

It was ugly. The dragon had overreached on that dive and come out of it tumbling, her bad wing flapping half open and the good one beating wildly, and for a long moment she hung low and lopsided over the shelf, close enough to reach if Glacia had had anything left to reach with. Then she found her balance and clawed her way up, stroke by stroke, the left wing trembling at the top of each one. Her tail flame had gone white again. Her eyes never left Glacia.

Glacia glanced down at herself. Her chest was shaking; she could see the feathers over it quiver. She could not feel it shake.

The wind gusted and dropped.

She sucked at the air with her whole body, beak wide, and it barely helped.

*Not like this. Not to you.*

The dragon hung above her on her failing wing, breathing smoke, and her blue eyes were still on her.
