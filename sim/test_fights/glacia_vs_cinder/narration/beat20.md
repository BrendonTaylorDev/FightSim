— Cinder —

The head was the worst of her, a hot, swollen pounding above the eye that rolled forward in her skull like water in a bowl when she moved too fast. The left wing came second, and she was about to ask it for everything.

Across the stone the bird was still lurching, trying to get her talons square under her after that wild swing. A thin sound came out of her every time her chest was jarred, even by her own step. The dead right wing swung loose and knocked against her ribs, and she did not even flinch.

She did not stop to think.

She snapped both wings open and beat down hard, and the shelf fell away. Grit and loose snow blasted out from under her in a ring. The left wing screamed at her at the top of the first stroke, a tearing burn from the joint all the way out along the membrane, and she bit down on the roar and beat again, and again, climbing in a tight spiral with her head turned over her shoulder the whole way, watching. The wing lagged on every stroke. Each beat came out lopsided and she corrected it with her tail, the flame streaming.

Below her the bird lifted her head, slowly, and looked up.

Glacia had curled round her chest without thinking, the left wing drawn across the front of her like a shield, her shoulders hunched in over the ruined feathers. It left her whole back bare to the sky.

*Low, then high. She always looks low.*

Cinder hung at the top of the spiral, and dust and snow lifted round her as the wind gathered under her good wing. She swept it down and across. The air left it in pale, hissing blades, two of them, and they went down at the bird's back with the dry rip of something sharp through feathers.

Behind her still face, something cold and pleased settled.

— Glacia —

She saw the shadow cross the stone and tried to turn, and her talons were still not under her, and she was too late.

The first blade took her across the upper back, between the shoulders.

It was not the worst thing that had happened to her. She knew that even as it landed. But it came down on a back that was still bruised from the bare rock and the cliff and the heat, and it drew a sharp cry out of her, *ah*, high and short, and her whole spine arched away from it as if she could step out of her own back. Her wings jerked half open with the jolt. She dragged them closed again, shaking, the dead right one slow and clumsy to follow.

She could feel the exact shape of the bruise from the inside: a long line across the top of her back, sharp at its edges and dull in the middle, and every breath stretched it. Straightening pulled it. Hunching pulled it differently.

*She waited for that. She watched me fall and she waited.*

The second blade went into her tail.

The long blue streamer of it lay out along the stone behind her, and the wind-blade cut down across it near the base. A cry tore out of her, *ngh*, dragged and pained, and her knees dipped under her. The tail whipped round on the stone of its own accord as if it could strike back at something, and that hurt worse, and she clamped it still. The hurt in it found the place the first blades had marked at the very start of the fight, high over the shelf, and settled there.

She stood with the tail held stiff and close behind her, not moving it. A gasp caught in her throat every time it shifted on the rock.

Slowly, she got her talons square under her again. She set her weight. The lurching was gone. She lifted her left wing back across her chest and turned her head, rigidly, to find the dragon.

Cinder hung above the shelf, out of reach, the left wing trembling at the top of every stroke as if it might simply stop. Somewhere along the cliff, the stunted pine creaked in the wind.

The ache in her back and her tail settled deeper, and stayed.
