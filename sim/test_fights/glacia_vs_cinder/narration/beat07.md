— Glacia —

*She will come down. She cannot help herself.*

The thought arrived before her body was ready for anything. Glacia lay on her back on the stone, wings spread flat on either side of her, and the cold of the rock came up through her feathers and was the only kind thing in the world. Her chest jerked. Air went in, a thread of it, and caught on the bruise and stopped.

The dragon was circling above her. Low, heavy, the shadow of her sliding round and round over the shelf. Glacia followed it with her eyes and did not move her head.

The left wing. Every time the dragon brought it down there was a hitch, a wince that ran right through her body to her snout, a fraction of a beat where the wing did not want to go and had to be made to. The frost from the beam was still on it, a faint grey bloom on the membrane, and she flew a little crooked round it.

Far out over the cloud, a hawk turned on the wind and drifted away, uninterested.

*Wait. Let her come round once more.* Glacia's beak opened a crack. *No. Not wait. She will see I am breathing.*

She took the breath in two halves. The first got halfway and hit the bruise and stopped; she held it, and drew the second on top of it, slowly, until there was enough. It hurt very much. It was enough.

A prickle ran along her spine as the power built.

The dragon came round over her, the wings spread wide above her like a roof, both of them, the near one and the far one, blue-green and lit from behind by the sky.

Glacia breathed out straight up into them.

It was not a beam. It came out of her as a mist, pale and glittering, a breath of air so cold it had stopped being air, and it rose off her in a column into the wings above her, with the tiny, bright tinkle of ice forming in it as it went.

— Cinder —

It touched the right wing and stung, a sharp, prickling chill on the skin that made her duck her head a moment too late and growl, *rrh*, short and irritated.

It touched the left wing and it was far worse.

The mist did not sit on the membrane. It went into it. It found every bit of moisture in the thin skin and froze it where it was, instantly, from the inside, and the wing that had already taken the shard and the beam and had been aching and stiff ever since went suddenly rigid with cold all the way through. A cry tore out of her, *yahh*, loud and cut off short against her teeth.

The wing would not beat.

She lurched. She threw everything into the right wing and held herself up, lopsided, and hung there with the left wing stuck out stiff and white-crusted and burning. It was a burning cold, which made no sense, and it went from the tip of the wing to the root of it in her back, and when she forced the wing to bend, it bent with a faint crackle and the pain flared so sharp and high that a second cry came up her throat and she bit it down to a hoarse growl.

*I've been hit before. I'm still here.*

The wing wanted to curl in tight against her and stay there. She made it straighten. She made it beat. Every downstroke tore along the length of it, a hot, wrenching pull in the frozen skin, and she gritted her fangs and did it again, because the other choice was the ground.

— Glacia —

The breath came back.

It came all at once, the way it had gone: a great ragged pull of thin cold air down her throat and into her, and her ribs, which had been jerking against nothing, filled. It hurt. It hurt in a sheet across the whole front of her. She did not care. She drew another, rough and shaking, and felt her head clear.

She rolled. Her wings beat once against the bare rock, hard, a burst of frost-dust off the stone, and she was over on her front with her talons scraping for purchase, and then she was up on her bent legs, crouched low, her chest drawn in and her beak open. She rested there a single breath, eyes on the sky.

Then she pushed, and stood.

Her chest throbbed. Her right wing hung a little lower than the left. She folded them both, slowly, and lifted her head.

— Cinder —

Cinder watched her rise. She hovered over the shelf, her left wing stuck out at a stiff, strange angle that she could not straighten all the way, and she watched the bird stand up as if lying on her back on the stone had been a choice.

The bird's chest was a mess. Scorched, dark, swollen out of its shape, the feathers on it singed and ragged. Every breath she took moved it, and every time it moved, her beak opened a little and closed again. But the red eyes were steady. They went straight to Cinder's left wing and stayed there.

*She knows.*

Cinder looked at it herself. The membrane was crusted white, and the ache had spread out of it into the muscles of her back until even holding it open felt like lifting a stone.

*I've got plenty left,* she thought. *Does she?*

Her breath was coming fast and she hardly noticed it. The flame on her tail flared, and she leaned forward on the wind.
