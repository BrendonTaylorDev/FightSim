— Glacia —

The shelf hung on the side of the mountain like something the rock had forgotten to finish. Bare grey stone, wide and flat and cracked, ran from the foot of the cliff to an edge that simply stopped. Past the edge there was nothing but cloud, white and slow and very far down, rolling over itself in the wind.

Glacia stood near that edge and let the wind come at her.

It came hard, in gusts that leaned on her and then let go, and every time it let go the air round her went still and cold and a few flakes of snow drifted down out of a clear sky, as if the cold had nowhere better to be. Frost had crept out from under her talons in two pale stars on the stone. Her long tail lay behind her, the blue feathers lifting and settling along its whole length. Her wings were folded. They were very large even folded, pale and half clear, and the light came through them and glittered.

She breathed out. The breath steamed and was gone.

Across the shelf, beside the scree at the foot of the cliff, the dragon was watching her.

*Orange. Heavy. Warm.* Glacia noted each thing and set it aside. *She will want to come close.*

— Cinder —

Cinder liked the place at once. She liked the wind, and the drop, and the fact that there was no roof on any of it, only sky going up and up until it went dark.

She liked the bird less. The bird was too still.

She stood on her heavy feet with the loose scree shifting under her claws, and she let her wings open a little, just enough for the wind to find the blue-green underneath and push against it. The flame on the end of her tail burned steady and bright behind her, leaning with every gust and coming upright again. A thread of smoke curled up out of her nostrils and was snatched away sideways before it got anywhere.

The bird had not moved. Not a feather. Only that long blue tail, rippling, and the small snow falling round her out of nothing.

*Pretty,* Cinder thought. *Let's see if she can take a hit.*

A stunted pine clung to a crack in the cliff a little above her, bent almost flat by the wind, its needles hissing. She could smell resin and stone and the clean, empty cold coming off the bird from all the way across the shelf, a smell like the inside of a cloud.

She rolled her shoulders. Her arms were short and strong and her claws flexed once, three on each hand, and settled. She lowered her long neck a little and showed the bird her fangs, not quite a snarl yet, more a promise of one.

The bird looked back at her with deep-red eyes and gave her nothing at all.

— Glacia —

The wind dropped.

For a heartbeat the whole shelf went quiet: no hiss in the pine, no rattle in the scree, only the soft tick of the dragon's tail fire and the far-off sigh of the cloud below. The snowflakes round Glacia hung where they were.

She held still. Utterly still, the way ice is still.

On the other side of the stone the dragon's flame flared up white at the tip, and her wings began, slowly, to spread.
