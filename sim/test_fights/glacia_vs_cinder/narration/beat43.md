— Cinder —

Anger kept her there. It sat in her hot and steady, low in her chest under the frost-ache, and it was the one thing in her that was not tired.

Her throat burned where the talons had gone in. Every swallow caught on it. Every breath scraped past it with a thin, dry wheeze, and she could taste smoke and something like iron.

She did not let the hold settle. A steady weight was a thing the bird could learn; so she shifted it, a little onto the chest, a little back onto the forearm across the neck, never the same for two breaths together. Her claws stayed in the left wing. Her own left wing she kept folded hard against her side, out of it, and fought one-sided, the right arm doing the work of two.

Her shoulders had begun to burn, and she pressed on.

The bird's feathers had gone dull. They hung loose and ruffled all over her, grey with grit, and she did not shake them out. The frost that had always drifted off her had all but stopped.

*Patient. She'll... come to me.*

The wind gusted across the shelf and dropped, and in the gap Cinder could hear both of them breathing, and could not tell which was worse.

— Glacia —

The wind sounded further away than it should have. The light had dimmed, though nothing in the sky had moved.

She had stopped trying to move her chest at all. The weight on it had found the place where the lightning-fist had landed before, and settled into it, and stayed. Her whole body was trying to get away from her own neck.

*Leg. Now.*

She drew the leg up slowly, because slow was all she had. She got it as far as the dragon's hip and kicked. It was a late, feeble thing. Her talons skated off the hard orange scales and caught nothing, and the leg fell back to the stone.

The dragon had felt it coming long before it came. The forearm on her neck bore down.

A hoarse, broken wail came out of her, *Hhaaah-hh*, cracking in the middle. Her breath caught and she choked on it, coughed, and could not swallow. Her head jerked against the stone and her talons scrabbled at the rock, and for a long moment there was nothing in her at all but the neck and the weight on it. The left wing under the claws twisted on its own, so hard her back came off the stone, and slammed back down.

Then it was over, and she was lying where she had been.

Her muscles were still quivering with an effort that never reached the surface. She had gone so still. She did not want to be this still.

— Cinder —

Cinder watched the bird's head come up off the stone, an inch, and sink back. It had stayed up for less than a breath.

The beak was open. The head bobbed with every breath she took, small quick jerks, and the red eyes slid half shut and opened again.

Cinder leaned her weight a fraction to one side to see what it did to the face below her. The eyes squeezed shut. A thin sound came out of the open beak and stopped.

*Still in there.*

She did not feel like a winner. Her arms trembled with the weight. Her throat felt as if it had been scraped raw from the inside. Her tail flame lay on the rock behind her, small and low, flattened by the wind, and she could not make it rise.

She did not roar. She could not have. She stayed silent above the bird, and kept her weight where it was.
