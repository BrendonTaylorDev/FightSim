— Glacia —

She had been watching the wrong thing. The face, the jaws, the fire. Those came and went. The wing did not.

She corrected it now. She stopped looking at the dragon's head and fixed her eyes on her shoulders, the place the wings grew from, and watched the left one work.

Cinder hung above the shelf a little lower than before, beating on the good wing and dragging the bad one through each stroke. It no longer folded properly. She held it out from her body, stiff, as if she did not trust it to touch her, and at the top of every stroke it shook. The frost-burned patches on its membrane had gone dark and raw-looking, the scales round the joint standing up stiff and swollen. Far out over the cloud a hawk turned slow circles on the wind, unbothered by any of it.

The dragon's head kept tilting, as if she were listening for something only she could hear.

Glacia drew in a breath. The cold built in her chest until her breath turned to glittering mist, and she shaped it in front of her beak: a single shard of ice, long as a talon, clear as water, spinning slowly in the air.

Cinder's eyes dropped to it. The left wing jerked in towards her side, too late and too slow, and the whole dragon braced round it.

*Scared. She's scared of that wing.*

Glacia twisted from the hips to throw it, and the twist pulled at her chest, and a grunt came out of her. She threw it anyway, straight up into the torn left wing.

*Make her work for every bit of it.*

— Cinder —

There was a cold, sharp snap.

The shard went into the wing near the joint, where it was already worst, and in the same breath the jolt of it ran down through the root of the wing into her left arm and out to her hand, the three claws jerking open on nothing.

She could not keep it in. A grunt broke out of her and ended in a growl, *hrrgh*, and the wing snatched itself in against her body in a hard flinch she could not stop, and for one stroke she was flying on nothing but the right. She dropped. The cloud swung up. She made the left wing go back out, and it went, and it hurt so much her eyes watered.

The upper arm was barely anything, a hiss through the side of her mouth, *tsss*. The hand stung sharply and she shook it once, a startled huff, *hff*, through her clenched fangs.

White frost spread out across the membrane from where the shard had struck and crackled every time the wing moved under it.

She did not go down. She held. She beat, and held.

— Cinder —

The ache in the wing was hollow and shaking and too big for the place it was in. It felt as though the wing were full of it, stretched with it, the way a sail fills with wind, and every stroke squeezed some of it out into her shoulder and back.

She looked at it. The scales round the joint stood up stiff and swollen. The membrane was patched with red and white, frost over frost-burn, and it trembled with every heartbeat. She felt, oddly, embarrassed for it. It had always been the best of her.

Her head would not keep still and she could not bear to move it. The two pains leaned on each other, head and wing, until she could not tell where one stopped.

A noise started in her chest. She swallowed it before it reached her jaws.

*Stupid. You let her see the wing. You showed her where.*

Her chest lifted her shoulders with each pull of air. She tried to count her breaths to keep them even, and lost count at three, and started again.

— Glacia —

The right wing woke up.

There was no warning. It had hung at her side for two exchanges, dead weight, a thing she dragged, and then all at once the numbness drained out of it like meltwater out of snow and everything it had been holding back came in behind it: the bite, the heat, every stroke she had flown on it. A raw wail tore out of her, thin and cracking, before she knew it was coming. Her body seized round it, the shoulder jerking up and in, the long feathers rattling against her side, a sparkle of loose frost spilling down onto the stone. The shaking started again in her legs.

She did not fall. She made her talons hold. She stood, head low, breathing through it, until the wail had stopped and only the shaking was left.

— Glacia —

The wind came off the cloud in long, cold pulls, and neither of them moved.

Glacia stood on the bare rock with her back to the cliff, her talons spread on the grit, her right wing clamped against her side. It burned now, from the shoulder out to the last long feather, steadily, the way a fire burns once it has caught properly. She let it. There was nothing else to do with it.

She took stock, the way she might have counted the feathers on a wing.

Her chest was the worst of her, still. It sat at the centre of everything, swollen and raked and singed, and every breath went in past it in a thin, careful sip. Her neck she could not turn more than a little; she had learned to move her whole body instead, and it had become almost natural. The throat was raw. Her left eye ached in a line where the sparks had crossed it, and the right one was bruised from the cliff. Her back stung between the shoulders. Her tail, the long, beautiful tail that had streamed behind her through the whole of the sky, she held stiff and close to the rock, because when it moved it hurt.

The left wing was whole. That was what she had. One wing, a beak, and whatever cold was left in her.

*Not much. Enough, perhaps.*

Above her, Cinder hung in the air.

She could see it costing her. The dragon was not hovering, not really; she was falling slowly and climbing back, falling and climbing, the good wing doing three strokes for every one the bad wing managed. The white crust of frost on the left membrane caught the light. Smoke came from her nostrils in short, ragged puffs, and her tail flame, which at the start of the fight had burned tall and bright as a torch, was low and yellow now and guttering in the wind.

*She cannot stay up there for ever. She knows it too.*

The shelf had changed. Glacia saw it in pieces, because she could not turn her head to see it whole. There was a long scorched fan across the rock where the heat had rolled, the frost burned off it in a clean dark shape. The scree at the foot of the cliff had been churned where she had tumbled through it, the stones turned over so their pale undersides showed. There was a scatter of blue feathers caught in the crack of one of the stunted pines, fluttering. Hers. There was frost in the shadow of the cliff that she had not put there on purpose; it had simply come off her, all fight long, the way it always did.

None of the rest of the mountain cared. The cloud went on below the drop. The hawk went on circling. A thin skirl of snow went off the edge of the shelf and out into the white and was gone.

— Cinder —

Cinder watched the bird from above and made herself breathe slower.

Her head pounded. Every beat of the wing pounded with it. Her belly still smarted where the frost had bitten, a dull, cold ache low down that tightened when she twisted. The left hand stung if she closed it, so she left it open.

But she was up here, and the bird was down there.

Glacia stood with her back to the cliff in a cloud of her own breath, and she was a ruin. Cinder could see it from here. The chest was a dark, swollen mess under the singed feathers. The neck was held so rigid she might have been carved. The right wing had been dead a moment ago and now it was not, and she had heard what it cost her when it came back. The long tail lay stiff behind her like something she had to carry.

And the red eyes were still on Cinder, steady, unafraid, not missing anything.

*That's what I don't like about you. You don't stop looking.*

A plan came together in her, the way they always did, fast and reckless and half-finished. Fold the wings. Drop. Come down on her out of the sky the way she had come down on Cinder at the start, all the weight and speed of the fall behind it, jaws first. The neck. Finish the neck.

Then the doubts, crowding in behind it. The wing. If she folded it, would it open again? If she dropped, would she climb back up, or would she hit the rock beside the bird and lie there? Her energy was low; she could feel it in her jaws, the fire inside her banked and sullen and slow to come.

She did not care. She had come up onto this mountain to fight something worth fighting, and she had found it, and she was not going to hang here in the wind and wait for it to rest.

Below her the bird lifted her head a fraction, and Cinder knew she had been seen thinking it.

Cinder's tail flame, which had been guttering, steadied and climbed. She drew her good wing in a little, testing. Her jaws parted. A thread of fire licked out between her fangs and was torn away.

She did not fold yet. She hung there one more breath, gathering it.
