— Glacia —

She had already decided before she came round over the shelf: the dragon would not be allowed to get up warm.

Glacia hung above the stone on slow, heavy wingbeats. Each one cost more than the last. The hurricane had taken nearly everything she had, and her breath came in short, ragged pulls she had to take round the front of her chest, never through it, and her right wing dragged on every stroke. She held herself up anyway.

Below her the dragon lay on her back on the bare rock where she had fallen. She kept the left wing tucked tight against her side and did not move it; when she moved at all, it was with the other side, an arm, a leg, her head turning. Her eyes were blinking too often. Every few breaths she shook her head as if there were water in it. And the fire on the end of her tail, crushed low by the wind, was climbing back. Slowly. But it was climbing.

*She will light herself again, given time.* Glacia watched the flame. *Then do not give her time.*

She drew a breath and held it, sparing the chest, and spread her wings wide and flat over the dragon.

There was no beam left in her and no storm. There was only cold, and she had always had cold. She beat downward, once, twice, fanning, and the air that left her wings was bitter and thin and full of tiny glittering crystals, and it poured down off her over the shelf in a long, freezing sheet.

The breath went out of her in a gasp.

— Cinder —

It came down on all of her at once with a cold, sharp snap.

Not hard. Not like the hurricane. It did not throw her anywhere; she was already as low as she could go. It simply settled over her, a freezing weight of air, into her face and over her belly and along her arms and legs and tail, and every scale on her tightened at once against it. She flinched in on herself from the cold, every muscle going tight, curling round the hurt wing without meaning to.

That was the mistake. The wing moved.

The cold had found it the same instant it found everything else. The frost-burned membrane, already crusted, already screaming, stiffened again under the new frost, and when her body curled, the wing curled with it, and the pain went through it from the tip to the root and out into her back. A cry jumped out of her, *hrrgh*, and her fangs slammed shut on the end of it. Her face clenched round it for a heartbeat. Then she made it smooth again.

Her tail flame was worse to watch than to feel. The wind poured over it and it shrank, guttering, down to a small, sputtering blue-orange tongue at the very end of her tail, and the root of it ached hard and deep, the old cold from the beam and the battering of the hurricane waking up together. Behind her eyes, where the bird's wing had struck and the wind had hammered, a dull knock started up again and did not fade when she blinked.

And she was cold. Properly cold, frost on her scales, a shiver starting deep in her belly and spreading out into her arms. Her claws felt slow and far away.

*Up. Get up. She's out of breath; she can't keep doing this.*

She rolled onto her side, away from the hurt wing, and planted both hands on the bare rock and pushed. Her arms shook with the cold. Her shoulders came up off the stone, then her chest, and then the left wing dragged against the rock as she rose and the pain flashed white, and her arms simply stopped, and she went back down onto her side with a thump that jarred her teeth but nothing worse.

She lay there a breath, two, her sides heaving, smoke coming out of her nostrils in thin cold wisps.

Then she did it again. Hands on the stone, slower now, the left wing held rigid and clear of everything, her jaw locked. She got her right foot under her, and then her left, and pushed straight up with her heavy legs, and the shelf swung once round her and steadied, and she was standing.

Shaking. Hunched round the hurt wing, the right wing half spread for balance, frost glittering in the creases of her scales, her breath coming hard. But standing.

She tried to look up at Glacia, and her head would only come halfway. Then, slowly, the rest of the way.

The bird hung above her on the wind, her own breath steaming, her own chest heaving, and neither of them moved. The pause stretched, thin and cold, and the wind ran between them.
