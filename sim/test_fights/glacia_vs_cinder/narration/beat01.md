— Glacia —

The dragon's wings were half open, and the left one, the one nearer the drop, caught the light: the thin blue-green skin stretched taut between the long bones, wide as a sail and doing nothing to hide itself.

Glacia went up.

One stroke. Her wings opened all the way, enormous and pale, and came down once, and the shelf fell away under her in a burst of grit and frost-dust. The wind took her the rest of the way. She climbed into it, turning, and at the top of the climb she hung with her wings spread flat and let the dragon look up at her.

She breathed out, and the breath kept coming and froze as it went.

It gathered in front of her beak, a hard white point turning in the air, and then a clear, bell-like note rang out of her and the shard went. It whistled down across the shelf and struck the spread left wing with a cold, sharp snap, square in the stretched skin near the edge.

— Cinder —

*Hnk.* The snort came out of her before she could stop it. The wing jerked in against her back on its own, flinching from the spot, and a cloud of steaming breath burst out of her as the cold went in. Then it was only a small, stinging chill, numb at the centre and smarting at the rim, and she shook the wing out wide again. Already it was nothing.

*Fine. My turn.*

She did not go straight up after the bird. She went sideways, low and fast, wings hammering, scree spraying behind her heels, and skimmed out over the edge where the shelf dropped into cloud, and only then did she tilt her wings back and climb. The wind caught her under the membranes and threw her upward. The bird was above her, pale against the sky, wheeling.

Cinder opened her jaws and roared, and the roar turned to fire.

It poured out of her throat in a long, bright stream, and she swung it up across the bird and held it there.

— Glacia —

A sharp crack of air, and then heat.

It took her across the chest and the whole underside of the right wing at once, one wide wash of fire, and her body had no idea what to do with it. Cold was what she was made of. The heat did not stop at the feathers the way a blow would; it went into them, soaked through the thin, clear vanes, and the frost on the right wing turned to water and then to steam all in an instant, and the wing beneath it was suddenly bare.

For a moment there was nothing. Then the pain came, all at once, and a startled breath, *hah*, broke out of her beak.

The wing snapped in toward her side by itself. A sparkle of loose frost shook off it and fell away, glittering, toward the cloud. Across her chest the heat had left only a dull, pressing warmth, and she drew breath against it with a small hiss and went on beating. The wing was another matter. It stung, a hot, crawling sting under the singed feathers that flared every time she brought it down, and it did not fade.

*Fire.* She noted it, calmly, and felt for the first time how little of her there was to stand against it. *I cannot take much of that.*

— Cinder —

Cinder saw it work. She saw the frost go off the bird's wing in a puff of steam, the feathers along the edge darken and curl, the flinch she couldn't hide. Her tail flame jumped higher behind her.

*There. That's where you're soft.*

She circled, flapping, and watched which side the bird turned away from. The right. Always the right, now.

The wind gusted and dropped. Below them the shelf lay empty and grey, two smears of frost and scorch on it, and the bird hung across the sky from her, her breath steaming, her singed wing held just a little tighter than the other.

Glacia let out one long, slow breath, a sigh of white, and the cold closed round her again.
