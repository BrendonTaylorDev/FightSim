— Cinder —

She was more tired than she had ever been. There was no use pretending otherwise, and she did not try.

Her arms shook from the shoulders down. Her legs had gone numb-heavy where they were braced on the rock. The left wing lay half open on the stone beside her like something that belonged to someone else, and every gust that caught its membrane sent a slow, sick ache down into her back. Her head pounded. Her throat burned on every breath, and every breath was short.

Still she drove her weight down through her hips onto the bird's chest and kept it there. Her forearm stayed across the long neck. Her claws stayed in the wing.

Her grip kept loosening on its own. She felt the fingers ease, and tightened them on purpose, and a little while later felt them ease again, and tightened them again.

Under her, the bird had gone very quiet. The red eyes were half closed. The breath rasped in the open beak, slow and dry. It looked like giving up, and Cinder did not believe it for a moment.

*Not much air... in her. Not much in me.*

She shut her eyes for a moment and opened them again, the hold unchanged.

A pebble, loosened by her own claws, skittered off the edge of the shelf into the cloud. It never landed that she could hear.

She stayed where she was. Stubborn was all she had left, and it was enough to keep her there.

— Glacia —

The stone under her back was cold and gritty, and a crack ran under one shoulder. She could hardly feel any of it now. Mostly she felt the weight.

She gathered what was left, and it was very little. She drove her middle up off the rock, bridging, her talons pushing at the stone, trying to lift the dragon off her chest. It was slow and late. It lifted almost nothing.

The forearm came down on her neck.

For a heartbeat there was nothing. Then the pain arrived all at once, and found the old pain already waiting there under the swollen bite, and the two of them became one thing. A hoarse, broken wail went out of her, *Hhaaah-hh*. Her head jerked down to guard her throat and could not. Her body shook. Her talons scrabbled at the rock and slid, and her eyes streamed, and for a long moment there was nothing in her head but the neck.

She sank back onto the stone, and the dragon's weight sank with her, onto her chest.

That was where it was now. The chest. The weight sat on the burned, ruined place where the fire and the lightning had been, and it did not move, and it did not need to. The pain there pulsed. Each pulse seemed to come from a little further away, and each one was bigger than the last.

*It hurts.* She let herself know it, plainly, once. Then she set it aside.

She drifted, and came back for a breath, and drifted again. The dragon's face above her went soft at the edges. The wind sounded as if it were blowing somewhere else, over some other mountain. She tried to move a wing. Only a faint twitch answered.

Her eyes found the dragon's one more time, and held there, and in them, for a moment, something cold and bright that had not given up.

Then the pain climbed past anything she could brace against, and her body shook with it, and everything dropped away at once.

Her eyes closed. The talons on the stone went slack. The long blue tail settled behind her and lay still. Under the dragon's weight her chest went on rising and falling, slow and shallow and steady.

— Cinder —

Cinder felt it go out of her.

The neck under her forearm went loose. The wing under her claws stopped pulling. The body beneath her was suddenly heavier, and softer, and nothing in it was pushing back.

She did not let go.

She held the weight where it was and the forearm where it was, and watched the face. She listened. The beak hung open, and breath went in and out of it, slow and even, with a faint mist on it again in the cold. The eyelids did not flicker. She leaned a little on the chest, and nothing answered.

A moment longer. Nothing.

Then Cinder lifted her claws out of the wing. She took the forearm off the neck. She got one heavy foot under her, and then the other, and pushed herself up off the bird with both arms shaking, and stood, swaying, on the bare rock.

The quiet came down over the shelf all at once. The wind blew across it and over the drop and took nothing with it. No cry, no roar. Only her own breath, rasping, and the smaller, steadier breath of the bird at her feet.

The ice bird lay on her back where she had been held, wings spread on the stone, her crest flattened, eyes shut. A few last snowflakes lifted off her feathers and blew away.

Cinder looked down at her for a long time. Her tail flame, low and orange on the rock behind her, flickered once, and did not grow.
