— Cinder —

Her belly was still shivering under the scales where the frost had been. She clenched against it, and it stopped showing, and went on underneath.

Across the stone the bird stood with her back to the cliff and her chest drawn in. Cinder had watched her gasp every time she twisted, and now she had simply stopped twisting. The right wing hung against her side and shook all the time, a fine, fast shiver that ran from shoulder to tip and scattered loose frost on the scree.

Cinder's own left wing was tucked so tight against her ribs that it ached from the holding; twice already she had let an opening go past because the wing would have had to move. Below the lip of the shelf, cloud tore past in long grey rags.

*Keep it short. Nothing wild.*

A steadiness settled over her, like a hand laid flat on her back.

She did not need to close in. She spread her good right wing wide, the blue-green membrane snapping taut in the wind, and drew a long breath through her jaws. The fire came up out of her chest and spilled round her fangs, and she lined up the bird with her head low and her gaze level.

Then she beat the wing.

The heat went out of her in a wall. It rolled off the shelf in a heavy *whump* of force, shimmering, bending the cliff and the scree and the pale shape against them, the air in it so hot it shone. It took the whole width of the stone in front of her, and the bird had nowhere to go.

— Glacia —

It reached all of her at once.

There was no single place it struck; there was only heat, everywhere, on her face and her crest and her beak and her eyes and down her long throat and over her chest and her belly and both wings to the tips and her thin legs and her talons and the whole long length of her tail, and every frost feather she had began to weep and soften in the same instant.

She could not shed it. She was made of cold, and the heat went in where her cold could not follow, and she knew, with a clear, terrible calm, that nothing in her body could stand against this.

Her eyes screwed shut, both of them, a hiss of pain through her beak, *sss*, as the left one burned along the old line the sparks had left. Her crest wilted against her skull. A dry, hacking cough tore out of her throat.

Then the neck.

The neck had been bad. It had been past bad. And now the heat found it and lay along the whole swollen length of it, and the pain went over a line she had not known was there. She screamed. It was not a bell; it was a thin, cracking wail that broke into pieces, *aaah*, and her head jerked down to guard her throat and the movement itself was more pain, and she shook all over in the scree with it, her breath coming in ragged pulls.

The chest was a band of iron, tightening. The right wing blazed once, white, from shoulder to tip.

And then the right wing went quiet.

Dead. Heavy. She could see it hanging at her side, the feathers singed and curled at their edges, and she could not feel it there at all. She looked at it once and did not look again.

*Breathe.*

Breathing had become the hardest thing in the fight. She kept doing it, a sip at a time, the hot air scraping her throat. She did not fall.

— Cinder —

Cinder let the wing fold and watched.

The bird had not gone down. But the scream had cost her everything she had been holding back, and Cinder had heard all of it. Now Glacia stood with her legs locked straight under her, the knees braced the way legs are braced when they cannot be trusted to bend. Both wings drooped from the shoulders, the tips of the feathers resting on the stones, and the right one was not shaking any more. It just hung.

The long neck was held low and still. The proud head on it barely lifted.

*She'll be slower to that side now.*

Cinder's breath came in a steady, grinding rhythm. Her left wing throbbed, and her head throbbed with it. None of it mattered as much as the shape across the stone.

There was no ice coming off the bird now. No defiance in her face. Only the breathing was left, thin and wheezing and stubborn.

Something caught in Cinder's own throat, a small, rough sound, and she swallowed it.
