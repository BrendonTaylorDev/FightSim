— Glacia —

The wind ran cold over the swollen wing, and for once the cold did not help.

Glacia felt it slide along the scorched edge and find the place where the blade had gone, and the ache there answered it, slow and heavy. She held the wing a little lower than the other and let the air carry it.

The dragon was still above her, but not by much now, and she was not watching Glacia. She was watching her own left wing. Every few strokes, when she thought she was not seen, she gave it a short, hard shake, as if the cold were something she could fling off it like water. Each time, her head dipped. Each time, for a moment, her eyes left the bird.

*There.*

Far down, the shadow of the cliff lay across one end of the shelf, and in the shadow the stone had gone grey-white with frost. Glacia did not look at it. She was counting the shakes.

The dragon dipped her head again.

Glacia folded.

Her wings drew in tight along her body and she fell, not away but across, straight down past the dragon's flank in a long, steep slant, the air rising round her to a howl. At the last instant she flung the left wing open. Not the hurt one. The good one, frost-hard along its leading edge, stiff as a blade, swung out wide with all the speed of the fall behind it.

She went in quick and light, with strength to spare.

The edge of the wing caught the dragon across the side of the horned head with a solid whump, like a sack dropped.

And as they crossed, the dragon's arm came round the other way, quick as a reflex, and her claws met Glacia going by.

— Cinder —

It came from nowhere. One moment she was shaking out her wing, the next the whole left side of her head rang.

The edge of the bird's wing had laid a long, straight, stinging line from just behind her eye to the root of her horns, and the shock of it ran down the front of her face to the end of her snout. Her head jerked to one side and she shook it slowly back, blinking. Her eyes had flooded; she blinked and blinked again and still the sky swam. A short hiss came out of the side of her jaw, *tss*.

Her snout throbbed lightly where the force had carried through. Her head was worse. A bruised, tight ache had settled in under the scales along the side of it, not deep, not sharp, the kind that would be there every time she turned her head too quickly.

She squinted against it, jaw clenched, and found she was already turning to follow the bird.

That arm. She had not even meant it. As the pale shape went past her she had lashed out on pure instinct, and she had felt her claws strike something hard and smooth and narrow.

— Glacia —

The beak.

It was not a heavy blow. The claws had only clipped her as she passed, struck the side of her beak with a hard little clack that she heard inside her own skull more than outside it, and snapped her head sideways a finger's width. But all the dragon's speed and all of hers had been in it, more than either of them had put there, and for a moment the shock of it ran back through the bone of her face and buzzed behind her eyes. A grunt came down her nostrils, *hnh*.

She opened her beak. Closed it. The two halves met cleanly. A faint, dull sting sat along the left side, near the tip, where the claws had struck, and when she clacked it shut a second time to be certain, the sting flared and faded.

*Nothing. A scratch on stone.*

But it had been fast. Faster than she had expected from something so heavy. The dragon had been hit squarely across the head, and still her arm had come round to meet Glacia, and she had not even had to look.

Glacia let the dive run out, opened her wings and swung up and round, climbing again, putting air between them. She turned her head as she rose. The dragon had turned too. The blue eyes, still wet, still blinking, found her across the sky and fixed there.

— Cinder —

*Quicker than she looked,* Cinder thought.

The flame on her tail had dropped low with the blow, spitting. Now, slowly, it climbed back.

Across the gap, the bird's face was as calm as ever. Only her red eyes had changed, very slightly. They had narrowed, as though she had just learned something, and meant to keep it.
