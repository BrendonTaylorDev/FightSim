— Cinder —

She let out a breath she seemed to have been holding since the bird's talons found her throat, and it went on and on, a long, ragged rasp that ended in a cough. The cough hurt. She waited for it to stop hurting, and it did not, quite.

The fight was going out of her body a piece at a time, and the pain was coming in to take its place.

Her throat first. It had been a bright, sharp sting while she held the pin; now it was a swollen ring that tightened every time she swallowed, so she tried not to swallow. Then her head, heavy as a rock on the end of her neck, with a slow thud behind the swollen eye. Her belly, cold and aching deep inside where the beam had gone in. Her neck, stiff along its whole length. And the left wing. She tried to fold it properly against her back and it would not go; it stopped halfway, trembling, the membrane sagging, and a hot ache ran out of the root of it and all the way down her spine. She left it where it was, hanging half-folded at her side.

The heat of the fight was leaving her too. The thin mountain wind came across the shelf and found every scale, and she shivered, a long, shuddering ripple from her shoulders to the base of her tail, and could not stop it. Her tail flame burned low and tired on the rock behind her, a small orange tongue the wind kept pushing flat. When she willed it higher, it did not rise.

*Won.* The word did not feel like much. *Won it. Barely.*

She stood with her legs braced wide and her head hanging, smoke threading from her nostrils, and breathed.

— Glacia —

The ice bird lay on her back on the bare rock, exactly where she had been held.

Her wings lay spread out on the stone on either side of her, the left one still flattened where the claws had pressed it, the feathers along its edge ragged and bent. The right wing lay a little crooked, swollen at the joint. Her long tail trailed out behind her across the grit. The crest of three dark feathers lay crushed sideways against the rock.

She was breathing. Her chest rose under its scorched, flattened feathers, slow and shallow, and fell, and paused, and rose again. Each breath came out as a faint thread of mist that the wind took at once.

Her eyes were closed. Without the fight in it her face had gone smooth and still, the hard set of it gone; only the swelling round her neck and the darkened feathers on her chest said what had been done to her.

A little frost still clung to the tips of her feathers. Now and then the wind lifted a flake of it off her and carried it away over the edge of the shelf, out over the cloud.

The wind gusted, and dropped, and in the quiet between gusts there was nothing on the shelf but the two of them breathing, the dragon's ragged and loud, the bird's slow and soft.

She did not stir.
