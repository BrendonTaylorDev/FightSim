— Cinder —

The wind was all there was for a while. It came up over the lip of the shelf in long, hollow gusts and went hissing through the needles of the pine on the cliff and moaned off over the drop, and the bird hung in it above her, and neither of them moved.

Cinder stood on the stone with frost in the creases of her scales and her breath smoking, shivering in long, slow waves that started in her belly and ran out to her claws. She let them run. She was watching.

The bird's chest was shaking. Even from down here Cinder could see it: the scorched, swollen front of her jumping with every short breath she took, a tremor she could not hold still. And the right wing. The bird held herself up mostly on the left now, and the right came down late every time, a little lower, a little slower, with frost shaking off its singed edge like dust off a rug.

*Fire found that wing once already. It knows the way.*

Her own left wing hung half folded against her side, crusted and stiff and burning with cold. She did not want to fly on it. She was going to fly on it anyway.

*Once. Up, bite, done.*

She crouched, low, her shoulders down, in no hurry at all, and shook the frost off her head with one hard jerk that set her horns aching. Then she sprang.

Her good wing cracked open, then the bad one, and the first downbeat boomed like a sheet shaken out, and the shelf dropped away under her, and the pain in the left wing came up through her back so sharp she bellowed with it. She beat again. And again. Each stroke on the left side tore and burned and she drove it down anyway, climbing straight up the wind, the bird growing above her, pale and huge and turning to see her come.

The bird's head swung toward the cliff for a heartbeat, looking for room.

She timed the bite to the instant Glacia looked away.

Her jaws opened wide and the fire came up into them, filling her mouth, wrapping round every fang, and she came up under the right wing at full speed and closed them on it.

— Glacia —

The wet clack of teeth closing, and then fire inside the wing.

The heat went through her and kept going, and her body had nothing in it to stop it.

It was not on the feathers. It was in them, and under them, the burning fangs driven in near the joint where the wing was already scorched and swollen and the blade had laid its line, and the heat poured out of them into everything round them. Her own cold was no shield. It only made it worse, every bit of frost in her flashing to steam, the chill of her flesh making the fire feel twice as hot as it was. Every feather on her body lifted at once with the shock.

Then the jaws let go and the dragon was past, below her, a roar and a gust of heat and gone.

The pain crossed the line.

It did not stay a hurt. It became the wing itself: from the joint at her shoulder to the long flight feathers at the tip, one solid blaze of burning, throbbing pain that pushed out everything else. A cry tore out of her, high and thin, *yip*, and broke off into a wheeze, and she clamped her beak shut on what came after it.

She did not fall. She would not. The wing came down on the next stroke because she made it come down, and the cry came again, smaller, through her nostrils, and the wing came up, and the dragon below her was turning.

— Glacia —

She hung on the wind, and breathed.

It came quick and shallow at first, like a small bird's, the air going in no further than the top of her chest before the bruise there shut the door on it. Her breath stuttered once on the way in. Then she had it again, and made it slow, deliberately slow, and watched it frost in front of her beak.

She looked down at the wing.

The feathers along the edge were blackened and crushed together where the jaws had closed, the frost gone off them entirely. Underneath, the swelling had already started; she could see it lifting the plumage, a hot, tight ridge across the bone. The wing twitched on its own, a small jerking at the joint, and she could not make it stop. She beat it, and it answered, but every stroke sent the pain up through her shoulder and into her crest, which prickled along its whole length with each throb.

This wing had been the first thing the dragon's fire found, at the very start. She remembered that first flinch, the frost turning to steam. It had been a small thing then.

A wave of heat went over her and left her shivering.

Every downstroke now took a decision. Each one tore at the wing, and she felt each one, and made the next.

*All right,* Glacia thought, looking down at the dragon labouring beneath her on one good wing and one crusted one. *Now I know her reach.*

Far below them, a pebble the wind had loosened skittered off the edge of the shelf and fell into the cloud, and made no sound at all.
