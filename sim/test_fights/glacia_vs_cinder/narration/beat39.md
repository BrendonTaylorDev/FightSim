— Glacia —

Her breath came short and shallow, and stopped every time against the same place in her chest. It told her what she already knew: there was not much left in her.

A few strides off across the bare rock, the dragon stood with her head hanging. Her left wing was folded tight against her side and stayed there; everything she did, she did with the other half of her. One blue eye had swollen nearly shut under the ragged scales of her brow. Below the edge of the shelf, cloud tore past on the wind, grey and endless.

*Breath. A little cold. One beam.*

Glacia could not stand. She did not need to. She sat propped on her spread wings, the burned chest turned a little away even now, and she held herself unnaturally still. The dragon's head came up a fraction, then sagged again, and her guard sagged with it, and the pale cream belly was there, open, rising and falling.

She lifted her head on the stiff, aching neck. The air in front of her beak went white and smoking. Then the last cold she had went out of her in a thin, shaking beam, pale blue, so cold it smoked as it crossed the stone, straight into the dragon's chest.

— Cinder —

It hit her with a cold, sharp snap and spread across her chest and down over her belly in one blast, and the belly took the worst of it.

*Khhrr.* The snarl choked off halfway. Her whole body flinched in on itself round the cold, muscles clamping tight over her middle, much harder than a blow that size should have folded her, and her feet stayed exactly where they were. Frost bloomed white across the cream scales and crackled.

Then she tried to breathe, and nothing went in.

Her chest heaved. Her jaws opened on air that would not come. The world narrowed to that, to one breath, and the breath would not be had. Every limb went rigid for a heartbeat. Then a ragged gulp tore its way in, high and thin, with a faint whistle at the top of it.

— Cinder —

The cold sank in after the blast had gone.

It lay in her belly like a stone, and the old ache from the blizzard was already there under it, waiting. Her belly shook when she asked anything of it. She folded at the middle and had to haul herself straight, slowly, and the hauling made her wince hard. Her chest had gone stiff with the shock, and a cough waited behind her ribs that she was afraid to let out.

So she breathed high and shallow, gulping the thin air like water. It was never enough. Her tail flame had shrunk to a nervous sputter and grew back only slowly. Every jar of her body rang up through the torn left wing and into her teeth.

A low, long groan came out of her. She had nothing to hold it in with, and she let it come.

*Still... standing.*

— Glacia —

Glacia watched the frost crack on the dragon's belly as the muscles under it shook, watched the jaws open and shut on air that would not come, watched the two horns drift low and waver toward the stone.

*Not yet. Don't hope yet.*

She tried to rise while the dragon could not breathe. She pushed down on both wings at once and the right one skidded out from under her on the grit, and she came down hard on the stone, the jolt going straight into her bruised upper back. *Ngh.* She hissed it out and lay a moment, then got her wings under her and her body back up to sitting.

The second try was shorter. The left wing pushed, the shoulder above it blazed, and it gave.

The third was hardly more than leaning. Her talons scraped, and nothing in her answered.

She stayed sitting on the cold rock, wings spread out flat on either side to hold her, her breath steaming and catching. A few strides of bare stone lay between them, and for the moment neither of them moved to cross it.
