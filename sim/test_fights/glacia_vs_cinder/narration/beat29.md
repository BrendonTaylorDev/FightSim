— Glacia —

The stone under her talons was cold and gritty and she did not want to stand on it any longer.

The brightness was still running through her. It sat over the pain like clear ice over dark water: everything was still down there, the chest, the neck, the burned wing, but she could see straight through it to what mattered. The dragon. Standing, swaying, the left wing clamped against her body like a hurt arm, the right hand still pressed to its joint. The head held low and stiff. She was squinting, Glacia saw, as if against a glare, though the sky over the shelf was flat and grey.

*Enough care. There is nothing left to save it for.*

She threw herself off the rock.

Her wings opened to their full span, and for a moment the shelf, the cliff, the dragon, all of it, seemed to shrink beneath them. Then the right wing had to work. It came down through the air in a long, burning drag, the swollen feathers shuddering along it and loose frost spinning off the tip, and she felt it tear a little worse on the downstroke and a little worse again on the next. Grit lifted off the stone below her in a grey swirl. She climbed anyway, two strokes, three, the wind catching under her and taking her up.

Below, the dragon turned her head to follow, too slowly. The left side of her was too guarded, curled round that wing so tightly that everything else stood open, and Glacia knew exactly why.

She held still on the wind, as she always did. Then the air around her stirred, swirled, and began to roar, and she twisted and whipped both wings down. The twist pulled at her chest and a grunt broke out of her beak. The blades of air went down at the dragon's head.

— Cinder —

She saw it come, and her head jerked aside, a fraction too late.

For an instant there was nothing at all. A clean, cold slap high on her skull, between the horns, then something hard and quick along the side of her neck.

Then the pain arrived all at once.

It landed in her head first, on the frost-burned swelling the bird's wing had cracked across earlier and the blizzard had packed with ice, and the head snapped back with it, her eyes streaming, and this time it did not stay in her head. It went through her. A bellow tore out of her that ran out of air halfway, *ahhh-hhk*, and broke, and her weight went backward with the blow before she could stop it. One step, two, three, her feet skidding and scraping on the stone, her tail swinging wild behind her, her body tipping back further than it could come back from.

It came back. Only just. Her heel caught on a ridge in the rock and held.

The neck had taken it too, a hard stripe of pain all along the long muscles, and now it buzzed as if the blow to her head had plucked a string between them. When she tried to growl the sound came out a husk, a long, hoarse wail with no breath behind it, *hhaaaa*, and the neck stiffened and would not let her lift her head.

Her eyes were screwed shut. Her whole body was hunched, waiting for the second blow, and she could not make it unhunch. She knew she had frozen. She hated every heartbeat of it.

*Move. Move, you fool.*

She blinked, slowly, like something surfacing from deep water. The pounding in her skull drowned out the wind. She pressed her face low, shaking from the shoulders up, and listened.

Above her, the bird's breath came ragged and quick, catching on every stroke of that burned wing.
