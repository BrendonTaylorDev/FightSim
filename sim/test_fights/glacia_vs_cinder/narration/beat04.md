— Cinder —

A bead of melted frost ran off the tip of Cinder's horn, hung there a moment, and fell away into nothing.

She shook her head, and then she shook the wing. It hurt to do it. The crust of ice on the left membrane cracked and broke and went spinning off in glittering flakes, and the ache under it flared, deep in the bone, and she set her jaw and shook it again until the skin was clear and dark and could catch the wind properly. It would carry her. It complained the whole way, but it would carry her.

The bird was above her. The bird was always above her.

*Not this time.*

Cinder climbed. She clawed her way up the wind in long, labouring strokes, grunting on every downbeat of the left wing, smoke streaming back from her nostrils, and the bird watched her come and turned a little side-on as she rose, the pale body angled away, the bruised chest tucked behind the line of a wing. Clever. She had learned already.

*Brave little thing. Not even trying to run.*

Cinder came level, and then she came above, and for the first time in the fight she was looking down at the bird instead of up. The whole of her was laid out against the cloud: the huge clear wings, the right one held stiffer than the left with its singed edge darker against the pale, and behind her the long, long tail of blue feathers streaming out on the wind.

The relief of being up here went through Cinder and came straight back out. Being above her once was not the same as staying there.

She drew the air in tight around her and then flung it outward.

Her wings came down together in one savage stroke, and the air in front of them sheared into thin, pale blades, curved and spinning, three, four of them, too fast to count, and they went down at the bird in a single scything sweep.

— Glacia —

It went through her in the time it took to hear it.

A hissing slice across the left wing, quick and shallow, there and gone. Something harder across the right, along the singed edge where the fire had been. A sharp blow on the tail behind her that jerked it sideways as if it had been grabbed, and a startled breath, *hah*, puffed out of her beak.

Then nothing. For a breath, nothing at all.

Then the right wing caught up.

It came all at once. The old sting of the fire was still sitting there in the scorched feathers, and the blade had laid itself straight across it, and the two pains did not stay two. They ran together and sank, became something duller and broader and much harder to ignore, a heavy, swelling hurt that seemed to fill the whole wing from the joint to the long flight feathers at the tip. A short cry came out of her, *ahh*, and the wind tore it off and threw it away over the cloud.

Her feathers flared out all over her and then clamped flat.

She beat the wing. It answered, but late, and the downstroke had a drag in it now, a reluctance, as if the wing had to be persuaded each time. A sparkle of loose frost shook off the edge of it and drifted down. She could feel the tightness starting under the feathers, the swelling coming up slow and hot along the bone.

The hurt in her right wing was dull and broad, the kind that spreads rather than fades.

The left one stung faintly and was quiet. The tail still tingled where the blade had struck it, and she let it ripple out behind her once, from root to tip, and felt nothing worse than a bruise. Little things.

And something else, something she noticed only because it had stopped. The hot, crawling sting of the burn, the one that had followed her since the fire, was fading. It thinned as she breathed, retreated into the singed feathers and lay down there, just a warmth now and no longer a sting. The cold came back into her right side in its place, slow and welcome.

She hissed once through her beak and kept her eyes on the dragon above her.

*Above me now. She wanted that very much.*

Below the edge of the shelf the cloud tore past in long white rags. Glacia let the wind hold her, her right wing drawn a little closer than the left, and waited for the ache to settle into something she could fly with.
