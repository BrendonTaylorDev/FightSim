— Glacia —

*Air. Before anything else.*

Her body had not caught up with the thought. It stood where the dive had thrown it, a stride out of place on the grit, swaying, and it was not breathing.

She braced. She spread both wings half open and set their tips against the stone on either side of her, the left firm and the right only just touching, trembling, shedding a slow sparkle of frost where the feathers met the rock. She opened her beak as wide as it would go and pulled.

Nothing. Her ribs jerked in under the singed feathers and nothing came, and a dry, clicking gasp, *hhk*, was all that got out of her.

She pulled again. The air up here was so thin. It had always been thin, and she had always loved it for that; now it was like trying to drink through a reed. A little came. A thread of cold went down her throat, scraping the raw place there, and stopped short.

Again. Again. Each pull dragged a hoarse rasp through her beak.

The strange thing was that it did not hurt where it should. Her chest lay under her like something borrowed, heavy and quiet and wrong. Her neck held her head up and she could not feel it doing it. She could see her own breast heave, the ruined feathers lifting, and feel nothing there but a cold, dead weight.

*It will come back. Both of them. Breathe while they're quiet.*

Then it came. One breath went all the way down, ragged and burning, and a second followed it, and her whole body sagged with the relief of it. She breathed, beak open, steam pouring from it in short white bursts, and the shelf stopped swimming at its edges.

She moved her talons. Left, then right. Her feet came back under her, and she planted them on the grit and stood square to the open sky, her left wing lifting back across the front of her chest where it belonged.

She looked up.

— Cinder —

She could not keep beating.

Each stroke of the left wing tore something new along the membrane, a hot, ripping ache that ran from the joint to the tip and back into her shoulder, and the frost the shard had left on it cracked and flaked away with each beat. After the dive and the clumsy, tumbling climb out of it, she had nothing left to give the wing.

So she stopped asking. She held it out stiff and flat and glided on it, and let the good wing do just enough, one slow beat, then another, and she sank. The shelf came up towards her a little with every breath.

The tumble had left her crooked in the air. She rolled her shoulders and felt the wind take her evenly again, felt her line come straight, her head lift, her good wing find its rhythm. Her guard was back. She was flying, not falling.

Her head pounded with every pulse. Her breath came hard and smoky.

Below her the bird was breathing again. Cinder had watched her stand there gaping like something landed on a riverbank, ribs heaving on nothing, and had known she had no blow in her at all. Now the breath was coming back, in white bursts of steam, and the bird had set her feet, and the red eyes were lifting.

*Still standing. Of course you are.*

Cinder held the wing out, and held it, and a tremor ran out through it from the root, along every long finger of it to the ragged edge of the membrane, and away into the wind.
