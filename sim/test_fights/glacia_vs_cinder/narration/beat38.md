— Cinder —

The next breath came, and it hurt. So did the one after it.

Cinder hung over the shelf on wings that had stopped being wings. The right one still beat. The left one only moved because it was fastened to her, a hot, swollen weight that dragged at the root of her back on every stroke and gave her nothing back. The fire she had poured into that bite had taken the last of what was holding her up. She felt empty right down to her claws.

*Down. Now. Before it drops you.*

She stopped fighting the left wing and let it fold. The shelf tilted up at her. She spread the right wing wide, caught a gust off the cliff with it and rode the air half falling, half gliding, the grey rock sliding in underneath, and at the last moment flared both wings as far as the torn one would go.

Her feet hit the stone. Her heavy legs took it badly; her knees buckled and her claws scraped long white lines in the rock before they held. She stayed up. A few strides away the bird lay on her back where she had fallen.

Cinder stood with her head hanging. Her breath sawed in and out of her open jaws, too fast, smoke leaking out with it in thin grey threads. Her sore jaw ached each time it opened. The flame on her tail had sunk to a small, dull orange flicker that the wind pushed flat against the stone. Her left wing hung half folded at her side and trembled on its own.

She did not move toward the bird. She could not have, just then.

— Glacia —

Glacia tried to get up.

She beat both wings against the bare rock, pushed down on them and tried to roll herself over onto her talons. She got partway. Her body came up off the stone, and everything tilted round her: the cliff swung across the sky, the dragon slid sideways, and the right wing folded under her weight like wet paper. She went back down onto her back. The jolt went through her neck and drew a thin, cracking wail out of her.

She lay and breathed. Then she tried again, smaller: the left wing only, pressed flat to the grit and pushing, the bruised left shoulder flaring hot. She rose a hand's width and sank.

The third try was barely a try. She dug her talons into a crack in the rock and dragged, and her body came round and up a little, and a little more, and stopped.

She was sitting. Not standing, not crouched to spring. Sitting on the cold stone with her legs folded under her and her ragged wings spread out on the rock on either side to prop her, the long tail lying stiff behind. Her head would not come up straight; the neck held it low and a little to one side. Her burned chest and throat were open to the dragon, and there was nothing she could do about that.

A pebble she had dislodged skittered over the lip of the shelf and dropped into the cloud. She never heard it land.

*Upright. Enough.*

She let her weight settle onto the wings, and stayed.
