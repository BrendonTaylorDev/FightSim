— Cinder —

The left wing was shaking, a fine tremor along the long bones under the membrane, and every beat turned it into a hot, tearing ache all the way into her shoulder.

*Enough. Down.*

She flared both wings, the good one wide and the bad one as far as it would go, and dropped. The shelf came up under her and she landed heavily on her feet a few strides in front of the bird, claws scraping on the bare rock, her tail flame guttering in the wind.

Glacia stood with her chest drawn in under her and her head held rigid. Every breath showed in her eyes before it reached her chest, shallow and careful, with the wheeze in it. Her body was turned a quarter away, so the right wing was furthest from Cinder and the left half-raised in front of it like a shield.

*Let her think I'm tired.*

She let her shoulders sag and her breath come loud and ragged, smoke curling from her nostrils. It was not much of a lie. Her weight shifted forward before she had decided to move, and she caught it.

Then she went in low.

The left side of her was dragging with the wing, so she struck with the right. The arm went back past her shoulder and came round with the three claws already open, the scales of her forearm glowing a deep violet-blue as the dragon-force gathered there, aimed under the shielding wing at the pale belly.

— Glacia —

She heard it before she felt it: a ripping hiss as the claws dragged.

They raked across her belly, and in the same stroke the heel of the hand jarred down against her left thigh and the knuckles drove up into her chest.

The thigh was nothing, a twitch, a short grunt, *hff*, as the leg shook under her. The belly was worse. A hollow, sick feeling folded her round it, and a low sound came up her throat, *mmh*, and she drew her body in over the raked feathers before she could stop herself.

The chest was the one that mattered.

The cry broke out of her, a groan dragged up from deep inside, *nngh*, and her ribs heaved in short, shallow jerks.

And then it stopped hurting.

All at once, the way a sound stops. The raw, burning weight she had carried there since the very first fire was gone, and in its place was a dead, heavy strangeness, a cold spreading out from the middle of her that had nothing to do with ice.

She stood very still.

*That is not better. That is wrong.*

— Glacia —

She looked down at herself.

Her breast was raked and singed and swollen out of its shape, and below it a new row of ruffled lines crossed her belly where the claws had gone, stinging with a bright, gritty prickle. The chest she could see moving, rising and falling under the ruined feathers, and she could not feel it move. It twitched once on its own, and she watched it do it.

She tested her talons on the rock, left, then right. They answered. The left thigh trembled once and steadied.

She breathed in halves anyway, out of habit, stopping where the hurt should have begun.

*Balance: mine. Wing: mine. Chest: gone quiet.*

She did not trust it at all. She lifted her eyes to the dragon.

— Cinder —

Cinder stood where she had landed, breathing hard, and watched.

The bird had not gone down. She had not even staggered. But the long tail hung low and still behind her now, a weight on the rock instead of a balance, and the three blue crest feathers lay flat against her head and stayed flat.

The groan had been real. Then the bird had gone too still, staring down at her own chest as if it belonged to someone else.

*Look how she stands. She doesn't trust it.*

Her own left wing hung half-folded against her side, throbbing, and she did not look at it. Far out over the cloud a hawk turned slow circles, and she did not look at that either. There was only the marked belly, the chest above it, and the red eyes coming up to find her, the pale shape of the bird a little blurred at the edges by the snow blowing off it.
