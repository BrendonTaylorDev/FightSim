— Glacia —

Her breath caught at the top, *hk*, on the swollen weight of her chest, and for a moment there was nothing on the shelf but the wind.

The dragon hung above her, closer than before. Glacia watched the left wing. It shook whenever it had to do anything at all, and the dragon held it pulled in tight against her side between strokes, as if she could hide it from the air. The head was worse. Every time the dragon turned, her blue eyes lagged behind the turn and had to come back and find the bird again.

*She guards the wing and forgets the head. I would do the same.*

She lifted her beak at the frosted horns. The dragon saw it and flinched: the head snatched back and down, the good wing beating hard to drag the body away from a blow that had not come.

Glacia held still. Unnaturally still.

Then she opened both wings. The left went wide and clean. The right came out shaking from shoulder to tip, the swollen feathers standing up along it, and loose frost fell off it in a glittering shower. She breathed in as little as she could and struck on the breath out. Her cry went up, clear as a struck bell, and the air over the shelf went white.

Blizzard. She swept her wings across, then back, so there was no edge to the storm and nowhere to fly out of it, and drove it upward at the dragon in a howling wall of snow and splintered ice.

— Cinder —

It hit all of her at once.

There was no first place. The cold came through every scale at the same instant, belly and back and tail and the soft inside of her wings, and her whole body locked rigid for a heartbeat in the air, every limb clenched, before a ragged gulp of breath tore back into her.

Then it sorted itself out, and found the worst places.

The left wing first. It had been hugged in against her side, and the storm got under it anyway and drove the ice into the frost-burned membrane, and the pain was so huge that she lost the wing for a moment entirely; there was only a white blaze where it had been. The bellow came out of her before she could stop it, deep and enormous, and broke halfway into a hoarse, shaking growl, *grrrhh*, that the wind tore away over the drop.

Her head next. Snow packed in round her horns, hard against the old swelling between them, and her right horn rang with a deep ache she felt inside her skull. Her eyes flew wide and streamed. She snorted, tossed her head, and that was a mistake; the head pulsed so hard it shook the breath in her.

The neck caught it all along its length. The cold went into the long muscles there and they cramped, and she gulped against something that would not go down.

Her tail flame shrank to a low, spitting stub. Snow hissed into it.

She was still up. The left wing had to beat too, and when she made it, it came down short and slow and burned the whole way, and she slid sideways on the wind and had to claw her way back level.

*Fine. Fine. Move.*

She held her head very still now, afraid to turn it. The neck had become something she had to carry: a stiff, aching column with a hot knot in it just under the jaw, where the bird's whirling gale had first wrenched it, early on, and the new cold had settled right on top of that. Every breath out came with a faint growl attached, small and rasping. Her chest heaved and her jaw hung open, fangs bared to the cold, and she did not care how it looked.

Then, slowly, the left wing went strange. The burning sank under a heavy, deadened weight, as if the wing were packed in snow, and she could hardly tell where it was.

Her eyes went down to the bird.

— Glacia —

The storm was spent and Glacia's wings sank. As they came down, the feeling came back.

It came back into her neck and her chest together, all at once, as if a door had been flung open, and it was every bit as bad as when the jaws had closed there and worse for being sudden. A thin wail cracked out of her beak, *eeehh*, high and splitting, and her body seized round it: the shoulders hunched, the chest curling in on itself, the long tail clamping tight to the rock. Her talons scraped short and hard at the stone. The shaking began in her neck, a fine fast trembling just behind the head, and went down through her into the folded wings, and frost fell from her feathers like sifted salt.

She did not fall. She stood there shaking, beak open, and dragged one more thin breath past the weight in her chest.
