— Glacia —

The air where the claws had passed was empty now, a gap in the wind that closed behind the dragon as she wheeled away below.

Glacia hung in the gusts and did not quite fly. Each stroke of the right wing was a separate decision, and each one cost more than the one before: the joint burned as it lifted, the swollen flight feathers dragged against the air, and on the downstroke something deep in the wing pulled hot and tight, as though the cold had finally gone out of it and left only the burn. The left wing was little better. The fresh claw furrows across it stung each time the wind found them, and loose frost sparked off its trailing edge and was blown away down into the cloud.

Her chest would not fold. She could not draw her head in over it, could not curl round the scorched, raw place where the feathers had come away, so she flew stiff and upright, like something hung on a string.

Below her, Cinder laboured round in a slow, uneven circle. The dragon's left wing came up late on every stroke and she was holding it close to her body between beats, shielding it, her whole right side turned out to the sky. Her head swung loosely on the long neck. One blue eye was screwed nearly shut.

*The head. Once more.*

Glacia held still.

Then she opened her beak. The air in front of her mouth went white with cold, and the cold gathered and hardened into one long, clear splinter, and with a thin, bell-like cry that cracked in the middle she flung it down at the battered horned head.

— Cinder —

She saw it coming and could not get her head out of the way. The best she could do was jerk her chin down.

The shard took her on the jaw instead of the brow, and the same blow cracked across her muzzle beside it, cold biting into the scales and a hard, bright jolt running up through every tooth on that side. Her head snapped back. Her eyes streamed.

*Rrnngh.* The snarl ground out through her clenched fangs, rose, and broke into a hoarse cough as frost burst off her face in a glittering puff and blew away on the wind.

The jaw throbbed, slow and heavy, a dull drumbeat under the scales where the old frost-bruise from the blizzard was already waiting for it. Her muzzle stung and tightened. She snorted, hard, as if she could blow the cold back out, and tossed her head, and the toss went through her neck and into the ruined skull above it and nearly turned her over in the air.

Her left wing lurched. For a moment she hung on the right one alone, dropping, the cloud below coming up white and close. Then the left caught a little air, at the cost of a long, tearing ache through the crushed scales at its root, and she was climbing again, slowly, smoke trickling from her nostrils. The flame at her tail had sunk to a low, sullen orange.

*Still... here.*

She worked her jaw once. It moved. It hurt to move.

— Glacia —

Glacia watched her steady, and spent no more than that on it.

The dragon's guard came up again over the left wing, a little lower than before, a little slower. Her mouth hung open, panting, and she no longer seemed to try to close it; the jaw sat at the angle of something sore that did not want to be asked for anything. Every few wingbeats her head gave a small, hitching shake.

Glacia's long tail rippled behind her in the gusts, and she let it. Her own breath came out in short white plumes that the wind tore off her beak. There was a hollowness in her, under the chest, where the last of the cold had come from. She reached for more and found very little.

*Not enough. Not yet.*

The wind gusted, dropped, gusted. She hung in it, both wings shaking, and kept her red eyes on the dragon.
