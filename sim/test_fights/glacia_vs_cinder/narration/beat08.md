— Glacia —

The left wing. Glacia's eyes went there and stayed.

The dragon was holding it out from her body now, stiff and wide and crooked, as if it no longer trusted itself to touch her. The membrane was still crusted pale with frost from the mist. When she beat it, it did not bend the way a wing should; it hinged, all at once, and her whole body dipped round it, and smoke jerked out of her nostrils on every stroke. She was staying up on one wing and a promise.

*She is not as hurt as she looks. She is heavy, and stubborn, and she has a great deal of fire left.* Glacia noted it calmly. *So take the air away from her.*

At the shelf's edge the wind came up the cliff face in a long, steady push. Glacia could feel it on her feathers, rising.

She ran three steps on her thin grey legs and threw herself off the edge into it.

Her wings opened to their full span, huge and pale and glittering, and the updraft caught them and threw her upward, and the shelf shrank to a grey ledge beneath her. Her chest screamed at the first stroke. She grunted, *hnn*, and the grunt had pain in it, and she beat again anyway and again, climbing on the wind until she was level with the dragon, close enough to see the frost cracking on the hurt wing.

Then she stopped climbing, and beat her wings as hard as she had ever beaten them.

Not once. Over and over, great sweeping strokes that bent the long feathers back, the right wing burning, the chest a white blaze of hurt on every downstroke, the air in front of her packing tighter and tighter and beginning to turn. It spun. It howled. Snow came out of nowhere into it, her snow, and the whole spinning column of wind tore loose from her and went for the dragon.

She threw the air forward with all her force, and the gust howled with it.

— Cinder —

It hit everywhere at once.

There was no one blow to brace for. The wind closed round her like a fist, a booming roar that took her whole body at the same instant, horns and face and throat and belly, arms and legs and tail, and wrenched at every part of her in a different direction, and the snow in it stung her eyes shut, and the roar of it drowned out even her own roar.

And the left wing went.

The hurricane took it and bent it back, the frozen membrane straining against the wind like a sail about to tear, and the pain that had been bad crossed into something else. It went from the tip of the wing to the deep root of it in her back and blazed there, enormous and white. A bellow tore out of her, raw and huge, and broke in the middle into a hoarse, grinding growl, *grrhh*, that she clamped down on with her fangs.

The wing folded under her in mid-beat. The sky let go.

She fell.

The shelf came up spinning, grey and hard, and she hit it on her back. The stone struck across her shoulders and drove a grunt of air out of her, *uff*. Her left shoulder smacked down a heartbeat after with a jolt that ran up her neck, and she hissed through her nose; her right leg slapped against the rock as she bounced and came to rest, a glancing knock on the thigh. Then she was still, flat on her back with her belly to the sky and her tail flung out across the stone.

She lay there and let the world come back.

Her head throbbed, a bruised, hot ache along the side where the bird's wing had struck it before and the wind had battered it now. Her horn stung. Her snout stung. Her eyes ran. Her tail flame, which the beam had stung before, had been crushed low by the wind and was now crawling back up, thin and pale and slow, and the root of it ached as if a fist had closed round it. Her right wing smarted along the edge. She coughed once, a dry hack, her throat raw from the wind.

All of that was nothing.

The left wing lay out on the stone beside her, half open, trembling. She had not told it to tremble. She looked at it and did not move it, and even not moving it hurt: a deep, pulsing, white pain, swollen and slow, as if the whole wing had been packed full of hot clay. She pulled it in toward her side. Halfway there it caught on the stone, and the pain went so sharp that her lips peeled back from her fangs and stayed there, and her breath hissed in and in and would not come out until the worst had passed.

She let her head fall back against the rock, once. Then it came back up, eyes hard and wet, and found the bird.

The bird was hanging in the sky above the shelf, wings spread on the updraft, her breath steaming, her chest drawn in.

*Pay her back,* Cinder thought. *Right where she did it.*

A small muscle at the hinge of her jaw jumped, and jumped again.
