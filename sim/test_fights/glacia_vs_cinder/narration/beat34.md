— Glacia —

The dragon's right arm was drawing back, slowly, the hand opening and closing at her side as if testing whether there was still anything in it. There were no sparks on it now. The tail flame behind her had sunk to a low, spitting orange.

Glacia had nothing left to throw. She knew it the way she knew the cold: without having to look. There was no shard in her, no beam, no storm. There was only the wind, coming up over the lip of the shelf from the cloud in long, hard gusts, the thing her whole body was made for.

At the foot of the cliff a stunted pine creaked as a gust bent it.

She spread her wings.

They did not snap open. They came out ragged and slow, the left one hanging wrong with the wire of the lightning still in it, the right one shuddering from the shoulder and shedding frost in a glittering trickle. She turned her body sideways to the dragon, to the drop, and took three stumbling steps across the stone with her wings half lifted, and on the third the gust came under them and she let it take her.

She went off the shelf low and sideways, skimming out over the edge with the cloud a long way below her talons, and only once she was clear of the rock did she try to climb.

— Cinder —

Cinder watched her go. She did not move to follow; she could barely keep her feet.

The bird hung out beyond the edge of the shelf, a pale ragged shape against the grey, flying lopsided. The left wing did most of the work. The right one came down short and late, a beat behind, and every time it did the whole bird sagged toward that side and had to drag herself level, and a thin, cracked sound came back on the wind, *hhee*, that might have been a breath or a cry. She was not climbing far. She was not climbing fast. But she was up, and getting higher, a few strides at a time, out of reach.

Cinder stood on the rock with her head hanging and her sides heaving and looked up at her.

*Of course. Of course she goes up.*

Her own left wing lay along her back like a bar of hot iron. She did not want to think about what it would cost to open it.

— Glacia —

Up on the wind, Glacia felt the right wing tearing. Not all at once. A little more with each downstroke, a hot, deep pulling along the bone from the shoulder outward, and each time the wing came up again it was a little slower to answer. She flew with her beak open and her eyes half closed, letting the gusts carry what they would.

Below her the shelf and the orange shape on it turned slowly as she circled.

In her right wing, at the shoulder where it hurt most, she could feel her own pulse beating, quick and thin and hard against the hurt.
