— Glacia —

The scree pressed into her, a hundred small sharp stones, and the cliff was cold against her back. She sat with her legs out in front of her and lifted her head. Slowly. The neck made every finger's width of it a decision.

Cinder stood a few strides out on the bare rock, the left wing tucked tight against her side, doing everything with the other. Her eyes kept sliding off Glacia to the cloud and being dragged back. The frost-burned side of her head was still dull with ice.

A pebble rolled off the lip of the shelf and dropped into the cloud and did not land.

*Which hurt will slow me. The neck. Keep the head low, then. She'll think it can't come up.*

Glacia drew breath for it in two parts, stopping halfway where the wheeze began. The numb weight in her chest simply lay there and let the air through.

She narrowed her eyes, and the air between them went brittle with cold.

Frost crept out across the rock ahead of her, crackling. Then she opened her beak and breathed, and the breath came out as a pale, glittering mist that rolled low across the stone and rose straight into the cream-yellow chest and belly. Freeze-Dry. A cold, sharp snap, like a frozen branch breaking.

It reached her. Glacia's shoulders loosened, just for an instant.

— Cinder —

The cold hit her chest first, a hard, tight grip across the scales, and her hand came up and pressed flat against it of its own accord. A short snarl, *rrh*, got out through her fangs.

Then her belly took the brunt of it.

The mist went into the soft cream scales there and froze, all at once, a shock so cold it felt for a heartbeat like being struck. Her whole middle clenched. She flinched in on herself, muscles going tight round the spot, her feet staying exactly where they were, and a grunt came out of her, *uff*, more surprise than pain. Her near wing jerked out from her side and settled back not quite flat.

She looked down at herself. A crust of white had formed across the pale scales of her belly, and under it the cold was biting in, a deep, clean sting that made the muscles there want to curl. She did not let them. Her eyes squeezed shut for a blink and opened again.

*That's what you've got left? A breath of cold?*

It was more than that. It smarted, a sharp, hollow ache low in her gut that made every breath a little more careful. But it was not deep. She rubbed at the frost with the back of her claws and it flaked away, and the ache under it faded to a dull throb.

Her tail flame, shrunk low while the cold was in her, flared up again, yellow and then white, and she straightened and looked at the bird.

— Glacia —

She had to get up.

She drove her left wing against the stone and pushed with her legs. She got partway up, her back scraping against the cliff, and then there was no air in her for the rest of it. Her legs folded and she sat back down hard in the scree, gasping.

Again. This time she got almost all the way, her talons braced, her wing spread flat against the rock face, and then the loose stones rolled out from under her feet and she went down on her side and slid.

Third. She did not think about it. She heaved, one ugly lurch, the left wing beating wildly against the cliff and the right one flaring from her side in spite of her with a sparkle of loose frost, and she was up, swaying, nearly toppling forward onto the dragon before she caught herself.

She was standing. Her sides heaved.

And her chest woke up.

The numbness drained out of it all at once, and everything it had been holding back came in behind it, the fire, the claws, the cliff, as bad as it had ever been and worse for having been gone. A raw, cracking wail tore out of her before she knew it was coming. Her body seized round the chest, her shoulders hunching in, the left wing clamping against her breast, and a hard trembling ran through both her thin grey legs at the knee.

She did not fall. She stood there shaking, beak open, and held herself up.

Below the lip of the shelf another loose stone went over and dropped, falling and falling into the white.
