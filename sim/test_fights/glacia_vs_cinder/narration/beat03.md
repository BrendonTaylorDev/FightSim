— Glacia —

There was a long reach of empty air between them now, and Glacia measured it.

She had climbed. The dragon had come round in a wide, heavy turn below her and was hanging there, wings working, her head tipped back to keep the bird in sight. From up here Glacia could see all of her at once: the orange back, the pale belly, the long tail swinging out behind for balance with its fire bent flat by the wind. And the wings, the great blue-green sails of them, spread wide and beating, the left one still carrying a faint white star of frost where the shard had gone in.

The dragon's breathing was quiet. Easy. She could have hung there all day.

Glacia's own breathing was not quiet. Each one came short, stopping where the bruise across her chest told it to stop, and there was a hot sting high on her right side, near the joint of the shoulder, that flared each time she brought that wing down. She noted it and set it aside.

*She is strong. She is also very large, and all of her is in the open.*

She wheeled once more, slowly, and let the wind turn her until she was looking straight down the line of the dragon's spread left wing, across her chest, to the bright tip of her tail. Everything lined up.

For a moment Glacia was entirely still on the wind.

She opened her beak. The cold came up from deep in her, the way it always did, gathering in her throat until her breath stopped steaming because there was no warmth left in it to steam. A clear, ringing note came out of her, high as a struck bell, and the beam went with it.

It lanced down across the empty air, white-blue and perfectly straight, and it was thinner than she wanted. The burn had taken something from her. It was enough.

— Cinder —

It swept across her with a crisp crackle of frost, and the wing took the worst of it.

Cinder felt the cold go in. Not onto the membrane but into it, a deep, biting chill that pushed through the thin skin and found the long bones underneath and closed round them. The wing had been stung once already and it remembered; the old cold under the shard's mark woke up and joined the new one, and the two of them spread out together along the whole leading edge. A grunt came out of her, *hngk*, choked off in her throat. The wing jerked inward against her side, flinching from the blast, and for one long instant it would not open again.

She hung there, lopsided, beating with the right wing alone, the air sliding out from under her.

*Open. Come on. Open.*

Her left wing twitched once, a small involuntary jump, and then answered her. It came out stiff and slow. Frost had gone across the blue-green skin in a pale crust, and it crackled faintly as she stretched it, and the ache in it was not a sting any more but a real, deep, clenched hurt, the kind that made her want to fold it tight and not use it.

She did not fold it. She beat it, carefully, and grunted on the downstroke, and beat it again.

The rest she barely felt. The beam had raked across her chest too, a cold slap on the hard cream scales that made her pull a sharp breath through her teeth and then was gone. Her tail was worse. The blast had caught the flame at its tip and for a moment it had shrunk to a hissing blue bead, and the whole tail had clamped hard against her legs, and the pain of it had been strange and sharp, a cold where nothing was ever cold. A grunt went out through her closed fangs, *hff*. Now the flame flared up again high and bright, orange, then white at the core, and sat there flickering, sore at its root.

She shook frost off her horns. She bent her neck and looked up.

The bird hung above her in the sky with her pale wings spread, breath steaming again, and looked back down. Neither of them gave anything away. Cinder bared her fangs at her and smoke curled from her nostrils, and the bird only tilted her head a little, as if noting something she wanted to remember.
