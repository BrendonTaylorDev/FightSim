— Cinder —

Her eyes cleared first. Then, all at once, the breath came back into her: a long, ragged, wheezing pull that hurt her belly and her chest on the way down and was still the best thing she had ever felt.

The frost on her belly had gone grey and was melting in small cold trickles over the scales. She could stand. That was all she could do, and she could not do it for long.

Across the bare stone the bird sat propped on her spread wings, her head low and crooked on the long neck. Her talons rested on the rock half open and slack; they were not gripping anything any more. Only her red eyes were still working, fixed on Cinder, steady and cold.

*She's... waiting. For me to fall.*

Cinder set her jaw, and the sore jaw ached at the hinge. She did not mean to give her the chance.

She went. It was not a charge; it was hardly a walk. She lurched the last strides across the shelf on her heavy legs, the torn left wing dragging half open at her side and scraping the stone, her tail flame barely a glow, past the strip of old frost still lying in the shadow of the cliff. The bird saw her coming and could do nothing with it.

At the end of it Cinder did not stop. She let her legs go and toppled forward onto her.

— Glacia —

The dragon's weight came down on her chest and bore her over backwards onto the stone.

There was no blow in it, only weight, and the weight was enough. It settled full on the scorched, ruined place where the fire and the lightning had been, and her chest could not take even that. A raw scream tore up out of her, cracking, and her legs kicked wildly at nothing.

A forearm came down across her neck before the scream had finished, heavy and hot, laid right over the swollen bite. Her whole body shuddered and bucked under it, and sounds came out of her she could not stop: thin, broken, bell-like and wrong.

Then the claws. The dragon's hand found her left wing where it lay spread on the rock, the claw-furrowed one, and pressed it flat, three claws sinking through the ragged feathers into the bruises underneath. Every part of her jerked against that grip, and none of it moved the wing at all.

She hissed up into the long orange face above her, every limb straining a different way. Her talons scraped at the stone and found nothing.

*An edge. Find... the edge.*

There was no edge. There was the weight on her chest and the arm across her neck and the hand on her wing, and the hard cold stone behind her back.

— Glacia —

She stopped heaving, because heaving cost air, and looked up.

The dragon was not looking at her. Her eyes were half shut, the swollen one shut altogether, and her breath came in long, wheezing pulls that rattled at the top. The ruined left wing lay half open against her side, trembling. Smoke leaked from her nostrils in thin grey threads that the wind took away over the drop. She was not holding Glacia down so much as lying on her, all her weight let go, as though she had nowhere else to put it.

*She is as empty as I am. Nearly.*

Glacia breathed out, a long careful stream of white, as if pouring something. Her own limbs felt very far away. She had to think about each talon before it would move, and when it moved it moved only a little. She had stopped trying to use her chest at all; under the dragon's weight even the thought of it hurt.

The edges of the sky had begun to grey. The wind sounded further off than it had.

Each breath she took was a little shorter than the one before, pressed short against the weight above her.
