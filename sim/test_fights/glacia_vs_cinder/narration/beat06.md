— Cinder —

A shiver ran down Cinder from her horns to her tail, and it was not the cold.

Her head still sang along the left side where the bird's wing had cracked across it. She could feel the line of it, hot under the scales, every time she blinked. The bird had done that. The bird had dropped out of the sky past her like a falling icicle and *hit her in the face*.

The flame on her tail flared white.

She wheeled and found her. The bird was below and off to the left, beating her way back up from the dive, and Cinder took her in all at once, the way she was learning to: the right wing coming up slower than the left on every stroke, a drag in it, a sparkle of frost shaking loose. And the chest. The bird flew with her chest drawn in, the pale feathers there dark and singed, and every few strokes her whole body hitched round it as if she were guarding it from the wind.

*Fire got in there. Claws got in there. Nothing else has.*

She knew what she was going to do before she had decided to do it.

The heat came up out of her belly and she did not breathe it out. She let it out through her skin. It rose off her scales in a shimmer and then in a sheet, and then she was burning, all of her, wrapped head to tail in a roaring skin of fire that tore and streamed behind her in the wind. Down on the cliff, the stunted pine creaked as the gust swung round.

She folded her wings halfway and drove.

All the speed of the last pass was still in her, and she poured more after it. The bird saw her coming. The red eyes went wide, the good wing tilted to turn, and she was too slow by the length of a heartbeat.

Cinder hit her chest-first, horns down, with everything.

— Glacia —

A deep, slapping thud, and then the fire.

It went in where the claws had gone in and where the first flame had been, the same place, as if it had been told where to find her. The heat did not stop at the surface. Her feathers had nothing to hold it off with; it went through them as if through mist and sank into the bruise underneath and opened it up and filled it, and the whole front of her lit up white.

Her cry came out of her and stopped short, cut off by the force of the blow.

She was not flying any more. The dragon's weight had smashed her off her wingbeat, and the air that had been holding her up was simply not there. She dropped like a stone dropping off a ledge, wings flailing open too late, the sky wheeling, the cloud, the cliff, the grey stone of the shelf coming up very fast.

She hit it on her back.

The rock struck across the top of her shoulders first, hard and flat, and a grunt went out of her nose, *hnh*. Her right shoulder came down after it, the side that had been jarred by the claws, and smacked onto the stone with a hiss of pain. Her right leg cracked against the rock as her body bounced and settled, a glancing blow on the thigh, and then she was lying still, face up to the sky, her long tail flung out across the stone and her wings spread wide on either side of her.

She could not breathe.

Her chest heaved and nothing went in. Her beak opened on air that would not come. Her ribs jerked in short, shallow pulls against the ruin of the bruise, and each one dragged a sharp gasp out of her that was not a breath at all, only the shape of one.

*Breathe. Simply breathe.*

The pain in her chest crossed a line.

She felt it go over. Until now it had been a hurt, a thing she could set beside her and fly round. Now it was not beside her. It was the whole front of her body, a deep, scalding, pulsing pain that reached from her throat to her belly and would not be ignored. A thin, cracking wail, *aaah*, broke out of her beak and she bit it off, clamped her beak shut on the end of it, and her eyes squeezed tight and opened again, already hard.

*It does not get to have the rest of me.*

The rest of her was still hers. Her wings answered when she pressed them flat to the stone. Her talons curled. The ache along her shoulders and her thigh was only an ache. She lay on her back on the cold rock with the breath gone out of her and her chest on fire, and she held her head up off the ground and watched the sky.

— Cinder —

The fire peeled off her skin and blew away in tatters, and the recoil came back up into her.

She felt it in her right horn first, a hard, bright shock that snapped her head back and made her eyes stream, *hnk*, a clipped snort through her nose. Then her left shoulder, jarred by the force of her own body stopping, a dull bump that sucked a quick breath in through her nostrils. Small things. She shook them off.

She hung in the air above the shelf, wings beating, and looked down.

The bird lay where she had fallen, spread on her back on the bare stone like something pressed flat for drying. Her chest was the worst of it: the pale feathers there scorched dark and ragged, and the swelling under them rising out of its shape, and it was jerking, short and fast, too fast, a breath that was not getting in. Every time it jerked, the bird's beak opened a little wider.

But the head was up. The red eyes were open and watching her. The wings lay flat and still on the stone, not twitching, held there on purpose.

*She's holding it in,* Cinder thought. *She's shaking it off already. Don't let her.*

Her own breath was coming hard after the charge, smoke spilling from her nostrils with every gust of it, and her left wing ached on every downstroke and her head still sang. None of that mattered. She circled once over the bird, low, her shadow sliding across the stone and across the pale spread wings, and felt herself lean forward into the wind, every part of her wanting to go down to her.
