— Cinder —

Her heart was going hard and heavy in her chest, and she let it set the pace. Beat, beat. Stroke, stroke. A grinding, stubborn rhythm, because rhythm was easier than thinking about the left wing.

Snow was still caked on her horns. She did not shake it off; shaking her head was out of the question.

Below her the bird stood on the bare rock with her wings tight to her sides, and she moved as if she carried something breakable in her chest. Her head was held oddly still, the long bruised neck balancing it like a dish. The right wing was clamped in. The left was the only one she had.

*Then I'll take that one too.*

The left wing had to help with the climb, and Cinder winced every time it took its share, the deadened weight of it dragging like wet hide. She got high enough. At the top she tipped over, folded what she could fold, and came down like a dropped stone, faster than last time, the wind roaring past her horns and the shelf leaping up at her. She swung her hand out wide with the three claws open, wanting to tear rather than to knock, and aimed it across the bird's good wing.

— Glacia —

It was like being struck by something thrown off the cliff.

The claws raked across her left wing and slammed it flat against her side, the shoulder behind it jarring, and the same sweep went on across her chest, into the raw, bitten ruin there, and the whole length of the wing shuddered out to its tip with a raspy, tearing swish of feathers parting. She twisted away from the rake, far more than a blow like that should have moved her.

The scream came out of her with no breath behind it, *aaa-h*, high and cracked in the middle and then nothing, because her chest had emptied and would not fill.

But the claws had snagged. Three hooks deep in the feathers of her wing, and the dragon still coming past at the bottom of her dive, low and heavy and lopsided.

*There.*

Glacia did not think it through. She clamped the wing down hard on the scaled arm, trapping the hand in her own feathers, and wrenched round with the twist she was already making, all her weight thrown into it. The dragon's good wing beat once, enormous, and the frozen one could not answer. Glacia felt her come off the wind.

She let go. The dragon went past her and down.

The impact came up through Glacia's talons: a deep, flat *whump* of weight hitting stone, and a skitter of scree.

Glacia was still on her feet. Her eyes were streaming. Her chest jerked at the smallest movement of her own body and every jerk dragged a hoarse sound out of her, and the breath came in gasps that hitched at the top and stopped short. Her left wing hung slightly open, a sour, nagging ache gone right through it, and the shoulder above it, which had already taken the stone once today, throbbed in answer.

— Cinder —

She lay face-down on the rock with her chin in the grit and did not know, for a moment, which way was up.

The stone was cold against her belly and chest. Her breath had gone out of her in one hard gust and come back thin. Her left wing lay half spread on the rock beside her like something dropped there, and the head, which had hit nothing, pounded anyway, as if the jolt had shaken it loose inside. The tail flame behind her was a low, sputtering stub in a nest of frost.

*Pulled me down. She pulled me down.*

She did not try to rise. Not yet. She lay where she was and breathed, ragged and hot, and turned one blue eye up, very carefully, to where the bird stood a few strides off, her wings half open and trembling at the edges, her crest flat.

The taste of grit and old smoke lay sour on her tongue.
