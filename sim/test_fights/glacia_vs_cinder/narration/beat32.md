— Glacia —

The dragon's eyes left her for a moment, flicking down to her own frost-stung hands, and in that moment Glacia let herself sag.

They stood a dozen strides apart on the open shelf, the cliff rising sheer on one side of them and the drop on the other, where cloud tore past below the edge in long grey strands. The wind came across the rock in gusts and pushed at both of them. Between them the stone was still wet and dark where the steam had settled.

Glacia swayed. She could not stop it. Her thin legs shook at the joints and her talons shifted on the rock, finding grip, losing it, finding it again, and her whole body tilted slowly to one side and came back, like a reed in a current. Her wings had come down off her back without her asking and hung half open, the tips touching the stone. The right one was the worst. It throbbed from shoulder to tip and shed a fine sparkle of frost every time the wind touched it.

Her beak hung open. She dragged at the thin air, and it would not come. Each breath stopped against the crushed weight of her chest, and her throat, still hurting from the throw and the heat, rasped on the way in with a dry, thin whistle, *hhee*, that the wind took away. Her neck could barely hold her head. The bitten swelling there pulsed with every heartbeat, and the heartbeats were quick and shallow and uneven.

*There is less of me than there was. Considerably less.*

She tried to fold her wings and could not.

— Cinder —

Cinder looked up from her hands.

The claws were steady. The rest of her was not. Her head throbbed, a huge, swollen pounding between the horns that came and went with her pulse, and when she turned it the shelf slid a little before it caught up. The neck under it was stiff from skull to shoulders, hot and tight where the beak had sat in it and the blades had cut across it. The left wing lay along her back, burning. Under all of it the last of that white-hot rush was still running in her, keeping her heart quick and her eyes clear, but she could feel it thinning, like a fire that has eaten its kindling.

Her breath came hard. Smoke threaded from her nostrils. Her tail flame was still high, but it flickered now in the gusts, white at the core and ragged at the edges.

And the bird was swaying.

Cinder watched her tilt and come back, tilt and come back, the wings trailing on the rock, the beak open. She watched the scorched chest heave and stop short. She watched the talons slip and grip.

*Look at that. She's just about out on her feet.*

Cinder flexed her right hand once, slowly, and felt the strength still in it. She shifted her weight to her front foot and kept her eyes on the bird.
