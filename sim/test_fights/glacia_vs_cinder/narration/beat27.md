— Glacia —

The rake across her chest was still there. It had not faded; it had only stopped arriving, and every breath went in past it and caught.

The dragon lay a few strides off, face-down on the stone, her left wing sprawled out beside her across the rock. Her eyes were open. One of them was turned up and fixed on Glacia, blue and steady, and that was the worrying part. She was not stunned. She was resting.

*Now. Before she remembers how to stand.*

Glacia went across the stone in three short, hopping steps, her wings half lifted for balance, the right one clamped shut and useless and the left one aching where the claws had gone through it. Somewhere at the foot of the cliff a stunted pine creaked in the wind. She did not look at it.

She came down on the dragon's back.

She had meant to come down hard, with her chest, all of her. Her chest would not let her. The moment it touched the warm scales a white flare went through it and she pulled it back, and so she found the one way to brace that spared it: her body low across the upper back, her weight driven down through her legs and her belly instead, the breast held a finger's width off the scales. It spared her chest and nothing else. Her belly ached against the dragon's spine. Her neck shook with the effort of holding her head.

She put one foot on the frozen left wing where it lay against the stone, the talons spread over the stiff, swollen membrane, and leaned. Then she lowered her head along the dragon's long neck and set the point of her beak into the back of it, just below the horns.

She shifted her weight by tiny, careful amounts until it was all where she wanted it.

— Cinder —

The weight came down across her shoulders and pushed her into the rock, and her chest flattened on the cold stone and the breath went out through her teeth in a short, hard *hff*. That much was nothing; her back had barely been touched all fight and it could carry a bird. She shoved up against it once, hard, from the shoulders. It did not move.

The beak was something else. It sat in the back of her neck, right on the place the blizzard had frozen through, and pressed: a hot, tight circle of hurt in the middle of all that cold, and the old ache in the long muscles there rose to meet it and became one thing. She arched against it with a ragged, grating sound, *rrhh*, dragged up out of her chest, and the beak followed her and stayed.

Then the talons closed on her left wing.

It came to her dull and far off, like pain heard through a wall, the wing still half dead from the cold. That was almost worse. She knew what was under the dullness. She felt the talons spread, felt the membrane pressed down on the rock so hard she could feel every pebble under it, and she strained against them once and felt the whole frozen span answer with a slow, deep throb that went up into her shoulder and stopped her.

She lay still.

*She has to breathe sometime.*

She pulled air in big, deliberate draughts through her nostrils, smoke curling out across the grit by her face, and let her body go slack under the bird as if she were resting. Behind her the tail flame burned low and steady. Her eyes stayed open. She was rationing every breath, and she knew it, and she hated knowing it.

The bird's breathing rasped above her, thin and fast, and when it caught, the silence over the shelf was huge.
