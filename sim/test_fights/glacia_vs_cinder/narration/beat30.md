— Glacia —

The wind brought the smell of her up: scorched scale, old smoke, and something sharper under it now, sour with effort.

Glacia hung above the shelf on the cold, thin air and looked down. The dragon was still hunched where the blades had left her, her feet planted wide, her left wing hanging at an odd angle against her side as if she had given up holding it right. Her jaw hung open. A thread of spit swung from it in the wind and she had stopped noticing. Her head was low and stiff and very still, the way an animal holds a thing it cannot bear to have touched.

*I was right about the head. She has nothing left to cover it with.*

Something close to pleasure moved in Glacia, and she put it away.

She kept her chest turned a little from the wind, sparing it, and folded. Both wings came in against her body and she dropped, steep, so steep that the shelf came up at her like a thrown stone and there was no time for anything below to see her coming until she was there. At the bottom of the drop she opened only the left wing, its leading edge rimed hard and white with frost, and swung it at the bowed orange head.

— Cinder —

She saw it at the last instant and her whole body flinched, the head snatched down and in, too late.

The wing hit her across the skull with a dull, heavy thud.

Her head rocked back on her neck, far further than it should have, her chin going one way and her eyes the other, though her feet never left the stone. Then it arrived: the head, the same head, the swollen frost-burned place between the horns struck again on top of everything, and the pain had nowhere left to go but out. A broken bellow came up out of her and ran out of air halfway, *ahhh-hhk*, and she could not stop it and could not make it into a roar. She buckled. Her knees went soft and dipped and only pride held her off the ground.

The shelf swam. The cliff slid one way and the cloud the other, and she could not make either of them stand still.

She breathed around it in short, careful pulls. Her hands opened and closed on nothing. A shudder went through her, beginning at the head and running out to the tip of her tail, and a few heartbeats later another.

There was nothing in her head now to fight with. Only the hurt, filling it from the inside.

Then her feet, which had been somewhere behind her for a while, came back under her. She planted them. The freeze that had locked her since the last blow let go, all at once, and she could move again.

And under the pain, something else rose.

It came up from her belly like heat from a banked fire, hard and fierce and white. Her heart slammed. Her eyes, swimming a moment ago, sharpened round the edges, though the shelf still would not settle. The pain did not go anywhere; it simply fell back behind something louder. Her tail flame, which had been a guttering stub, stood up tall and roared white-hot in the wind.

*Ow. All right. Ow. Keep moving.*

— Glacia —

Glacia had come down to land, wings spread wide to stop her, and her talons met the stone a few strides off. The jolt went up into her chest. She stood and watched.

There was a looseness in the way the dragon held herself, and she read it like tracks in snow. Her eyes were glazed. When her head turned, it turned late, hunting for Glacia a little to the left of where she was. Every flinch went onto a slow, careful tally.

Then the tail flame flared, and Glacia's tally stopped.

She placed each talon with care, and waited, and watched the dragon square herself on the rock with her guard coming back up and her eyes burning blue through the haze.
