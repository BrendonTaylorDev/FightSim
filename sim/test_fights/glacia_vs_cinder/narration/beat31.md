— Cinder —

*Where is she?*

Her head was ringing like a struck rock and the shelf would not hold still. There were two cliffs, then one, then one and a half. The bird was a pale smear somewhere ahead, blurred at the edges, and Cinder could not trust her eyes to tell her where.

But she could feel the cold. It came off the bird the way heat comes off a fire, a steady breath of frost on her face and on the scales of her chest, stronger from a little to the right. She turned toward it. There: bent nearly double over that ruined chest, the right wing clamped tight to her side, everything being done with the other one.

*She's still standing. Fine. Then burn her where she stands.*

The heat that had flooded up through her a moment ago was still there, fierce and white, and she leaned on it. She led with her right side, the left wing held back and folded tight. She planted her feet wide on the stone, dug her claws in, filled her chest until the old cold-burn in it ached, and held the fire in her throat a heartbeat longer than was comfortable, to make it count. Smoke poured from her nostrils. Her tail flame stood up white.

The air between them shivered. Then she opened her jaws and the Flamethrower came out of her with a deep, rumbling roar, a thick orange stream aimed at the cold, steady as a pointing claw.

— Glacia —

She did not try to move. There was no time and nothing to move with.

She lifted her head instead and the cold went out of her beak in a single hard, glittering shard, flung straight down the line of the fire with a clear, ringing cry.

They met halfway.

The Ice Shard went into the stream and the stream went into the Ice Shard and for an instant neither of them gave. Then they burst. A thunderclap of steam blew out in every direction, white and roaring, and the shock of it hit both of them in the face like a slammed door. Glacia rocked back on her talons. A hot, wet mist rolled over her, warm for a breath and then cold again on her feathers, and through it she heard the dragon grunt as the same blast reached her. Nothing else came. The fire was gone and the shard was gone, and the steam tore away over the edge of the shelf in long rags. At the foot of the cliff a little scree slid and settled.

Then the brightness left her.

It drained out of her the way warmth leaves a stone at nightfall, all at once and without asking, and every hurt she owned came back at its full size. The chest first, a deep crushing ache under the bitten feathers that took her breath and kept it. The neck. The burned right wing, throbbing from shoulder to tip. The left shoulder, where the dragon had thrown her off. Her legs shook at the joints. Her long tail pressed itself flat to the stone.

Across the shelf the steam cleared, and the dragon's blue eyes came through it, single and sharp again, and found her at once.

Glacia stood very still and held the hurt inside her, all of it, and did not let it show more than it already did.
