— Cinder —

The cold was still in her left wing. Every downstroke drove it deeper into the joint, a stiff, frost-burned ache that made the whole wing feel a size too heavy, and the membrane bucked against the wind where the raw patches had stiffened.

She flew on it anyway.

Below and ahead of her, the bird had stopped beating. Glacia lay out flat on the updraft off the cliff, wings spread wide, turning in a long, slow circle above the shelf, and from up here Cinder could read all of her. The right wing was held too stiff, the feathers standing up over the place Cinder's jaws had closed on it, a sparkle of loose frost shaking off its trailing edge on every gust. The chest was drawn in under her. The long head came round each time the circle brought her face to the cliff, and the deep red eyes swept the sky and went on.

*Resting. Thinks she's out of reach.*

Cinder let her breath out through her nostrils, slowly, and two threads of smoke tore away on the wind.

She did not follow the circle. She cut across it.

Two hard beats on the good wing, one on the bad, a hiss between her fangs at the third, and she was coming in from the inside of the turn, low and fast, the cloud sliding by far below. Speed piled up behind her from the last pass and the one before it. Her right fist closed. The air round it began to crackle, a fizzing sting that lifted the scales along her forearm and lit her claws blue-white, and the smell of a storm came up off her own hand.

The long blue neck swung into her path.

She threw the punch with everything the dive had given her.

— Glacia —

There was a sharp hiss of sparks, and then the world went white.

The fist hit the side of her neck, and in the same instant the charge leapt from it into her throat and up across her left eye. It did not stop at the feathers the way fire did. It went in. It ran through the cold in her like a crack through ice, stiffening everything it touched, and she understood with a terrible calm that her body had no defence against this at all.

The cry, *ah*, came out of her clear and high and broke in half.

Her head snapped down on its own, trying to fold over her throat. Her throat seized on a swallow that would not go down; a grunt through her nostrils, *hnh*, was all that got past it. Her left eye filled with a narrow white line of ache where the sparks had skidded across it, and she screwed it shut.

Her wings missed a beat.

The right one could not take her weight. It folded wrong, the burned joint flaring, and the air simply let go of her. The shelf swung up out of the cloud and grew, grey and huge, and she could not level out in time.

She hit the bare rock on her back.

The stone took her across the top of the back with a flat smack that drove a huff from her, more jolt than hurt. Her right shoulder came down next on the rough rock, and that one she felt: a low sound in her throat, *mmh*, the shoulder hunching up of its own accord. Her right thigh struck last and jerked, her talons clenching on nothing, and she drew a short hiss and lay still.

Sky. Cold, empty sky above her, and the wind going over.

Her neck was a rigid post of pain from shoulder to jaw. She turned her head a finger's width and the whole length of it lit up, and she stopped, and breathed, and the breath steamed above her beak and blew away.

*It reached me. Up there, it reached me.*

— Cinder —

She came round in a wide, labouring turn above the shelf and looked down.

The bird lay face-up on the stone where she had fallen, wings half open beside her, the long tail lying in a loose blue curl that had stopped rippling. Her neck was held dead straight. When she breathed, her throat worked and caught, and once a thin sound got out of her beak before she shut it in. The left eye stayed closed.

Cinder's own breath went out of her, and she had to take it again. Her left wing was burning now, worse with every stroke, and she spread her good one wider to carry more of her.

*That's one. I'll need more than one.*

The red eye that was open found her, steady, cold, not beaten. Frost had already started to spread across the feathers of the bird's chest.

Cinder hung there on the wind, smoke curling from her nostrils, and watched the open eye watch her.

*If I go down to her now, she'll—*
