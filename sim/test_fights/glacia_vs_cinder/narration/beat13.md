— Glacia —

*Up.*

Not yet. The stone was hard and cold under her back, and her neck would not let her lift her head more than a finger's width, so she lay there and looked at the sky instead, and the sky had the dragon in it.

Cinder hung above the shelf, a little higher than before. The left wing was beating wrong. It came down late and shallow, and every few strokes the dragon let it hang at a crooked angle while the good wing did the work.

The electric sting was still in her throat. Every swallow found it.

*Hit, then gone. Hit, then gone.*

She held still. Utterly still, the frost creeping out across the feathers of her breast, her tail lying quiet on the rock. Then she swept her left wing up off the stone in one long stroke, and the twist of it pulled at her chest and dragged a grunt out of her, and the air in front of the wing began to shimmer and hum. It left her in pale blades, three, four of them, thin as glass and singing, climbing straight at the cream-yellow belly above her.

The dragon's jaws opened.

Fire came down to meet them. A roaring orange column, Flamethrower, poured out of Cinder's throat, and the blades went into it and the two struck each other in the open air between them with a flat, booming crack. For a long breath they held, the wind-blades bending, the fire spreading out round them in a burning disc that lit the whole shelf orange. The heat of it reached her on her back on the stone.

Then the fire won.

It drove her own blades back down at her, wrapped in heat. They came in high across her body. The first went into her chest, where the feathers were already raked and raw, and the cry was out of her before she could stop it, *ah*, clear and high and bitten off at once. Her chest jerked in on itself under the wing. A second skimmed the beak, a hot jolt along its edge that drew a grunt through her nostrils, *hnh*, and made her flinch her head aside; the last cracked across the top of her head, and she sucked a quick breath in, *hk*, and blinked.

Then it was over. Every breath after that came with a thin wheeze in it.

She would not lie here.

She beat her good wing against the bare rock and pushed. Her talons skidded on the grit, and she came down hard on her back again, the jolt going up between her shoulders. A low sound in her throat, *mmh*. She breathed.

Again. She got her wing tip braced, heaved, came almost all the way up, and her talons slid out from under her on the loose dust and she went down onto her upper back with a short hiss, holding it stiff and careful.

The third time she did not let herself think. She drove with her left wing and pushed off with her legs, and the stone held, and she was up on her talons, swaying, the right wing clamped tight to her side and shedding frost, her sides heaving and the wheeze going in and out of her.

— Cinder —

The fire had cost her. She tasted smoke at the back of her throat and her chest burned with the effort of it.

The left wing had cost her more.

Holding steady in the air to pour out the fire, she had had to work it, and now it would not work any more. She held it stiff and flat, gliding on it, and the shelf came slowly up towards her as she lost height. The frost-burned patches on the membrane stung every time the wind pushed on them. A growl ground out between her fangs and she let it.

Below her the bird had fought her way up on the third try. Not straight: head held rigid, the right wing pressed against her, the chest heaving with a wheeze Cinder could hear even up here over the wind. But on her feet, and the red eyes were already lifting to find her.

*Tough. Fine. So am I.*

Her shadow lay across the shelf between them, broad-winged and lopsided, one wing spread and the other held crooked. It slid slowly over the stone towards the bird as she sank.
