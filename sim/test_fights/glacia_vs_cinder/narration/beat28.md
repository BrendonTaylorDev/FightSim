— Glacia —

She held on.

Her weight lay across the dragon's upper back, driven down through her legs and belly, and she felt the big shoulders under her grind against it, scales shifting over muscle like stones in a sack. Her beak sat in the back of the long neck, and every time the dragon breathed the neck swelled against the point and Glacia pressed it back. The talons of one foot stayed spread on the frozen wing, holding it flat to the stone.

It cost her. Her own breath came thin and fast and would not go deep; the chest she was holding off the scales shook with the strain of being held off, and the bitten neck trembled behind her head. Far out over the cloud a hawk turned in a slow circle, and she found she was envying it.

Under her the dragon's claws were dragging at the rock. Short, hard scrapes, again and again, the hands working at the stone. Her breathing had changed too: deep, slow, deliberate pulls through the nostrils, smoke curling out over the grit.

*She is gathering. Not resting.*

Glacia pressed harder with the beak. The dragon's whole body went still.

— Cinder —

Then she moved.

She threw her hips one way and her shoulders the other, a corkscrew from the tail up, all the strength left in her heavy legs going into it at once. The beak scraped off the back of her neck. The weight slid. As she came round onto her side she drove her right shoulder up and back with everything behind it, and felt it slam into the bird's left shoulder, hard bone to hard bone.

The bird came off her with a sharp cry, *ah*, the breath rushing out of her, and staggered back across the stone with her wings flung out to catch herself. She did not fall.

Cinder was loose.

She planted both hands on the bare rock and pushed. Her arms locked, her chest came up off the stone, and the grit under her right hand slid and she went straight back down on her front. The drop jarred her upper back where the bird's weight had been and she growled, low and short, *grr*, through clenched fangs.

Again. She got her knees under her this time, got almost all the way up, and then the loose stone skidded under her heel and she went down again, hard, onto her chest, and the same place across her shoulders took it. Her shoulders hunched up round it and a hiss came out of the side of her jaw.

The third time she did not try to be careful. She heaved, one ugly lurch from the ground to her feet, nearly overbalanced forward, swung her tail out behind her and caught it.

She was standing.

And the left wing woke up.

It came back into her all at once, the deadness torn off it like a sheet. Every place the cold had frozen and the talons had pressed was suddenly there, the whole span of it blazing from shoulder to tip, and the bellow broke out of her before she had any say in it, huge and raw and cracking into a hoarse, shaking growl. The wing jerked half open and clamped shut again against her side. Her right hand flew up to clutch the joint at her shoulder. She stood with her knees bent and her whole frame shaking from the shoulders down, her tail flame shrunk to a sickly, guttering flicker.

*There. Now it's real again. Good.*

— Glacia —

Glacia had kept her feet. She stood a step further off than she needed to, her left shoulder throbbing in a deep, swelling ache that made her clamp her beak shut against it, and watched.

The dragon's head turned to find her, slower than before. She was not hiding anything now. Every hurt was there to read at once: the wing, the head held stiff, the neck, the dimmed flame.

Something moved in Glacia then.

It began low in her body, under the ruined chest, and came up cold and bright, like a clean wind straight off a glacier. Her heart, which had been labouring, began to beat fast and hard and easy. The pain was still there, all of it; it simply stood further back. Frost raced out across her feathers from the breast to the wingtips and her long tail rippled once along its whole length.

Her breath went out of her slow, and steamed.
