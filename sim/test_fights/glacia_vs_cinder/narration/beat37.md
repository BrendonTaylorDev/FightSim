— Cinder —

She heard the bird before she looked for her: the dry, uneven rustle of wings that were no longer beating together, somewhere above and to the right, torn about by the wind.

Cinder found her with the eye that still opened. Glacia hung over the shelf like a kite on a broken string, the right wing barely lifting, the long tail held stiff and close, her body tipped so that the scorched chest was turned in and away. Every stroke the bird made, a little frost shook off her and blew away to nothing.

Cinder's own left wing came up late again, and the whole of her slewed sideways in the air. The ache in the root of it had stopped being an ache. It was a hot iron bar laid across her back, and every beat pressed it in deeper.

*One more. Make it count.*

She had very little left. She could feel exactly how little, the way she could feel the cold in her tail flame: a low, guttering thing. So she gathered all of it. She dragged it up from under the pain in her head and her neck and her wing, from her heavy legs and the bottom of her lungs, and she put it into her jaws. The flame on her tail flared white for one breath. Fire licked up round her fangs and over her lips until her whole mouth glowed.

She chose the blow with care, because she could not afford to waste it.

Then she beat upward with everything at once, the good wing and the ruined one together, and climbed at the bird from below.

Glacia saw her. The long neck snatched back, and the chest curled in behind the left wing, too late and too slow. Cinder saw which part the bird was trying to save, and went for it. She tilted her head as they met and closed her burning jaws on the long blue neck.

— Glacia —

The fangs met with a hard click, and the fire went in with them.

It did not stop at the feathers. Cold was the whole of her, all the way down, and the heat simply walked into it as if nothing were there. It poured into her neck where the old bite was already swollen, and the same rush scorched across her throat beside it, and for an instant there was nothing in the world but that heat and the jaws behind it.

It was not the strength of it that frightened her but how easily it had gone in.

Her cry came out raw and cracked in the middle: *Hhaaah.* It broke into a choked, airless gulp as her throat seized round the burn. Then the jaws were gone, the dragon was past and turning, and her right wing folded under her in the middle of a stroke.

The sky let go of her.

She fell with her wings half open, turning, the cliff and the cloud and the cliff again. The bare rock of the shelf took her across the upper back, hard. Her left shoulder hit a moment later, the shoulder that was already bruised, and drew a short, sharp cry out of her, *Ah*. Her left thigh hit last and drew less. She skidded a body's length on the grit and stopped.

She lay on her back with her face to the sky.

The hurt in her neck went quiet for a breath. She did not trust the quiet, and she was right not to: it came back pounding, a thin cry inside her on every beat of her heart, and the burn in her throat wrapped round it and squeezed. She tried to lift her head to look at herself. The neck would not let her. A thin shriek scraped out of her beak, and she let it, because holding it in cost breath she did not have.

Her chest would not breathe for her. She breathed with her shoulders instead, short and shallow, the left one flaring every time it moved. Her eyes streamed. Frost spread in a pale, uneven crust over the feathers of her neck and could not cool what was under them.

*Lie still.*

High above, the dragon's wings laboured against the wind, slower and heavier than before.

Glacia lay still, because she chose to.
