— Glacia —

Three strides of bare rock lay between them, grey and gritted, the wind combing loose snow across it. The dragon had not stepped back after the blow. She stood where she had struck from, too close, breathing smoke.

*Her guard came down when she landed. The left side. For one heartbeat it was all open.*

Glacia saw it again, coldly, and filed it away.

She could not fly from here. The right wing hung clamped against her, aching to the shoulder, and every breath came short and steaming. So she would stand on her two thin legs on the stone and fight from there.

Cinder held her left wing folded tight against her body, the torn membrane trembling with each breath, and fought from the right. Her head was carried tilted, the side where the frost had bitten her first turned a little away. Above it the horns were still rimed white.

Something narrowed in Glacia's eyes. Hunger, cold and precise.

She turned her body a quarter, the numb chest away from the dragon. The strangeness in it sat there, dead and heavy. She ignored it.

She lowered her head.

Her eyes narrowed to fix the aim, and everything else in her went still. The cold gathered at the back of her throat, the air round her beak paled and crackled, and frost raced out across the feathers of her face. It started as a thin white thread of light between her open beak and the orange throat. She fed it with the next breath, and the next, and it thickened into a beam as white as the cloud below the shelf, and drove.

— Cinder —

There was a creak, like ice forming on a pond, and it came from her own scales.

The beam took her across the neck and the side of the head together. The neck was a hard, gripping cold that clamped round her throat and squeezed; her breath caught on a choked cough and a groan came up from deep in her chest, *nnngh*. Her chin jerked down to cover her throat a heartbeat too late.

The head was the one that hurt.

The cold went into the place where the bird's wing had already cracked across her skull and the wind had frozen her horns, and it did not stop at the scales. A bark of pain, *ahk*, was knocked out of her and cut short. She flinched in on herself, shoulders drawn up tight, her knees dipping, every muscle round the place gone rigid, and then she was square again, feet planted where they had been.

Frost crusted the side of her face. She snorted it out of her nostrils with a puff of smoke and tossed her head once, and the toss sent a twisting ache wringing through her skull that did not let go when she breathed.

She held her head very still after that, as if it might spill.

Her strength was all still there, but the pain stood between her and it.

— Cinder —

She blinked the frost out of her right eye and looked at the bird.

Glacia had not moved. She stood with her beak still parted, a last thread of white steam trailing from it, her chest turned away and the right wing pressed tight to her side. Her breathing was quick and shallow, sides working, the wheeze in it louder now. That beam had cost her. But the red eyes were fixed on Cinder's head. On the frosted side. Already looking for the same place again.

*She always resets her stance first. Then she looks where she wants to hit.*

Her neck ached in a hard ring round her throat that tightened when she raised her chin. The head throbbed with her heartbeat.

The wind gusted, dropped, gusted again.

She drew a quick sniff of the air, cold and frost and singed feathers, and her breathing settled. She still had more than the bird had. She was nearly sure of it.

Across the stone, the long blue tail stirred once against the rock, and was still.
