— Glacia —

The dragon's weight hung below her and a little behind, heavy and lopsided on the wind, and Glacia could feel it there without turning her head, the way one feels a stone in a pocket.

She stopped beating.

It was the hardest thing she had done all fight. Every part of her wanted to keep the wings going, to stay high, to stay ahead; but the right wing would not take many more strokes, and her breath was gone, and the cliff was breathing up beside her in a long, steady, rising push of air. She spread both wings flat on it, as wide as they would go, and let it hold her.

The updraft took her weight. She tilted, and began to glide in a wide, slow circle out over the shelf and back toward the cliff, out and back again.

It did not spare the wing. Nothing would now. Holding it out flat against the wind was its own kind of work, a long, steady pull through the swollen joint and down the whole length of the bone, and the burning there did not ease when she stopped flapping; it only changed. It settled. It sank into the wing until there was no part of it that did not hurt, deep and hot and throbbing in time with her heart, and the feathers over the bite stood up stiff and blackened and would not lie flat. She held the wing out anyway, rigid, trembling at the tip, a sparkle of loose frost shaking off it with every gust. A low, thin sound escaped through her beak on one of them, *hnn*, and she closed her beak and did not let the next one out.

She breathed. Short at first, round the ruined front of her chest. Then a little longer. Then, slowly, almost a whole breath, and the cold air went down into her and she felt her head clear.

Below the edge of the shelf the cloud tore past in long, ragged strips.

*She cannot keep circling on that wing either,* Glacia thought, watching the dragon. *Neither of us can stay up here for ever. Let her come to me.*

— Cinder —

Cinder watched the bird stop flapping and float.

She could not do that. Her left wing would not lie flat; it would barely open. So she beat, and every stroke of it came slow and short and tore all down the frozen membrane like cloth ripping, and she drifted sideways with each one and had to correct with the right wing and drift again. A bellow kept wanting to come out of her. She ground it down to a growl between her fangs on every downstroke, *grrh*, and smoke spilled from her nostrils.

The bird circled above her, holding her bitten wing out stiff, its tip shaking. Cinder could see the effort in it. Every gust that rocked her, the bird's head dipped and her beak opened and closed. She was gritting through it too. She was only better at hiding it.

The cold, at least, was leaving. Cinder felt it go: the frost in the creases of her scales turning to water and running off, the deep shiver in her belly loosening, her claws coming back to her one by one, warm and quick. Her tail flame lifted behind her, steadied, and burned a little higher, orange again all the way to the tip.

She flexed her hands, both of them, three claws each, and felt them answer.

*She's gliding the same circle. Out over the drop and back to the cliff. Out and back.*

Cinder watched the circle come round. She watched where it crossed nearest the cliff, where the bird had to turn, slowest, her wings tilted, her long neck stretched toward the stone.

That was where she would be.
