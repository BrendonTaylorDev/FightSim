— Glacia —

Not much left. She could feel the bottom of it, the way you feel the bottom of a breath: the cold in her ran thin now, and every move drew on what was left.

Her right wing hung at her side, dead and heavy. She could not feel it at all. That was one thing she would not have to spare, then.

Cinder stood a few strides off, the left wing hugged tight against her ribs, squinting, though there was no glare on the shelf, only flat grey light and frost in the shadow of the cliff. Her head was tilted, the side the beam had frozen held low and away.

A moment ago, when she had shifted her weight, her right foot had slid a little on the grit. Glacia had seen it.

*Every time I move, she curls round that head.*

Something hard and bright came up in her, and her weight came forward onto her talons before she had decided anything.

She could not bend into it properly; her chest would not fold. So she did not try. She turned instead, swinging her whole body round from the hips with her neck held rigid, and the left wing snapped out from her side with no wind-up at all, its leading edge gone hard as ice. She braked and swung it into the space in front of her, at the frosted side of the horned head.

It was too much. She knew it as she threw it. Too far, too late, all her weight behind it and nothing left to catch it with.

— Cinder —

She saw it coming and turned her head away without meaning to, a fraction too late.

The crack of it came first.

Then the edge of the wing, cold and hard as a frozen branch, slammed across the side of her head over the eye, and in the same instant the end of it jarred against her jaw. Her head rocked back with it. Her feet stayed where they were.

The head had already been bad. Now it was something else.

The cold edge landed exactly on the old place, on top of the frozen scales the beam had left there, and on top of the very first blow the bird had struck her, high above the shelf at the start of everything, and the pain went over a line. A sharp bark of it broke out of her, *ahk*, high and broken, before she could stop it. She snorted hard, a sneeze-like burst of smoke, and tossed her head as if she could throw the hurt out of it, and that only made it worse.

The jaw was nothing. A clipped snort through her nostrils, *hnk*, the muscles there jumping once.

Her skull pounded so hard the edges of everything throbbed with it.

— Cinder —

She stood with her head low and her eyes screwed half shut.

The pain sat in her skull like a hammer still coming down, again and again, on the same spot above her eye. She could feel the frost-stiff scales there and, under them, a hot, tight swelling rising. Her right hand kept opening and closing at her side, the claws slow and stiff, as if it wanted something to grip and could not find it. She made it stop.

Her breath came out of her hard, with a little rasping sound in every one, and she held it to a rhythm anyway. In. Out. Her own. She ground her fangs together on the sound that wanted to come with it, until it died.

The bird was still in front of her. Lurching. She had thrown everything into that blow and it had carried her round, off balance, her talons scrabbling for grip on the grit and the dead right wing swinging out from her side as she fought to stand square again.

*You'll pay for that. I'll put you down and keep you there.*

Cinder lowered her horns, and ground a breath out through her nostrils, and no more.

The wind dropped. For a moment there was nothing on the shelf but the two of them breathing, and the hiss of her tail flame, and under it a long, cold quiet that was full of the next thing.
