— Nocturne —

She went still. The left foreleg throbbed where the small jaws had closed on it, a row of dents in the white fur just above the elbow spike, and she did not look down at it. Pride kept her from that. She made herself so quiet that she could hear the water ticking from her own drying ruff onto the flagstones.

The otter was still close, a body's length off, in the green shade between two standing pillars. Too close for the horn. Close enough for a paw.

When the orange body twisted, it winced. Not much: a tightening around the cream belly where the dark claws had raked it and the black pulse had broken over it after, swallowed at once. She hid it well, and that told Nocturne where it was.

*Hit, then gone. Hit, then gone.*

The sickle tail would not keep still behind her. It flicked against the base of a pillar, flicked again, and she let it, because the rest of her had to be stone. The air smelled of moss and warm wet rock, and of the otter, river-sour and close.

The left shoulder, where the little paw had chopped at the start, still ached deep in the joint. So it would be the right paw, the one the teeth had not touched.

She shifted her weight onto the left foreleg and let it take her, bitten or not. The stroke started at the hip. The whole long white body uncoiled behind the right paw, her flank turning, her shoulder driving it, the paw itself coming last and fastest, swung low and across.

She kept her claws close together, aiming for one deep line rather than a spread.

The otter's left thigh was right there, bare orange fur over a strong leg.

— Ripples —

She heard it before she felt it: the snick of claws catching in her fur and pulling free.

Then the thigh caught up. Three hot lines laid down the outside of her left leg in one stroke, and the same blow jarred up into the point of her hip, a dull knock in the joint, as if someone had rapped it with a knuckle. Her body twisted right on its own, away from the rake as it went across her, and before she had decided anything she was square again, feet where they had been.

*Hff.* It came out of her nose, short and cross.

The fur on her thigh was parted in three neat lines, and the leg under them gave a fine shiver, a tremble just under the skin that ran from the knee to the hip and stopped. A light flush of warmth followed it, the kind a slap leaves. She let her weight off the leg for one step's worth of time and put it back, and it held. Of course it held.

A brief sting crossed her left thigh and was gone before she had finished noticing it.

What stayed was the hip. Not pain, quite. An offended stiffness in the joint, the kind that would remind her it was there each time she pushed off. She tested it with a shift of her feet, and a small bark came out of her, *hah*, more insult than hurt.

Her stomach was the real trouble, and the twist had woken it. The scored belly pulled when she turned, a deep sullen pressure under the cream fur where the claws and the black ring had both found her, and that she could not shrug off. She tucked her chin, rounded her shoulders over it, and breathed through her teeth until it settled to a steady ache.

For an instant her small black eyes had gone wide. Now they went flat.

*A scratch. That's all she gets for that.*

She watched the white face. The red eyes had not left her. Nocturne had taken the paw back already, set it down, and stood on it the way she stood on everything, as if the floor were hers. Behind her the bitten foreleg was planted too, and Ripples saw her shift her weight off it, slightly, when she thought nobody was looking.

One long stride of cracked flagstone lay between them now, a shallow puddle in the middle of it holding a piece of the green sky. Ripples kept her feet on her own side of it, and waited.
