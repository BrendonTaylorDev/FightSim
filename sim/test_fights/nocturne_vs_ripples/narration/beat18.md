— Ripples —

Her body had got heavy. Not tired, she told herself; heavy, as if the river she came from had soaked into her and stayed. Every step carried the bruise in her chest along with it, and she kept her chest turned half away from the white shape, the shoulder forward, the sore middle behind it.

The burn on her head was going out. She felt it go, the hot hand over her skull lifting a finger at a time, until there was only a warm itch between her ears and then not even that. She shook her head, quick, and it was gone.

Nocturne was angry now, and anger had made her watchful, not careless. But Ripples had seen things. When she feinted with a shoulder toward the right side, the white body flinched from it, even from a feint, the ribs drawing in before anything came near them. And the left shoulder came up slower than the right, and not as high.

*Quick on the right. Slow on the left. Go left.*

She went in low on the left, under the line of the horn, where the slow shoulder could not get across to stop her. Her twin tails whirled behind her and threw her forward the last stride. The white neck was in front of her, the thick ruff round it, the dark face turning above it too late.

She took hold as if she meant never to let go again.

Both arms went round the neck under the jaw, webbed paws locking on each other through the ruff, and she pulled herself in close and squeezed.

— Nocturne —

The otter was on her neck.

Small arms clamped round it under her jaw, and the squeeze sank in, a hard band that dug a trench through the thick white ruff and pressed on the soft front of her throat until the fur bunched up round the dip. The muscle under it quivered. It did not hurt much. It was the closeness that was wrong: the otter's face against her ruff, river-smelling, her breath hot on the fur, her heartbeat knocking against Nocturne's own throat.

She pushed at it. She shoved her head forward and down, setting her forepaws, and the little body came with her and did not let go. Her breath stayed steady. She made sure of that.

*Clinging thing.*

She could not spring away now. Neither could the otter. They stood locked together in the middle of the cracked floor, one white body and one orange one, too close for anything but what came next.

— Ripples —

She hung on and watched from a hand's width away.

The fur all along Nocturne's spine had stood up and would not settle, a white ridge from the nape to the base of the sickle tail. Under Ripples' arms the muscles of the neck jumped and twitched where she was squeezing, and something in her went very calm.

*Remember this. Remember exactly where.*

The white body took one more breath than it needed before it moved, each time, a long one in past the bad ribs, and Ripples felt each of them through her arms. She was annoyed it was not more. The squeeze had gone in short of what she wanted, a trench in the fur and not much under it.

Her own chest pressed against the white ruff and ached for it. Her stomach ached. She held on anyway.

Over by the wall a vine swayed, though nothing had touched it. The air in the hall had started to cool.
