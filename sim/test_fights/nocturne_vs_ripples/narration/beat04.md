— Ripples —

Her heels were still going backward. The step the claws had knocked her into had not quite finished with her, and she let it carry her, let her weight roll off her heels and up onto the balls of her feet, small and quick, her belly stinging with every breath.

The white one had come down off her rear onto all four paws. She came down carefully. Ripples watched the left shoulder take the weight: it went down slower than the right, and it did not lift as high when she settled. There was a hitch in it now. A small one. Hers.

The breath that came out of the white one as she landed was short and clipped at the end, through the nose, the way a breath goes when somebody would rather nobody heard it.

*That one's still talking to her. Good.*

Too close to chop again. Too far, with her feet still going the wrong way, to do anything clever with her arms. So she used the one thing that did not need her feet.

She dropped low, sudden, knees bent under her, so that her head went below the line of the white jaw. Her arms came together in front of her chest, webbed paws cupped toward each other with a hand's width of air between them, and the water came. It came up out of her the way it always came, from somewhere under her ribs, cold and heavy, and gathered between her palms into a sphere that wobbled and then went taut, a ball of water as big as a fruit, its surface humming. It rang faintly, a high, pure note, like a wet finger drawn round the rim of a stone bowl.

Outside the walls the birdsong stopped and started again.

She did not aim at the shoulder this time. She aimed high, at the white head and the long neck under it, where nothing had touched her yet.

She let the power go in one hard push that rocked her back where she stood.

— Nocturne —

She heard it coming as a hissing roar of spray, and then it was on her.

It broke against the side of her head and the top of her neck at once, one heavy slap of cold that burst into spray, and the ringing note inside it went straight through her skull. Her head snapped back. Her eyes streamed. Water went up her nose and ran down under her ruff, and the white fur across her face and neck went flat and dark and dripping in a single instant.

She hated it, the cold and the weight and the cling of it.

A low growl came up out of her chest, *grr*, and the hit to her neck caught it halfway and turned it into a short, dry, hacking cough. Her shoulders hunched up round her neck before she could stop them. Her whole coat shivered outward from where it had hit, every hair trying to shed the wet.

Then the ringing.

It did not stop when the water did. It went on inside her head, a thin, pure note she could not shake, and the hall moved. The pillars were not where she had left them. The nearest one leaned, and then it leaned the other way, and she turned her head to find the otter and the otter was somewhere to the left of where her eyes said, and when she turned the other way the light swung with her.

She swayed on the spot, all four paws planted, her weight going from one side to the other like a tree in wind.

*Where. Where is she.*

She shook her head hard, once, spraying water off the tip of her horn. It did not help. A point of ache sat in her neck where the blast had caught it, and opened out slowly into a dull pressure across the back of her skull, nothing she could not carry, but under it everything was tilted and slipping. She could not trust where she put her paws.

She gave a sharp sniff, clearing the water from her nose, and her eyes went hard.

The otter was there. Square again, her feet caught up with her, both planted flat, guard back up where it belonged, watching her sway.

Nocturne could still feel all of it: the cold soaking down to her skin, the throb in her shoulder, the ache in her neck. What she had lost was the floor.
