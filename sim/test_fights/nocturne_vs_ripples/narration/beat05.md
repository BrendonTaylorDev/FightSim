— Nocturne —

She caught a breath and held it, as if holding it would hold the hall still.

It did not. The pillars kept their slow lean, first one way, then back. Water dripped from her ruff onto the flagstones in front of her paws, and a little beyond them rainwater stood in a hollow of the floor, flat and green, and even that seemed to tilt.

But the otter was there. Orange, small, square on her feet, near enough. Near enough was enough.

*The shoulder. Take the shoulder.*

She would not use the claws again; the otter would be watching for claws. She would turn instead, the whole of her, and bring the tail.

She drew on it and felt it change: the sickle at the end of her tail went heavy and cold, the fur along it flattening as the blade beneath hardened, and when she flexed it, it no longer bent like a living thing. It held its shape like forged metal. She planted her forepaws, took her weight onto them, and spun.

The world spun faster than she did.

Halfway round, the floor was not where her paws expected it. Her left forepaw came down on stone that seemed to drop away beneath it, and she lurched. Her hindquarters were already swinging, and the tail with them, and her head was coming round to find the otter, and the two of them, head and tail, were not going where she had sent them.

She heard it before she understood it: a hollow, ringing clack, close, right against her own ear.

Then the side of her head lit up.

The flat of her own tail, hard as iron, had come round wide on the lurch and clipped her across the skull just behind the horn, where the water had already struck. The fur there pressed flat under the blow and sprang back slowly, still dripping. The new knock found the old one, and the two settled into the same dull ache, the old cold throb and the new bright one becoming one hurt above her eye.

Her head turned away from it on its own before she dragged it back. She blinked hard, three times over, her eyes watering, and a hiss came out through the side of her mouth, *tsss*.

She rode the knock with a half-step and was set again before the half-step had finished, all four paws on the stone, the tail gone loose and soft behind her as if ashamed of itself.

A body's length of wet flagstone lay between them, and the otter had not moved at all. She had not needed to. She stood with her arms up and her head a little to one side and watched, her small black eyes very steady, and that look was worse than the blow.

Indignation went through Nocturne, hot and absurd and steadying. Her own tail. In front of *her*.

*She will pay for that.*

Her eyes went narrow and very bright. The pillars still swam at the edges, but less.

She breathed out once, long and slow through her nose, and let it go.
