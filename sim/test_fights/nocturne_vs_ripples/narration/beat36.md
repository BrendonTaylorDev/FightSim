— Nocturne —

Far off, beyond the walls, something called through the trees: one long falling note, and then the jungle took it back.

Nocturne did not lift her head to it. Her forepaws were where they had been, sunk deep in the otter's chest, and the orange fur had parted under them in a trench, the muscle beneath quivering against her pads in small, constant tremors. Her claws had dug in a while ago, and she kept them there. The sickle tail was pressed across the throat hard enough now that the fur there had folded into a deep crease along it, the skin drawn tight on either side. The small throat changed its shape round the blade at each swallow. Under her hind paw the belly had gone wide and flat, the fur crushed into it.

Whenever the otter moved, she leaned in a little harder. Whenever the otter stopped, she eased back by less.

Outside, the birds stopped and started again.

Her eyes kept wanting to close. It was the heat and the stillness and the long ache in her right side, all of them pulling the same way. She opened them wider and fixed them on the small face below, and kept them there.

*Don't give her anything back.*

It sat in her like a set jaw: a tired, flat stubbornness with no heat left in it. It did not need any.

— Ripples —

There was nothing left in her to push the chest away with. She simply lay in it.

She drifted. The sky went a long way off and the paws went a long way off and the sound of her own breath went somewhere else. Then a breath caught and she came back, all at once, to the weight, and the green, and the white face. Then she drifted again.

She was not sure any more which way was up. The stone might have been above her. The sky might have been underneath.

The stomach was the last clear thing. Whatever else went soft and far away, it stayed exactly where it was, under the hind paw.

She could barely keep her eyes even half open. She kept them on the face anyway, the dark blue-grey of it, the red eyes. She would not close them while it was looking at her.

*Hips. Up.*

She bucked.

It started in her heels and went up through her thighs into her hips, and her back came off the stone in a shallow arch, her whole small body heaving up against the weight on top of it. It lifted the white body by nothing at all. It was slow and it was late and the hunter was ready for it.

The hind paw came down into her stomach, and stayed down.

A cracked, squealing cry broke out of her, *eeeyaaah*, and went up off the pillars and stopped every bird outside. Her body hunched round her middle, curling up off the stone round the paw as far as the forepaws would let it, her knees jerking up, her left arm clawing at the flagstones in long scrapes, water streaming out of her eyes. Her jaw shook. Her head rolled to one side, jerked back, rolled again, raw sounds spilling out of her on every breath. For a long time there was nothing in the world but that one place.

Then her back came down onto the stone, and stayed down.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne watched the cry run out.

The twin tails, which had been twitching against the stone all through the hold, went still. Both of them. They lay out flat behind the otter and did not move again, and Nocturne knew what that meant.

Everything the small body did now was heavy and plain. The tricks had drained out of it: no feints, no slipping, just weight and breath and the slow, shaking work of lying there. The arms had sunk to the stone on either side, webbed paws open and loose. The left one twitched once, then lay still.

*No guard left. Only stubbornness.*

The black eyes were still on her, half shut. Nocturne gave them nothing.

Her own breath came short on the right side. The ribs ached with every one, a deep, hot pressure that had stopped rising and falling and just stayed. She held herself still above it, nothing wasted, the way she held herself before a strike.

She let her weight settle the last small way into her paws.
