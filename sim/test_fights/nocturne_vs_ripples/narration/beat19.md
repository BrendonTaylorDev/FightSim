— Nocturne —

There was a tremor in her right foreleg, low down, a fine shiver above the paw that came up from the ribs every time she breathed. She set the paw harder on the stone and it stopped. Whether the otter had felt it through those clinging arms, she could not tell.

The small body was pressed against her ruff, so close she could smell moss and warm stone and wet orange fur all at once. And she could feel what the otter was guarding. Every time Nocturne's shoulder shifted toward that cream chest, the whole little body went tight against her, stiff and hunched, the arms clamping harder on her neck as if holding on could keep the chest out of reach.

It could not.

*The ribs will make me slow. So be short.*

There was no room to swing. Fine. She did not need room. She went still, a hunter's stillness with nothing wasted in it, and turned her head into the otter's grip instead of away from it, her horn sliding past the small ear, her forehead leaning in against the otter's head so that she could work by feel.

She was already inside the otter's reach.

Her right forepaw came up off the stone between their two bodies. The claws darkened as they came, a shadow pouring down them until they were black to the root. A grunt came out of her with the effort, and there was pain in it; the twist pulled across her ribs like a hook.

She raked the black claws down across the cream chest, short and hard, where it was already bruised.

— Ripples —

The claws dragged with a ripping hiss, across her chest and the inside of her left arm in the same stroke.

For an instant there was nothing. Then the chest went up like dry grass.

It was the bruise, the old one, the one the white shoulder and the paw had pounded into the middle of her, and the claws went straight across the top of it. The pain that had been large was suddenly bigger than her. A cry tore out of her, a broken, chattering scream, *ki-ki-kyah*, and it rang off the pillars and up out of the roofless hall, and the birds outside stopped.

Her arms let go.

She did not decide it. They jerked off the white neck on their own and came in to cover the place, and her whole body pulled in round it, head down, ears snapped flat and rigid. The jolt in her left arm was nothing, a knock, a sting she flicked out of the limb with one shake and forgot. The chest was everything.

She tried to breathe and the breath set it off again. A cough hacked out of her and she screamed through it, short, and could not get enough air behind it to make it a proper cry.

Her chest would not do what she told it; it only shook and burned.

*Move. Do something. Do anything.*

Nothing came. Her body would not answer. She stood frozen a half-second behind the world, curled round herself, and she knew it, and she hated every heartbeat of it. The white shape was free of her and right there, and she could not make her arms go out again.

She kept her face turned square to Nocturne anyway, and tucked the hurt place in closer.

— Ripples —

The first breath she managed was small and high, up under her collarbone, and she held her belly and her chest as still as she could while she took it. Little bursts of the throb went down through her into both her tails and pricked at the tips.

Nocturne stood a stride off, her neck free, the ruff dented where Ripples' arms had been. She was breathing hard too, and she held her right side drawn in, the ribs barely moving. Her left shoulder sat low. But the red eyes were on Ripples' chest and they were not tired. They were interested.

*She's quick, but she's loud about it. She grunts on that side.*

Ripples glanced down. Under the cream fur of her chest the skin had gone dark and puffed, and the claws had cut the fur into tufts, with thin red scratches showing angry beneath. The chest twitched as she watched, a little jump of the muscle, and then another, and she could not stop it.

Her legs still held. Her arms worked. The chest was the trouble, and the chest was the trouble everywhere.

She drew her shoulders up over it, chin down, one arm across, and did not even notice she was doing it.
