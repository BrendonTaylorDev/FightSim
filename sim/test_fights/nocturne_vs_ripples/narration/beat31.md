— Nocturne —

The ribs reminded her first. She had stopped listening to them while the otter's last chop rang off her horn, and now, in the stillness after it, they spoke up again on the right side, deep and hot under the raised fur, so that she had to take the next breath in two halves.

She did not give them anything more than that.

The otter stood a body's length off on the cracked flagstones, swaying. She had both arms folded across her chest now, openly, the right forearm with its blue fin laid flat over the worst of it, and she was hunched round that arm as if it were the only thing holding her together. Her cream belly barely moved. She was breathing in tiny, careful sips, high in her throat, so as not to stir anything lower down.

The wild light that had carried her through the last few exchanges had gone out of her eyes. What was left was a small orange body and a stubborn set of the jaw.

*Don't wait for her to set.*

Nocturne dropped her head. The horn swung low and to the side, out of her way, and she went in under it, sinking at the shoulders until her ruff was nearly brushing the moss in the cracks, her broad paws spread and scraping for purchase on the damp stone. A lizard flicked off a flagstone ahead of her and was gone. She did not aim at the chest the otter was covering. She aimed through it. Through the folded arms, through the place where the otter stood, out the far side.

Every part of her pointed the same way.

She came up into it at the last stride, all her weight behind her right shoulder.

— Ripples —

It hit the arm first. The white shoulder slammed into her right forearm, square on the fin she had laid across her chest, and drove the fin, her right paw and the upper arm behind it back into her ribs all at once, with a deep, slapping thud that went up off the pillars.

Her mouth opened. For a moment nothing came out of it at all. Then a sharp, splitting *yaaip* tore loose from her throat and her eyes screwed shut.

The fin was the worst: a hard, hot knot right where the teeth had first gone in, struck now with the whole weight of the hunter behind it. The right paw was folded under it and took the jar, and that was a real blow on a hand that already throbbed from the bite and the lightning, and it rang all the way up into her teeth. A thin chittering *ki-ki* broke from her nose. The upper arm took the rest, and she yelped, short and startled, as it bent back.

Above her, Nocturne's head snapped back off the impact. The white hunter snorted hard through her nose, *hnk*, tossed her head once and set her jaw against the jolt, her red eyes narrowing to slits.

Ripples went over backward.

Her feet left the stone. Her upper back struck first, a flat smack that made her spine flinch straight and pushed a string of barks out of her, *ack, ack*. Her left shoulder skidded along the flagstone after it, gritting the fur, and her right thigh bounced once on the edge of a crack and jerked out sideways. Then she was lying on her back in the dust and the green light, with the open sky over her and the tops of the vines swaying against it.

— Ripples —

She lay perfectly still. She noticed it, distantly, and found that she did not dare stop.

Her chest was heavy and hot and shaking under her folded arms, and all of it was pain. Lower down her stomach turned over, slow and sick, and the flagstones felt as if they tilted away under her. She tried for a full breath and it stopped short, somewhere under the bruises, and stayed high. She took it in short, uneven hitches instead, her bare throat working with each one.

Her right arm was trembling from the elbow down, fine and fast, the fin clamped flat to it. She glanced at it. The fur over the fin was puffed and dull, knocked flat in a patch where the shoulder had landed, the old arc of tooth marks on the paw beneath it. She looked away again quickly.

A groan came out of her, long and low and rasping. There was nothing left to hold it with.

*Shoulder. Low. Next time...*

Somewhere over to her left, stone groaned.

She rolled her eyes toward it. One of the leaning pillars beside Nocturne had shifted on its cracked base, grit pattering from its carvings. Then it went, with a roar that sent every bird outside the walls up shrieking, and the top of it burst into pieces on the floor.

The white hunter was right beside it. A broken section came down across her hindquarters and shoulder in a hail of chips, cracking off her left thigh, her right hip, her right shoulder. Nocturne hissed out of the side of her mouth, *tsss*, flicked one hind leg and shook the dust from her coat, and stayed on her feet.

Ripples watched her through the settling dust, and did not look away.
