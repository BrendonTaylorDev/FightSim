— Nocturne —

The two of them were alone in the long hall: the white hunter standing a stride back from the altar end of the floor, the small orange body lying on its back between the pillars, and the fan of broken stone off to one side where the pillar had come down. The green light lay over all of it. Nothing moved.

It came apart in her in order.

Her breath went first. With nothing left to hold down, it ran away from her, great hauling breaths through her open mouth that lifted her ruff and dropped it and lifted it again. Then the shaking came. It started in her forelegs, at the elbows, a fine fast tremble she could not stop, and climbed into her shoulders and down her back to her haunches, until the sickle tail behind her quivered at the tip.

Then the hurts, one at a time, as if they had been waiting for her to finish.

The right ribs first and loudest, a hot, swollen band down her side that caught every breath at the top and held it there. She stood with that side turned in and her weight leaned off it, and still it burned. The left shoulder after it, deep and grinding where the otter's first chop had landed, and the recoil of her own charge after that. Then her neck, stiff where the small teeth had gone in. Then the left hind paw, cold and tender, as though the frost from that bite had never quite left the pads.

The quiet was very loud. After all the cries off the pillars, the barking and the thud of bodies, there was only her own breathing, and the drip of water somewhere from the vines.

Her eyes slid shut. She stood like that a moment, swaying, before she noticed and forced them open again.

*Not yet. Stay standing.*

She had won. It was in her somewhere, under the shaking. She did not feel like gloating.

— Nocturne —

She looked down at the otter.

Ripples lay on her back exactly where the pin had left her, in the grit and the moss between the cracked flagstones. Her arms lay open at her sides, the webbed paws turned half up, the right one still drawn in a little closer than the left, as if even now some part of her was guarding it. Her chin had tipped down toward her chest, toward her throat, by the same old habit. The fur there still showed the line the blade had pressed into it.

Her chest was a great dark bruise under matted orange fur, swollen and puffy, and her belly was no better. It rose, and fell, and rose. Slow and steady. Each breath was shallow, but it came, and the next one came after it.

Her twin tails lay side by side on the stone behind her, their cream tips touching. All fight long they had been whirling, binding, stuttering, lashing. They were still now.

Nocturne watched the small chest go up and down for a long time. She did not go closer.

The light had shifted while she stood there. It came slantwise through the vines now, warmer, and lay across the otter's face and the cream spots above her closed eyes. Somewhere outside, a bird tried a few notes, stopped, and tried them again.

Nothing else moved in the hall at all.
