— Nocturne —

A single orange hair had caught in the claws of her right forepaw. It lifted and fell in the warm air, a thread of colour against the white, and Nocturne looked at it for a moment before she looked past it.

The otter had not come back at her. She stood with a leaning pillar a few strides behind her, its carved face tilted out over the floor, and she had turned a little side-on, the cream belly hidden behind her hip as if Nocturne might forget it was there. Her breathing had gone careful and short. Each breath stopped before it reached the bottom of her chest, where the paw had just landed on the bruise that was already there.

She did not need a second look. The chest was the door, and it was standing open.

*Let her think I'm tired.*

Nocturne let her head sink, let the horn droop toward the flagstones, let one breath out long and slow through her nose. Then she lowered her head the rest of the way and went.

She came in from the right, the side the otter's bitten paw guarded worst, and the charge bent as it came, curving across the cracked floor to close the gap between the otter and the open hall. Her legs surprised her, a small blink of it: the bitten foreleg took her weight, the shoulder took it, and the stride kept lengthening. Moss tore under her claws. The pillar loomed up behind the small orange shape and she aimed at it, meaning to fold the otter against the stone.

She did not slow as she closed; she leaned into it.

Her shoulder dropped, her neck tucked, and every bit of her went into one point.

— Ripples —

The crack of it went up the pillars and out of the top of the hall.

The white shoulder hit her square in the chest and the force jarred out into her right shoulder in the same instant, and it rocked her back on her heels so hard that her arms flew out from her sides, webbed paws spread wide, before her feet, planted, held. The pillar was a stride behind her. She never reached it.

A cry came out of her, a pained chittering, *ki-ki*, high and broken, and her knees dipped under her for an instant and she forced them straight again.

In the same moment Nocturne's head snapped back with the blow. Ripples saw it: the horn jerking up, the red eyes screwing shut and streaming, a low growl, *grr*, ground out between her fangs. The ram had jarred back into the white shoulder too, the one Ripples' own chop had found at the start, and a shiver ran along the white coat from neck to flank. The big body held its back stiff and careful afterward, as if carrying something that might spill.

Ripples could not enjoy it. She had no air.

Her chest heaved and nothing went in. Her mouth opened on air that would not come, and her bare throat worked, and worked, and her ribs pumped against nothing. She folded over the place with both arms and rocked once on her heels, a small sound forced out through her nose because her teeth were clamped on her own lip.

The pain was not new. It was the old pain made bigger: the first lunge, the black ring, the paw a moment ago, all three found by the shoulder and pressed into one hot, pulsing patch in the middle of her chest. A line of fire ran up from it into her right shoulder, drawn in one stroke, and stopped. That one was only a jolt. She ignored it.

Her chest pulsed with a heat the rest of her could not ignore.

*Breathe. Come on. Breathe.*

A thread of air went in, thin as a reed, and whistled.

— Nocturne —

She had stepped back off the impact and she stayed back, and she watched.

Her own head rang. She shook it hard, once, and the ringing went down to a buzz behind the horn. The left shoulder had a new heat in it. She kept it where it was and gave it nothing on her face.

The otter's feet had set wider. She reset them, unhappy with them, and reset them again. Her arms stayed wrapped across her chest, the fins on her forearms clamped flat, and her breathing could be heard now from across the floor: a thin, catching wheeze on every draw, and a little whistle at the top of it. Her eyes kept going down to her own chest and coming back up, and down again.

*There. That's where she lives now.*

Nocturne fixed on it, the cream fur over the otter's breastbone, and the rest of the hall went quiet around that one spot. A hollow in the floor between them held rainwater, and the reflection of the otter shook in it with each breath she took.

Nobody moved.

The hall held its breath with them. The birds outside, which had gone silent at the crack of the blow, came back one at a time, a single whistled note from the trees beyond the left wall, then an answer from further off, then a dozen voices at once, as if nothing in the world had happened. The light came down green through the vines along the tops of the walls and lay in long bars across the flagstones, and dust that the charge had kicked up hung in those bars and turned slowly.

Nocturne began to walk. Not toward her. Across, a slow curve of three paces to the right and then back, the bear-like rolling gait that let her keep every paw under her and her head level. Each time the left forepaw came down, the shoulder above it answered. It was a deep ache now, solid, as if a stone had been set inside the joint and left there, and with every step it pressed outward a little against the muscle. She did not limp. She would not limp in front of this thing.

Her right ribs had their own opinion. They had gone quiet during the charge and now they spoke again, a dull bruise under the fur, low and steady, where the freezing teeth had sunk in and the water charge had broken over them after. She breathed around it, shallower on that side.

The head was nothing. A buzz. She shook it again anyway, short and sharp, and the white tuft on top of her head flicked.

*Small. Stubborn. Hits harder than she should.*

She did not like admitting it, even inside her own skull. But the shoulder had been fine when the fight began and it was not fine now, and the paw that had done it was attached to that orange body standing over there with its arms wrapped round itself.

— Ripples —

Air came back to her in pieces.

The first breaths were small and sharp and they whistled, and each one met a wall halfway down. She let them be small. She bent a little at the waist, arms crossed over the hot place in her chest, and took the next one and the next, and gradually the wall moved down an inch, and another.

She watched the white shape walk. Three paces and back. Three paces and back. The horn stayed level the whole time, which meant the head behind it was steady again, and that was annoying. But the left forepaw came down a fraction early every time, quick, as if the ground were hot, and the white shoulder above it rode a little stiff.

*There's my door.*

She took stock. The chest first, because it would not let her do anything else first: a heavy, swollen heat right over the breastbone, and with every breath in it pulsed. Below it the belly, the claw lines and the flattened place where the black ring had broken over her, a deep, sullen ache she could fold away if she kept her body turned. The right paw, where the teeth had gone in, was stiff when she curled it. The thigh, the hip: nothing worth counting.

Her twin tails had gone still behind her. She made them move, a slow half-turn each way, to feel them work. They worked.

The pillar at her back leaned out over her head, a carved face worn smooth by rain looking down at her with its mouth open. Water dripped off its lip into a crack in the floor. The flagstones were dark with old rain in the hollows and pale where the sun reached them, and somewhere in the middle of the hall a white crust of frost on one puddle was melting into nothing. She had made that. It hadn't touched her.

*Next time it does.*

— Nocturne —

The otter straightened.

She did it slowly, one arm at a time coming away from her chest, and Nocturne saw what it cost her in the way the black cheek marks twitched when the second arm moved. But she straightened. Her chin tucked down over her bare throat and her shoulders came up around it, and she set her feet a third time and this time she left them where they were.

Something sat in Nocturne's chest that she did not want there. Not worry. A grudging, sour sort of respect. The otter was down to wheezing and still she squared up.

The birds were in full voice again. A vine shifted on the wall in a breath of air and tapped against the stone.

Nocturne stopped walking. She stood with the sore shoulder turned away and her weight forward over the good one, and the sickle tail lifted behind her and went still, as rigid as a blade.

Across the floor the otter's small black eyes were on her face. Her mouth was half open. Her chest jumped with each short breath, still not getting all the way down, and her throat worked as she swallowed.

She was gathering something. Nocturne could see it in the throat.
