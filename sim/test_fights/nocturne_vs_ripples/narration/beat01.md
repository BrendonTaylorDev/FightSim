— Nocturne —

She drew one breath through her nose, deep, and it cost her nothing. Nothing on her hurt yet. Her body was all spring.

The otter had not finished settling. She was up on the balls of her feet, rocking a little, coming up onto her toes between one small shift and the next, the way a thing does when it has decided to wait and has not yet found the patience for it. Her left shoulder sat a fraction higher than the right. Her chin was down. The whole small body was turned a few degrees to the left, as if to keep the right side of her a little further away.

*She keeps the throat. She gives the chest.*

Nocturne breathed in again, and the second breath went in shorter than she meant it to, catching somewhere under her ruff. Her ears had gone forward without her asking. She let them.

Then she went.

There was no gather to it, no crouch the otter could read. Her hind legs pushed and she was simply across the flagstones, low, her chest skimming a hand's width over the stone, the moss in the cracks flashing past under her. A puddle went by on her right in a smear of green light. Something dark came off her as she moved: not a shadow, the light was too even for that, but a darkening along her shoulders and the line of her face, as if the air round her had thickened and gone cold for the length of the rush. The ruff flattened back against her neck with the speed of it.

The otter's weight was still on its way down into her heels. Her paws had started to rise and were nowhere near high enough.

Nocturne did not reach with claws or horn. She crowded in, took away the room the small one needed to swing, turned her body a quarter at the last instant and drove her left shoulder into the middle of the orange chest, all her weight riding behind it.

— Ripples —

She heard it first.

A deep, slapping thud, loud in the stone hall, flat and close, and for one strange half-instant it seemed to belong to somebody else. The white shoulder was in her chest. Her left arm, still on its way up, had been caught by the same weight and knocked hard against her ribs, so that the whole front of her took it at once.

Her small black eyes flew wide.

Then nothing. A gap. The pillars stood where they were. The puddle at her heel held still. A little dust sifted down from the top of the leaning pillar behind her and drifted through the green light.

Then it arrived, all of it together: a dull, driving ache in the middle of her chest, a pressure that went in under the cream fur and sat behind her breastbone. Her ribs jerked twice, short and shallow, the breath knocked flat for a moment before she could draw it, and a thin *eek* got out of her before she could close her teeth on it.

Her head went down, chin to chest, as if ducking a second blow that was not coming. The fur on the back of her neck stood up. Her feet did not move. She had planted them on the dry stone and they stayed there.

*No. Not another one of those.*

The ache spread a little, the way a ripple spreads on a puddle, out from the chest and up into her left arm, where the shoulder had pinned it against her. It sat there, dull and wide, a bruised feeling just above the elbow. She flexed the webbed paw at the end of that arm once, and the fingers closed and opened as they always had.

It was not much. She knew it was not much. Her chest lifted in one deeper breath, then settled, and after that her breath came and went without her noticing it. On the orange fur over her breastbone a rough patch stood up the wrong way, the shape of the white shoulder pressed into it.

She looked up the length of that shoulder into the red eyes, so close now that she could see the dark of the face around them.

Something small and hard settled in her, not quite anger: a sullen, gathering resentment. She had been hit before she was ready, and she would remember that it was this one who did it.

She let the blow pass through her without giving it anything to hold on to.

Her fins, which had flared out wide at the impact, came down slowly against her forearms. Her twin tails, which had stopped dead, began to turn again behind her, slow at first, then steadier.

She would not back up. She would not give this one the satisfaction of a single step.
