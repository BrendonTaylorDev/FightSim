— Nocturne —

She heard the otter before she could properly see her: quick, careful little breaths, short in and short out, each one stopped early, coming from somewhere ahead and a little to the left of where the hall said she was.

Nocturne shook her head hard, once, ears slapping. The pillars swung and settled. She shook it again and they stayed, mostly, though the edges of things still crawled. Her right side was a solid throb with her heartbeat in it, and every breath went in shallow round it.

The otter stood side-on to her now, turned to hide the chest, and that only showed her where it was. The orange arms were crossed low and the body was hunched round the middle, and as Nocturne took a step, the whole small figure curled in tighter round the belly, one hand drifting down to cover it.

*Every time I move, she curls round that spot.*

She felt pride at that, and she kept it off her face. The water had rung her head and soaked her coat and found her ribs, and the otter had paid for every bit of it already. She would pay for the rest now.

*Finish it clean.*

Nocturne stepped after her. The floor tilted under her paws and she let it, put her weight through it anyway, and came in on the right, the side the otter's guard was furthest from. The otter saw it. Her belly went in, her body snatching away from it, the hand coming across, too late.

Nocturne's forepaw swept up and across. At the last instant she turned the paw so that the claws would meet their mark edge-first. Slash.

— Ripples —

She heard it before she felt it: a raspy swish, and the tug of fur parting.

Then it was in her. Three claws went across her belly, right across the old furrows the dark claws had cut at the very start, and the force of it went through her middle and jarred her lower back and jolted down into her right thigh in the same rip of a moment.

The stomach took all of her.

It was too big to brace against. It went straight through every brace she had. A sound tore out of her that she did not know as her own, *hhhiiieee*, hoarse and breathless and cracking in the middle, and it rang off the pillars and out into the open air. Her whole body clenched round her middle. Her knees buckled and caught. Her arms clamped over her belly and she folded round it and could not unfold, her feet scraping and shifting under her on the wet stone just to stay upright, her tails jerking. Her eyes streamed. For one long moment there was nothing in her head at all except that place.

Behind it, quieter, her lower back answered, a sharp pull that she twisted away from and could not reach. She barked at it, *ack, ack*, without meaning to. Her right leg shook as she set it down again, and a little yip got out.

*She read me. Like a puddle.*

And then something else came.

It came up from somewhere under the pain, low and hot, out of her legs and her belly and the very bottom of her, and it climbed. Her heart slammed. The shaking in her arms changed, steadied, went tight. The noise in her head pulled back like water drawing off a stone, and behind it everything was suddenly sharp: the white shape, the red eyes, the dip in that right side.

Her tails stopped twitching and went still.

— Ripples —

The hurt in her stomach rose and broke and rose again, each crest higher, and she stood up inside it.

It had not gone. She could feel every bit of it. But it was behind something now, as if she had stepped back from it and pulled a door almost shut. Her right paw stiffened between breaths and every time she moved it the stiffness wrenched; she could feel her heartbeat arrive in it, in the swollen fingers, and spread out from there. She noted it and let it be.

A gasp, then another, smaller, and then she had her breath back under her. It came in threes: two short, one long. Her sides pumped. Her mouth hung open and she did not care.

She looked down. The cream fur of her belly was ragged, the old claw lines crossed with new ones, the skin under them dark and puffed, the bruise spreading even as she watched. Lower, the right leg was trembling. She leaned on it. It held.

*She stepped in too far. If I'd had anything left—*

Then she did. That was the strange part. She had something left.

A fast, furious chitter ran through her gritted teeth, the black cheek marks jumping, and she lifted her head and looked at Nocturne. The smell of moss and warm stone came up off the floor. Neither of them moved. For a breath the whole hall was still, and Ripples stood in the stillness with her eyes very bright.
