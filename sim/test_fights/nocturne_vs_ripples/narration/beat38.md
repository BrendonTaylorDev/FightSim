— Nocturne —

She wanted it to be over. The want had no words in it. It sat at the back of her throat like thirst.

Her forepaws were still driven deep into the otter's chest, the small ribcage pushed out of its shape beneath them. Her sickle tail still lay across the throat. The throat was pressed in under it now, a clear dip in the cream fur, the muscle bunched up hard on either side of the blade. Her hind paw was sunk in the belly. Each time her weight shifted even a hair, the body under her flinched from end to end, and a cry came up out of it, thin and cracked.

Her own body had begun to argue with her. The right ribs had stopped aching and started burning, a hot band round her side that tightened at every breath. The left shoulder shook under the load. The neck where the otter's teeth had been was stiff as wood. She felt her grip loosening on its own, the claws easing out of the matted fur, and she set them again on purpose. A breath later they eased again. She set them again.

Every part of her that was bearing down had begun to tremble.

Above them the carved faces on the pillars looked down, worn and patient, green with moss.

*Tired enough to make a mistake. Don't.*

She lowered her head until her face was a hand's width from the small one, the horn swung aside, her breath stirring the fur round the black cheek marks. The otter's eyes dragged open and had to look at her. They were dull and wet and very far back, and they found her slowly.

Nocturne held them. She kept her fear out of her paws, though it was there. She did not know how much more this small, stubborn thing had in it.

— Ripples —

The ache in her chest was blurring. It ran out at the edges into the grey that was coming in from every side, so that she could no longer tell where the pain stopped and the grey began.

*Eyes. Keep them... open.*

A soft, sorry tiredness was rising through her, warm, wanting so badly to be let in.

Not yet.

She found her feet. She did not know how; they were a long way down. She set the soles of them flat on the gritty stone, drew her knees up, and pushed. Her back arched. It came up off the flagstones, hips and spine lifting in one long shaking curve, and for a moment the white weight on top of her came up with it, a hair, no more.

The hind paw came down into her stomach.

All of her clenched round it. A hollow grunt was driven out of her first, her belly pulled in tight under the paw, and then the cry came after it and cracked into hoarse squeaks, *aaah-ih-ih*, and her back fell to the stone. Her body curled up round the middle as far as the weight would let it. Her heels scraped at the ground in short jerks, and her left paw scrabbled at the flagstone beside her, and the shaking would not stop. Water ran out of her eyes into the fur at her temples. There was nothing in her head but the stomach.

Then there was the chest.

It came up under everything else, under the stomach and the throat and the arm, the deepest place, where the claws and the steel tail and the weight had all found her. It climbed. She had braced against it for so long, and now there was nothing left to brace with, and it climbed past where she could follow it, and her whole body shook with it, from her jaw down through her shoulders to her feet.

Her face went slack. The fight smoothed out of it.

Her lips moved on something she had no breath to say.

The sky went white, and then grey, and then it went.

Her eyes closed. Her left paw slid off the flagstone and lay open at her side. Her twin tails lay flat. Her breathing was all that moved, and it barely moved, a small, slow rise and fall under the white paws.

— Nocturne —

She felt it go out of the body. The push from below was there, and then it wasn't. The muscles under her pads went soft all at once.

She did not move.

She kept her forepaws where they were and her weight where it was, the tail across the throat and the hind paw on the belly, and she watched. She watched the closed eyes for a flicker. She listened. Nothing came but breath, slow and shallow. She felt down through her paws for anything gathering, any tightening, any trick.

Nothing.

She waited one breath longer.

Then she lifted the tail off the throat, and the cream fur there sprang back slowly, still marked with a dip where the blade had lain. She took the hind paw off the belly. She stepped back off the chest, one forepaw, then the other, and the small ribs rose under the crushed fur a little more freely than before.

Nocturne stood over her on four shaking legs, breathing hard, her right side held stiff, and looked down.

The otter did not stir. The hall was very quiet. One by one, outside the walls, the birds began again.
