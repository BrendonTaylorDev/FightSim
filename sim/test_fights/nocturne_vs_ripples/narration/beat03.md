— Nocturne —

The shoulder wanted her to step back. She did not.

She stood exactly where the chop had found her, the left foreleg set carefully under her, and let the otter see her not move. She had been hit. She had been hit by something small. Neither of those things was going to decide where she put her feet.

The air in the hall was close and warm, and it smelled of moss and sun on old stone.

The otter stood close in front of her, loose and easy on her two feet, weight even, nothing held back, as if the shove into her chest had already gone out of her. Her arms were up. Her chin was tucked. All of her guard was high, round that bare throat and the cream face.

Which left the belly.

Nocturne saw it the way she would have seen a gap in a hedge. A long run of cream fur from the chest down to the hips, soft, round, and nothing at all over it but those two small arms, both of them too high.

*There.*

She did not lunge. She came up.

Her hind legs bunched and she rose off her forepaws, rearing, the white ruff lifting with her, so that for a moment she stood taller than the otter by a head and more and the green light fell across her chest. The left shoulder protested the lift with a deep pull at the joint and she ignored it. Her right forepaw came up and out to the side, the three claws spreading, and as it rose a darkness gathered along them, a sheen like the edge of a shadow drawn tight round each claw, so that they seemed longer than they were and blacker than anything else in the hall.

The otter's eyes went to them. Her arms started down.

Too slow. The paw was already coming across.

— Ripples —

She heard it before she felt anything: a dry rip, like a dead palm frond torn down its length, the sound of claws going through fur.

Then her belly was on fire in three straight lines.

The dark claws had raked across the cream of her stomach from right to left, and at the end of the sweep the force of the paw jarred into the top of her left thigh, a dull knock that pulled the leg in tight against her body. The blow turned her. It carried her, and her feet scrambled under her to catch up with where her body had gone, one single step backward, her left heel dragging on the flagstones as she caught herself. For one instant her guard was nowhere. Her arms were out wide for balance, the fins flared stiff, and the white shape was right in front of her with all that space between them open.

She knew it. She was a step out of place, and slow, and she needed her feet back now.

A startled peep, *pip*, came out of her nose, high and short and ridiculous, and she hated it before it had finished.

She got her feet. Both of them, flat on the stone, knees bent.

Her stomach felt thick and heavy for a breath, a hollow sickness folded up under the stinging lines, and her whole body wanted to curl round it like a fist closing. She let it curl, a little, just her shoulders rounding over, and tightened her middle against it and breathed through her teeth. Her belly clenched and held a moment, then let go.

Her paws came down to the place and hovered over it, not quite touching. Three thin lines of cream fur lay parted across her stomach, the skin under them stinging pink, a bead of red standing in the middle one and going no further.

The hurt in her belly pulled at her left thigh as she straightened, like a rope drawn taut between them. She shifted her weight onto the right leg and then, deliberately, back.

*Fine. Belly. Noted.*

Her twin tails had stopped turning when the blow carried her. Now they started again, slowly, holding her steady.

She lifted her eyes to the red ones over that dark face and the white ruff, and held her breath at the top, and waited for whatever came next.
