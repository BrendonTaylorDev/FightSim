— Nocturne —

The heat of the fight drained out of her slowly, the way warmth goes out of stone after sundown, and what it left behind was stiff and cold.

She felt her body set. The left shoulder that had been grinding now locked up, so that when she shifted her weight it moved late and all of a piece. The muscles along her back had tightened round her right side without asking her. Her head ached dully behind the horn, where the ram had thrown her own weight back into it. Even her jaw, which she had barely felt during the fight, had begun to throb a little at the hinge.

Each one spoke up in turn, small and specific, and she listened, because there was nothing else to do.

She turned her head and nosed at her right side, carefully, the way she might have tested a strange scent. The swelling under the white fur was bigger than she had thought, hot and tight, and the lightest touch of her muzzle sent a sharp line of pain up under her shoulder. She drew back from it with a short hiss through her teeth.

She let it be.

*She found that side early, and she never let it go.*

There had been a moment, under the otter's tails, with her hind legs bound and the small body chopping at her again and again, when she had not been sure. She did not like remembering it. She remembered it anyway.

She lowered herself, slowly, until she was lying on her left side on the flagstones, her right side up and her legs folded under her. The stone was cool through her coat. It helped a little.

— Nocturne —

From there she could see the otter's face side-on, a body's length away across the floor.

Ripples had not moved. She lay on her back as before, arms open, head tipped a little toward the near pillar. The blue fins on her forearms lay flat and limp against the stone, all the flare gone out of them. Her small black eyes were closed. The grit from the floor was ground into her orange coat everywhere it had touched, her shoulders, her flanks, the backs of her arms.

A bird called sharply from the top of the wall. One of the otter's ears twitched at it, a single small flick.

She did not wake.

Her breathing went on as it had: shallow, slow, even, the swollen chest lifting a little under the matted fur and settling again. Once it caught, and Nocturne's head came up off the stone. Then it went on, the same as before, and she lowered her head again.

A drop of water ran down a hanging vine, gathered at the tip, and fell into the puddle in the hollow of the floor. The ripples from it spread out across the little square of sky and crept to the edge and were gone.

The sun had moved a hand's width along the far wall. The light lay warm and green over both of them, and over the cracked altar, and over the carved faces on the pillars.

Neither of them stirred.
