— Nocturne —

The otter had not tried to rise.

She lay where the ram had left her, flat on her back on the flagstones with her short legs bent and her arms drawn up over her chest, the hurt right one folded closest. Her small black eyes were open and on Nocturne. Her chest jumped in short, shallow pants, quick as a bird's, and every one of them was watching.

The stance said nothing was finished. It said she would bite whatever came near enough.

Nocturne came anyway. She walked in at that slow, rolling pace of hers, dust from the fallen pillar still in her coat, and the green light through the leaves at the top of the walls slid over her back as she went. The left shoulder ached at each step. She stopped over the small body and looked down at it for a breath.

*If she gets out, I don't give her ground again. Not one stride.*

She set her right forepaw on the otter's chest, then her left beside it, three claws each spread on the matted orange fur, and let her weight go down through her shoulders into them. Her sore places woke one after another as she did: the left shoulder first, grinding under the load, then the neck where the otter's teeth had been, then the right ribs. The ribs did not want the press. As she leaned in they flared, hot and deep, and a growl caught in her chest before she could stop it. She shifted her weight further onto her left side and held it there, stiff, and the ache settled to something she could carry.

One hind paw came forward and found the soft cream of the belly.

Then the tail. She swept the long sickle blade round past her own flank and laid it flat across the bare throat, edge turned away, the cold hard length of it resting in the hollow under the otter's jaw.

She tucked her head in close and gave the body under her nothing to strike at.

Relief came into her slowly, and she did not trust it.

— Ripples —

For a beat there was only weight.

Then the pain arrived, all of it at once, and it was the chest.

The two broad forepaws sank into her ribs where the claws and the steel tail had already been, and the bruise under them, the worst place on her, took the whole of the hunter's weight. It pressed her in until her chest changed shape under the fur, flattened and puffed up tight round the edges of the paws. A raw, cracked *aak* tore out of her, and the next breath brought another one with it, and the next. Her arms flew up and scrabbled at the white forelegs without aim, webbed paws clawing at fur, at the elbow spikes, at the air, and her feet kicked and scraped at the stone.

The hind paw came down on her stomach.

The flagstones were under her and the paw was on top of her and her stomach was caught between them, and neither one gave. Her whole body twisted on the ground, hips rolling, back arching up off the stone and slamming down again, and a long broken sound came out of her that went up off the pillars into the sky. Outside the walls the birds stopped singing.

The tail she barely noticed. The smooth blade lay across her throat, pressing the cream fur flat in a clean line, firm and cold. She huffed against it, *hff*, and tucked her chin down onto it by habit, and that was all.

Beneath the weight she twisted, searching for an angle. There wasn't one. Her muscles jumped and twitched under the hold, quivering in her thighs and along her arms, and none of them had anything left to give.

She glared up past the white ruff at the dark face. She memorised it.

*Get. Off.*

— Ripples —

Her sides were heaving. She watched them as if they belonged to someone else, the orange fur rising and falling round the white paws in quick little shudders.

The hunter above her was breathing hard too. Ripples could feel it come down through the forepaws, a heavy rhythm, a little hitch at the top of every breath on the right side, where her own chops had gone in again and again. The white body sat lopsided on her, its weight leaned off that side. The left shoulder was stiff. The red eyes were not on her face. They moved over her slowly, chest, belly, throat, checking each press, cold and attentive and in no hurry at all.

*Can't trade with her any more.*

The deep bruise in the middle of her chest beat with her heart. Her arms shook at the elbows and the right one would not close its paw properly.

*Make it count.*

Her breath rattled faintly going in and dragged out slow.

Somewhere behind them, on the fan of broken pillar, a last loose chip of stone slid down and ticked onto the floor.
