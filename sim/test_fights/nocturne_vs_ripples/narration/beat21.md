— Nocturne —

The otter was still close, too close, crouched by her hindquarters where the twin tails had wound round her and been shaken off. Nocturne could feel where they had been, a damp, flattened band in the fur of her hind legs, and it annoyed her more than it hurt.

She did not like how near the otter stood. A small doubt pricked at her, quick and cold. Close like that, the little one could get her teeth into anything.

*She's looking for one lucky shot. Don't hand it to her.*

Nocturne kept her weight forward over her forepaws, a tight, humming alertness running down her back to the root of her tail. She watched the otter breathe. The orange shoulders were hunched high and the arms were drawn in, crossed low over the chest, and every breath went in short and stopped early, as if there were something breakable in there and she was carrying it carefully across the floor.

Nocturne turned. The twist ran through her right side and the ribs there took it badly, a hot pressure under the bruised fur where teeth and the edge of a paw had already been. She held her breath through it, jaw set, and did not let it out.

The sickle tail came up. She felt it go hard all the way along, from the base to the curved blade at its tip, the fur flattening and the whole length of it turning heavy and cold and dense as iron. Iron Tail. She swung it up and over, bringing it down like a flail at the small orange head.

Then she let the breath out, in a hiss.

— Ripples —

It hit her high on the head with a solid *whump*, like a sack dropped on stone, and the side of it caught her left cheek and the side of her neck in the same instant. The fur of her face blew flat. Her mouth fell open and the air went out of her in a rush.

For a moment nothing happened at all. She stood there and let it land.

Then it arrived, all at once: a deep knock behind her eyes that set them watering, so that she blinked and blinked and the green light swam. A chirrup of surprise, *prrt*, jumped out of her before she could stop it. Lower down along one side of her face a slow warmth was spreading, the kind of ache that would be a bruise by evening, and she winced at it, her black cheek marks twitching. Under her jaw her throat caught. She swallowed hard round a little cough and tucked her chin back down where it belonged.

That was all. She squinted, jaw tight, and waited for more, and there was no more.

Steel. She almost laughed. Her body took it the way the river takes a thrown stone: it went in, and it was gone. Her eyes went to Nocturne with something close to scorn.

*That's the one you swing at me? That?*

— Ripples —

She stayed where she was and watched the white one.

Nocturne was not as easy as she pretended. When she breathed, her right side moved less than her left, and there was a small stiffness every time she turned, a hitch she covered by turning slower than she needed to. The left shoulder dipped too, now and then, when she set her weight on that forepaw. The red eyes were calm. They kept going to the same place: the crossed orange arms, and what was under them.

*She'll go there. She keeps looking at it.*

Ripples felt her own chest under her forearms. It was the worst of her by a long way, a band drawn tight round her ribs and tightening, so that each breath came quick and shallow and ended before she wanted it to. Her stomach ached under it, lower and duller. The knock on her head was already settling into a sore throb above her eye, small beside the rest.

Off to one side a hollow in the floor held rainwater, very still, the green light lying on it.

She shifted her feet on the flagstones. They were cool and gritty under her soles, a little moss soft in the crack beneath her left heel. She set her toes, and held.
