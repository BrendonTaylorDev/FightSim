The hall had lost its roof a long time ago, and the jungle had been coming in ever since.

Vines hung down over the tops of the walls in green ropes, some of them long enough to brush the floor. Two rows of carved pillars ran the length of the hall, and not one of them stood quite the way it had been built: most were upright, a few leaned out of true, and two lay across the flagstones in broken sections, their carvings turned up to the sky. At the far end an altar stone squatted under a cracked statue whose face had worn down to a suggestion. Moss grew in every seam of the floor. Rain from the night before still stood in the hollows of the flagstones in shallow, flat puddles that held the light. The light was warm and green, filtered through the leaves outside, and beyond the walls the birds kept up their noise as if nothing in here concerned them.

— Nocturne —

She came in between the pillars at her own pace, the slow, rolling pace of something that knew it could be fast whenever it chose.

Her fur was white, and in that green light it looked whiter. The thick ruff at her neck and chest stood out round her like a mane; the dark blue-grey of her face showed through it like a mask. The horn curved off one side of her head, scythe-shaped and black, and behind her the sickle of her tail swung once and then held still above the flagstones. Her broad paws came down without a sound. Three claws on each, and she let them touch the stone just enough to feel it: dry here, gritty, slick with moss where the cracks ran.

She stepped round a puddle rather than through it, and did not look at it while she did.

Her red eyes were on the far end of the hall.

— Ripples —

The other one was small.

She stood upright on two feet beside a leaning pillar, orange from her ears to her heels, with a cream face and a cream belly, a single cream spot on her back, and her arms loose at her sides. A blue fin lay along each forearm. Her webbed paws opened and closed once, slowly, as if counting something. Two black marks ran across each cheek, and above each small black eye sat a cream spot that made her look as if she were raising her brows at the world.

Behind her, the twin tails turned over lazily, cream tips tracing circles in the air.

She watched the white one come and did not move to meet her. Her chin was down. Her shoulders had risen a little, the way they always did, closing over the bare cream fur of her throat. There was nothing on her neck to protect it but that habit, and she did not forget it now.

*Big. Long reach. Look at that tail.*

She shifted her weight from one foot to the other, testing the stone. Wet, there, by the puddle. Dry a step to the left. She took the step to the left.

— Nocturne —

They stopped with the length of the hall between them and two rows of pillars standing witness.

Nocturne looked her over once, from the tucked chin to the planted feet. A water creature. She could smell it on her: river, wet fur, cold mud.

*Small. Patient. Waiting for me to make the mistake.*

Her lip lifted off one fang, very slightly.

A bird somewhere outside the walls stopped mid-call. A drop of water fell from a vine and hit a puddle with a small, clear sound.

The otter's feet were still settling on the stone when Nocturne moved.
