— Ripples —

Her body wanted to sit down. It wanted it very simply and very badly, the way a stone wants to roll downhill: let the knees go, sit on the cool flagstones, curl up round the stomach and the numb dead weight where her chest should be. She made it stand up instead. Her fur was still prickling all over from the lightning, a faint singed smell coming off it, and her legs shook at the knees.

*Breath, a bit. Legs, just. One good trick.*

High on the wall a vine swayed, though nothing had touched it.

She looked at the white one through the water still running out of her eyes. Nocturne was breathing hard after the bolt, and she was carrying her left shoulder a little high and a little close. But it was the right side Ripples watched. Every breath the white chest took, the right ribs moved less than the left, the bruised fur there raised in a swelling over the place her teeth and her paw had been. That was the soft place. That was where everything she had done in this fight had gone.

She did not want to wait. She did not have the waiting in her any more.

Ripples dragged in a breath, deep, the numb chest swelling with it, and she felt nothing there at all, which was frightening and also useful. Between her webbed paws, held out together in front of her, the water began to gather: a fist-sized sphere of it, then bigger, spinning, a deep hum building inside it until it rang like a struck bowl. She angled her body away as she set her feet, keeping her stomach turned from Nocturne even now. She narrowed one eye and sighted down along her arms at the right ribs.

Nocturne saw it. The white body flinched before anything had left Ripples' paws: the right side snatched in, the shoulder dropping over it, the whole body bracing. Too late, and too clumsy, the guard going down a moment behind the ribs.

*There. You know where it's going too.*

She gathered what she had left and put all of it into this one. Water Pulse.

— Nocturne —

It came at her ribs and she knew it would before it left the otter's paws, and knowing did her no good at all.

It hit with a drumming splash, square on the right ribs and across the chest beside them, one heavy ringing blow of water. She felt it go in. That side had gone soft already, she had known that for some time, and the water did not stop at the fur. It went into the bruise and kept going, a deep shuddering drum that rang through the old ache of teeth and frost and the edge of a paw, all of it struck at once.

The pain broke out of her.

*Rrnngh*: a snarl dragged through her teeth that wanted to be a cry, and she bit it off the instant it was out. Her body curled round the ribs before she could choose anything else, the right side jerked in hard, her head coming down. Her ribs heaved in short, shallow jerks. She could not finish a breath. Anything bigger than a shallow pant, a cough, a growl, would go straight into that side, so she did none of them, and wheezed instead.

Across her chest the water left a flat, low ache. A growl, *grr*, rolled low in her throat, and she pressed the chest down toward one forepaw as if to hold it in. The ribs leaned on it until that ached too.

She gasped hard, once, twice, then clamped her mouth shut and forced herself upright.

The hall moved.

It slid a little to the left, the pillars leaning with it, and came back, and slid again. The ringing of the water had got inside her head and stayed there. She swayed on her four paws, and the otter's small orange shape doubled for a moment and settled and doubled again. She could not trust where anything was.

At least the cold was going. The stiffness was leaking out of her left hind leg, the frost on the fur there melting into the wet now soaking her coat, the old chill gone along with it. That was the only thing about her that felt better.

Sweat had run down into the corner of one eye and it stung. She did not blink it away.
