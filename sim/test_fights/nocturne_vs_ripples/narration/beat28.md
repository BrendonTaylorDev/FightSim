— Ripples —

Her right paw was trembling against her chest, a fine fast shiver in the swollen webbing that she could not stop and did not try to.

Nocturne was still swaying a little. Every breath the white one took showed on her face as it went past the right ribs: a tightening at the corner of the mouth, a flicker in the red eyes, then gone. She was guarding with her right side turned away and the left shoulder left out in the air, carried stiff. And above it, under the ruff, the long white neck where Ripples' tails had once been wound.

Outside the walls the birdsong stopped, and started again.

The surge was still in her. Her breath had found a rhythm, in and out and in, grinding and steady, and her legs felt lighter than they had any right to.

*Don't think. Go.*

Her weight went forward before her feet did, and then her feet caught up. She lunged up at the white throat, low to high, jaws wide, the twist of it pulling hard at her chest so that a grunt jolted out of her. It was too much. She had thrown it from too far and too tired, and she felt her balance go out ahead of her even as her teeth reached the fur. Crunch.

— Nocturne —

Teeth closed on the side of her neck with a hard click of fangs, high under the ruff, and the jar of it ran into her throat and up into her left ear in the same instant.

It was clumsy. Nocturne felt that even as it landed: the jaws came in at a bad angle, half on fur, the small body behind them already falling past her. The bite clamped, ground, and let go. Whatever it was meant to do broke against her and went no further. The dark in it thinned and ran off her like rain off a roof. Her own kind of strength, that. She was built to take it.

It still stung. A short irritated snarl, *rrh*, came out through her teeth, and she bared them at the sting and held her head very still while the throat stiffened, a hiss slipping from the side of her mouth, *tsss*. When she swallowed it went down rough. The left ear twitched flat and she tipped her head away from it with a short *tss*.

The otter stumbled past her shoulder, flailed one arm out, and caught herself a step beyond.

And the hall stopped moving.

It came back to her all at once: the pillars standing where they stood, the floor flat, the otter exactly where her eyes said she was. The ringing in her head had drained away. Nocturne blinked, and blinked again, and knew where everything was.

She also knew, for the first time, how tired she was. Her breath was heavy. Her legs were heavy. The right ribs pulled with every breath and the blood thumped in her neck.

*Clear now. Enough.*

— Ripples —

She got her feet back under her and turned, and looked.

The white ruff was standing up and stayed up, raised round the neck long after the teeth were gone. The forelegs were set wider than before. The guard was coming up lower and slower. The red eyes were clear now, though, and that was worse.

*She felt that. Not much. But she felt it.*

Then her chest came back.

The numbness drained out of it like water out of a cracked bowl, all at once, and the pain poured in behind it and filled the whole of her, every bruise and claw line and the deep scorched ache the lightning had left, all of it, worse for having been away. A raw cry tore out of her, *aaah*, rising and cracking. Her body seized round her chest, her arms clamped, her shoulders came up round her ears, and the shaking started again from her elbows outward.

The surge held her up. Just. She stood bent over her own chest, mouth open, the small black eyes fixed on the white one, her breath sawing.

She did not give herself even a second. Her feet were already setting.

She stood like that, bent and braced and burning, and did not fall.
