— Ripples —

For the first time she noticed that the white one blinked slowly. Long, unhurried blinks, the red eyes closing all the way and opening again, as if nothing in this hall could happen faster than she could see it.

Ripples' breath was still not hers. It came in thin and short, scraping over the hot knot in her belly, and every time her lungs tried to fill, the stomach pulled tight and stopped them. So she did not breathe in to go. She would go on the breath out.

*She's waiting for the big one. Give her small ones.*

The left shoulder was where she looked, as she always looked there now. When the white one shifted her weight it was the left shoulder that came up last and least, and the foreleg under it stood a little stiffly, taking the weight without wanting to. The leg on the hurt side. Long and white, with the dark elbow spike jutting back from it, planted right in front of her.

Outside the walls a bird called once and stopped.

She let her breath go, all of it, in one shallow push, and went in on the end of it.

Low. Lower than the horn could follow, her chin tucked and her shoulders up round her throat, her belly protesting hard at being folded, under the white jaw and in against the left side of the chest where the foreleg met the body. The stiff white fur brushed her face. Her ears flattened and her jaws opened in the same instant.

She bit the upper foreleg above the elbow spike and bore down with everything in her jaw.

— Nocturne —

There was the wet clack of teeth closing, and a hard, pinching weight on her left foreleg.

The little jaws had her just above the elbow, and the bite crushed down through the wet fur into the muscle and jarred all the way down the leg, a dull knock through the lower foreleg and into the paw below, so that the paw jumped on the stone. A grunt went out of her, *uff*, more surprise than pain, breath and a little spit knocked out together. The breath she had been saving for her own next move was gone, and she had to fetch another.

Then she felt what the bite was made of.

It was not only teeth. Something dark rode in on them, the same thing that lived in her, the cold weight of malice that she herself had rolled across the floor not long before. And it did nothing. It spent itself against her. It soaked into her like rain into dry ground, and her body drank it in and was hardly changed for it. The worst of it slid off her and went somewhere else.

*Mine. That's mine. You can't hurt me with mine.*

The jaws let go. The otter's teeth had been in her and out again in one bite, the small body already pulling back from her chest. Not far. A body's length, no more, between two standing pillars in the green shade.

Nocturne's eyes flicked down to the leg and back, measuring. A row of small dents sat in the white fur above the elbow spike, the skin under them pinched and smarting. The forepaw throbbed faintly where the jolt had run out into it, and she shifted her weight onto it to show herself that it would take it. It took it. The limb jolted once more and was steady by the next breath.

She let out a breath she did not need to have held, slow, through her nose. Something like satisfaction went with it.

Across from her the otter was breathing again, really breathing: one great ragged gulp that dragged in over her teeth, *hahh*, and then another, deep and shaking, her chest heaving as the air came back into her, ragged but real. Her small black eyes were on the leg she had bitten. They had seen it do nothing much, and Nocturne watched her understand it.

Water dripped from her ruff onto the flagstones. She set her feet, all four of them, and braced.
