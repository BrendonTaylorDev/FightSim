— Nocturne —

Her mouth had gone dry. She had been breathing through it since the ram, and now her tongue lay rough against her teeth and every breath scraped going in.

From above, the hall looked very still. The broken pillar lay fanned across the floor behind them, a thin trickle of dust still sifting from the top of the one beside it, and in the middle of the cracked flagstones the white body stood braced over the orange one like a thing that had grown there. Settled in, she looked like something meaning to stay.

Her forepaws sat square on the otter's chest. The fur there was crushed into the skin beneath her pads, and she could feel the bruised muscle under it spread flat and hot, flinching each time the small heart beat against it. Her sickle tail lay where she had put it, across the bare cream throat, pressing a shallow line into the fur. Her hind paw rested on the belly, and the soft give of it under her toes told her exactly how much it hurt.

The twin tails lay out on the stone behind the otter, still for once, their cream tips in the dust.

*Small thing. Stubborn thing.*

She set the thought aside.

The body under her heaved, and every heave came up through her paws and her shoulders and into her right ribs. She grunted each time, a short sound through her nose. She kept it off her face. Her breath was coming fast now, keeping time with the otter's, and she wanted, briefly, to look away from the small black eyes below her.

She did not.

— Ripples —

Her chest was a hot, throbbing mass under the paws, and every heartbeat pushed more hurt into it, on top of the claws and the steel tail and the weight that had found it before. Her stomach had stopped being a place at all. It was one long climbing ache with a paw in the middle of it.

Her body wanted to rest. It kept trying to, and she kept refusing it, and she was losing the argument.

Somewhere she lost a moment. When she found it again the green sky had moved a little over the wall and she did not know how long it had been.

*Heavy. Should've... eaten less.*

She tried to fix her eyes on Nocturne and could not hold them there.

So she stopped trying to look and gathered what was in her legs instead. It took a long time. She drew her right knee up as far as the paw on her belly would let her, and her whole side shook with the effort of it, and then she drove the foot out sideways at the white hind leg as hard as she had anything left to do it with.

It was slow. It was late. Her heel skidded off the thick white fur at the hock and hit nothing at all.

The hunter had felt it coming. The hind paw on her stomach pressed down, deliberate, all its weight, and ground in.

The scream cracked in the middle. A thin, wavering *iiiiih* went up off the pillars, and her body curled round the paw without asking her, shoulders lifting off the stone, knees jerking up, her left arm scrabbling at the flagstone beside her in short useless scrapes. Water ran out of the corners of her eyes and into the fur of her cheeks. There was nothing in her head but her stomach.

When it let her go she lay back down, flat, her head rocking slowly side to side on the stone, her mouth open on small raw cries she did not have the strength to close it on.

The paw stayed where it was.

— Nocturne —

She watched.

The otter's jaw clenched, unclenched, clenched again, the black marks on her cheeks twitching each time, and Nocturne knew she was biting down on a sound and losing it. The left paw kept creeping across to the cream belly, to the edge of her own hind paw, touching the fur there and falling away.

*She keeps touching it. She can't help it.*

Then the mouth hung open and stayed open. She had stopped trying to close it.

Nocturne's own breath was still coming hard, the right ribs hitching at the top of each one. Her mouth was dry as dust. She shifted her weight a little forward onto the chest and turned her head half away, unhurried, as if the struggling had already been put somewhere behind her.

She had felt how slow that kick was. The next one would be slower.

She settled lower, and waited for it.
