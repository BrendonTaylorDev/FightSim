— Nocturne —

*Hurry and she slips you.* It was still there, turning over in her head, the last thing she had told herself.

She had stopped thinking about the hold and simply was it.

The otter was breathing so hard now that her whole small body rocked with it under Nocturne's paws, a short, rolling heave from the hips to the shoulders and back again. Nocturne could feel the chest give under her forepaws at every one. The bruised flesh had been pressed in so far that it bulged up thick and hot round the edges of her pads. The orange fur there was crushed flat and dark. The sickle tail lay across the cream throat. The coat had creased into a line along its edge, the fur parting where the blade pressed. Her hind paw on the belly sank in deeper than it had at first. The soft middle had changed its shape round her toes, puffed and tight, and the otter flinched under it each time Nocturne's weight shifted, so that Nocturne had begun to hold her balance very still.

She kept one eye on the small face and the other on the left arm, the one that could still move.

Her own breath was going ragged. She caught it and forced it back into a slow, even rhythm through her nose, and on the third breath her ribs flared under the strain, and a grunt came out of her that had more pain in it than she meant.

Warm stone and wet moss. The hall smelled of both, close and green, the air not moving.

*This should be over.*

Yet the struggles were shortening. She could feel it in her paws, each one a little smaller and a little later than the last, and something hard and quiet settled in her chest at the feel of it.

— Ripples —

Every breath she fought for came back out of her as a whimper, thin and high, squeezed up from the chest under the paws. Her stomach had begun to cramp around the hind paw, twisting on itself in slow knots on top of the old claw marks and the slash and everything since. Each cramp pulled water out of her eyes.

She squinted up. The white face would not hold still. It slid, and doubled, and came back.

She was sinking. She had not moved at all, and she was sinking, down through the stone.

*Arm. Left arm. Legs, a bit. Tails.*

Not much.

She used it anyway. She dug her left elbow into the flagstone, set her heels, and wrenched her shoulders round to the right, trying to roll her body under the weight and get onto her front, where she could get her feet under her. Her shoulders came a hand's width off the stone. Her twin tails stuttered against the floor.

The forepaws came down.

The hunter's whole weight dropped into her chest, both paws together, and the chest that had nothing left to take it took it anyway. A gasping, hiccuping shriek broke out of her, *hik-eeeh*, and cracked in the middle and went on. Her shoulders slammed back flat. Her right paw flew up and pressed itself to her chest beside the white forepaws, clutching at her own fur, and her left one scrabbled at the stone in short, frantic scrapes. Her legs pushed and slipped, pushed and slipped on the grit. Her whole body shook from the jaw down, and for a long moment there was nothing in her head at all but her chest.

It eased off. It did not go away.

Her muscles trembled with an effort that went nowhere. Her paw stayed where it was, curled against the matted fur, too heavy to lift away.

A tiredness was coming up through her now, so deep and soft it felt almost kind, like someone close by telling her she could stop.

She did not answer it. She lay and breathed in whimpers, the white face swimming above her, and the hall went quiet around them, and the quiet went on.
