— Nocturne —

The otter's left arm drew back across the stone, slowly, the webbed paw dragging, and stopped halfway and lay there.

She was not guarding anything any more. The arms that had been folded over her chest had come apart, the right one lying curled close against her side, the left one wherever it had last fallen. There was nothing left in them to guard with.

Nocturne kept her own breath slow and even through her nose, so the body under her could not find a rhythm in it to time anything against. Her forepaws were sunk in the otter's chest, the crushed fur bunched up hard in ridges round her pads, and she could feel the whole small ribcage squeezed out of its shape beneath them. The sickle tail lay across the cream throat, its edge turned away, printing a clean groove into the fur. Her hind paw sat on the belly. The coat there had creased under her toes, the fur parting along the edge of her pad in a thin line.

She kept her right side turned away, her weight angled off it. It did not help much. The ribs set up a steady, hot ache that rose to meet every small heave from below, as if her body was answering the otter's on her behalf.

Up on the wall, a vine swayed where nothing had touched it.

She felt every shift beneath her before it became anything.

*Don't hurry it. Hurry and she slips you.*

And under the impatience, under the pain in her side, there was something else, something without words. The fight had not gone out of the small body. She could feel it under her paws, refusing.

— Ripples —

The hind paw did not move. It did not have to. Her stomach had been holding on round it for a long time, and now, all at once, it could not.

The pain went over a line. It stopped being a hurt in one place and became the whole lower half of her, and a sound came up out of her that she had never made before, a long, cracked, broken *aaahhh* that rose and wavered and ran out of breath and started again. Her hips twitched against the stone. Her legs shuddered, both of them, from the thigh down to the webbed toes, and would not stop. The waves of it rolled up through her into her chest and left her gasping at the top of each one.

Water ran sideways out of her eyes and back into her ears.

Her limbs felt very far away, and very heavy, and not quite hers.

For a moment she forgot what she was fighting. Then she remembered, and the forgetting frightened her awake.

*Out. Side. Go.*

She gathered it into her hips and her shoulders, what there was, and twisted. Her back slid an inch on the flagstones, then another, her shoulder blades scraping grit, her twin tails stuttering against the stone as she tried to worm her body out from under the paws to the left, toward the open floor.

It was so slow. The hunter felt it begin.

The sickle tail turned against her throat and pressed down. The hard smooth length of it bore into the bare fur under her chin, and her breath caught behind it. A yip and a hiss came out of her together, *yipss*, choked short, and she coughed against the blade and swallowed hard, and swallowed again. Her chin jerked down to cover her throat. It met the tail, and could not.

She stopped sliding. She lay where she had got to, which was nowhere.

The stone under her back was cool. It had been cool all along, a small, nagging thing. Now it seemed to be climbing into her.

Her gaze wandered, lost its mark, and found it again more slowly.

— Ripples —

The white shape above her swam and settled.

The hunter was breathing heavily, but her face gave nothing away. Ripples watched her side instead. The right ribs, where the fur stood up over the swelling, hitched at the top of every breath, and the white body was leaning off them, holding that side stiff. The left shoulder sat a little low. The red eyes went over Ripples' belly and her throat and came back to her face, unhurried, checking.

*She wants me looking at her eyes. Watch the shoulders.*

She tried to look down at herself. Grit had been pressed into the cream fur of her stomach, round the edges of the paw, dark grains in the matted coat. Her ribs creaked when she twisted to see more, and she stopped twisting.

She grunted on every breath out now, a small sound squeezed up from her belly whether she wanted it or not, *hnh*, *hnh*.

Above the white shoulder the sky came back into focus, green-edged, with a single bird crossing it.
