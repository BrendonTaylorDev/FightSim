— Nocturne —

She had less than she'd had. She could feel exactly how much less: it sat in her right side like a stone wedged between two ribs, and every breath went in around it.

She had to take the breath for this in two parts. Half, a catch where the ribs were, then the rest, dragged in past it. Her lungs filled at last, the first full breath since the paw had gone in, ragged but real, and she let it out slowly through her teeth.

The otter was breathing high and short, sparing her own chest. One forearm had drifted across her stomach between blows and stayed there, the blue fin lying flat against the cream fur. Both of them were holding themselves together in the middle now.

Nocturne shifted her footing half a step to the left. Then half a step back. The angle was never the same twice, and the otter's small black eyes followed each shift and could not settle.

*One more like that and she'll start to doubt.*

Her horn began to glow. Violet light gathered along its curved edge from the root to the tip, cold and humming, and the air round her head tightened like a held breath. The last of the water was drying off her face in the heat of her own skin, and the ruff on her chest lifted a little, no longer plastered flat, as the fur began to come back to itself.

She swept her head sideways in one hard stroke. The light tore off the horn and went across the floor in pale crescents, one after another, spinning, aimed low at the orange back as the otter turned.

— Ripples —

She read it in the shoulders.

The white shoulders bunched and the head dipped, and before the first crescent left the horn Ripples was already moving, springing back off both feet. The blades came after her. The first one whispered past her chest so close that the fur there lifted in its wind. The second one cut the air where her head had been and struck a pillar behind her, and dust sifted down from the top of the carved stone in a thin pale stream.

The rest snapped at nothing and faded.

She landed light. The burn was in her head now, crawling along the top of her skull between her ears like a hot hand laid there, and it flared when she landed. She did not let it slow her. The horn was still swinging through the end of its stroke and the white muzzle was right there, low, following it round.

*Now. While she's turned.*

She came back in on the rebound and slapped her left paw across the end of the dark muzzle, hard, edge first, on the nose.

— Nocturne —

The nose.

Of everything on her, the nose. It was a small blow, a quick flat crack from the side, and it lit the end of her muzzle up like a struck spark. Her eyes flooded. Her head tossed back on its own, a snort blowing out of her, sneeze-like and wet, and for one ridiculous moment she could not see the otter at all through the water in her eyes.

A low growl, *grr*, came up from her chest.

The sting spread backward along the bridge of her muzzle, thin and bright and stupid, a pain without any weight in it. It did nothing to her. It changed nothing about her legs or her breath or the stone in her ribs. But it was her face. Her nose. A thing that size had slapped her on the nose.

She blinked hard. The hall came back, green and wavering, then clear. Her nose throbbed, and it went on throbbing, small and steady, every beat of her heart.

*That,* she thought, *was a mistake.*

She shook her head once, hard, and the last drops of the otter's water flew off the tuft on top of it. Her breath came in, the whole way now, catching only once on the ribs. Her ears came up. Her lips drew back slowly from her fangs.

Across from her the otter had danced back half a stride and stood with her eyes wide open, black and bright, watching to see what she had started.
