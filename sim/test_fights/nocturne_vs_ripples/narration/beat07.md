— Nocturne —

The otter's weight settled back onto her heels, and Nocturne felt rather than saw her own body answer it, her paws shifting under her before she had asked them to.

Close quarters. The two of them a short lunge apart on the cracked floor between the pillars, the white and the orange, the green light lying over both. The frost was still in the fur over her ribs, stiff and cold, and she shook herself once, hard, from the ruff to the hips, to get it out. It did not all go. Her joints answered slowly, as if the cold had got into the hinges of them, and the shiver ran on along her flank after she had told it to stop.

She kept her eyes off the otter's face. Faces lied. Shoulders did not.

The otter's eyes were on her all the same, small and black and quick, missing nothing. And her arms were coming up, the right one first, the forearm turning outward to guard, so that the blue fin along it stood up between the two of them like a little shield held edge-on.

*That. Take the fin and she has nothing to block with.*

Eagerness went through her, sharp, and her weight rocked forward a half-beat before she meant it to. She let it carry her. Her breath hissed in through bared teeth, and then she lunged.

Her head went down and in under her own horn, her jaws opening wide, lips peeled back off the long white fangs, all of her aimed at that blue fin. Her timing was right. The chill in her made her line a hair wrong, a hair low, the neck not quite answering as quickly as her eyes.

Her jaws closed.

— Ripples —

The wet clack of teeth closing came first, right in front of her face.

Then the hurt arrived, and it was in her hand.

She had got her right paw in the way of it, more by luck than by design. The fin had been what the teeth wanted, and they had missed it by a finger's width and found the webbed paw below instead, closing across the back of it and the soft web between her fingers in one hard crunch. The same bite jarred up the arm behind it, a dull knock through the fin and a duller one into the upper arm, all of it in the same instant and gone again as the jaws let go.

For a moment she simply looked at her own paw.

Then it stung. It stung all at once, deep and hot, a ring of hard points across the back of her hand, and the web between her fingers throbbed. She snatched the paw back and shook it hard, flicking it out to the side as if she could shake the sting off onto the flagstones. A quick, surprised *prrt* came out of her, more indignation than pain, and then an angry chitter through her teeth, *tch-tch*, and her black cheek marks twitched.

Her fin had clamped flat to her forearm. She made it lift again.

Behind the stinging there was the arm: an odd, faint jar, the forearm fin aching as if it had been flicked, the upper arm bruised-heavy for a breath from the knock. She rolled the right shoulder once, checking, and everything above the wrist moved as it should.

The paw itself she curled into a fist, slowly, and opened it again. The fingers came. They came a little stiffly, and the web pulled when she spread them, and a few small beads of red stood in the orange fur across the back of it where the fangs had pressed in. When she turned her wrist there was a nagging pull, all the way through to the palm.

She would favour it now. She knew she would, by a hair, the way a body favours a stubbed toe, so little that only an opponent would see. This opponent would.

A lizard flicked across a flagstone behind the white paws and was gone into the moss.

*All right. Now I know her reach.*

She brought the paw back up into her guard anyway, beside the good one, and tucked her chin. Her twin tails had stuttered at the bite; now they found their rhythm again behind her, steady and low.

She had seen something, while the jaws were open in front of her face: the white head had come down a little slow, and a little crooked. The cold she had put into those ribs was still in there.
