— Ripples —

A little grit stirred round her toes as she shifted her feet, and the stone under them was dry and sure.

The white one had pulled her head back after the bite and stood with her front squared to her, all four paws planted, the frost still clinging in a pale patch over her ribs. She was keeping the left shoulder a little behind her now, turned out of the line of the fight, so that her right side came forward to meet whatever came next. The right side, with the cold still in it.

Ripples' ears went flat and stayed there. Her paw throbbed. Her belly pulled when she breathed. Everything she got out of this white thing she had to work for, and she was sick of working.

*Close the gap before she breathes.*

She bounced once on the balls of her feet, and on the way down she called the water.

It came up round her from nowhere, from the damp in the air and the puddles at her heels and somewhere inside her, a skin of it sliding over her fur from her ears to her feet, cool and tight and alive. Her twin tails began to spin behind her, faster and faster until they blurred into a disc, and the water round them roared.

Then she went.

Not running. Launched. Her feet left the flagstones and her whole body went flat and straight in the air, arms tucked, chin tucked, head down, the water streaming back off her in a long, hissing sheath, like a spear thrown from a hand. The hall went past in a smear of grey and green. She angled her rush a fraction wide, to hit from the side, where the right ribs were and the frost was, and drove the crown of her head and her shoulders at the middle of the broad white chest.

— Nocturne —

There was a heavy slap of water, and then she was wearing it.

The otter hit her in the chest like a thrown stone in a bucket of river: a hard, bony knock through the middle of the ruff, and the force of it running out along her right side into the bruised ribs and down the flank behind them, and all of it at once, all of it cold and wet and roaring past her ears. Her front paws skidded a finger's width on the wet stone and held. She took it where she stood.

Water sluiced off her in sheets. It ran down her face into her eyes and her mouth, and she sputtered and blinked, hacking it back out, *hff*, through her closed teeth.

The worst of it was the ribs. The hurt that the frost had left there was still there, and the new one landed squarely on top of it and woke it, so that for a breath the whole right side of her chest ached tight and warm under the soaked fur. She hissed through the side of her mouth, *tsss*. Somewhere further back along the same side, the flank answered with a small, sick twinge and then let her be.

She drew in a long breath to test her chest. It went all the way in. It held.

Her face tightened for a moment, then smoothed out on purpose.

*Low and fast. I should have had that.*

She rocked back on her haunches and came forward again with her teeth bared, and lowered her horn, her jaw tight.

The water had done what water did. It had gone right through her coat to the skin, every hair of her plastered flat and dark and heavy, the ruff hanging off her neck in sodden ropes, the tail dragging a little with the weight of it. The cold from the frost was gone; it had washed out with everything else, and her joints moved freely again. But she was heavier. She could feel it in every step. And under the fur she was wet to the skin, and some part of her did not like that at all, a prickling unease all down her spine.

Green light came through the leaves above the walls and lay on the flagstones.

Below her, in the puddle her own dripping was making, a white shape with a dark face looked up at her, broken into pieces by the falling drops.
