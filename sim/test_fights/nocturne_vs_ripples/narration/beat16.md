— Ripples —

The red eyes wanted her to be afraid of the fire. Ripples could see it in them: a narrow, patient, waiting look, as if the burn would do the rest of the work.

The burn had moved down. It had crawled from her shoulder into her right paw while she breathed, and the webbing of it stung now as though she had dipped it in hot ash. She flexed it. It throbbed. She let it.

Nocturne shook her left shoulder out, a quick roll of the joint under the wet white fur, when she thought nobody was looking. Ripples was looking. And below that shoulder, on the right side, the fur over the ribs still lay in a flattened, dented patch where her freezing teeth had gone in and her water charge had broken after.

*Enough careful.*

Green light came down through the leaves along the top of the wall and lay on the white flank like a target.

Her chest would not fold. She tried to crouch for the spring and the bruise in the middle of her stopped her halfway, so she went in standing up, short quick steps across the flagstones, timed to the white body's breath out, when the ribs sagged and the muscles over them went slack. Her right paw came up, burned and stiff, and she turned it edge-on.

She aimed past the surface, at a point somewhere behind it.

The chop came down with all her small hard weight behind it, the edge of the paw driving at the dented place over the ribs. The right blow, at last, after all of it.

— Nocturne —

It went in with a hollow thump that carried round the pillars.

Not into the fur, not into the muscle. Through them. The edge of that small paw found the gap between two ribs, the very place the teeth had marked, and something in it went straight past everything she was and into a part of her that had no armour against this, had never had any. The jolt ran along the ribs into her chest beside them in the same instant, a duller knock under the wet ruff.

Every muscle she owned jumped at once and then hung slack for a heartbeat.

Her mouth opened and nothing came out. Her chest heaved once, twice, against a wall, and then the sound arrived, a strangled grunt, *ghk*, that was more shock than pain, and after it the pain, and her front knees dipped under her and she caught them and locked them. She would not go down for a paw that size. She did not go down.

The air would not come. Her flanks pumped and the breath stopped halfway in, every time, at the place where the ribs were, as if a hand were pressed flat there. A round, hot ache bloomed out from the spot where the paw had gone in and spread into the ruff over her chest, and the two hurts ran together into one sore stretch of her down that whole side. Her ears roared with her own pulse, each beat in time with the throb.

A snarl came out of her, short, *rrh*, the most she could make with no air behind it.

She knew without thinking that she could not afford many more of those.

She set her teeth, rounded her shoulders over the ribs, and forced her head back up.

— Ripples —

She stood where the blow had left her, a pace off the white flank, and watched.

It had worked. It had worked better than she had dared hope; she had felt it go in, felt the white body give round her paw in a way it never had before. Now the big body stood with its shoulders rounded and its right side drawn in, the tail rigid, and the ribs on that side barely moving while the rest of her heaved.

The horned head turned to keep Ripples in view, and it turned slower than it had.

Then the red eyes came up to her, and the anger in them was new. Not the cold, measuring contempt from before. Something hot.

*Careful. She's still got all her tricks.*

Ripples kept her face still and let the small cold pleasure of it sit behind her teeth. She tilted her head back a little to get more air in herself. Her chest still ached with every breath. Her paw stung where the burn had settled, and it stung worse for the chop, the webbing hot and tight.

She ran her tongue over her teeth. They tasted of river water and, faintly, of the white fur she had bitten earlier, bitter and dry.
