— Nocturne —

She blinked, and when her eyes opened again the otter was still bent nearly double over her chest, a step past where she had meant to be.

Nocturne watched her. Every time the small body shifted on its feet, even with its own weight, a sound jumped out of it, a short catch in the throat, as if the belly and chest were being jolted from inside. Rainwater stood in a hollow of the floor beside the orange feet, and the feet were set wrong, the weight still out past them where the clumsy lunge had thrown it. The otter was still finding her balance.

Nocturne tilted her head. It was a cold sort of interest, almost idle. Her own breath was heavy and her ribs dragged at every pull of it, but her head was clear, and that was all she needed.

*She's trying not to show me. Too late.*

The otter's arms came in over her chest the moment Nocturne's weight shifted: snatched in, shoulders hunching, the whole body bracing round the one place. It told Nocturne exactly where to put it.

She was already moving before the opening had finished showing itself. The sickle tail went hard and heavy along its length, cold as iron. The turn started at her shoulders and finished at the tip of her tail, and the tail came down across the orange chest from above. Iron Tail.

— Ripples —

She saw it coming and pulled her arms in over her chest and tucked her head down, the way a cold animal tucks itself up, and she was a whole heartbeat too slow.

It landed with a deep, slapping thud straight across her chest and through the crossed left arm in front of it.

Steel. Her body knew steel. Anywhere else it would have rung off her and gone. But it came down on the one place she had nothing left in, and there was no shrugging that off.

It carried her. Her feet scrambled under her to catch up with where her body had gone, one step back, two, three, her heels skidding on the wet moss, her back arching as she tipped. For a moment she was falling. The sky was there above the broken wall, green-gold and wide. Her tails thrashed out in every direction and then dug down behind her and pushed, and somehow her feet found the stone and held her there, swaying, upright.

Then the pain arrived.

It began in the middle of her chest as a single point, very small and very white, and for an instant she thought that was all. Then it opened. It spread out from that point through every bruise and claw line and scorch-mark on her, across her ribs, up under her collarbones, into her throat and down into her belly, until there was nothing of her that was not inside it. A cry broke out of her, *aaah-ih-ih*, cracking into hoarse squeaks, and she could not stop it or make it smaller. Her ribs heaved in short shallow jerks. Her eyes streamed and the hall swam through them.

She tried to breathe and got a sip. Another sip. Each one ended in a whimper she could hear but did not choose.

Her left arm had gone in front of her chest and taken its share, and now every heartbeat landed there like a knuckle, deep in the bitten muscle above the elbow. An *eek-* squeaked out of her and was cut off. When she tried to lift the arm it came up slow and shaking, and she let it fall back over her chest and clutched it there.

The shaking was everywhere now. Her knees, her elbows, the hanging tips of her tails.

Her chest sagged, and she let it sag and set the rest of herself.

She lifted her eyes to the white one. There was no anger left in them. There was something harder, quieter, very small, the last of the surge burning down to a coal.

*Not yet. Feet. Find your feet.*

Her feet were under her again. Her balance had come back with them, and her guard came up, late and low. Somewhere under all of it, in her right arm, in the edge of her paw, something was still ready.
