— Ripples —

Her belly reminded her as she breathed in: three thin lines of sting under the cream fur, pulling when she straightened.

She set it aside and looked.

The white one stood a body's length off with her own blow still ringing in her, water dripping from her ruff, her head not quite steady. Down the hall behind her the carved faces on the pillars looked on with their worn stone eyes, as if none of this were new to them. The left shoulder was carried a little high now, and a little close, tucked in against the body like something being protected. The right foreleg took more of the weight because of it. It was planted. It was the leg she was standing on.

*Low. Under the head. Take the good leg.*

Something close to enjoyment went through her, a quick bright flicker, and she put it away before it could reach her face.

She went in low. Not a charge, just a sudden drop and a scuttle, her knees bent, her head down below the line of the white jaw, so that the horn passed somewhere above her. Her twin tails held her steady behind her. The cold was already coming up into her mouth as she went, the way the water had come into her hands: a numbing ache along her gums, frost forming on her teeth, her breath smoking white in the warm green air.

Her teeth showed white and wet as she closed the distance. She turned her head sideways to get her jaws round the right foreleg, just above the elbow spike.

The white one twisted.

It was a small movement, a quarter turn of the body away from her, and it was enough. The foreleg went back out of her reach. Her teeth went where she had sent them, near enough, just not where she meant: into the fur of the white ribs behind the elbow.

She bit anyway.

— Nocturne —

Half a turn of her body was enough to give the bite her right ribs instead.

There was a grinding squeak of teeth on hide, close under her own ear, and then cold.

Not the cold of water. Something worse. It went in where the teeth went in, a hard, aching freeze that seized the muscle over her ribs and spread out through it in a flat, burning sheet, and the force of the bite jarred inward through her chest a heartbeat later, duller, so that the breath she had been taking stopped halfway. Her red eyes flew wide, the pupils shrinking. She huffed out through her nose, *hmph*, short and hard, and could not finish the breath that should have come after it.

The otter's teeth were out of her at once; it had been a bite and nothing more.

A tight, frozen sting lay across the right side of her chest where the teeth had been. She hissed at it through her teeth. She glanced down once at her ribs: frost had bloomed in the white fur there in a pale crust, and a ring of tooth marks sat in the middle of it, the skin under them red and stinging. Then she refused to look again.

*Don't show it. Don't show anything.*

Her horn jerked up and came down again, a short, lowering nod with all her fury in it.

The cold did not stay where it had been put. It crept outward over her skin from the bite, through the wet fur of her neck and ruff, finding the water still clinging there and stiffening it. A shiver went through her shoulders. Her legs felt slow, as if the joints had thickened. When she shifted her right forepaw, it came a little late.

But her head was clear. The pillars had stopped leaning. The hall stood still round her for the first time since the water, every flagstone where it belonged, and the otter was exactly where her eyes said she was.

Nocturne shut her eyes for the length of one breath, letting the cold settle, and opened them on the orange shape in front of her.
