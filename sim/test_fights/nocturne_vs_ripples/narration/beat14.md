— Ripples —

Her weight sat mostly on her right foot. The left had the claw lines down the thigh and the stiff hip above them, and it held, but it was the right she trusted, flat on a cool flagstone with a skin of moss under the toes.

Her breath was still short. It went in in small, whistling pieces and each one bumped against the hot bruise in the middle of her chest and stopped there. She could not run on air like that. She could not jump. But she could spit.

The white shape had stopped walking. The sickle tail stood up stiff behind her, and the horn was level, and she had turned the left shoulder away and was guarding with the right side of her body, the sore side kept out of it. The face was the thing she had turned toward Ripples: dark blue-grey, the red eyes in it narrow, the muzzle a little open as she breathed.

*Face. She hates the wet.*

The carved faces on the pillars looked down on both of them, mouths open, as if they wanted to see.

There was no time to aim properly and Ripples did not try. She gathered the water up from her belly, felt it push up past the bruised chest so that the twist of it pulled at her and a grunt came out of her nose, and she filled her cheeks until the black marks on them stretched.

Her body hummed, and she pointed the whole of herself at Nocturne.

She fired from where she stood, quick and ugly, a hard jet of water straight out of her mouth at the white face.

— Nocturne —

It hit with a wet crack.

The jet took her full on the muzzle and in the same instant spread and slapped up across the side of her head into her left ear, one cold blast over both at once. Her head rocked back and she was square again before she had finished being surprised, her feet exactly where they had been. Her jaw clacked shut with the force of it, teeth knocking together, and the water poured off her chin in a stream and then fell in a patter of drops on the stone round her paws.

A grunt came out through her closed teeth, *hff*.

The muzzle stung, a flat, slapped sting like a cuff from a heavy paw, and her face screwed up tight against it for a heartbeat. She gave her head a single brisk shake. Water flew off her in a fan.

The ear was worse for a moment, because it was full. Cold had gone right down inside it, and the world on that side was a muffled roar. A hiss came out of the side of her mouth, *tsss*, and her right forepaw lifted toward the ear on its own and stopped halfway. She put it back down. She was not going to paw at her own head in front of the otter.

The ear flicked. Flicked again. Water ran out of it down her cheek.

*Hurt?* she asked herself. *No. Wet.*

That was the worst of it, and she hated it. The water was everywhere. It had gone down her ruff and through it to the skin, and the thick white fur that had been drying since the otter's charge was plastered flat and dark again from her throat to her chest, heavy, clinging, cold. It dripped from the tuft on her head and from the tip of her horn. She could smell nothing but river.

Her guard did not even drop.

Across the floor the otter was breathing. Really breathing now: one long ragged gulp that went all the way down and came out with a shudder, then another, the cream chest lifting behind her raised arms. The spit had cleared her. Her small black eyes stayed on Nocturne's face through all of it, watching to see what the water had done.
