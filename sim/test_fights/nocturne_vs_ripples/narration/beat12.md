— Ripples —

She stopped waiting.

Close was where the white claws lived; she had three fresh lines in her thigh to prove it. So she gave the distance back. She sprang away off both feet, backward across the cracked floor, landed beside a leaning pillar and felt the hip jar as she came down, and did not care. Out here the horn could not reach her. Out here she could breathe.

She did not breathe well. Her stomach pulled at every gulp, and she kept her body turned half away, the cream belly shielded behind one shoulder, even as she set her feet.

Across the hall Nocturne stood in the green light with her head low. Ripples watched the left shoulder. It sat a little lower than the right, and every so often, when the white body forgot to hold it up, it dropped out of her guard altogether, the flank behind it bare.

*In and out. Don't let her grab you.*

She drew the cold up from somewhere under her ribs. It gathered at the back of her throat and numbed her tongue, and frost needled along her lips and the edges of her small teeth. Outside the walls the birds stopped singing, and started again.

She closed one eye, sighted along her own muzzle at that dropped shoulder and the long white flank behind it, and opened her jaws.

She breathed out, and the breath kept coming and froze as it went.

The beam came out of her pale blue and smoking, a straight hard line of cold, and she put more into it than she needed, all the anger from the thigh and the belly, and drove it across the hall at the white side.

— Nocturne —

She was already gone.

She had seen the otter's jaw open and her own legs had decided, springing her back off all four paws, light and fast, the bitten foreleg and the sore shoulder taking the landing without a word from her. The beam went through the place where her flank had been. She felt the cold of it pass, a breath of winter across the damp fur of her ruff, and then it struck the flagstones beyond and laid a white crust of frost over a puddle there, with a small dry crackle.

Close. A tail's width.

She landed, and held, and watched the end of it fade to nothing. The otter's jaws were still open. She was still standing where she had fired from, chest out and breathing hard, and the cold had cost her: her guard was nowhere.

*Now.*

Nocturne went back in the way she had come out. Two strides. The right forepaw came up and drove flat into the cream chest with the whole weight of her shoulder behind it, the blow landing high where the black pulse had already broken over that chest and her first lunge of the fight had gone in before that.

The otter's breath came out of her in a squeak bitten off short, *ik*. The new hurt sank straight into the old one and became one bruise, bigger, and her arms pulled in across it. She doubled a little round the spot, shoulders hunched, chin down, and stayed there for a heartbeat, shaking at the elbows.

Then she straightened. Nocturne saw the cost: a thin wheeze on the next breath in, and on the one after.

Nocturne drew back a step to watch. A tail-width from the cold, and the otter had paid for it.

The frost on the puddle was already melting in the warm air. A drip ran off the edge of a vine somewhere behind the altar, and the hall was quiet again.
