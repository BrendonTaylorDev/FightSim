— Ripples —

Her breath went out of her in a long ragged shudder, and before the next one came she set her feet.

They were still sliding. She had been driven back across the stone and her body was still trying to catch up with itself, but she planted one foot and then the other and made them stay, square under her, and they stayed.

Her chest would not bend. She could not fold round it any more; it had set like something poured and left to harden, and every movement had to go round it. Her left arm hung in front of it, useless for anything but covering. That left the right. The paw throbbed, swollen and shaking. It would have to do.

Across the floor the white one stood waiting. Even from here Ripples could see the right ribs shaking under the wet fur, a fine fast tremor she could not hide, and the left shoulder carried back, out of the line of things. The ribs. Her teeth had gone there, her paw had gone there, her water had gone there. All fight long she had been working that one place.

The surge was going. She could feel it draining down out of her, the way a river drops after rain. There was one more thing in it. Only one.

She gathered all of it up, everything still left in her legs and her back and her one good arm, and came. Slowly at first, a lurch, a step, then all at once, like a tree coming down. Her weight went forward onto her toes. Her right arm came up and over, the edge of the paw hard as a blade, straight down at the white ribs. Brick Break.

Her breath went out in a hard huff behind the blow.

— Nocturne —

She heard it coming, the scuff of feet and the huff of breath, and her head came round on its own.

She did not think about it. She knew what a blow like that would do if it reached her ribs: it was the one kind of blow her body had never known how to take, and it had found that side three times already. Her head dipped and swung, and the horn came across her body like a raised blade, and the chop came down on it instead.

It landed with a solid whump, like a sack dropped, on the flat of the horn. The shock went down through the root of it into her skull and ran on into her neck and shoulders, and her head was knocked half aside. Her balance went for a breath and came back. She snorted hard, a sneezing huff, and tossed her head, and a low growl, *grr*, rolled out of her.

That was all. The horn was the hardest thing on her. It took the blow and kept almost none of it, a dull ring at the root and nothing more.

She blinked once, hard, and her gaze sharpened.

The carved faces on the pillars looked on.

— Ripples —

It hit the horn.

She felt it through her own paw before she understood it: not the give of fur and bruised flesh but a hard curved edge, solid as the temple floor. The jolt came back up her arm into her swollen fingers, and into her chest, and she heard her own thin squeal as if from a long way off.

Then the surge went out of her completely.

She could feel it go, like a plug pulled from the bottom of her. Everything it had been holding back came in behind it at full size: the chest, the belly, the arm, the paw, the head, all of it at once and all of it hers again. Her knees shook. She was still standing, but only because her feet happened to be under her, and she knew it.

Her balance came back. She stood square, guard up where it belonged, both arms across her chest. There was nothing behind them now. She had put it all into that one, and the white one had taken it on her horn.

*Nothing. All that, and nothing.*

She stood there, gasping, emptied, and looked at Nocturne.

— Nocturne —

The ring in her horn faded. Nocturne straightened and set her paws.

The otter stood in front of her with her guard up and nothing left behind it. Nocturne could see that as plainly as the shaking in the small orange knees.

Her own ribs throbbed. She was tired, and her breath came heavy. She lowered her head and gathered her legs under her, ready.
