— Nocturne —

The otter's weight moved first, a small shift from the left foot to the right, before anything else of her did.

Nocturne had seen her do it three times now. Shift, and wait, and watch to see if the white one shifted to answer. She did it on purpose. She wanted to be followed.

Nocturne did not follow. She stood in her own dripping on the flagstones, heavy with water, a body's length from the orange shape, and let the otter's feet do their small dance with no partner. The left shoulder throbbed in time with her pulse. The right side of her chest ached tight when she breathed deep. Nothing she could not carry a long while yet.

The otter was breathing faster than she was. Her cream belly rose and fell above those three thin claw lines, and her right paw was held a little lower in the guard than the left.

Then the small black eyes flicked, for half a heartbeat, to the water still running off Nocturne's ruff.

Nocturne was in that half heartbeat.

She did not lunge. This was not a thing to throw on the run. She planted all four paws, spread them, set her weight low, and lowered her head until the curved black horn pointed at the otter's middle. Something gathered under her ribs, cold and heavy and black, a pressure that was not breath, and it came up through her chest and along her neck into the horn, building, building, until her whole skull ached with holding it and the air round the horn darkened and trembled.

She let it build until it hurt to hold, then let it go.

— Ripples —

A heavy whump of force came across the stone, and the light went out of the air in front of her.

It was a ring of black, rolling outward from the white horn along the flagstones, wider than she was, and there was nowhere to put herself where it was not. It hit her all at once: a blunt shove through her chest that drove the breath out of her in a rush, a knock to the side of her head that set her eyes watering, and in her stomach, where the claws had already opened the fur, something much worse. It went into the stinging lines there and found the bruise under them and sank in, cold and sick and heavy, as if something with no hands had reached into her belly and closed.

A sharp, startled *yeep* came out of her, high and cracked. Her knees dipped. Her body folded a fraction round her middle before she could stop it, shoulders curling in, both arms coming down across her belly.

She held her ground. She held it. Her feet did not move on the stone.

But she could not breathe.

Her ribs jerked, once, twice, three times, short empty pulls, and nothing went in. Her mouth was open. Her bare throat worked on air that would not come. Her eyes swam with water. The stomach felt packed with heat, the skin straining round it, and every pull of her lungs tugged at it and made it worse.

She held her middle rigid, breathing shallow above the ache, and the air came back at last in a thin, ragged gulp, *hhk*, and then another.

Her head felt weak with it, light and far away from the rest of her. One ear kept flicking back toward her own belly, as if it could hear the hurt there.

*Get the air back first. Then she pays.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne stayed where she was and watched.

The horn still ached from what had gone out of it. Her chest rose and fell, slower than the otter's, and her sickle tail had gone still behind her; only its tip twitched, once, and again.

The otter had stayed on her feet. That was the first thing. But she was folded over her belly, her arms locked across it, and the cream fur there was dark and hot looking round the three claw lines, flushed under the fur. Her ribs were working in quick, shallow jerks, nothing deeper. Her throat kept swallowing. A little dust sifted down from the top of a pillar behind her and she did not even turn her head to it.

Her twin tails had dropped. They hung behind her, barely turning.

*That one went in. Look at her.*

Nocturne held her breath a moment too long, waiting for what the otter would do with herself now, and caught herself at it.

The otter was very still. Hunched, quiet, only the ribs moving.

Still could mean hurt. Still could mean waiting. Nocturne had seen enough of this one to know it might be both.
