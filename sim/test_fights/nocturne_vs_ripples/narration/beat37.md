— Nocturne —

She had nearly drifted. She knew it only when her eyes cleared again and the small face beneath her came back into focus, sharp, the black cheek marks, the cream spots above the eyes, the lids half down.

Even now the otter kept her chin tucked. Even flat on her back, her throat under a blade, she had drawn her head down toward her chest to cover it from above, the way she had all fight long. It covered nothing. The sickle tail lay across the bare throat and had pressed it flat, the cream fur crushed into the skin, and every time the otter swallowed Nocturne felt it as a small jerk against the blade.

Her forepaws had sunk deep into the chest, the bruised muscle squashed flat beneath them and swelling out round their edges. Her hind paw sat in the belly, and the belly had bunched up hard on either side of it.

She drove her weight down through her hips and kept it there. Her right ribs pulled, and her left shoulder, and a sound came up from somewhere in her chest that she did not let out past her teeth, a growl that had gone thin and ragged at the edges. She shifted her balance a fraction to rest one side, then the other.

*Now's when she's dangerous. Nothing left to lose.*

Her eyes went to the small face, and away to the twin tails, and back to the face. Then back again. She checked it more often than she needed to, and knew she was doing it.

— Ripples —

Her eyes closed. It took everything she had to open them again.

Her chest hurt so steadily now that it had become the only way she could tell one moment from the next: each push of pain, then the next, then the next. Her stomach hurt so much that the hurt itself seemed to be lifting her and carrying her somewhere. Under her back the flagstone was hard and gritty and cool. Its cracks pressed into her through the fur, a ridge of moss soft along one edge. A loose chip of stone lay under her shoulder blade. Off to the side, just past the reach of her left paw, rainwater stood in a hollow of the floor, and she could smell it.

Her body had stopped answering. She had stopped asking it.

*When did I last... all the way in.*

She asked it anyway, one more time.

She threw herself up against the weight. Her hips jerked off the stone, and fell, and jerked again, short ugly heaves with no rhythm to them, her heels skidding on the grit, her back slapping down on the flagstone each time. Each one was smaller than the last. None of them lifted anything.

The tail bore down on her throat.

It pressed her throat flat against the stone. Her breath stopped behind it, and a string of short, sharp barks came out of her instead, *ack, ack, ack*, each one cut off by the blade. Her neck went rigid. Her whole body jerked round it, her legs kicking out straight and her left paw flying up to scrabble at the white tail and slide off. Then the tail eased a hair, and the air came back into her in a long, ragged pull, and she shook all over, from the jaw down through her shoulders to her feet. For a long moment she could do nothing at all.

She lay limp beneath the weight, her breathing faint.

Her eyes were still open. Barely. Still on the face.

— Ripples —

Her limbs seemed a long way off. She had to think about each one before it would move, and then mostly it did not.

The white hunter was breathing hard above her, she could see that much: the ruff lifting and falling, the right side held stiff and away, a small catch in every breath. The red eyes kept coming back to her face, and going away, and coming back. They looked tired.

The weight shifted, the faintest bit. Her chest sent up a flash of pain that turned the whole world white, and then let it come back in pieces.

She sucked at the air with her whole body. It barely helped.

*Breathe. Watch. Don't waste anything.*

Past the white shoulder the water in the hollow of the floor held a small square of green sky, very still.

She did not make a sound. She did not have one left to make.
