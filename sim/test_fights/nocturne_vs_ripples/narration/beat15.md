— Nocturne —

The otter was hurt in the middle. Nocturne could see exactly where.

She stood a little bent, the small body curved forward over its own chest the way a leaf curls round a bruise, and when Nocturne shifted her weight toward her, even a little, the breathing changed. It went shallow and quick, and the otter's arm drifted down across her stomach as if by accident. The chest and the belly. Everything else she could still use.

Nocturne shook her head once, hard. Water flew from her horn and her ears, and the ruff hung heavy and cold on her chest and dripped onto her paws. She hated it. She hated the smell of it. And she was not going to go near that spitting mouth again while she was dripping from the last one.

So she would not go near.

She set her paws with care, almost fussily, each one placed flat on dry stone between the puddles, the sore left foreleg last. Then she reached down inside herself for the other thing she carried, the cold dark heat that did not come from any fire, and let it climb.

The air in front of her face began to waver. It bent and shimmered as though over sun-hot rock, and in the middle of the shimmer a flame opened, small, pale blue at the edges and almost colourless at the heart, the kind of fire that gave off no smoke and no sound at all.

*She'll try to step round it. Let her try.*

The last thing she did before she fired was lower her head and sight along it.

She breathed out. The wisp left her and drifted across the floor, too slow, swaying like a moth, and the air buckled behind it as it went.

— Ripples —

She watched it come and did not believe it.

It was so slow. She could have stepped round it, she thought, and then it swerved, the way a gnat swerves, and before she could move her feet it was at her right shoulder and had gone into the fur there without a sound.

It burned.

Not like the sun on a rock. Like ice held too long, a hot, crawling sting that went into the skin and spread out under it in a ring, and her breath came out in a squeak through her teeth, *eek*, short and furious. The fin on her right forearm clamped flat to her arm. She slapped at the shoulder with her left paw and the fur there crackled under it, singed and stiff, and the smell of it came up into her nose, sharp and wrong.

The sting did not go out. She rolled the shoulder and it flared. She held it still and it went on, a steady throb, as if something small and hot had settled into the joint to stay.

*Fire. From her. Of course she has fire.*

Across the floor Nocturne had not moved. She stood soaked and dripping, her ruff plastered dark against her chest, and watched with those red eyes narrowed, the corners of her mouth drawn back a little from her fangs.

A lizard flicked across a flagstone between them and was gone into a crack.

Ripples breathed. The breath reached her chest now, all the way down, and hit the bruise there, and came back out. The belly ached under her arm. And over all of it, high on her right shoulder where the pale fire had gone in, the burn throbbed in time with her heart.
