— Ripples —

She had let go. That was the mistake, and she meant to put it right.

Not now. Now she could not. Her chest would not let her do anything but breathe, and it would barely let her do that: each gulp went in high and short, under the collarbone, and stopped hard against the burning place in the middle of her, and she let it out again through her teeth in a thin whistle, *hhh*, and took the next.

The freeze was going out of her. She felt it leave in pieces. First her paws, which uncurled from the fists they had made against her chest; then her arms, which she could lower a little now, one still across the hurt place; then her feet, which she shifted on the flagstone just to feel that they would shift. They did. She could move again. Her body was hers.

She did not back away. Backing away was where the horn and the pale fire lived. Close was hers. She took one stiff step after the white body as it turned, staying in near the flank, near the hind legs, close enough to touch.

The stomach pulled when she stepped. The burned paw throbbed, a dull warmth now and no longer a sting. The left arm where the claws had glanced off it had a small bruise starting, nothing. And the chest. The chest pulsed with every heartbeat, huge and hot, and the fur over it was cut into tufts that she could see when she looked down, the skin dark and puffed underneath.

She stopped looking down.

*Teeth. Tails. Something that holds. She can't claw what she's tied to.*

Her twin tails twitched behind her, stuttered once, and went still, waiting.

— Nocturne —

Her neck still felt the otter's arms. A damp band in the ruff, flattened, where the small body had clung, and she shook it out with a roll of her head and it would not quite fluff up again.

She stood and breathed. Every breath in caught on the right ribs, on the one place there that was hot and swollen under the fur, and she learned to take them in two halves without thinking about it: half, a catch, the rest. The left shoulder ached deep in the joint. The chest under the ruff was a dull bruise. She noted each of them, coldly, and set each of them aside.

The otter had not run.

Nocturne watched her come back to herself. It happened in the arms first, the paws opening, and then in the eyes, which had been fixed and blank and now were black and quick again and looking at her flank. The breath was still terrible, high and whistling, but the body under it had come unstuck. And she had stepped in, not out. She stood close by Nocturne's hindquarters, hunched over her chest, near enough to touch.

*Stubborn. Stupid, or brave. One of those.*

Outside, the birds that had gone silent at the scream started again, one note, then three.

Nocturne's tail went rigid behind her. She turned her head to keep the otter in sight past her own flank, and her weight shifted forward over her forepaws, ready to go.
