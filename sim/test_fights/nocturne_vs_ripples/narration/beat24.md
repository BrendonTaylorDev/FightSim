— Ripples —

Her breath caught on the way in, a small hitch high in her throat, and for a moment after it the hall was quiet. The birds outside had not started up again since her scream. She could hear the drip of water somewhere off among the pillars and nothing else.

She stopped trying to breathe in deeply. Breathing in was where the hurt lived. Breathing out was easier, a slow leak through her teeth, and she would move on that.

Nocturne stood a body's length away, weight forward, red eyes half-lidded, as if none of this were costing her anything. But when she shifted, she pushed off the left forepaw and her mouth tightened, just at the corner, every time. The shoulder above it was still sore. And her hind legs were set wide and easy behind her, a long way from the dangerous end of her.

*I'd watch my feet, in her place. She isn't.*

Ripples kept her shoulders low. She felt a grim sort of patience settle in her, the patience of the river bank, in no hurry at all. Green light came down through the leaves along the top of the wall and lay in pale patches on the floor between them.

She chose her mark, somewhere low and out of the way, and let her breath go.

On the breath out she dropped. Her knees folded, the chest screamed at being bent, and she went in under the white body, low to the flagstones, and the cold came up into her mouth as she opened it: a hard, clean, biting chill gathering along her teeth until they ached with it. Ice Fang. Her jaws were wide over the broad white hind paw, her breath frosting on the fur. For one still moment nothing moved.

Then they shut.

— Nocturne —

Cold teeth closed on her left hind paw with a crunching grind, right through the fur between the toes, and the jolt went up through the hock and into her hip in one stroke.

It was a moment before it hurt. First there was only the shock of it, the wrongness of something gripping her down there where nothing ought to reach, and a huff through her nose, *hmph*, more outrage than pain. Then the cold came, spreading out from the teeth like water poured into the fur. She wrenched back against the jaws and her whole body jerked with them, hips and spine and shoulders, until the paw tore free of the teeth, and her feet stayed exactly where they were.

The hock above it jumped and shook as she set the paw down again, and her breath went in sharp, *hnh*, through her nose. Higher up, at the hip, there was only a twitch.

The fur over the paw was crushed flat in a deep print of small teeth, and the print stayed there. It was white with frost.

Her left hind paw throbbed once, hard, and then the throb shrank to a dull warmth under the ice. The leg prickled when she planted it, all the way up, and then held.

*Under me. She went under me.*

— Nocturne —

She watched the otter come back up out of her crouch, slowly, a hand pressed flat to her chest as she straightened.

Frost was creeping through Nocturne's fur. She could feel it along the leg, stiffening the fur in little crisp tufts, and a shiver ran up through her that she could not stop, out of the cold paw and along her flank and into her jaw until her teeth clicked. The limb felt slow. She watched it take her weight and saw it shiver with the strain, and hated it.

The otter was breathing badly. Short shallow pulls, her ribs barely moving, every one stopping early. Her black eyes kept going to Nocturne's legs now, low, and her tails twitched at the tips.

*She's pleased with herself. That's when they slip.*

Nocturne took a big breath without meaning to, and it stopped halfway with a grunt as the right ribs caught. There was a cough waiting in there, low in her chest, and she would not let it out.

The paw still stung with cold. The shoulder still ached. Under the frost, the old print of teeth on her ribs throbbed with every heartbeat. She could feel all of it.
