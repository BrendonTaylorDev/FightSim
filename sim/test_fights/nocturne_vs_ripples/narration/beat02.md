— Ripples —

*Now.*

The white shoulder was still against her, still pressing into the ache it had left, and that was the mistake. Too close. Close enough that the horn was over her head and useless and the long reach meant nothing at all.

Over the white back she could see the tail, held high and loose, its sickle tip ticking from side to side like something keeping time. She did not like that rhythm. She meant to break it before the white one learned what it was counting.

She did not step back. She rolled with the push instead, let her chest give under it, let her own weight turn on her heels a quarter of the way round, the way water turns round a stone, and the turn brought her up inside the white guard, small and low, under the jaw and to the left of it, where a four-legged thing has the hardest time reaching.

The left shoulder was right there. White fur over hard muscle, the joint working under it as Nocturne began to draw back from the charge.

Her right arm came up. Her fin flared out along the forearm, stiff as a blade. Her elbow tucked tight to her side so that none of the force would leak away, and the webbed paw at the end of it went flat and hard, fingers together, the edge of it turned down like the edge of a stone.

No need to be clever. Only to hit the thing hard and in the right place, and keep doing it.

She brought the edge of her paw down.

— Nocturne —

The crack of it went up between the pillars, sharp and dry, the sound of something hard meeting something that had not been ready for it.

Her left shoulder and the ruff beside it took it in the same instant. The chop landed on the point of the shoulder and its force jarred inward through the thick white fur at her chest, so that the whole front of her rocked on its legs. The ruff only caught the edge of it: a dull thump through all that fur, a quick breath sucked in through her nose, *tsss* through the side of her mouth.

The shoulder was something else.

It was not the size of the blow. A blow that size should have bounced off muscle like hers. This one did not stop at the fur or at the muscle. It went in along the joint, found the seam where the shoulder met the body, and spread there, and the hurt doubled back on itself, sharper the second time round than the first, as if the small orange paw had known exactly where the door was.

Her red eyes went wide, then narrowed to slits. Her left foreleg had been knocked out of line, splayed out sideways on the flagstones, and she pulled it back under her, deliberately, a hair late. A groan came up from deep in her chest, *nnngh*, and she bit it off before it reached her teeth.

She did not move from where she stood. She held her ground, four paws on the stone, her hackles up along the ruff.

*That kind. She fights with that kind.*

Her body knew it before she let herself think it. That chop had got in under everything she was. Her left shoulder burned with a wrongness that sat much deeper than the blow itself.

For one breath, two, she hunched over it, the shoulder drawn in tight, the sickle tail gone rigid behind her. Then she made herself stand tall. She flexed the left forepaw, testing it, and the three claws were slow to close.

Close under her jaw, the otter stood with her chopping hand still raised and looked at that shoulder, and her small black eyes were bright with what she had just learned.

Somewhere along the wall a vine swayed where nothing had touched it.

Nocturne peeled her lips back off her fangs and showed the otter every one of them.
