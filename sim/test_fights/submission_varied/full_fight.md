# Ripples vs Nerine: a submission match, all 28 beats (seed 21)

The lakeside clearing (scene 15), `finish.submission.enabled true`. Every beat was played on the real engine through `drive.py`. The prose is written by hand from the engine's notes for each beat (hits, pain levels, sounds, the refusal signs it picked), in the same parts the program asks the narrator for: the side of the one who acts up to the moment of contact, then the side of the one it happens to.

---

## Beat 1 — the fin and the shoulder

— Ripples —

The clearing was warm enough to make anyone lazy. Sun lay across the long grass in a single gold sheet, and the
lake beyond the pale sand was so still that the pines on the far shore stood upside down in it. Out past the
shallows a fish rose, left a ring, and was gone. Somewhere in the oaks a bird was saying the same three notes over
and over.

Ripples didn't look at any of it. She was looking at Nerine's throat.

The other Buizel stood a few lengths off with her chin up, bouncing on the balls of her feet, both tails swinging
loose behind her like a pair of skipping ropes. She had the cream throat that their river line had, short-furred
and bare, no sac to pad it. And she carried it high, the way a fighter carries something she has never once had
to protect.

*She's never been hit there,* Ripples thought. *She will be.*

Nerine's eyes, meanwhile, were lower. Ripples felt them go to her legs and stay there: her ankles, her shins, the
way her weight sat in her knees. It was a cold, interested look, the look of someone choosing the softest place
to cut into a fruit. Ripples set her feet a little wider and didn't let it show that she'd noticed.

She went in on the diagonal, quickly, before Nerine could finish her bounce. Left foot planting in the soft turf,
hips turning, the right forearm sweeping across flat and backhanded with the hard blue edge of the fin leading
the way. It was still wet from the lake. She could feel the water on it, cool, and the whole weight of her body
coming round behind it in one long pull from her planted foot to the tip of the fin.

Nerine saw it at the last instant. Her chin snapped down and her shoulder came up, and the throat disappeared
behind it.

Ripples didn't have time to change anything. The fin was already there.

— Nerine —

It caught her on the round of the left shoulder, and it caught her perfectly.

She had been halfway through a breath and halfway through a bounce, both feet light, nothing set, and the fin
came in at the one angle that let every bit of it go in and nothing glance away. The edge bit through the fur with
a raspy swish she felt more than heard, the tug of the hair parting along a line from the front of the shoulder
to the back, and the force of it went on through into her upper back as if Ripples had thrown it there on purpose.

A cry came out of her. It was out before she knew it was coming, a high, sharp *ah!* that cracked in the middle,
and she bit it off as fast as she could, angry at it. She twisted to her right away from the rake as it went
across her, the shoulder jerking in so hard her whole arm clamped against her side, and then she made herself
straighten, made herself square up again, because Ripples was right there watching.

The water was the only part of it that did nothing. It splashed off the fin and over her and ran down her
shoulder and side in cool sheets, and her fur shed it the way a Buizel's fur sheds a lake. It didn't get in. It
just left her soaked and dripping, the coat on her left side plastered flat and heavy, a little cold where the
breeze found it.

The shoulder throbbed. Not badly. It was a clean, sharp sting along the cut line and a duller knock behind it, in
the meat between her shoulder blades, already easing as she rolled it. Nothing in it was going to stop her arm
working. But it had been far, far harder than a fin had any right to be, and she knew why: she'd been floating,
pleased with herself, and Ripples had been waiting for exactly that.

*Fine,* she thought. *Fine. I'll keep my feet under me.*

She shook the water off her left arm with a sharp flick that sent drops glittering out over the grass, and when
she looked at Ripples again there was nothing left of the bounce. Her eyes went straight back down to the legs.

## Beat 2 — the knee in the thigh

— Nerine —

Nerine came back with her feet flat this time, flat and sure in the springy turf, and she didn't go anywhere near
the fins.

She had watched Ripples recover from the slash. That was the thing about fighters who came in on the diagonal:
they had to plant that left foot to do it, and for a moment, after the swing, all their weight sat on it. The left
leg did the work. The left leg would be the one to go.

*Start at the top,* Nerine thought, *and work down.*

She closed the distance in two quick, scuffing steps, ducking low under the line of the right forearm in case
Ripples had another one ready. She hadn't. Up close, Ripples smelled of lake water and crushed grass, and her eyes
were very calm and very black, and her paws came up to shove Nerine off a moment too late. Nerine got a fistful of
the fur at the top of Ripples' shoulder for balance and pulled her in, and as she pulled, she brought her own
knee up hard into the front of Ripples' left thigh.

She felt it go in. All of her weight was behind it, the pull and the step and the lift of the knee together.

The shoulder Ripples had cut complained as she pulled, a hot line across the top of it, and she ignored it. That
was the thing about pain that only stung: you could put it somewhere and leave it. She'd find out soon enough
whether Ripples could do the same.

— Ripples —

The knee landed with a solid *whump*, like a sack of wet sand dropped on a floor, right in the long muscle at the
front of her thigh.

It was a short, ugly, practical blow, and the meat of the leg took all of it. The shock didn't stop there. It ran
up the bone into her left hip, a dull, deep jolt in the joint, and for a moment the whole leg seemed to go
somewhere else. It buckled. Not far, a dip at the knee, but her weight slid off it onto her right foot all by
itself, without asking her, and her breath went out through her nose in a hard snort.

The leg held. She made sure it held. She drove the heel of her paw into Nerine's chest and shoved, and Nerine let
go of her shoulder fur and skipped back out of reach, light and pleased, and Ripples set the left foot down again
and put her weight back on it and stood square.

The thigh was hot. That was all, so far: a bruise starting, a tightness at the front where the knee had gone in,
and a faint stiff ache in the hip above it. But she could feel exactly where
it was, a hand's width above the knee, and she knew Nerine could see her feeling it.

*Leg,* Ripples thought. *It's the leg she wants. Not my face, not my ribs.*

She filed that away the way she'd file away a current in a river: where it ran, how hard, how to stay out of it.
Then she lifted her forearms again, the fins out, and moved left, keeping the hurt leg back and away.

Nerine moved with her, smiling a little.

## Beat 3 — thrown onto the roots

— Nerine —

Ripples was moving left, keeping that leg back. It meant her weight was all on the right foot, and a fighter with
all her weight on one foot was a fighter with nothing in the other.

Nerine went for her bodily.

She feinted at the face with a quick jab of her right paw, and when Ripples' fins came up to meet it Nerine
dropped low and drove in under them, shoulder first, and got both arms round Ripples' middle. Up close, the other
Buizel was all hard, wiry muscle and wet fur. Nerine dug her feet into the soft ground, turned her hips, and used
the weight Ripples had piled onto that right foot to swing her right off it.

It was easier than she'd expected. Ripples came up off the grass, light as a bundle of reeds, her tails whipping
round behind her for balance and finding nothing. Nerine heaved and let go.

Over by the treeline the old pines had pushed their roots up out of the ground in long humped knots, and that was
where she sent her.

— Ripples —

The sky turned over. The pines swung past upside down, the sun went streaking across her eyes, and then the ground
came up and hit her across the back.

It wasn't the grass. It was a root, thick and hard as stone under a skin of bark, humped up out of the earth
right where she came down. It caught her square across the upper back, between the shoulder blades, and along the
top of her left shoulder at the same time, with a hard flat crack that drove the air out of her lungs in a single
*huk*. The rest of her came down after: her left hip hit the soft grass on the far side of the root, which gave
under her and hardly hurt at all.

She lay there. She didn't roll, didn't skid, didn't try to scramble up at once. Her body simply stayed where it had
landed, on its back across the root with her arms flung out and her tails tangled under her, and for a few long
moments the only thing she did was breathe.

It came back slowly, the breath, in short, thin pulls that didn't fill anything. Her back throbbed along the line
of the root. Her shoulder had a knot of hurt on top of it, deep in the joint, like a bruise pressed with a thumb.
Neither of them was bad. They were just there, loud and new, and the ground was very hard underneath her.

Above her, the pine branches turned slowly against the blue, and a few needles came down. One landed on her
nose. She didn't have the breath to blow it off.

She made herself take stock: everything in order, from the top. Head, fine.
Neck, fine. Shoulders: the left one was going to bruise, she could feel it gathering. Back, sore along a line.
Hip, nothing much. Thigh, still hot from the knee. Feet, both there. Nothing broken, nothing that wouldn't work.
Just a body that had been picked up and thrown at a tree root, and was saying so.

*Get up,* she told herself. *In a moment. One more breath first.*

She turned her head. Nerine was standing a few lengths away on the open grass, not coming in, not doing anything.
She was shaking out her arms and watching. She looked like someone who had all afternoon and knew it.

That was almost worse than the root.

## Beat 4 — up in one go

— Ripples —

The breath came back properly at last, all at once, a great cool lungful that went right down to the bottom of her.
Ripples held it, let it go, and got up.

She did it in one movement, without thinking about it. Her paws went flat on the grass either side of her, her
stomach tightened, and she pushed and came up off the root and onto her feet as smoothly as if she'd only been
lying there to rest. Nothing failed. Nothing gave. The thigh grumbled as it took her weight, and her back was stiff
along the line where the root had been, but she was up, and standing square, and she shook herself from her ears
to the tips of her tails so the grass seeds and bark flew off her.

She rolled the left shoulder once in its socket. It ached at the top of the roll and loosened after. Good enough.

— Nerine —

Nerine watched her come up and felt a small, sour twist of disappointment. She'd thrown her well. She'd thrown her
onto a root. And the other Buizel had lain there for ten breaths and then just stood up, like someone getting out
of bed.

*All right,* Nerine thought. *It's going to take more than one.*

She had drip-dried while she waited. The sun had been on her the whole time, warm on her back, and the lake water
that had soaked her down the left side was mostly gone now, steamed out of her coat and run off into the grass.
Her fur had stopped clinging. She could feel it lifting as it dried, light again, and she gave her arm a shake that
threw off the last few drops and felt it move freely, nothing dragging at it.

The shoulder Ripples had cut still stung along its line when she shook it. She didn't let her face move.

They stood and looked at each other across the clearing. Ripples' sides were going in and out a little faster
than they had been. So were her own, if she was honest. Neither of them moved toward the other yet.

Out on the lake, a breath of wind wrinkled the water and went away again.

---

## Beat 5 — the root trip

— Nerine —

Nerine had been looking at the roots too, while she waited.

There was one near Ripples' heels now, a low hump of it coming out of the grass at an angle, thick as an arm, the
bark worn smooth by rain. Ripples hadn't looked down at it once. She was watching Nerine's paws, and Nerine's
shoulders, and Nerine's eyes, the way a careful fighter does. She was guarding everything above the waist.

So Nerine gave her something above the waist to look at.

She came in sideways and flicked a paw up at Ripples' face, quick and open, nothing behind it. Ripples' fins came up
to meet it, and her eyes went with them. And while they did, Nerine stepped in close and hooked her right foot
round behind Ripples' left leg, low, just under the knee.

She didn't kick. She pulled, hard, toward herself, and felt the knee fold forward under her shin, felt Ripples'
heel slide backward over the grass and catch against the hump of root.

*Knee,* she thought, with a hard bright pleasure. *I've got the knee.*

— Ripples —

The knee bent under the hook the wrong way for a moment, forward and inward, and Nerine's shin dragged across the
side of it with a meaty smack.

It was the first thing that had really hurt. A deep, wrenching pull went through the inside of the joint, as if
something in there had been stretched further than it was meant to go, and a lesser jolt ran down into her left
foot as the heel jammed against the root. Her breath snagged in her throat. She knew, the instant before it
happened, that she was going over.

She went over backwards across the root. The ground came up into her upper back and her left shoulder again, and
into the back of her right thigh, a scatter of three quick knocks against the earth, light, nothing like the throw
had been.

But this time she went with it instead of against it. She let the fall run on into a backward roll, tucked her
chin to her chest, threw her legs over her head and came right over the top and onto her feet on the far side of
the root, a little crooked, with grass in her fur and her fins out for balance. She came up turned the right way,
facing Nerine, before Nerine had finished stepping after her.

*Not twice,* she thought. *You don't get me down twice like that.*

But the knee. She put her weight on it to settle her stance, and it answered with a slow, sullen ache that sat low
in the joint, the way a tooth aches before it has quite decided to hurt. It wasn't loud. It was just there, and it
hadn't been there before, and it didn't go away when she shifted.

She kept her weight on her right foot and watched Nerine look at the knee. Nerine didn't pretend not to.

## Beat 6 — Brick Break on the knee

— Nerine —

Something was different about the way Ripples was standing. Nerine noticed it as she straightened up from the trip:
the other Buizel had always stood square before, weight even, ready to go either way. Now she was leaning, very
slightly, onto her right foot. Her left knee was soft and a little bent, and every so often her ears flicked back
when she shifted on it.

*There,* Nerine thought. *That's the one.*

It irritated her, a little, that Ripples was still standing at all. She'd been thrown onto a root and tripped over
another, and she kept just getting up and squaring up again with that same calm face. Nerine wanted that face to
change.

She didn't feint this time. She came in from the left, the side Ripples was guarding least because she was so busy
keeping the leg back, and she lifted her right paw high and stiff with the edge of it turned down like the blade
of an axe. Brick Break. The trick was not to stop at
the surface. The trick was to aim at the far side of the thing and go through.

She picked the spot she wanted, just to the outside of the joint, and put the paw there with all of her behind it.

— Ripples —

It landed with a meaty smack right on the side of her left knee, and every muscle in the leg seized at once.

The blow was enormous. It wasn't the knee alone: the force of it ran down the shin into her foot, a solid jarring
knock that made her toes clench, and up into the hip, a deep thud in the joint, all in the same instant, so that
the whole left side of her seemed to lock solid around that one point. A short, startled yelp jumped out of her,
*yeep*, high and thin, before she could catch it.

Then she let out the breath she'd been holding behind it, long and ragged, through her teeth.

She didn't go down. She hopped, once, on her right foot, the left one lifted clear of the grass for a moment as if
the ground were hot, and then she made herself put it down again. It shook as she set it on the turf. It took her
weight. It complained about it in a long, deep, throbbing ache that started in the joint and spread out into the
muscles around it, but it took it.

*Hurting,* she thought. *Hurting, not broken. Stand on it.*

She stood on it. Her face had gone tight, and she knew it had, and she couldn't make it stop. When she shifted her
weight there was a catch in the knee, a moment where it didn't want to bend, and then it did, slowly. It would be
slower now. She'd have to think about it every time she moved.

Nerine had stepped back to look at her handiwork, chest going in and out, both tails lashing. She looked pleased.
She looked like she'd found the place she'd been looking for all along.

Ripples looked back at her and held her face as still as she could.

Inside, she was doing sums. The knee had been tripped and now chopped. The thigh above it had been kneed. The hip
had been jarred twice. Every single thing Nerine had done to her, apart from the throw, had gone into the left leg
somewhere between the hip and the foot, and that was not luck. That was a plan, and the plan was working, and the
only answer Ripples had to it so far was standing on the other leg.

*Then hit her somewhere she has to think about,* Ripples thought. *Make her guard something.*

## Beat 7 — a leg for a leg

— Ripples —

She was not going to stand there and be looked at like that.

Ripples came in limping, and she didn't hide the limp, because there wasn't time to. The knee caught on every step
and she let it, and she watched Nerine's eyes go to it and stay there, smug. *Good. Look at my knee. Don't look at
my paw.*

She wanted the left thigh. A leg for a leg. She wanted Nerine to know what it was like to stand on something that
wouldn't stop throbbing. She raised her right paw the way she'd just seen it done, stiff and high with the edge
turned down, and brought it chopping down at the front of Nerine's thigh with her weight dropping in behind it.

It cost her to do it. Bringing the chop down meant planting the left foot for a moment to drive off, and the knee
caught as it took the weight, a deep, grudging twist of pain that nearly made her pull the blow. She didn't pull it.
She set her teeth and let the knee hurt and swung through.

Nerine saw it. She was quick, quicker than Ripples, and she twisted away from the chop, turning her whole body to
get the thigh clear. She couldn't get the rest of her clear in time. All she could do was give it her back.

— Nerine —

The chop came down across the small of her back with a solid *whump*, like a sack dropped off a cart.

It drove into the muscle low down beside her spine and pushed her forward a little, her back arching away from it
so sharply her chin went up, and the jar of it spread both ways at once: down into her right hip, where it knocked
the joint like a fist on a door, and up between her shoulder blades, a glancing echo along the top of her back.
Her breath went out of her in a grunt she didn't mean to make.

She was square again in a moment. She made sure of that, turning back to face Ripples with her paws up, her face
set. None of it was anything. A bruise low on her back, a dull ache where it had gone in, already settling into a
warm throb. The hip had only been jarred. She rolled it once and it moved.

But it annoyed her, and she wasn't good at hiding when she was annoyed.

*She went for my leg,* she thought. *She went for my leg. The cheek of it.*

Without quite meaning to she put a paw back to the small of her back and rubbed at it, hard, with the heel of her
hand, as if she could push the ache out through the fur. She took it away again almost at once. Ripples had seen.
Of course she had. Ripples saw everything, standing there on her one good leg with her calm black eyes, and the
little twitch at the corner of her mouth said she'd enjoyed it.

Nerine shook her shoulders loose and started circling again, slower this time, and more careful about where she
put her back.

## Beat 8 — the throat, at last

— Ripples —

Nerine was circling slower now. Ripples liked that better than the bouncing. A fighter who bounced was hard to read;
a fighter who was being careful about her back was thinking about her back, and every scrap of thought she gave to
her back was a scrap she wasn't giving to her throat.

Ripples let her circle. She turned with her on the spot, a small shuffle at a time, keeping the bad knee behind
her and her weight on the right foot, and she watched the cream fur under Nerine's chin. Every time Nerine looked
at the knee, her chin dipped. Every time she looked at Ripples' paws, it came up again. Up, down. Up, down. Like a
fish deciding whether to take a fly.

*Look at my knee,* Ripples thought. *Go on.*

She let it buckle a little, on purpose, a small dip and a wince that was only half pretended, and Nerine's eyes
dropped to it, bright and quick, and her chin came up and out as she leaned in to see.

Ripples came off her right foot and swung.

It was the same blow as the first one: the backhand, the flat arc, the hard blue edge of the right fin leading. But
this time she came up from underneath instead of across, so the fin rose into the throat instead of sweeping past
the shoulder, and this time Nerine was a breath too slow tucking her chin. Ripples felt the edge meet the bare
cream fur. She felt it catch, and drag, and keep going.

— Nerine —

The fin raked across the front of her throat with the snick of something catching and pulling free.

It went through the short fur and over the skin underneath in one hot line from one side of her windpipe to the
other, and the force of it carried on into the side of her neck and down into the top of her right shoulder, a
glancing knock in each, as if the blow had been too big for the throat alone and had to spill somewhere. She
twisted away from the rake as it crossed her, chin jerking down and round, and she was square again almost before
she'd finished twisting.

And that was nearly all.

That was the strange thing. It should have been much worse than it was. Ripples had thrown it with everything,
lake water flying off the fin again in a bright fan, and a blow like that across the throat ought to have dropped
her coughing to her knees. But the water did nothing. It splashed over her throat and chest and ran away down her
front, and her fur shed it, and her body took the rest of it in that easy, river-bred way it took a wave. The
sting was there, a thin, raw line under her chin. The jolt in her neck was there. They were small.

She coughed once anyway, a short dry cough, more surprise than hurt. She put a paw to her throat and took it away
and looked at it, half expecting to see something on the pads. There was only water.

*That's all?* she thought. *She threw all that, and that's all?*

She saw that Ripples knew it, too. The other Buizel had stepped back with her fin still raised, and her face had
gone very still, the way a face goes when it has been sure of something and has just found out it was wrong. She
was looking at Nerine's throat as if it had cheated her.

The lake water was all through Nerine's coat again, heavy and cold, soaking her down the front and both arms.
Her fur clung to her. She was dripping steadily onto the grass between her feet, and when she moved she could feel
the extra weight of it, the wet drag at her elbows and her tails. She shook herself, hard. It barely helped.

She lifted her chin again, high, cocky, right where Ripples could see it.

*Go on,* she thought. *Try that again.*

## Beat 9 — lifted and slammed

— Nerine —

Nerine didn't wait for her to try it again. She was done with being cut at.

She had the measure of the fins now. They were fast and they were sharp, but they came from the elbow, and an
elbow needed room. So she took away the room. She dropped her head and ran in under Ripples' arms before the right
one could come back round, and she slammed her shoulder into Ripples' chest and wrapped both arms round her middle
and locked her paws together at the small of her back.

Wet fur against wet fur, slippery as a fish. Nerine squeezed until she felt Ripples' breath go short.

It was not how she liked to fight. She was a darter by nature: in and out, strike and spin away, never close
enough for anyone to get a grip on her. But this one kept getting up, and this one had fins, and up close the fins
were nothing but elbows. Nerine set her feet wide in the grass, bent her knees, and straightened them.

Ripples came up off the ground. All of her: both feet clear, her tails flailing, her paws beating at Nerine's back
and shoulders and finding nothing they could hurt from there. For a moment Nerine held her right up off the
clearing, the whole wiry weight of her, and felt her own legs shake under it, and liked it.

*Down you go,* she thought, *and stay there.*

She brought her down.

— Ripples —

The grass came up and hit her flat across the back, all of it at once.

Upper back and lower back together, a solid knock along the whole length of her spine, and Nerine's weight came
down on top of it, driving her into the turf. The grass was soft. It wasn't soft enough. It knocked every scrap of
breath out of her in a wide-mouthed *hah* that had no sound behind it, and her head bounced once against the ground,
and then she was lying there face-up with Nerine's arms gone from round her and the sky very large and very blue.

She couldn't breathe. For a long, frightening moment her chest simply wouldn't move. It had forgotten how. Her
mouth was open and nothing was going in, and the edges of everything had gone bright and sharp and far away, and
she lay there with her paws open at her sides and her tails trapped under her and waited for her body to remember.

It remembered. The first breath came in with a creak like an old door, thin and painful, and the second was a
little better, and the third was almost a breath.

Her back throbbed from the shoulders down to the base of her tails, a broad, flat ache, like the bruise you get
from lying on a stone all night. It wasn't sharp. It was just everywhere at once. Her bad knee had been jolted in
the slam and was aching again in its slow, sullen way, deep in the joint.

*Get up,* she thought. *Get up, get up.*

She didn't. Her body didn't even try. It lay where it was, every muscle gone slack and stupid, and let her breathe.
The grass was cool under her shoulders and smelled of sap where she'd crushed it.

Nerine was standing over her, just out of reach, panting a little. Ripples could see the wet fur standing up in
spikes on her chest where it had dried in clumps. She could see Nerine looking down at her, and then looking, very
deliberately, along the length of her body to her left leg.

---

## Beat 10 — the second try

— Ripples —

She tried to sit up first, the way she'd done after the root: one smooth push. It didn't happen.

She got her paws flat on the grass either side of her and pushed, and her back went taut, and she came up perhaps
halfway, and then the left knee simply wasn't there. She had leaned on it without meaning to, to get her feet under
her, and it folded. It didn't hurt worse when it went. It just gave, the way a rotten branch gives, and she dropped
back flat on the grass with a hard thump that rattled her teeth and jolted her bruised back all over again.

She lay there for a breath with her eyes shut. *Stupid,* she thought. *Don't lean on it. You know not to lean on
it.*

So she did it the slow way.

She rolled onto her front first, which hurt her back and made her hiss through her teeth. She got her paws flat
on the grass under her shoulders. She brought her right knee in under her, all the way, and set it firm. Then the
left, slowly, carefully, an inch at a time, feeling the joint protest as it bent and taking it only as far as it
would go without complaining too loudly. She stayed like that for a moment, crouched in the grass, her sides going
in and out.

Then she pushed up off her right leg, with her paws helping, and came up onto her feet.

It wasn't graceful. She wobbled at the top of it. Her back was stiff and hot from the slam and the knee ground when
she straightened it, a dull, grating ache that made her breath catch. But she was standing, both feet on the grass,
with her weight on the right one and the left just touching, her paws up and her fins out.

She was breathing harder than she wanted Nerine to see.

— Nerine —

Nerine had watched the whole thing from a few lengths away, bouncing on her toes again now that her back had
stopped bothering her.

She'd watched the first try fail. She'd watched the knee give, and seen the little flash of something cross
Ripples' face when it did, something that wasn't pain exactly, more like being betrayed by her own body. And she'd
watched the second try, the slow one, the careful one, the knees coming in under her one at a time like an old
Buizel getting up off a riverbank in winter.

*That's twice now,* Nerine thought. *Twice I've put her down and twice she's got up.*

She wasn't sure how she felt about that. There was a part of her that was impatient with it, that wanted the
Buizel to stay down when she was put down, the way things ought to go. But there was another part, smaller, that
was beginning to be interested. A fighter slammed like that ought to get up angry, and stupid with it. This one got up
careful. Every time, she got up careful.

The careful ones were the ones who lasted.

Nerine shook out her paws and let her eyes go back to the knee, where Ripples could see her looking. If she was
going to last, Nerine would just have to take the lasting out of her a piece at a time.

---

## Beat 11 — the knee, missed

— Nerine —

The soaking had nearly gone out of her. The sun had been on Nerine's back all through Ripples' slow get-up, and she
could feel her coat lifting as it dried, the fur coming loose from her skin in tufts, the heavy wet drag going out
of her arms. She shook herself once more and felt light again, and quick, and ready.

She went for the knee. She didn't even try to hide it. There didn't seem to be any point: Ripples knew what she
wanted, and Ripples couldn't do anything about it standing on one leg.

She came in straight, fast, and raised her right paw high with the edge down, the same as before. Brick Break. She
watched the knee as she came, the soft bent left knee with the weight off it, and she put her whole body into the
drop and aimed at the far side of the joint.

*Again,* she thought. *Right where it was.*

At the very last moment, the knee moved.

— Ripples —

She'd seen it coming. It would have been hard not to. Nerine had stared at the knee all the way in like someone
walking toward a door.

Ripples didn't have time to get out of the way, not on that leg, but she had time to turn it. A fraction. She
swivelled on her right foot and turned her left hip in toward Nerine, and the edge of Nerine's paw came down an
inch above where it was aimed and landed square on the bone at the top of her left leg instead, with a solid
*whump* like a sack dropped off a cart.

It was a heavy blow. It knocked the breath out of her nose in a hard huff and the jolt went straight down from the
hip into the thigh, the same thigh Nerine had kneed at the start of the fight, so the old bruise woke up and
throbbed along with the new one. For a moment the whole top of her leg was one dull, beaten ache, from the joint
down to a hand's width above the knee.

But it wasn't the knee.

She stood there with her teeth clenched and let it ache, and it settled after a moment into a deep soreness, a
heavy, bruised feeling in the hip like a stone sitting in the joint, and a matching one down the front of the
thigh. Sore. Not worse than sore. She could feel how much better it was than the other thing would have been.

*I'll take that,* she thought. *I'll take that any day.*

She looked at Nerine and let her see it, just for a moment: not a smile, but close.

Nerine shook out her paw with a sharp flick, frowning down at it as if the hip had been harder than she'd
expected. The last of the lake water had dried off her coat in the sun. She looked fluffier again, and cross, and
very slightly less sure.

---

## Beat 12 — a blade out of the lake

— Ripples —

The fin wasn't enough. Ripples had known it the moment she'd seen the throat cut shrug off: whatever she did with
water, Nerine's body simply took it, the way the lake took the rain. She needed an edge that didn't run off.

She backed off two steps, toward the shore, and then two more. Nerine let her go. Ripples saw her decide to: saw
her think *she's retreating*, and saw her think it with satisfaction.

She wasn't retreating. The pale sand began under her heels, then the warm shallows, lapping at her ankles. She
dragged her right forearm through the water as she went, low along the fin, and drew it up out of the lake in a
long thin sheet clinging to the edge. Then she reached down inside herself for the cold, the same cold she'd
have put into her fangs, and pushed it out into the water.

It froze where it lay. She felt it go hard along her arm with a faint crackle like stepping on frost, and when she
lifted the arm clear there was a blade on it: a long, glassy edge of ice running the length of the fin and a hand's
breadth beyond, thin and clear and beaded with melt, smoking faintly in the warm air.

Nerine's ears went flat back against her head.

Ripples came out of the water at her and went for the neck.

— Nerine —

She saw it come and ducked her head away, down and to the side, and the ice missed her neck and came down across
her upper back instead, between her shoulder blades, with the scrape of something hard dragged over hide.

It was cold first, before it was anything else: a long, shocking line of cold laid across her back. Then the sting
under it, sharp and bright where the edge had parted the fur. The force of it ran up into the side of her neck, a
solid knock that made her head jerk, and she hissed through her teeth and twisted away.

And again, like the fin, it was less than it should have been. The cold soaked into her and stopped. Her body had
been made in cold water, and it knew what to do with cold: it took it and it spread it and it let it go. The ice
didn't chill her. It didn't creep into her muscles or slow her down. It was just a sharp, hard thing that had hit
her in the back, and it stung, and the sting was already fading.

But she'd felt how sharp the edge was. That stayed with her.

*She made a knife,* Nerine thought. *Out of the lake. While I watched her do it.*

She came round to face Ripples again with her back stinging and a new kind of attention in her. She looked at the
blade, at the clear wet ice on the other Buizel's arm with the sun shining through it. She looked at how far it
reached. She worked out, quickly, how close she'd have to get before it couldn't reach at all.

Ripples stood at the edge of the shallows with water around her ankles and the blade held out low, her bad leg back,
her face calm. She was breathing hard. She didn't look like someone who was losing.

---

## Beat 13 — inside the blade

— Nerine —

The answer to a blade was simple. Nerine had worked it out while her back was still stinging. You didn't stay out
where it could reach you. You went in so close it had nowhere to swing.

She came in fast and low, faster than she'd come in all fight, straight at the ice-arm side so Ripples had to turn
to meet her. The blade swept round. Nerine ducked it, felt the cold of it pass over her ears, and was inside it
before it could come back, her shoulder in Ripples' chest, her face down by Ripples' left hip.

The thigh was right there. The front of it, where her knee had gone in at the start. She could see the fur
roughed up over the bruise.

*Mine,* she thought.

She opened her jaws wide and bit down.

— Ripples —

The teeth went into the front of her left thigh with a crunching grind, right into the bruised meat where the knee
had been, and stayed there.

It was a hard bite and a deep one, quick as a snap but not letting go at once: Nerine clamped down and shook her
head once, hard, side to side, like a dog with a rope, and the pain went up out of the thigh into her hip and round
into the small of her back in one hot, tearing pull. Ripples yelped. It came out high and short and furious, a
sound she'd never have chosen, and her whole body jerked against the grip.

She clubbed at the back of Nerine's head with her left paw, once, twice, with the blade arm useless above them both,
too close to swing. On the second blow the jaws came loose.

Nerine let go and skipped back out of reach, licking her teeth.

Ripples looked down at her leg. There was a ragged ring of tooth marks pressed into the fur at the front of her
thigh, a hand's width above the knee, and a few beads of blood standing up in them, small and dark. The bite throbbed
with every beat of her heart. The thigh under it had gone hot and tight all the way round, and when she put weight
on it, it hurt properly now, a clear deep ache, not the sore bruise it had been.

Her hip was sore with it. Her lower back had a dull new knot in it too, where the shake of Nerine's head had
wrenched her.

*She's eating my leg,* Ripples thought, and almost laughed, and didn't, because it wasn't funny. *Bit by bit. The
knee, then the hip, then the thigh. She's working her way round it.*

It was a strange, cold thing to understand about someone. She looked at Nerine, standing there with her muzzle wet
and her chest heaving, and for the first time she understood that Nerine had been doing this on purpose since the
very first blow. Not fighting her. Taking her leg apart.

The ice blade was still on her arm, clear and cold. She lifted it again and limped to put the bad leg back where
it had been, and found she had to think about it, this time, every step.

---

## Beat 14 — the water turned back

— Ripples —

She wanted Nerine off her. Not close, not inside the blade again, not anywhere near the leg. She wanted room.

Ripples dropped back a step, the ice held out low to keep Nerine honest, and filled her lungs right to the bottom.
She could feel the water in herself, the way she always could, cool and heavy and waiting. She opened her mouth and
let it go.

The Water Gun came out of her in a hard, straight jet, aimed at Nerine's face and throat. It wasn't meant to do
much. It was meant to blind her and fill her mouth and nose and make her cough, to push her back and buy three
breaths of distance.

*Back,* Ripples thought. *Get back.*

— Nerine —

Nerine saw it coming and didn't dodge.

She'd had enough of being pushed. She planted her feet in the grass, opened her mouth and let go a burst of
stars, a bright spray of little stars that came out of her in a rush, spinning and sparkling, gold in the sunlight.

They met the jet in the air halfway between them and slammed into it.

There was a sound like a fist hitting a pail of water. Light burst out of the meeting point, and spray, and a shock
of force that Nerine felt in her own chest. And the stars won. They tore the jet apart where they hit it and threw
what was left of it straight back the way it had come, half the force and all of the cold, and none of it touched
Nerine at all.

— Ripples —

It hit her full in the face.

The broken water came back at her in a ragged, slapping sheet, stars still spinning in it, and burst across her
head and chest. It went into both her ears, the right one worse, a hard punch of water driven right down into it
so that her head rang and the whole world went to a roaring hiss. The left ear took a solid slap of it a moment
later. Her chest got the last of it, a glancing splash across the cream fur.

She staggered back a half step, her head jerking to the side, her free paw clapping to her right ear. She couldn't
hear. Everything had gone muffled and far away, as if she'd put her head under the lake, and her ear ached deep
inside where the water had gone in.

She shook her head hard, once, twice, water flying off her ears in a spray. On the third shake her hearing came
back in a rush, birdsong and lapping water and her own heavy breathing, all too loud.

Nerine was laughing.

It wasn't a big laugh. It was a short, surprised bark of a thing, as if Nerine hadn't quite expected it to work so
well and was delighted that it had. She was standing exactly where she'd been standing, dry, untouched, both tails
swinging.

Ripples lowered her paw from her ear. The ache stayed inside it, small and sharp. She didn't say anything. She
wasn't sure she trusted her voice not to come out as angry as she felt.

---

## Beat 15 — the blade lost

— Nerine —

The laugh was still in her when she went in. Nerine couldn't help it: there was something about the look on
Ripples' face, wet and furious and trying not to be, that made her want to do it all again.

She did something else instead. She did the thing that had worked.

She ducked under the ice arm, the same as before, but lower this time, so the blade couldn't come down on her back.
She came up inside it with her shoulder in Ripples' stomach and her arms already round the other Buizel's middle,
locking tight at the small of her back. Ripples twisted. Nerine squeezed. She set her feet in the grass, bent her
knees, and lifted.

Ripples came up off the ground again, kicking. Her blade arm flailed out to the side. Nerine held her up there for a
breath, all that wiry wet weight, and felt the muscles of her own back burn with it.

*This one's going to stay down,* she thought.

She slammed her.

— Ripples —

She knew what was coming. It didn't help in the slightest.

The grass hit her across the upper back and the lower back in one flat blow, harder than the first slam had been,
and drove her into the turf with Nerine's weight following her down. Her breath went out of her in a crushed *hff*.
Her back, which had been aching broadly ever since the last time, woke up and began to throb from her shoulder
blades to her tail, a deep, heavy, bruised soreness that settled in along her spine and didn't go anywhere.

Her blade arm hit the ground out at her side. She felt the ice crack against it.

She lay there face-up, gasping, and watched the pine branches turn. She didn't try to get up. She couldn't have
yet; her lungs were still finding their way back to breathing. Somewhere off to her right she heard a soft, wet
sound, and when she turned her head she saw why.

The blade was coming apart on her arm. The ice had cracked through where it had hit the ground, and the warm sun
was on it, and it was melting faster than she could have held it together. As she watched, the long clear edge
slid off the fin in two pieces and dropped into the grass beside her, and lay there glittering, and began to run.
Her forearm was wet and bare and cold.

*Gone,* she thought, and was surprised by how much she minded.

Nerine stood over her, breathing hard, and didn't come in. She was looking down the length of Ripples' body again,
slowly, the way she'd looked at the start of the fight. Past the chest, past the hips. To the left leg, lying in the
grass with its bitten thigh and its aching knee. She looked at it for a long time.

Ripples saw her decide something. She didn't know what. She knew she wasn't going to like it.

---

## Beat 16 — the heel hook

— Nerine —

Ripples was trying to get her legs under her without being seen to. Nerine watched her do it: the right foot
sliding in a little across the grass, the hip shifting, the weight easing over a hair at a time. It was very
patient. It was the most patient thing Nerine had ever watched anyone do flat on her back.

She didn't wait for it to work.

She stepped in on Ripples' left side and stood over the hurt leg, one foot either side of it, and dropped her
weight down into a crouch. The thigh, the bitten one, went between her knees, and she clamped them in on it and
hooked her shins round behind it, so the leg was trapped from the hip down in a tangle of her own. Then she bent
forward, reached down past the knee, and scooped Ripples' left heel up into the crook of her right elbow, tight
against her ribs, the way you'd tuck a fish under your arm to stop it flapping.

The foot was wet and warm and much smaller in her arm than she'd expected. She could feel the tendons along the
back of the ankle, hard as cords. She set her left paw over her own right wrist to lock it.

*There,* she thought. *Now let's see you get up.*

She turned it.

Not much. She didn't need much. She turned her whole upper body, her shoulders and her ribs and the arm clamped to
them, a little way to the outside, and the heel went with it. The twist didn't stay in the foot. Nerine felt it go
up the leg like a turn of a key: through the ankle, through the shin, and into the knee, the knee she'd been
working on since the root, and she felt the joint go tight and hard and strange under the strain, as if it had
come up against the end of something.

Under her, Ripples' whole body went rigid.

— Ripples —

It was the knee. Everything was the knee.

She hadn't understood what Nerine was doing until it was done. A shadow over her, a weight across her thigh, her
foot lifted up off the grass, and then the turn. The heel was clamped somewhere hard and warm and she couldn't feel
exactly where, only that it was held and that it was being turned, slowly, outward. And the turn came up through
her ankle as if the bones of her leg were one long rod, and arrived in her knee.

The pain was deep and wrong. It wasn't the sharp bright thing a blow was. It was a pressure inside the joint that
built and kept building, a wrenching on the inside of the knee as if the joint were being asked to bend a way it
had never been made to bend and was being told, firmly, that it would.

Ripples bucked. Once, hard, her back coming right up off the grass in an arch and her free leg kicking out, a
single violent heave of her whole body. Nothing moved. Nerine's knees clamped tighter round her thigh and pressed
the fur flat into a soft give under them, and the heel stayed where it was in the crook of that arm, and Ripples
fell back flat onto the turf and lay there shaking.

The foot wasn't bad, not yet. Nerine's arm was pressing a trench into the fur round her heel, and she could feel
the muscle at the back of the ankle quivering under it, and she shifted her foot a little in the grip, looking for
give, and found none. The thigh ached under Nerine's legs, a bruised ache on top of the bite. It was the knee that
was the trouble. The knee was being squeezed out of its shape, the muscles bunched up hard on either side of it,
and it was hurting in a clear, sustained way that didn't come and go with her heartbeat. It simply stayed.

She breathed in through her teeth. Out. In.

*All right,* she thought. *All right. Think.*

She lay on her back with her face to the sky and the clearing tilted round her, and she looked down the length of
her own body. Nerine was crouched over her left leg like something over its kill: knees clamped round the thigh,
the heel tucked under her arm, her head bent, watching the knee. Nerine's twin tails were stretched out behind her
across the grass for balance, flat and still. Her ears were forward. Her mouth was a little open. She looked
absorbed, like someone threading a needle.

Ripples' right foot was still free. It dug at the turf, pushed, and found nothing to push against that would take
her anywhere. It stopped.

There was no clock. Nobody was counting. That was the thing she understood, slowly, lying there. Nerine wasn't
trying to hold her down for anything. Nerine was trying to make her give up. And the only thing that would end this
was Ripples herself.

She looked away from the knee, up into the pine branches, and set her jaw.

---

## Beat 17 — the first refusal

— Nerine —

Nerine leaned back into it.

She'd felt the buck go through Ripples and die, and she'd felt the long shaking afterward, and she'd waited for the
tap. It hadn't come. So she sat her weight down further into her crouch, tightened her knees on the thigh, and
turned her shoulders another small way to the outside, and felt the twist go up the leg again, more this time.

*Come on,* she thought. *You know how this ends.*

Under her arm, the foot did something she didn't expect. It went hard. Ripples pointed it, all at once, the toes
stretched out straight and the whole leg stiffening from the hip down into one long rigid line, and then she
pulled. The heel slid. Nerine felt it slip in the crook of her elbow, wet fur over wet fur, a claw's width, two,
and her grip had to shift to chase it, and for one breath the twist went slack.

Nerine didn't like that at all.

She ducked her head and bit the nearest thing she could reach. It was Ripples' left hip, the round of it just past
her own knee, and she couldn't get much of it from where she crouched; she got her teeth into it shallowly and
clamped and let go again in the same snap. It wasn't a real bite. It was a warning. And while Ripples' body
flinched from it, Nerine dragged the heel back into the crook of her arm and locked her wrist again and turned.

— Ripples —

The knee answered before she did.

When the twist came back it was worse than it had been, much worse, as if the slack she'd won had only been a breath
drawn before it. It went up her leg and into the joint and kept going, and the pressure in there turned into
something with edges. She strained against it until every muscle in the leg was shaking, and then she couldn't
strain any more, and she sagged back with a groan that came up out of her chest low and long and broken in the
middle.

The knee had gone past hurting into something worse, a deep, hot, grinding pain that
sat in the joint like a coal and made her breath come short and her eyes water. Her foot hurt now too, a sore,
pinched ache all round the heel where Nerine's arm was clamped on it. She tried to shift it from under the pressure,
and couldn't, and tried again. Her thigh ached under the knees that held it. The little bite on her hip stung, a
bright small sting on top of everything, almost funny in how little it mattered.

She was thrashing. She noticed that as if it were happening to someone else. Her right leg was kicking at the grass
and her paws were clawing at the turf on either side of her and her back was bucking against the ground, all of it
together and none of it doing anything, and the sounds coming out of her were getting louder, rougher, catching in
her throat.

And the word was right there.

She could feel the shape of it. Not even a word: a slap of her paw on the ground, two slaps, and it would stop. Her
paw knew how. It wanted to. It was lying flat in the grass beside her hip, and every part of her was asking it to
lift and come down, and the knee was asking loudest of all.

She set her teeth into her own bottom lip and bit down, hard, until she tasted copper.

She held it there. She held it there until the pain in her lip was the thing she was thinking about, small and
sharp and hers, something she was doing to herself instead of something being done to her. The paw stayed where it
was.

*It slipped once,* she thought. *It slipped. If I last one more breath, it'll slip again.*

— Nerine —

Nerine felt the thrashing come up through her own knees and arms, frantic and graceless and strong, and then she
felt it change. Something in it went quiet. The leg was still fighting her, but Ripples' paws had stopped scrabbling
for a moment, and when Nerine looked up along the body she saw why.

Ripples was biting her own lip. There was a thin line of red at the corner of her mouth. Her eyes were screwed shut.

Nerine looked at the paw lying in the grass by Ripples' hip. It didn't move.

*Huh,* she thought, and turned the heel a little more.

---

## Beat 18 — the second refusal

— Ripples —

She tried rolling with it.

If she could get onto her side, the way the twist ran, the knee would have somewhere to go other than further. That
was all she wanted: for the knee to have somewhere to go. She didn't have a plan beyond that. There wasn't much
plan left in her at all; there was the knee, and the heat in it, and the one idea, *roll with it*, that her body
grabbed at like a branch in a flood.

She threw her shoulders over to the right. Her free leg kicked to help. She got halfway onto her side, her cheek
pressed into the grass and her paws clawing for purchase, and then Nerine's legs, still locked round her thigh,
simply didn't come with her. The hips stayed where they were. The trapped leg stayed where it was. And the knee,
caught between the half of her that had turned and the half that hadn't, took the whole of the spin.

She came back flat onto her back with a sound she'd never heard herself make before. It started low in her chest
and climbed, and broke apart at the top into a string of short ragged yelps, one on every breath, *ah, ah, ah*, that
she couldn't stop.

Her foot hurt. Her teeth were showing in a grimace, she could feel it, the lips drawn right back off them as she
braced against the arm crushing her heel. Her thigh ached, and her claws were flexing into the ground with each
press of Nerine's knees, sinking in and drawing out of the soft earth, in and out. The knee was beyond aching. The
knee was a deep trench of pain with Nerine's grip at the bottom of it, and the muscle around it was quivering on its
own, a fine fast trembling that she could feel but not control.

It was desperate now. She knew that the way you know you're cold. She was heaving against the hold with everything
she had and it was all graceless and frantic and useless, her breath coming in sobbing gasps, her paws scrabbling at
the grass and at her own leg and at nothing, and some animal part of her had begun to panic.

And then she stopped.

All at once, for a moment, she stopped fighting the hold at all. Every muscle in her was called off it and given a
different job, and the job was her paws. She brought them both up onto her own chest, one over the other, and
pressed them down flat, hard, as if they might get away from her. Her whole body went still around that. She lay on
her back with her eyes shut and her paws clamped to her chest and let the knee do whatever it was going to do,
because the only thing that mattered, the only thing she had left any say over, was that those paws didn't touch
the ground.

*Not to her,* she thought. *Anyone else. Not to her.*

She didn't know why it mattered so much. It had something to do with the bouncing, and the laughing, and the way
Nerine had looked at her leg from the very start. It mattered more than the knee did. It was the only thing that did.

— Nerine —

Through her own arm Nerine felt the struggle stop, and for a moment she thought it was over.

She waited for the tap. It ought to come next. That was what happened when they stopped fighting: their body gave
up first and their paw followed it. She held the twist steady and watched Ripples' paws, and they didn't go to the
ground. They went to Ripples' own chest and stayed there, pressed flat, the knuckles white under the fur.

Nerine looked down the length of the trapped leg at Ripples' face. The eyes were screwed shut. The jaw was working,
the lip still bloody where she'd bitten it. Every so often a shudder went through her whole body from the knee
outward, and her paws pressed down harder when it did, and that was all.

Nerine's own arm was tiring. The crook of her elbow ached from the heel, and her wrist, locked under her other paw,
was beginning to burn, and her knees were sore from clamping. She held it anyway. But something in her own chest went
quiet as she watched, something that wasn't quite pity and wasn't quite anything else she had a name for. She hadn't
expected this one to last. She'd expected her to tap at the first real crank, the way anyone sensible would.

"You've done enough," she said, low. It came out sounding more like a fact than a taunt. She hadn't meant it to come
out any way at all.

Ripples didn't answer. Her paws stayed on her chest.

---

## Beat 19 — the third refusal

— Ripples —

The kick came out of nowhere, even to her.

She hadn't decided to do it. Her trapped leg simply jerked inside the hook, short and stupid and hard, the only
thing it could still do, as if her body had given up on asking her and was going to try tearing the foot loose by
itself. It jerked once, twice. It got her nothing. The heel stayed exactly where it was in the crook of Nerine's arm,
and the knee paid for both kicks, and a whine slipped out of her as she strained.

She squirmed. Her breath was hissing in and out of her through her teeth. Her foot ached, a deep crushed soreness
round the heel and up the back of the ankle, where Nerine's arm had flattened the muscle and pushed it aside into a
crease. The thigh hurt where the knees held it, pressed in so deep that the fur had creased up into a thick ridge
round them. And the knee. She bit down on the sound the knee wanted to make, and it came out anyway, past her
teeth, a thin high keen that she couldn't stop and didn't recognise.

She had no thoughts left that were thoughts. There was the knee, and the grass under her shoulders, and the sky,
which had a cloud in it now, small and white and very far away. She watched the cloud. She didn't know why.

Somewhere along the way her eyes had started to stream.

She didn't know when. She didn't feel it begin. The wet was simply there, running down both sides of her face from
the corners of her eyes and into the fur under her ears, cooling as it went, and she didn't wipe it and didn't blink
it away. She didn't seem to know it was there at all. Her mouth was open. Her breath came in short pulls that caught
at the top, one after another, a little hitch at the end of each like a sob that never quite finished.

Her paws were still on her chest. She'd forgotten they were there, too, but they were.

There was no reason left in it now. She wasn't thinking about the slack from before, or about Nerine, or about why
it mattered whose paw went where. She wasn't thinking about anything except the one thing her whole body was asking,
which was *please*, and it wasn't asking Nerine. It was asking the knee. It was asking the afternoon.

*Just stop,* she thought, not in words. *Just let it stop by itself.*

— Nerine —

Nerine watched the tears and adjusted her grip by a claw's width, so the twist would run straighter into the joint.

It was a cold, small thing to do and she did it without hesitating. She'd felt the kick come through her arm, weak
and wild, and she'd felt the leg give up kicking afterward, and she'd looked along it to the face and seen the wet
running down into the fur. She'd waited a breath for the paws to move. They hadn't.

*Still no?* she thought. *Then it isn't enough yet.*

That was all it meant. The other Buizel was still refusing; so the hold needed more. Nerine's arm was aching all the
way up into her shoulder now, and the joint of her wrist had gone hot under her locking paw, and she was breathing
harder than she wanted to be. She shifted her knees on the thigh and dug them in again, and sat a little lower, and
turned her shoulders the last small way they'd go.

Ripples made a sound at that, and her paws pressed down harder on her chest, and stayed there.

Nerine set her jaw. Somewhere behind her, out on the lake, a fish jumped, and the sound of it falling back came
across the clearing very clearly, a single clean splash, and then nothing.

---

## Beat 20 — out of it, barely

— Nerine —

She couldn't make the knee give her any more from where she was. Nerine had turned it as far as her shoulders would
go, and the heel was locked in her right arm as tight as she could lock it, and still Ripples lay there with her paws
on her chest and wouldn't give up.

So she took her left paw off her own wrist. Only that one. The right arm stayed clamped round the heel and her knees
stayed clamped round the thigh, and the twist held, steady and grinding. But her left paw was free now, and she
raised it high over Ripples' head with the edge turned down, and brought it down.

— Ripples —

The Brick Break hit her on the top of the head with a solid *whump*, like a sack thrown down off a cart, and drove
her skull back into the grass.

The grass gave nothing back. There was nowhere for her head to go. It was struck from above and met from below in
the same instant, and the jolt of it went down through her neck and out into her left ear, which rang with a high,
flat, jarring note that went on after the blow was gone. Her whole body jolted and slid a little where it lay, an
inch along the turf. Her eyes went bright and blank for a moment.

She hadn't been able to cover up. Her paws had been on her chest. She hadn't even thought of lifting them.

And under the blow, the hold. That was the worst of it, the two together: the head throbbing and the ear singing
and underneath all of it, steady, unchanged, the knee. Nerine's paw went straight back to her own wrist after the
chop, and the grip on the heel locked again, and the twist came on harder, a heavy grinding crank that drove the
joint out of its shape. The foot was hurting now, properly, the pressure crushing down round the heel so that the
whole foot seemed to give round it, and a whine slipped out of Ripples as she strained. Her thigh tensed all over
under Nerine's knees, her breath held, and then let go in a long hiss.

Her paws had come off her chest. She didn't know when. They were at her knee now, pushing at it, at Nerine's arm,
at anything, pushing and pushing, frantic and useless.

She curled her toes. It was the last idea she had, and it came from somewhere below thinking: curl the toes, lock
the ankle, make the whole foot one hard knot so there was nothing in it to turn. She curled them so hard her calf
cramped. She locked the ankle until it shook.

It didn't stop the twist. Nothing stopped it. But when Nerine shifted her arm to get a better purchase on the knot,
Ripples felt the heel move in the crook of the elbow, felt it slip, and every scrap of her that was left went into
one wrench of her whole body. She threw herself sideways with her back and her hips and her free leg all at once,
and the knot of her foot tore out of Nerine's arm like a cork out of a bottle, and her thigh dragged out from between
Nerine's knees, and she was rolling away across the grass, and she was free.

She didn't think about it. Her body did it before she could.

She got her paws under her on the grass and pushed. Halfway up, the left knee folded and dropped her.

She landed square on it.

It was a new kind of pain. Sharper, higher, completely different from the long grinding ache of the hold: a white
flash that went off inside the joint like a struck stone and blanked out everything else. A short, cracked yelp got
out of her before she could stop it. Then she shut her mouth on the rest and held it in, her teeth clenched and her
whole body rigid over the knee, and the pain stayed there, in the knee, where she kept it. She breathed through her
nose. In. Out. The knee had crossed a line in that fall. She could feel it. Whatever it had been before, it was
something worse now, something that would not let her forget it for a single step.

*Up,* she thought. *Up anyway.*

The second try got her almost upright. She came up off her paws, got her right foot planted and her left foot
under her, rose nearly to standing, and then both the knee and the thigh above it gave at once and she went down
again hard on her side in the grass.

On the third she did it the slow way. She rolled onto her front first. She got her paws flat under her shoulders and
her right knee in under her, and left the left leg out straight behind her, dragging, and then pushed herself up off
the right with her paws helping, slowly, slowly, the left foot barely touching the grass.

She stood. All her weight was on her right leg. Her left foot rested on the grass the way you'd rest a cup you
weren't sure of. Her sides were heaving. Her face was still wet.

She looked at Nerine, and Nerine looked at her, and neither of them said anything at all.

---

## Beat 21 — the tails miss

— Nerine —

Nerine let her stand there. She needed the breath herself.

Her right arm ached from the elbow to the shoulder where she'd held the heel, and her wrist was hot and stiff, and
her knees felt as if she'd been kneeling on stones. She shook them all out, one at a time, and watched Ripples watch
her do it. The other Buizel was standing on one leg and a half. The left foot hardly touched the grass. Every time
she shifted, her whole face tightened round the knee, and her ears went flat, and came up again slowly.

*She can't move on that,* Nerine thought. *Not really. Not fast.*

So Nerine moved for both of them. She circled to Ripples' left, quick and light, the way she liked to fight best,
and made her turn on that bad knee to follow. Ripples turned. She did it slowly, hopping, keeping the weight on the
right foot, and the knee caught every time it took the smallest share of her.

On the third circle Nerine spun.

It was a whole-body spin, low, from the hips, and both her tails came whipping round after it at shin height,
flat and fast, meant to crack across the hurt shin like a pair of wet ropes. It was the kind of blow that took the feet out from under a fighter who couldn't jump.

She felt it go wide before she saw it.

— Ripples —

She saw the hips turn. That was all, but it was enough: Nerine's hips swung, low and hard, and there was only one
thing that could come round after a turn like that.

Ripples didn't jump. She couldn't have. She twisted instead, from the waist, her body going sideways out of the
line of the swing as if it had been pulled by a string, her bad leg dragged after her, and the tails went past her
ankles with a whistle like a whip cracking empty air. She felt the wind of them in the fur on her shin. They missed
her by a hair. They missed her completely.

Nerine came round out of the spin a step further than she'd meant to, off balance, her tails wrapping round her own
legs, and caught herself with a small, cross skip.

Ripples stood where she'd twisted to, breathing hard, her weight back on her right foot, and for a moment she didn't
quite believe it.

It was the first thing all fight that had simply missed. Everything else had landed somewhere: the knee, the thigh,
the hip, the head, the throw, the slam. She'd spent the whole afternoon being found by things. And this one had
missed. She'd seen it coming and she'd got out of its way, on one leg, with a knee that felt as if somebody had
packed it full of hot gravel.

*I can still do that,* she thought. *I can still see her.*

It wasn't much. She knew it wasn't much. But she held onto it the way she'd held onto the slack in the heel hook,
because it was something, and it was hers.

The knee was screaming at her for the twist. She let it. She kept her face still and her fins up, and when Nerine
straightened from her skip and looked at her, Ripples looked straight back.

Nerine didn't seem to quite believe it either.

---

## Beat 22 — the guard, and the gust

— Ripples —

Ripples went back to the throat. It was the only place she'd ever found on Nerine that wasn't guarded, and it was
the only idea she had left.

She couldn't come in fast. She came in the only way she could, a lurching hop off her right foot with the left
dragged behind, and swung the right fin across in the old flat arc as she came. Bare now, no ice on it, just the
hard blue edge. It was a good blow still. She put her shoulders into it and her hips, everything above the knee.

Nerine had learned.

Her left arm came up across her throat before the fin was halfway there, the forearm raised and the elbow tucked
in, the whole limb braced like a bar across a door. Ripples saw it and couldn't stop.

— Nerine —

The fin hit her left upper arm with the dry rip of an edge dragged through fur, and that was all.

It glanced off. The arm was all bone and hard muscle there, and she'd braced it, and the blow had come in slow off
one leg with nothing under it, and it skidded across the outside of her arm and flung it out to the side the way the
fin was going, and that was the end of it. A scrape. A thin warm sting along the outside of the arm, already fading.
And the water on the fin did what the water always did against her: splashed and ran off and was nothing.

She looked at Ripples over her arm. Ripples was standing there with the fin still out at the end of its swing,
panting, her face gone very still.

*That's the last time you cut my throat,* Nerine thought. *I've got you now. I know where you're going before you
do.*

She was lowering her arm when she heard the lake.

It was a sound like breath being drawn in, a long rising hush from behind her, and she turned her head in time to
see it coming: a gust off the water, sudden and cold, running in across the shallows and flattening the surface into
a dark ruffled band as it came. It hit the sand and threw a spray of it up. It hit the long grass of the clearing and
laid it down flat in a wave, a great silver ripple running inland.

Then it hit her. It slapped into her back and her tails with a shove of cold air and a fine stinging spray of
lake water off the surface, enough to make her stagger half a step and throw out her arms for balance, enough to
make her flinch and duck her head and glance back over her shoulder at the water.

It passed. The grass sprang up again behind it, rustling. The lake settled. Nothing else happened: nobody was
knocked down, nobody was hurt, the clearing was exactly as it had been a moment before. It had just reminded them
both, for a breath, that it was there.

When Nerine looked back, Ripples was still standing in front of her, panting, with nothing to show for her swing and
her eyes gone briefly to the water too.

They both looked at each other. Neither of them looked at the lake again.

---

## Beat 23 — Quick Attack into the thigh

— Nerine —

She'd seen everything she needed to.

Ripples was standing on her right leg and nothing else. The left thigh, the one she'd kneed and bitten, had gone
soft at the front where the bruise was; Nerine could see it from here, the fur roughed up and dark over it, the way
the leg hung a little forward off the hip as if it didn't want to carry anything. The knee below it was worse. But
the knee was low and she'd have to stoop. The thigh was right there, at the height of her shoulder.

*Fast,* she thought. *Before she can turn it.*

Quick Attack was the thing she was best at, the thing she'd been born for, and she'd barely used it all fight. She
gathered herself low in the grass and went, and the clearing blurred.

There was no gap between them. One moment she was three lengths away and the next her shoulder was there, and in the
instant before it landed she saw the leg move. Ripples had seen her coming. The thigh snatched back, the whole body
bracing, the hip turning away, a fraction late. Far too late.

Nerine knew what that meant. *You know it's going. You know.*

— Ripples —

The shoulder went into the front of her left thigh with a meaty smack, right in the bitten place, with every bit
of Nerine's speed behind it.

It went in like a fist into dough. The thigh, which had been hot and bruised and tooth-marked and aching, stopped
being any of those and became one thing, a deep, crushing, throbbing pain that filled the whole front of the leg from
hip to knee. The force ran up into the hip with a solid jolt. And it ran down into the knee.

The knee took her over.

It wasn't a flinch. It was something much bigger. The knee had been past bearing already, and the jolt went into it
like a spark into dry grass, and for a moment there was nothing in the world but the knee. The leg folded under her.
She couldn't put any weight on it at all. A whimper came out of her, high and thin and shaking, and she hopped
sideways off it on her right foot, once, twice, three times, her arms flung out wide, her guard gone, her whole body
lurching after the leg.

Her footing went on that side and kept going. She staggered two, three steps to her right before her right foot found
the ground solidly enough to stop her, her bad leg dragging, her paws out wide for balance and her fins splayed. She
nearly went down. She didn't. She caught herself at the end of the stagger, bent over, swaying, out of her stance
and out of place.

*Feet,* she thought. *Get your feet. Now. Now.*

She knew exactly where she was. A step out of place. Slow. Open. The hurt leg wouldn't brace; it was the reason
she'd gone so far, and she could feel exactly where it had failed her, in the thigh and the knee together. And she
knew Nerine could see it too.

Her breath had gone ragged. She noticed it as she straightened, a little at a time: the air coming in heavy and
short and going out in a rasp, her chest working too hard. It had been creeping up on her for a while, she thought.
She was tired. Not done. Tired, the way a swimmer is tired a long way from the bank, every stroke still strong but
costing more than the last. The next breath was harder to find than the last one had been.

The thigh throbbed along its whole length. The knee throbbed under it. She held her paws up, and they shook.

---

## Beat 24 — the third slam

— Nerine —

Nerine followed her in before the stagger was over.

Ripples was still bent and swaying, her paws out, her bad leg trailing, and she was a step too far to the right
of where she'd been and hadn't yet found her balance. Nerine came straight at her and ducked under the reaching
paws and got her arms round the middle again, the way she had twice already. She could feel how tired the other
Buizel was through her own arms: the ribs heaving under them, the heat coming off her.

*Third time,* she thought.

She lifted. Ripples came up off the grass more easily than ever, hardly kicking, her paws scrabbling at Nerine's
back and sliding off the fur, too slow to find a grip.

Nerine slammed her down.

— Ripples —

The sky swung over her. Then the grass hit her flat across the back, upper and lower, a solid knock all along her
spine, and drove what was left of her breath out of her in a long, thin wheeze.

There was nothing new about it except how little she could do. Her back had been sore since the second slam, a
broad heavy bruising from her shoulders to her tails, and the grass found every bit of it again and woke it up.
The lower back, especially, took it badly: a deep, tender ache settled in at the base of her spine, where the
muscle had already been knotted, and stayed.

She lay face-up. She could feel the grass under her shoulders, cool and crushed. She could feel the sun on her
face, warm, and the throbbing of the leg, and her breath coming slowly back in thin, painful pulls.

The stagger went out of her, lying there. That was the strange thing. The lurching, the feeling of being a step
out of place, of her body not quite where she'd left it: it drained out of her into the ground, and her head
cleared, and she knew exactly where she was again. On her back. On the grass. With Nerine standing over her.

Her left paw went down to her knee without her asking it to. It pressed there, on the outside of the joint, very
gently, as if she could hold it together from the outside. The knee didn't like being touched. She left the paw
there anyway. Her thigh above it was hot under her wrist.

She did not try to get up. Not yet. She was gathering herself for it, she told herself. She was getting her breath.
That was all. She would get up in a moment, the way she'd got up every other time, and Nerine would see her do it.

Above her, Nerine's head and shoulders blotted out a piece of the sky. She was breathing hard too. She was looking
at Ripples' paw on her knee.

Ripples took the paw away, quickly, and put it flat on the grass.

---

## Beat 25 — the stamp

— Nerine —

Nerine didn't let her gather anything.

She'd seen the paw on the knee. She'd seen it snatched away. And she'd seen Ripples lying there flat on her back
with nowhere to roll, both legs out in the grass, and the left one, the one she'd been taking apart all afternoon,
lying closest to her.

*Finish it,* she thought. *Finish the knee and the rest of her will follow.*

She stepped round to Ripples' left side and lifted her foot.

Ripples saw it. Nerine watched her see it: watched the black eyes go wide and drop to the foot hanging over her, and
the paw come flying back toward the knee to cover it, too late, far too late, and the leg try to draw up out of the
way and only get halfway before the foot came down.

— Ripples —

The heel came down on the side of her left knee with the crack of a hard hit, and drove it into the ground.

There was nowhere for it to go. The grass was under the knee and the foot was on top of it, and the whole of
Nerine's weight came down through that one heel into the joint and pressed it flat into the earth. The jolt went
straight down into her left foot, a hard jarring knock that clenched her toes and turned the ache in the foot into
something deep and sharp. Her own paw, the one that had flown to cover the knee, had got there just in time to be
in the way, and was knocked aside.

She screamed.

It wasn't a yelp. It wasn't anything she'd made all fight. It came from somewhere at the bottom of her and went on
and on, raw and loud and cracking, until there was no breath left in her to make it with, and then she dragged in
another breath and it went on some more. The pine branches above her blurred and swam. Her back arched up off the
ground and came down again. Her hands clawed at the grass on either side of her and tore it up in fistfuls.

The knee didn't feel like a knee any more. It didn't feel like anything she had a name for. It was a hot, enormous,
pounding thing pressed into the dent it had made in the turf, and every beat of her heart went into it and came back
out as more pain. It was past anything. The foot below it was hot and wrong, throbbing in a way that made her want to
draw it up and couldn't, because to move it she'd have to move the knee.

Nerine's foot came off it. The pressure lifted. The pain didn't.

She tried to get up.

She didn't decide to. Her body tried for her, the way it had tried every other time, because getting up was what
she did. She got as far as lifting her shoulders off the grass, and then everything below the waist refused, and she
fell back.

She tried again. Her paws pushed at the ground. Her head came up. She couldn't even get far enough to sit; her back
simply wouldn't fold with that leg attached to it, and she sank back into the crushed grass.

The third time she only lifted her head.

Her thigh throbbed with it, the bitten muscle tight and hot. She pressed her paw to it, flat, the only part of the
leg she could bear to touch. She lay there on her back with her chest heaving and her face wet and her paw on her
thigh, and the pain went on and on and on.

Nerine was standing over her. Ripples could see her out of the corner of her eye, very still, looking at the knee.
She was looking at it for a long time. She wasn't smiling.

---

## Beat 26 — the knee crank

— Nerine —

There was only one place left to go.

Nerine looked at the knee for a long moment before she touched it. It had changed since she'd stamped on it. It sat
wrong in the grass, swollen round the joint so that the fur stood out from it, and every time Ripples breathed, the
leg below it trembled. Ripples' paw was pressed to her thigh above it. She wasn't touching the knee itself. She
couldn't, Nerine thought.

*If she'd tap,* Nerine thought, *I wouldn't have to.*

She crouched over it, on her feet, one either side of the leg. She took Ripples' left knee in both paws, one above the joint and one below, the way you'd take hold of a
stubborn root to wrench it out of the ground. She felt the heat coming off it through the fur. She felt Ripples go rigid
under her hands.

And at the same time she reached back with her twin tails, both of them, and wound them round Ripples' left foot,
once, twice, the way a Buizel winds them round a reed to hold herself against a current. They tightened. Then she pulled
the foot one way with her tails and twisted the knee the other with her paws, against the grain, against the way it
was ever meant to bend.

— Ripples —

The knee had already been through too much. When Nerine twisted it, there was nothing left in it to argue.

The pain didn't build, the way it had in the heel hook. It was simply there, all at once, the whole of it, as if
someone had opened a door on it. Even the first steady pressure of Nerine's paws was unbearable. Ripples screamed.
It went on longer than she would have believed she had breath for, and it cracked at the top, high and thin and
splitting, and kept going, and when it finally ran out she dragged in another breath and screamed again.

Nerine's paws were sunk into the fur either side of the joint, so deep they'd left a trench, and the muscle under
them was quivering on its own, a fine, fast, helpless shiver she couldn't stop or slow. And down past the knee, the
tails had her foot. They'd wound round it twice and they were hauling, the fur pressed flat round the coils and the
shape of them printed into her ankle, and they pulled the foot one way while the paws wrenched the knee the other, so
the whole leg was being wrung like washing between them.

She screamed at that too. It had stopped being separate screams. It was all one sound, with breaths in it.

Her paw had come off her thigh. Both her paws were at the grass, clawing at it, digging furrows in the soft earth.
Her back arched and fell. Her right leg kicked out across the grass and found nothing.

The foot was changing. She noticed it the way you notice the light going: slowly, and then all at once. It had been
hot, and pinched, and aching inside the coils. Now it was going cold. Then it was tingling, a fizzing prickling
numbness that crept up from her toes into her ankle, so that the foot was there and not there, weak and far away and
slow to answer. She tried to curl the toes the way she had in the heel hook, and they barely moved. They didn't feel
like hers. The tails had them.

The knee didn't go numb. The knee went on, and on, and on. The knee was the only thing in the world.

She could feel the pressure stay. It wasn't letting go. It wasn't going to let go. It wasn't a hold you could wait
out, or slip, or roll with. It was just a choice, held out to her, every breath. Slap the ground and it stops.

She put her paws flat on the grass and pressed them down, and didn't lift them.

*No,* she thought. *No. Not yet.*

---

## Beat 27 — the fourth refusal

— Ripples —

She tried to find the angle the slow way.

She didn't have much else left to try with. Her body couldn't buck any more, not properly; it shook when she asked
it to and that was all. So she moved her shoulders, a little at a time, feeling for where the twist was running so
she could turn with it instead of against it, the way you find the run of a current with your fingers before you
swim. She found it. It ran outward, to the left. She threw herself after it, her shoulders over and her hips
following, rolling onto her side.

The tails on her foot simply followed her round. Nerine's paws went with the knee as it turned, and stayed exactly
where they were on it, and the knee was still caught, still twisted, and the roll had only given the crank more to
work with. She came back onto her back with a choking squeal dragged out of her, and another, one on each crank, as
Nerine bore down on the joint.

The paws on her knee were heavy now. They'd pressed the muscle flat and the flesh bulged up round the edges of them.
The tails had tightened again round her foot, a fresh hard cinch, and the foot was further away than ever, numb and
prickling and slow. The crease where the coils bit ran deep into the fur of her ankle. She could feel it as a line of
pressure in a foot she otherwise couldn't feel at all.

She was heaving against it. Frantic, graceless, sobbing heaves, her breath coming in gasps and her paws scrabbling
at the grass and at Nerine's arms and at her own leg, close to panic. She knew she was close to panic. She didn't
care.

"No," she was saying.

She didn't remember starting. It was just there, coming out of her on every breath, low and cracked and steady.
"No. No. No." She wasn't saying it to Nerine. She was saying it to the paw that kept wanting to drift away from the
grass by her hip and lift and come down, slap, slap, and end it. She was saying it to the voice somewhere inside her
that was so tired, so very tired, and only wanted to stop.

*I've given her nothing,* she thought. *Not one inch. Not all afternoon.*

She hadn't. She'd been thrown and tripped and slammed and bitten and stamped on, and every time she'd got up, and
she had not once said stop. She was not going to be the one who did. Not now. Not with the knee. She'd rather lie
here till dark.

"No," she said again, softer, to the sky.

— Nerine —

Nerine heard the *no* and kept the crank on, and didn't let her face move.

She had both paws on the knee and both tails on the foot and her whole body bent over the leg, and it was taking
everything she had. Her arms burned. Her tails ached right down to the roots from hauling. She was panting through
her open mouth, and her eyes were stinging with sweat. And she held it, and she turned it a little more on every
breath, and she listened to the other Buizel saying no.

Under her paws, though, she could feel something she didn't like.

There was a looseness in the knee that hadn't been there a beat ago. A give, at the very edge of the twist, as if
something deep in the joint had stopped holding quite as hard as it had been holding. It wasn't a sound. It wasn't
anything she could see. It was only a feeling, through the pads of her paws, a softness where there ought to have
been none.

*What is that?* she thought. *What's that doing?*

She didn't know what it was. She didn't know how much more of this the knee had in it. She found she didn't want to
find out, and the not wanting frightened her a little. It was not a thing she had expected to feel with a hold
half done.

She didn't stop. She kept her face still, so Ripples wouldn't see. But she held the crank where it was, and didn't
turn it any further.

---

## Beat 28 — the tap

— Nerine —

She had to end it. One way or another.

Nerine took her right paw off the knee. Only the right: the left stayed clamped on the joint, and the tails stayed
wound round the foot, and the twist held, grinding, steady. But the right paw was free, and she raised it high over
Ripples' head with the edge turned down, and she brought it down with everything she had left.

It wasn't anger. She wasn't sure what it was. It was the last thing she knew how to do.

— Ripples —

The chop came down across the side of her head with the crack of a hard hit.

It struck her skull and her right ear at once, and the end of it caught her throat, and there was nowhere for any of
it to go. The ground was right there. Her head was struck from above and met from below in the same instant and her
whole body shuddered along the grass with it and came to rest a little further on, her cheek in the turf. The *no*
she'd been saying was knocked out of her mouth and turned into a dry, hacking cough that bent her up off the ground.
Her right ear rang. Her throat burned where the blow had glanced across it.

Her head throbbed. Deeply, at the top and the side, a heavy pounding that didn't stop when the ringing did. She
hadn't covered up. She hadn't had anything to cover up with. Her paws had been flat on the grass, holding on.

Nerine's paw went straight back to the knee.

And the knee was what did it. Not the blow. The blow had only been the last thing, the thing that came along on top
of everything else. The crank came back on hard over the throbbing head and the ringing ear, and the paws sank in
round the joint and squeezed it out of its shape, and the tails cinched again round the numb, prickling foot. And
something in Ripples simply stopped holding.

She thrashed. There was no aim in it at all. Her paws clawed at the grass and at the air and at Nerine's arms, at
anything. Her paws pushed and pushed at the tails round her foot, frantic, and couldn't shift them. Her body heaved.
And her right paw came off the grass and went up, and she watched it, from very far away, as if it belonged to
someone else.

It came down on the grass beside her, flat.

And then again, and again, much harder than it needed to be, slap, slap, slap, as if she were afraid that one
wouldn't count. As if, having done it, she had to make absolutely sure.

— Nerine —

Nerine felt the slaps come up through the ground before she heard them.

She let go at once. Both paws came off the knee together, and she unwound her tails from the foot, fast, so fast the
coils whipped loose and slapped against her own legs, and she rolled away onto her back in the grass and lay there
with her arms and her tails and her chest all burning, panting at the sky.

It was over. She'd won. She waited to feel it.

Beside her, Ripples didn't move away. She couldn't. She curled onto her side round the leg as far as it would let
her, which wasn't far, her back to Nerine, one paw cupped over the knee without quite touching it. The pain that had
had nowhere to go for so long went everywhere at once now, out of the knee and into all of her, and her breath shook
coming in and shook going out. Her shoulders were heaving. She was awake for every second of it. She was making a
small sound, low and steady, under every breath.

After a while Nerine got up.

She stood over the other Buizel, favouring nothing, because nothing on her was badly hurt. A sting on her shoulder.
A bruise low on her back. Her arms were tired. That was all. She looked down at Ripples, curled in the crushed grass
with her paw over her knee and her face hidden, and found that she didn't feel like crowing. She'd thought she would.
She'd thought, all fight, about how it would feel when this one finally gave up.

She'd had to take that knee apart to get the tap. And even then it had been the knee that gave up, not Ripples.

*I don't think I could have done that,* Nerine thought. *Lasted that.* It was not a comfortable thought.

The lake lapped at the sand. The bird in the oaks had started up its three notes again. Nerine sat down in the grass
beside Ripples, close enough to touch and not touching, and waited for her breathing to slow.
