## Beat 26 — the knee crank

— Nerine —

There was only one place left to go.

Nerine looked at the knee for a long moment before she touched it. It had changed since she'd stamped on it. It sat
wrong in the grass, swollen round the joint so that the fur stood out from it, and every time Ripples breathed, the
leg below it trembled. Ripples' paw was pressed to her thigh above it. She wasn't touching the knee itself. She
couldn't, Nerine thought.

*If she'd tap,* Nerine thought, *I wouldn't have to.*

She crouched over it, on her feet, one either side of the leg. She took Ripples' left knee in both paws, one above the joint and one below, the way you'd take hold of a
stubborn root to wrench it out of the ground. She felt the heat coming off it through the fur. She felt Ripples go rigid
under her hands.

And at the same time she reached back with her twin tails, both of them, and wound them round Ripples' left foot,
once, twice, the way a Buizel winds them round a reed to hold herself against a current. They tightened. Then she pulled
the foot one way with her tails and twisted the knee the other with her paws, against the grain, against the way it
was ever meant to bend.

— Ripples —

The knee had already been through too much. When Nerine twisted it, there was nothing left in it to argue.

The pain didn't build, the way it had in the heel hook. It was simply there, all at once, the whole of it, as if
someone had opened a door on it. Even the first steady pressure of Nerine's paws was unbearable. Ripples screamed.
It went on longer than she would have believed she had breath for, and it cracked at the top, high and thin and
splitting, and kept going, and when it finally ran out she dragged in another breath and screamed again.

Nerine's paws were sunk into the fur either side of the joint, so deep they'd left a trench, and the muscle under
them was quivering on its own, a fine, fast, helpless shiver she couldn't stop or slow. And down past the knee, the
tails had her foot. They'd wound round it twice and they were hauling, the fur pressed flat round the coils and the
shape of them printed into her ankle, and they pulled the foot one way while the paws wrenched the knee the other, so
the whole leg was being wrung like washing between them.

She screamed at that too. It had stopped being separate screams. It was all one sound, with breaths in it.

Her paw had come off her thigh. Both her paws were at the grass, clawing at it, digging furrows in the soft earth.
Her back arched and fell. Her right leg kicked out across the grass and found nothing.

The foot was changing. She noticed it the way you notice the light going: slowly, and then all at once. It had been
hot, and pinched, and aching inside the coils. Now it was going cold. Then it was tingling, a fizzing prickling
numbness that crept up from her toes into her ankle, so that the foot was there and not there, weak and far away and
slow to answer. She tried to curl the toes the way she had in the heel hook, and they barely moved. They didn't feel
like hers. The tails had them.

The knee didn't go numb. The knee went on, and on, and on. The knee was the only thing in the world.

She could feel the pressure stay. It wasn't letting go. It wasn't going to let go. It wasn't a hold you could wait
out, or slip, or roll with. It was just a choice, held out to her, every breath. Slap the ground and it stops.

She put her paws flat on the grass and pressed them down, and didn't lift them.

*No,* she thought. *No. Not yet.*

## Beat 27 — the fourth refusal

— Ripples —

She tried to find the angle the slow way.

She didn't have much else left to try with. Her body couldn't buck any more, not properly; it shook when she asked
it to and that was all. So she moved her shoulders, a little at a time, feeling for where the twist was running so
she could turn with it instead of against it, the way you find the run of a current with your fingers before you
swim. She found it. It ran outward, to the left. She threw herself after it, her shoulders over and her hips
following, rolling onto her side.

The tails on her foot simply followed her round. Nerine's paws went with the knee as it turned, and stayed exactly
where they were on it, and the knee was still caught, still twisted, and the roll had only given the crank more to
work with. She came back onto her back with a choking squeal dragged out of her, and another, one on each crank, as
Nerine bore down on the joint.

The paws on her knee were heavy now. They'd pressed the muscle flat and the flesh bulged up round the edges of them.
The tails had tightened again round her foot, a fresh hard cinch, and the foot was further away than ever, numb and
prickling and slow. The crease where the coils bit ran deep into the fur of her ankle. She could feel it as a line of
pressure in a foot she otherwise couldn't feel at all.

She was heaving against it. Frantic, graceless, sobbing heaves, her breath coming in gasps and her paws scrabbling
at the grass and at Nerine's arms and at her own leg, close to panic. She knew she was close to panic. She didn't
care.

"No," she was saying.

She didn't remember starting. It was just there, coming out of her on every breath, low and cracked and steady.
"No. No. No." She wasn't saying it to Nerine. She was saying it to the paw that kept wanting to drift away from the
grass by her hip and lift and come down, slap, slap, and end it. She was saying it to the voice somewhere inside her
that was so tired, so very tired, and only wanted to stop.

*I've given her nothing,* she thought. *Not one inch. Not all afternoon.*

She hadn't. She'd been thrown and tripped and slammed and bitten and stamped on, and every time she'd got up, and
she had not once said stop. She was not going to be the one who did. Not now. Not with the knee. She'd rather lie
here till dark.

"No," she said again, softer, to the sky.

— Nerine —

Nerine heard the *no* and kept the crank on, and didn't let her face move.

She had both paws on the knee and both tails on the foot and her whole body bent over the leg, and it was taking
everything she had. Her arms burned. Her tails ached right down to the roots from hauling. She was panting through
her open mouth, and her eyes were stinging with sweat. And she held it, and she turned it a little more on every
breath, and she listened to the other Buizel saying no.

Under her paws, though, she could feel something she didn't like.

There was a looseness in the knee that hadn't been there a beat ago. A give, at the very edge of the twist, as if
something deep in the joint had stopped holding quite as hard as it had been holding. It wasn't a sound. It wasn't
anything she could see. It was only a feeling, through the pads of her paws, a softness where there ought to have
been none.

*What is that?* she thought. *What's that doing?*

She didn't know what it was. She didn't know how much more of this the knee had in it. She found she didn't want to
find out, and the not wanting frightened her a little. It was not a thing she had expected to feel with a hold
half done.

She didn't stop. She kept her face still, so Ripples wouldn't see. But she held the crank where it was, and didn't
turn it any further.

## Beat 28 — the tap

— Nerine —

She had to end it. One way or another.

Nerine took her right paw off the knee. Only the right: the left stayed clamped on the joint, and the tails stayed
wound round the foot, and the twist held, grinding, steady. But the right paw was free, and she raised it high over
Ripples' head with the edge turned down, and she brought it down with everything she had left.

It wasn't anger. She wasn't sure what it was. It was the last thing she knew how to do.

— Ripples —

The chop came down across the side of her head with the crack of a hard hit.

It struck her skull and her right ear at once, and the end of it caught her throat, and there was nowhere for any of
it to go. The ground was right there. Her head was struck from above and met from below in the same instant and her
whole body shuddered along the grass with it and came to rest a little further on, her cheek in the turf. The *no*
she'd been saying was knocked out of her mouth and turned into a dry, hacking cough that bent her up off the ground.
Her right ear rang. Her throat burned where the blow had glanced across it.

Her head throbbed. Deeply, at the top and the side, a heavy pounding that didn't stop when the ringing did. She
hadn't covered up. She hadn't had anything to cover up with. Her paws had been flat on the grass, holding on.

Nerine's paw went straight back to the knee.

And the knee was what did it. Not the blow. The blow had only been the last thing, the thing that came along on top
of everything else. The crank came back on hard over the throbbing head and the ringing ear, and the paws sank in
round the joint and squeezed it out of its shape, and the tails cinched again round the numb, prickling foot. And
something in Ripples simply stopped holding.

She thrashed. There was no aim in it at all. Her paws clawed at the grass and at the air and at Nerine's arms, at
anything. Her paws pushed and pushed at the tails round her foot, frantic, and couldn't shift them. Her body heaved.
And her right paw came off the grass and went up, and she watched it, from very far away, as if it belonged to
someone else.

It came down on the grass beside her, flat.

And then again, and again, much harder than it needed to be, slap, slap, slap, as if she were afraid that one
wouldn't count. As if, having done it, she had to make absolutely sure.

— Nerine —

Nerine felt the slaps come up through the ground before she heard them.

She let go at once. Both paws came off the knee together, and she unwound her tails from the foot, fast, so fast the
coils whipped loose and slapped against her own legs, and she rolled away onto her back in the grass and lay there
with her arms and her tails and her chest all burning, panting at the sky.

It was over. She'd won. She waited to feel it.

Beside her, Ripples didn't move away. She couldn't. She curled onto her side round the leg as far as it would let
her, which wasn't far, her back to Nerine, one paw cupped over the knee without quite touching it. The pain that had
had nowhere to go for so long went everywhere at once now, out of the knee and into all of her, and her breath shook
coming in and shook going out. Her shoulders were heaving. She was awake for every second of it. She was making a
small sound, low and steady, under every breath.

After a while Nerine got up.

She stood over the other Buizel, favouring nothing, because nothing on her was badly hurt. A sting on her shoulder.
A bruise low on her back. Her arms were tired. That was all. She looked down at Ripples, curled in the crushed grass
with her paw over her knee and her face hidden, and found that she didn't feel like crowing. She'd thought she would.
She'd thought, all fight, about how it would feel when this one finally gave up.

She'd had to take that knee apart to get the tap. And even then it had been the knee that gave up, not Ripples.

*I don't think I could have done that,* Nerine thought. *Lasted that.* It was not a comfortable thought.

The lake lapped at the sand. The bird in the oaks had started up its three notes again. Nerine sat down in the grass
beside Ripples, close enough to touch and not touching, and waited for her breathing to slow.
