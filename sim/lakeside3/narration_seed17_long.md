## opening
— Both —

The clearing lay at the bottom of the old forest with the lake along one side of it like a held breath. Pines stood straight and tall round the edges, and between them the oaks spread low and wide, their roots heaving up through the grass wherever the shade began. A fallen trunk lay along the treeline, sunk in moss. On the lake side the grass thinned to pale sand that ran down gently into the shallows, warm and clear over the bottom, and then the water went dark all at once a few lengths out.

It was warm. Fat white clouds came slowly over from the far shore and their shadows crossed the grass and went on. A breeze came in off the water, just enough to lift the leaves and set the long grass at the edge of the sand nodding. A grey boulder sat where the grass met the beach, warm from the sun. In the oaks the birds were singing.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne came out from under the pines and stopped where the light began. Her white coat flared in the sun; the dark slate of her face stayed shadowed, and the curved blade of her horn threw one thin line of light as she turned her head. She looked at the trees, and the trunk, and the boulder, and the open grass, and last of all the slim orange shape standing at the edge of the water.

She set her paws down on the dry turf. The sickle of her tail lifted and went still.

*Keep her on the grass. Keep her away from the lake.*

— Ripples —

Ripples stood ankle-deep in the shallows with the warm water round her webbed feet. She had already been under. Her coat lay flat and dark against her, and the bare cream of her throat showed pale; water ran from the blue fins on her forearms and dripped into the lake. Behind her the twin tails began to turn, slow, the cream tips drawing circles.

She rolled one shoulder, then the other, and watched the horn.

*Let her come to me. Let her come all the way.*

The birds in the oaks went on singing. The breeze turned the leaves silver side up, and set them back.

## beat 2
— Nocturne —

Her chest was the only part of her that felt anything yet, and that was only her heart going.

Nocturne came down off the grass in three long strides, low, faster than her size should allow, and lowered her head as she came, turning it flat so the edge of the horn went level with the Buizel's middle. She watched the eyes, not the arms. The eyes would give it away. Dust and pollen turned gold in a shaft of light between them, and she went through it with the dark rising along the claws of her right forepaw, a thin smoke clinging to each edge, and swung.

*Faster than her. Always faster.*

— Ripples —

Ripples saw the paw come and did not move until the very last instant. Then she turned side-on, and the dark-edged claws went past her chest close enough to stir the fur, and on, and through nothing.

She stepped round the Absol's shoulder as it carried past her. The miss had taken the big white body a full stride on toward the water, and for a heartbeat they stood almost side by side, close enough that she could hear the Absol breathing.

One drop of water ran off the tip of her fin, and fell, and darkened a single grain of sand.

## beat 3
— Ripples —

She was already crouching when the Absol turned back.

The water came up round her from the shallows and kept coming. It wrapped her legs and her hips and her shoulders in a skin of moving water, glassy and roaring, and then she kicked off the lake bottom and the skin took her. She went up the beach low and straight, sand bursting away on both sides, the tails spinning behind her into one pale blur, and hit the Absol square in the middle of the chest.

She did not stop when she hit. The water did not let her. It carried her on, and the Absol with her, back and back across the grass.

— Nocturne —

The Buizel's shoulder came into her chest with the whole weight of the lake behind it, and every scrap of air went out of her in one bark.

She was going backward. Her paws scraped at the turf and found nothing. The water was all over her, pouring into her face, scouring at her chest where the shoulder still drove into it, stinging the ruff and the point of her right shoulder and the soft fur of her flank, and it would not let up for a moment. It pushed and pushed. And then something hit her from behind, hard, across the back and the left shoulder and the hip, and she understood it was a tree.

An oak. She was pinned against an oak, and the Buizel was still coming.

The weight crushed into her chest against the trunk, and her chest gave up.

It had been hurting since the first instant. Now it was something else. It went white. It went enormous. It filled her from her throat to her belly with a crushing, bursting pressure that had nowhere to go, and a sound came out of her she had never made before: not a growl, nothing she could hold behind her teeth, but a ragged, rising howl torn up out of her chest, *Hhaaa-ahh*, that cracked at the top and went up into the oak's own branches and sent birds clattering out of them. Her forepaws scrabbled at the Buizel's shoulder. Her eyes were squeezed shut. She could not breathe in. She could not do anything but feel it.

*Too much. Too much. Get off. Get off me.*

— Ripples —

The water ran out of her all at once, and she stepped back, and the Absol did not stay up.

She slid down the trunk of the oak. Her legs folded under her one after another and she went down on her front among its roots, her soaked white coat streaming, her sides heaving in small quick pulls as if any bigger breath would break something.

Ripples stood over her, dripping, breathing hard.

*Down. Already down.*

She had not expected it to be so soon.

## beat 4
— Ripples —

She knew what to do with an Absol lying on her front. She had thought about it.

Ripples stepped over the white back and sat down on it, astride, her knees either side of the spine. The Absol jerked under her and she slid her paws forward along the soaked neck and under the chin and locked them there, fingers laced, and leaned back.

The Absol's head came up. Her chest came up off the ground after it. Her back bent like a bow under Ripples' weight, and Ripples kept leaning, hauling the head up and back by the chin with both arms, her own weight sitting square on the spine to keep it bent.

*Now you can't do anything. Now you can't reach me.*

Her mouth was right at the back of the Absol's head. She opened it and spat light.

— Nocturne —

Her head was being pulled up and back by the chin. Her spine was being bent backward over the weight on it. She did not understand it at first; she was still full of the oak and the water and her chest. And then she understood, and it was worse.

Something burst against the back of her skull. A spray of small sharp lights, from no distance at all, and the top of her head went from nothing to a hot, ringing hurt. She could not duck it. She could not get a paw up to cover it. Her whole front half was off the ground, lifted, and her forelegs hung in the air with nothing to push on.

The neck began to ache where the arms crossed under her jaw. The back began to ache under the weight. It was not much yet. It was going to be.

*What is this. What is she doing to me.*

The ringing in her head from the oak thinned out and went away, and left only the new one.

## beat 5
— Ripples —

She spat again. The stars came out of her too soon, badly aimed, and the force of it threw her off her seat on the Absol's back, so that she had to clutch tighter round the chin to keep from sliding off sideways. She was out of breath. She had thrown everything into that charge, and now she had nothing behind her.

But the stars still hit. They burst on the back of the white head from a hand's breadth away and she felt the Absol jerk.

She settled her weight again and leaned back harder.

— Nocturne —

The second burst landed on the place where the first had, and it was worse, much worse: the top of her head throbbed now, deep and steady, and she felt it give, a little, as if it would take the next one even harder.

And the arms under her chin pulled. Her neck went from an ache to a hot, strained, burning thing, every tendon in it stretched past where it wanted to go. Her back burned too, bent under the weight, the muscles along her spine knotted and cramping.

She fought it. She clawed up and back at the arms under her jaw with one forepaw and could not reach them properly, and clawed at the grass with the other for something to push on, and found nothing, and the arms did not move. They did not move at all.

*Off. Get her off. Get—*

Her claws raked the turf. The grip stayed exactly where it was.

## beat 6
— Ripples —

The Absol was fighting under her. Ripples could feel it through her knees, through the arms locked under the jaw, through the whole bent length of her. She leaned back against it and held.

And she opened her mouth again, right at the back of the white head, and this time she let the lake out of it.

The jet hit the back of the Absol's skull from no distance at all, hard and cold and heavy, and drove her head forward against Ripples' own locked arms.

Ripples' balance came back to her with it. She straightened on the Absol's back, got her knees set again, and leaned.

— Nocturne —

The water hit the back of her head, and her head broke open with pain.

That was what it felt like. The two bursts of stars had left the skull ringing and tender, and the jet came straight into the same place with the whole weight of the lake behind it, and the hurt went from bad to past anything she had felt in her life. It was white. It was everywhere inside her skull at once. She heard herself cry out, a high, cracked, whining shriek that cut off in the middle because the arms under her chin were dragging her head back and the jet was driving it forward and her neck was caught between the two.

And her neck went over the line too.

She felt it happen. A deep, tearing, burning strain all down the column of it, as if every cord in there was being pulled out of her by the roots, and it did not stop when the jet stopped. It kept going, because the arms kept pulling. Her forelegs flailed in the air. Water ran into her eyes, her ears, her open mouth. Her back screamed under the weight.

Then the world went strange.

The trees swam. The oak in front of her split into two oaks and slid back into one and split again. Sounds came late: the lap of the lake reached her a moment after she saw the ripple. She blinked and shook her head as much as the grip would let her, and nothing would settle. She tried to find the Buizel, the shape on her back, the arms under her chin, and she could not hold any of it still long enough to look at it.

*Can't see her. Can't find her. Everything's late.*

— Ripples —

Ripples felt the white head loll for an instant in her arms. She saw the red eyes go glassy and lose their focus, wander, blink.

*She's slow. She's gone slow.*

She tightened her grip, and leaned.

## beat 7
— Nocturne —

The arms pulled. The neck burned. She could not bear it anymore, and still it went on.

Every heartbeat was a fresh wave of agony through the stretched column of her neck, and every breath scraped past the arms under her chin. Her back was a long cramping fire under the weight. A low growl was coming out of her through clenched teeth, steady and broken, catching in her throat on every pull of the arms, *nnhh, nnhh*, and she could not stop it.

And then the world came back into one piece.

The oak was one oak. The lake lapped when she saw it lap. She could feel exactly where the Buizel was: the knees either side of her spine, the weight on her back leaning back, the laced fingers under her jaw.

She did not think. She threw her whole body sideways. She wrenched her head down and round against the grip, and felt the fingers slip on her wet fur, and she twisted again, harder, and her head came out of the arms, and the weight on her back tipped and slid, and she was rolling.

She was free.

— Ripples —

The head tore out of her grip and the back she was sitting on simply turned over under her, and she was off it, sprawling on the grass, the clutch gone.

She scrambled up on all fours and stared.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne rolled onto her front and tried to rise. She got halfway, and both her shoulders gave at once under the weight of her, and she went down on her chest hard. The chest. She cried out.

She lay with her chin in the grass. Then she got her legs under her again, one at a time, slowly, and leaned her weight against the trunk of the old oak, and pushed. She came up. She stood. Her sides were heaving. Her neck burned, her head throbbed, her chest throbbed, and she stood favoring both her shoulders and leaning on the oak, and turned her head very carefully to look at the Buizel.

## beat 8
— Ripples —

The Absol was up, but only just, and leaning on a tree.

Ripples did not give her the time to stop leaning. She went in low and fast, under the horn, her shoulder into the white chest, and took her down. They went over together, hard; Ripples heard the Absol's back hit the ground and her hip after it. Then Ripples was on top.

She did not waste any of herself. She pulled the right foreleg out straight from the Absol's side and laid her own forearm across it, pinning it flat. She let her weight fall across the chest. She dropped her head and set her teeth against the white throat, not biting, pressing. Her paws found the Absol's two forepaws and closed on them, and her claws went in between the pads. Her feet dug into the ribs on the left side.

*All of me. All of me on her.*

— Nocturne —

She was on her back. The ground had come up and hit her across the back and the right thigh, and now there was weight everywhere.

On her chest. That was the worst. The chest that had been crushed against the oak, that could not bear anything now, and the Buizel's whole body lay across it. Every breath she drew had to push up against that weight, and every push woke the crushed place under it.

Her right foreleg was pinned out straight under a forearm. Teeth were at her throat, pressing. Two paws held her forepaws and something sharp was digging in between the pads. Feet were pushing into her ribs.

*Everywhere. She's everywhere on me.*

## beat 9
— Nocturne —

The claws between her pads were the smallest thing and somehow she could not stop feeling them.

She lay under the Buizel with her chest burning under the weight and she felt every point of contact one at a time: the forearm on her foreleg, the teeth at her throat, the feet in her ribs, the claws in her paws. It was too much to take in all at once, so she took it in one at a time, and while she was doing it she found what she needed. The Buizel was lying across her chest. Not on her hips. Her hips were free.

She slid. She dug her hind paws into the grass and shoved, and her whole body slid backward out from under the weight, and the teeth came off her throat as she went, and as the Buizel's head came round after her she brought her own head up and slammed the side of her skull into the orange jaw.

She was out. She did not stop to feel it. She found the old oak with her shoulder and lurched up against it in one ugly heave, nearly going over the other way, and stood.

The soak had gone out of her coat. The sun had it.

— Ripples —

The skull hit her jaw and her jaw lit up.

It was a deep, hard, rattling pain that went straight up into her ears, and her head snapped sideways with it, and when she brought it back her mouth would not close right. She worked it. It hurt to work it. It hurt badly.

She was still on her feet, somehow. She had come up off the Absol on her knees and then her feet, and now she stood there with a paw against her jaw, breathing through her teeth.

*One for one. Fine.*

## beat 10
— Nocturne —

The one sound the Buizel was making was her breath: short, quick, hissing in and out through her teeth because her jaw hurt to open.

Nocturne heard it from across the grass. She saw the head carried tilted, the hurt side turned away. She was breathing in short pulls through her own nose, sparing her throat. They watched each other from a few lengths apart, both of them hurting, and Nocturne thought: *if I were her I'd keep my distance and spit at me. So I won't let her.*

She lowered her head, let a paw slide on the wet grass as if she had lost her footing, and swung her horn up out of the slip. A curved blade of force leapt off it, pale and bending the light, straight at the Buizel's body.

— Ripples —

Ripples got her forearm in front of it. Not to block it: to turn it. The flat of the fin met the edge of the force at an angle and it skidded off her, past her shoulder, clipping a few hairs and nothing else, and went on and on behind her until it was gone into the trees.

The breeze turned the leaves silver side up, and set them back.

Ripples looked at the Absol over the fin and bared her teeth, and then shut her mouth again because the jaw hurt.

## beat 11
— Ripples —

A muscle in her cheek jumped. She had not told it to.

Ripples stood on the grass and let her breath come. She was well ahead and she knew it. She could feel how little the Absol had hurt her: the jaw throbbed, but the rest of her was almost fresh, and the Absol had been crushed against a tree, bent backward, choked by her own water, and pinned. She could afford a breath.

*Calm. Calm, now. You have her.*

She watched the Absol favor both shoulders as she shifted her weight. She watched her hold her head carefully, as if it might fall off.

— Nocturne —

Sunlight glittered on the shallows behind the Buizel. Nocturne did not look at it.

She looked at the Buizel, standing easy on the grass, almost lazy, while every part of Nocturne's own body reported in one after another: chest crushed and burning, neck strained and burning, head throbbing at the back where the water had hit it, both shoulders aching where they had given out under her. She had been in this fight a few minutes and she was losing it.

She was afraid of the next exchange. She was angry at being afraid.

She let a breath out slowly, and it shook in the middle.

## beat 12
— Nocturne —

She stopped being afraid of it by going first.

She went at the Buizel straight, no feint, no trick, three strides and a lunge, and raked her right forepaw across the orange chest with everything her shoulder had. Her claws caught and dragged, and the jolt of it went out into the Buizel's neck and down into her stomach.

— Ripples —

It came in too straight for her to believe it, and she believed it too late.

The claws went across her chest and her chest went hot and tight and burning, a deep, bad hurt that took her breath with it. Her neck stung. Her stomach stung. She folded a little, and when she tried to breathe in, nothing went in. Her chest heaved and heaved and her mouth opened on air that would not come.

*Can't. Breathe. Can't—*

She knew she had nothing behind her now. Every movement cost her air she did not have.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne saw her gasping. She saw the mouth open on nothing, the chest heave on nothing. She knew what that meant: for a moment, nothing the Buizel threw would have any weight behind it.

She did not have the breath to throw anything else, herself. She stood and watched.

## beat 13
— Ripples —

*Breathe.*

The thought came first, before the body did anything about it. Then the breath came: ragged, scraping, real. Air went down into her and her chest burned with it and she did not care. She stood bent over with her paws on her knees and breathed and breathed.

Behind her the small waves lapped on the sand. She stood up straight, slowly. Her chest hurt every time it moved. Her jaw hurt every time it moved. She was still ahead. She was ruder about it now.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne watched her straighten, and stood where she was, and breathed, and was afraid, and was angry, and did not move.

## beat 14
— Ripples —

She had made a mistake, getting that close without the lake. She meant to fix it.

She looked at the Absol across the grass. The big white throat moved when she swallowed, and every swallow was an effort Ripples could see from here. The Absol flinched from the light, from her shadow when the cloud crossed it, from nothing.

*Now. While she's turned.*

Ripples opened her mouth, which cost her, and opened it anyway, and called.

She threw her good arm up at the sky and let her tails spin, and felt the lake answer. The breeze dropped and came back cold. The fat white clouds over the far shore went grey at the bottoms, then dark, then they were not drifting anymore but coming, piling in over the clearing. The light went grey and soft. And everything began to shine, because it was raining.

— Nocturne —

The rain came down warm and straight and heavy, hissing in the grass. Nocturne felt it soak through her coat at once.

The footing went slick and treacherous in a moment. The turf under her paws turned to wet grass over mud, and the pine needles at the edge of the trees went dark and soft. She shifted her weight and felt her paws slide a fraction.

She opened her mouth to say something, and did not.

## beat 15
— Nocturne —

The rain had made the grass slick under the Buizel's feet too.

Nocturne came round fast and low, and the sickle of her tail came with her, hardened grey along its edge, and swept through the Buizel's right knee.

— Ripples —

It hit her knee from the side and the knee buckled. There was a deep, bright hurt in the joint and a jolt down into her foot and up into her hip, and then she had nothing to stand on and her legs went out from under her on the wet grass.

She landed on her back. Her upper back hit, and her right shoulder, and her left thigh, three dull thumps into the soaked earth. Rain fell straight into her face.

She lay there, breathing, and blinked it away. She would not try to get up yet. She needed a moment for the knee.

## beat 16
— Ripples —

She was on her back in the rain and the Absol was standing over her, and Ripples did not wait to be stood on.

She swung her right arm up off the ground, the paw stiffened into a blade, and chopped at the white head bending toward her. She did it too fast and with no breath behind it, and it went wide of where she meant it, and the swing carried her half over onto her side. But it landed: across the base of the horn, the jaw, and down into the neck.

— Nocturne —

The edge of the paw came down across the root of her horn, and the horn rang in her skull. It jarred down into her jaw. And then it reached her neck.

Her neck, that had been strained and bent and burned in the Buizel's arms until there was nothing left in it that did not hurt, took the chop, and went past anything she had a word for.

It was not a sharp pain. It was huge and deep and total, a slow, sick, swelling agony that rose up the whole length of her neck from her shoulders into the back of her skull and kept rising. Her head dropped. She could not hold it up. A sound came out of her from deep in her chest, a long, low, shuddering groan that wavered as it went, *hhhoooh*, and would not stop, and her forelegs trembled under her.

*Not my neck. Not again. I can't. I can't.*

She stood there in the rain with her head hanging and her neck screaming and could not make herself move it.

— Ripples —

Ripples rolled the rest of the way onto her side, out from under the white head, and got her feet under her, and her good paw on the boulder at the edge of the beach, and climbed up it. She stood. Her breath was rough. Her knee hurt. She favored it, and her jaw, and she got her balance back and her guard up.

The Absol had not moved. Her head was still hanging.

## beat 17
— Ripples —

She came in close. The Absol's head was down and her neck was right there, and Ripples knew what to do with a neck.

She took the Absol by the shoulder and walked her backward, fast, faster than four legs could follow on wet grass, until the white legs tangled and the Absol went over sideways. Ripples went with her. She threw her legs round the Absol's neck from the side, one over, one under, and locked her ankles, and squeezed.

Her forearm came down across the Absol's left foreleg and pinned it. Her chest pressed hard against the white ruff.

*There. Squeeze. Squeeze.*

— Nocturne —

She was on the ground and something had her neck.

Legs. Locked round it. Squeezing the sides. And her neck, her neck that could not bear anything, was caught between them.

She could not make a sound. She did not have the breath for one, or the room. Her mouth opened and shut. Her free forepaw beat at the grass. The left one was pinned under an arm and the Buizel's chest was pressed against her own, and the rain was falling straight down into her eyes.

## beat 18
— Nocturne —

She bucked. She threw everything she had up and sideways at once, every leg, every muscle in her back.

It did not work. The Buizel felt it coming and drove her forearm down across the pinned foreleg, hard, and the leg flared with a deep bruising ache, and Nocturne's whole body went down flat again.

And the legs round her neck squeezed.

It was steady. It was not even very hard. It did not need to be: the neck was ruined, and the steady pressure of those legs on it was agony she had to clamp her whole body round to keep in. She held it in. She did not know how. Her teeth were locked. Her eyes were squeezed shut. A thin, high whine leaked out of her nose with every breath.

Above them the rain was easing. It came down softer, then in scattered drops, and the clouds were tearing open, and the sun came down hot on her face.

— Ripples —

Ripples locked her ankles tighter and felt the white body shake against her. The rain was stopping. She did not need it anymore.

## beat 19
— Nocturne —

The left foreleg. It was pinned under one forearm. Everything else of the Buizel was round her neck.

She wrenched it. She twisted it at the shoulder and jerked it out from under the arm, and the moment it was free she swung it up and round and drove it into the first thing it found, which was the Buizel's right arm, and she felt it land like a club.

The legs round her neck loosened. She tore her head out of them, and rolled, and was free.

— Ripples —

The paw hit her right arm high up, between the shoulder and the elbow, and the arm went bright and loud and useless for a moment. A deep, grinding hurt that went all through the muscle. She cried out and let go of everything without deciding to, and the Absol's head tore out from between her legs.

She scrambled back and up, and stood, holding the arm against her side.

— Nocturne —

Her legs were numb. All of them: pins and needles from the shoulders down, slow to answer. She tried to get up against the boulder and halfway there her pinned foreleg and her right shoulder gave out together and she dropped back down hard, gasping.

She tried again. She got her chest against the warm stone and pushed with what she had, and came up, and stood, unsteady, leaning on it, her sides heaving, holding the left foreleg a little off the ground.

## beat 20
— Nocturne —

The Buizel was still wet. Her coat was dark with the rain that had only just stopped, every hair of her soaked through.

Nocturne lowered her head. The numbness was still in her legs, slow and thick, and she could feel herself moving like something half-asleep. But the horn did not need her legs. She let the charge build in it, the prickle crawling up the curved blade and gathering at the point, white-blue, spitting, and she swung her head and let it go.

The stiffness was working its way out of her limbs. By the time the light left her horn she could feel her paws again.

— Ripples —

It went through her.

Every part of her at once: a crack inside her skull, and then her whole body locked. Her arms flew out stiff, her tails stood straight out behind her, her back arched, her jaw clamped shut so hard it hurt, and the white-blue fire crawled over her and through her and would not stop. She could not breathe. She could not make a sound.

Then it let go of her.

She stood swaying. Her face was the worst of it. Her nose burned, a deep raw throb, and it felt wrong when she breathed through it, too soft, a give in it she had never felt before. Both ears rang. Her cheeks and muzzle stung as if she had been slapped hard on both sides at once. Her throat ached.

*Her horn. Watch the horn.*

## beat 21
— Nocturne —

The Buizel was still swaying, and her neck was right there, and it was Nocturne's turn.

She lunged, and her jaws closed on the side of the orange neck, and she bit down with everything.

— Ripples —

The teeth went into her neck, and the neck, which had only been hurting, went past it in one bite.

It was deep and crushing and tearing at once, a clamped agony that went into her throat and up into her jaw and her cheek, and she screamed. It came out cracked and strangled because the teeth were round her neck, and she clawed at the white head with her good paw and could not make it let go, and then it let go on its own and she staggered sideways with both paws clamped to the side of her neck.

Her throat hurt to swallow. Her cheek throbbed. The neck was the thing. The neck burned and throbbed and would not stop, and she stood bent over it, shaking.

*She bit me. Like that. She just bit me.*

## beat 22
— Ripples —

She straightened up. She did not have anything else left for words, so she used water.

She opened her mouth and let it out of her in a ring, a thick pulsing blast that hit the white face full on.

— Nocturne —

It hit her in the face and went straight into her mouth.

She had it open, breathing, and the water went up her nose and down her throat at the same time, and she choked on it. She coughed, a huge, wrenching, whole-body cough that brought her head down almost to the grass, and gasped, and pulled in more water instead of air, and coughed again. Her eyes ran. Her nose burned, deep and sharp, and her muzzle ached where the blast had struck it, and her throat burned inside and out.

*Can't stop. Can't stop coughing. Can't fight.*

— Ripples —

Ripples saw her choking, bent double and hacking water into the grass. She saw her blind with it, eyes streaming.

*Now. Close in now, while she can't.*

## beat 23
— Nocturne —

She was still coughing when the Buizel came in, and she swung at her without looking.

The sickle tail came round low and up, hard and grey, and caught the Buizel under the jaw and lifted her. Nocturne saw her go: up off her feet into the air, arms flung out, the twin tails trailing, the orange face snapped back. And before she had come down, before she could come down, Nocturne swung her head up and the horn flashed and a curved blade of force came off it and caught her in mid-air across the stomach and chest and thigh.

— Ripples —

The tail hit her jaw and the jaw went white.

It had been aching since the Absol's skull hit it. She could hear the lake. It was a long way off. She could see the sky past the curve of the white shoulder, very blue, with a fat cloud going over it, and its shadow went over them both and went on.

— Nocturne —

This was the worst of it. Nocturne knew it, lying wrapped round the small body, her own breath going in and out short and ragged. Neither of them was doing anything. Neither of them could. It could still go either way: a little more strength in the Buizel, a little less in herself, and it would all come apart.

She held on, and it was very quiet.

## beat 51
— Ripples —

She was still awake. She did not know how.

The breath came thinner every time. The teeth at her throat did not have to do anything now but stay where they were. She could feel her heartbeat in her throat, under them, pounding against them, too fast and too hard.

Her paws twitched under the claws. Her hip twitched under the hind paws. Her body was still making its sound, on its own, a faint creaking whimper at the bottom of each breath, *hh*, but the sound was getting smaller.

*Stay. Stay awake. Stay.*

— Nocturne —

The small body in her legs had almost stopped fighting. Nocturne could feel the difference: the jerks were weaker, slower, further apart. The sound coming out of the Buizel's throat under her teeth was getting quieter.

She kept her jaws exactly where they were.

## beat 52
— Ripples —

She tried to slide out. Backward, under the Absol, the smallest movement she had ever made. It was nothing.

The forepaw came down on her crushed right paw, and the paw went past the end of everything, and she could not even scream about it. Her body jerked once under the weight. A thin, raw squeak came out past the teeth at her throat, *ih*, and stopped.

Because the teeth closed.

Not hard. Just enough. The front of her throat pressed in, and the breath that had been coming thin stopped coming at all. And the sides of her neck were squeezed shut between the jaws, and the pressure in her head swelled and swelled, roaring in her ears, pushing behind her eyes.

She opened her mouth and nothing came. She tried again and nothing came.

The blue sky past the white shoulder went grey at the edges. The grey came in. It came in from all sides at once, and there was a small round piece of sky left in the middle, very blue, and then that was going too.

The sound of the lake went away. The weight of her own paws went away. The pain in all of her went away, last of all, all at once.

— Nocturne —

The Buizel went still.

Not all at once. Nocturne felt it happen through every place they touched. The jerking stopped. The legs went loose against her own. The paws went slack under her forepaws. The breath against her teeth, when it came, was very slow and very shallow and made no sound.

She opened her jaws. She unlocked her hind legs, slowly, and let them slide off the small body, and took her forepaws off the crushed little paws, and lifted her head.

The Buizel lay on the grass with her eyes closed, her coat torn and matted, her chest rising and falling, slow and shallow and steady. Out.

Nocturne lay beside her a long moment. Then she got her legs under her, three of them, and stood up over her, swaying, her ear ringing, her chest and neck and back and leg all burning, and looked down.

*Done. It's done.*

The clouds were drifting in from the far shore again. Their shadows crossed the clearing and went on. In the oaks, one bird, then another, began to sing again.
