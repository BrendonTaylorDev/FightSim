— Both —

The clearing lay at the bottom of the old forest with the lake along one side of it like a held breath. Pines stood straight and tall round the edges, and between them the oaks spread low and wide, their roots heaving up through the grass wherever the shade began. A fallen trunk lay along the treeline, sunk in moss. On the lake side the grass thinned to pale sand that ran down gently into the shallows, warm and clear over the bottom, and then the water went dark all at once a few lengths out.

It was warm. Fat white clouds came slowly over from the far shore and their shadows crossed the grass and went on. A breeze came in off the water, just enough to lift the leaves and set the long grass at the edge of the sand nodding. A grey boulder sat where the grass met the beach, warm from the sun. In the oaks the birds were singing.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne came out from under the pines and stopped where the light began. Her white coat flared in the sun; the dark slate of her face stayed shadowed, and the curved blade of her horn threw one thin line of light as she turned her head. She looked at the trees, and the trunk, and the boulder, and the open grass, and last of all the slim orange shape standing at the edge of the water.

She set her paws down on the dry turf. The sickle of her tail lifted and went still.

*Keep her on the grass. Keep her away from the lake.*

— Ripples —

Ripples stood ankle-deep in the shallows with the warm water round her webbed feet. She had already been under. Her coat lay flat and dark against her, and the bare cream of her throat showed pale; water ran from the blue fins on her forearms and dripped into the lake. Behind her the twin tails began to turn, slow, the cream tips drawing circles.

She rolled one shoulder, then the other, and watched the horn.

*Let her come to me. Let her come all the way.*

The birds in the oaks went on singing. The breeze turned the leaves silver side up, and set them back.

Her chest was the only part of her that felt anything yet, and that was only her heart going.

Nocturne came down off the grass in three long strides, low, faster than her size should allow, and lowered her head as she came, turning it flat so the edge of the horn went level with the Buizel's middle. *Faster than her. Always faster.*

— Ripples —

Ripples saw the paw come and did not move until the very last instant. Then she turned side-on, and the dark-edged claws went past her chest close enough to stir the fur, and on, and through nothing.

She stepped round the Absol's shoulder as it carried past her. The miss had taken the big white body a full stride on toward the water, and for a heartbeat they stood almost side by side, close enough that she could hear the Absol breathing.

One drop of water ran off the tip of her fin, and fell, and darkened a single grain of sand.

— Ripples —

She was already crouching when the Absol turned back.

The water came up round her from the shallows and kept coming. It wrapped her legs and her hips and her shoulders in a skin of moving water, glassy and roaring, and then she kicked off the lake bottom and the skin took her. She went up the beach low and straight, sand bursting away on both sides, the tails spinning behind her into one pale blur, and hit the Absol square in the middle of the chest.

She did not stop when she hit. The water did not let her. It carried her on, and the Absol with her, back and back across the grass.

— Nocturne —

The Buizel's shoulder came into her chest with the whole weight of the lake behind it, and every scrap of air went out of her in one bark.

She was going backward. Her paws scraped at the turf and found nothing. The water was all over her, pouring into her face, scouring at her chest where the shoulder still drove into it, stinging the ruff and the point of her right shoulder and the soft fur of her flank, and it would not let up for a moment. It pushed and pushed. And then something hit her from behind, hard, across the back and the left shoulder and the hip, and she understood it was a tree.

An oak. She was pinned against an oak, and the Buizel was still coming.

The weight crushed into her chest against the trunk, and her chest gave up.

It had been hurting since the first instant. Now it was something else. It went white. It went enormous. It filled her from her throat to her belly with a crushing, bursting pressure that had nowhere to go, and a sound came out of her she had never made before: not a growl, nothing she could hold behind her teeth, but a ragged, rising howl torn up out of her chest, *Hhaaa-ahh*, that cracked at the top and went up into the oak's own branches and sent birds clattering out of them. Her forepaws scrabbled at the Buizel's shoulder. Her eyes were squeezed shut. She could not breathe in. She could not do anything but feel it.

*Too much. Too much. Get off. Get off me.*

— Ripples —

The water ran out of her all at once, and she stepped back, and the Absol did not stay up.

She slid down the trunk of the oak. Her legs folded under her one after another and she went down on her front among its roots, her soaked white coat streaming, her sides heaving in small quick pulls as if any bigger breath would break something.

Ripples stood over her, dripping, breathing hard.

*Down. Already down.*

She had not expected it to be so soon.

She knew what to do with an Absol lying on her front. She had thought about it.

Ripples stepped over the white back and sat down on it, astride, her knees either side of the spine. The Absol jerked under her and she slid her paws forward along the soaked neck and under the chin and locked them there, fingers laced, and leaned back.

— Nocturne —

Her head was being pulled up and back by the chin. Her spine was being bent backward over the weight on it. She did not understand it at first; she was still full of the oak and the water and her chest. And then she understood, and it was worse.

Something burst against the back of her skull. A spray of small sharp lights, from no distance at all, and the top of her head went from nothing to a hot, ringing hurt. She could not duck it. She could not get a paw up to cover it. Her whole front half was off the ground, lifted, and her forelegs hung in the air with nothing to push on.

The neck began to ache where the arms crossed under her jaw. The back began to ache under the weight. It was not much yet. It was going to be.

*What is this. What is she doing to me.*

The ringing in her head from the oak thinned out and went away, and left only the new one.

She spat again. *Off. Get her off. Get—*

— Ripples —

She tried to slide out. Backward, under the Absol, the smallest movement she had ever made. It was nothing.

The forepaw came down on her crushed right paw, and the paw went past the end of everything, and she could not even scream about it. Her body jerked once under the weight. A thin, raw squeak came out past the teeth at her throat, *ih*, and stopped.

Because the teeth closed.

Not hard. Just enough. The front of her throat pressed in, and the breath that had been coming thin stopped coming at all. And the sides of her neck were squeezed shut between the jaws, and the pressure in her head swelled and swelled, roaring in her ears, pushing behind her eyes.

She opened her mouth and nothing came. She tried again and nothing came.

The blue sky past the white shoulder went grey at the edges. The grey came in. It came in from all sides at once, and there was a small round piece of sky left in the middle, very blue, and then that was going too.

The sound of the lake went away. The weight of her own paws went away. The pain in all of her went away, last of all, all at once.

— Nocturne —

The Buizel went still.

Not all at once. Nocturne felt it happen through every place they touched. The jerking stopped. The legs went loose against her own. The paws went slack under her forepaws. The breath against her teeth, when it came, was very slow and very shallow and made no sound.

She opened her jaws. She unlocked her hind legs, slowly, and let them slide off the small body, and took her forepaws off the crushed little paws, and lifted her head.

The Buizel lay on the grass with her eyes closed, her coat torn and matted, her chest rising and falling, slow and shallow and steady. Out.

Nocturne lay beside her a long moment. Then she got her legs under her, three of them, and stood up over her, swaying, her ear ringing, her chest and neck and back and leg all burning, and looked down.

*Done. It's done.*

The clouds were drifting in from the far shore again. Their shadows crossed the clearing and went on. In the oaks, one bird, then another, began to sing again.
