— Nocturne —

And there it was.

The Buizel had landed with her weight on the right leg, the leg that had taken six blows and a fall, and it was turned square to Nocturne with nothing in front of it: no arm, no tail, nothing. Off balance. Still stumbling. Nocturne did not think about it. Her claws went into the exact place they had been before, the dark smoking off their edges, the outside of the right thigh, where it was already torn and swollen and soft.

It did not feel like the other blows. The others had met muscle and fur, something that pushed back. This one met nothing at all. It went in, and in, and kept going, and she felt the leg give round her claws like wet sand.

*That one. That went all the way in.*

She was almost startled. Then something cold and hard and satisfied came up through her chest, and she held the claws there half a heartbeat longer than she needed to.

— Ripples —

She knew it was wrong before it finished landing.

The thigh had been hurt before; she knew how it hurt. This was not that. This went past the bruising and the torn fur and found the deepest, rawest place underneath, the place every one of those six blows had been building, and arrived there with everything behind it. It didn't hit her leg. It went through her leg, into her hip, into the base of her tails, and somewhere in the middle of it the whole right side of her went white.

The cry was out before she could stop it, sharp and high, a sound that went out over the lake and sent two birds clattering up out of the oaks. She didn't fall, quite. She caught herself on her hands and her good knee, and stayed there on all fours with the right leg stuck out stiff behind her, and the leg began to shake. A hard, fast tremor ran from the thigh up into her hip and the base of her tails and would not stop.

Her body flinched away from it so hard and so completely that for a moment she could not move anything else at all.

*Something's wrong. Something's really wrong with my leg.*

— Ripples —

It did not stop when the claws left.

It kept arriving. A first hot wave, then a second under it, deeper, then a third that seemed to come up from inside the bone itself and spread out through her hip and down to her foot. The thigh felt too big for her body, swollen tight and hot and wrong, and when she pressed her paw to it she could feel her heartbeat there, thudding against her palm.

She breathed through her teeth. Small breaths, because the big ones made the whole leg jump. A thin whine leaked out between them, and she clamped her jaw on it, and it leaked out anyway, a high, steady thread of sound she could hear and could not stop. Her cheek marks were twitching. Cold sweat had come up under her fur, though nothing about her was cold.

She tried to pull the leg in under her and it would not come. It lay there behind her, trembling, and every tremor was a fresh small jolt all the way up into her spine.

*Okay. Okay. That— that was bad. That was really bad.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne stood over her and watched.

She watched the leg stuck out behind, the tremor that would not stop and the way it climbed into the hip and the root of the tails. She watched the webbed paw pressed so hard against the thigh that the webbing spread white. She watched the Buizel try to swallow the sound and fail, and try again and fail again, the cream throat working round it. She watched the black eyes, which had been bright with something all fight, go wide and fixed on nothing at all.

Her own breath came back slowly in the sun. She did not hurry it.

*That's where you break. Right there. I'll remember.*

Ripples got her feet under her at last and stood, swaying, one paw braced against a boulder at the edge of the beach.
