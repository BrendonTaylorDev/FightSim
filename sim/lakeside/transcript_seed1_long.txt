— Both —

The clearing lay open to the lake like a held breath. Old pines and broad oaks stood round three sides of it, their roots humped up through the grass where the shade began, and along the treeline a fallen trunk lay half sunk in moss, soft and green and warm to the touch where the sun reached it. On the fourth side the grass thinned and paled into a beach of fine sand that ran down gently into the water. The shallows there were clear and warm, the colour of weak tea over the sand; a few lengths out, the lake went dark all at once, cool and deep.

It was the middle of a warm afternoon. Fat white clouds drifted over from the far shore, and their shadows slid across the clearing one after another, dimming the grass and letting it go. A breeze came in off the water, never strong, just enough to turn the oak leaves silver side up and set the long grass at the edge of the sand nodding. Pollen hung gold in the shafts of light under the trees. Somewhere high in an oak a bird was singing, the same bright phrase over and over, as if nothing in the world was about to happen.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne came out of the pines with the heavy, rolling pace of her kind and stopped at the edge of the shade. The sun found her at once: white fur gone almost too bright to look at, the dark blue-grey of her face, the scythe of her horn throwing a thin line of light as she turned her head. Her red eyes went over the clearing piece by piece, the way they always did: the trees, the trunk, the boulder at the edge of the beach, the long open stretch of grass, and last of all the lake.

She looked at the lake for a long time.

*Water. Of course it would be water.*

Her lip lifted off one fang and settled again. She set her broad paws down on the dry grass, well back from where it turned to sand, and felt the ground under her: firm, warm, a little springy with roots. Good ground. She meant to keep it. The sickle of her tail swung once behind her and went still.

— Ripples —

Ripples came up out of the shallows without a splash. One moment the water was flat and bright; the next there was a sleek orange head in it, then shoulders, then the whole slim length of her walking up the sand on two webbed feet with the lake running off her in sheets. She shook herself at the waterline from ears to tail-tips, a hard, whole-body shake that threw a spray of drops glittering into the sun, and then stood there dripping, her fur flattened dark to her body, her bare cream throat pale in the light.

The blue fins on her forearms opened and closed once. Behind her, her twin tails began to turn, lazily, the cream tips drawing slow circles in the air.

Her small black eyes went over the Absol from horn to sickle tail. They lingered on the horn, then the jaws, then dropped to the four careful paws planted on the dry grass, a long body-length back from the sand, and the marks on her cheeks twitched.

*Doesn't want her feet wet. Good. That's good.*

The bird in the oak sang its phrase again and went quiet.

Nocturne straightened off the oak and stood in the dapple under its leaves with the lake running out of her fur. She looked up through the branches. The sun was already warm, high and white beyond the clouds.

Her cheek marks twitched. She squinted across the white glare at the Absol steaming under the oak and knew exactly what she had done.
