## opening
— Both —

The clearing lay open to the lake like a held breath. Tall pines and broad oaks stood round it, their roots humped up through the grass at the edges, and along the treeline a fallen trunk lay furred with moss. On the lake side the grass thinned to a beach of pale, soft sand that slid into warm shallows, and a few lengths out the water went dark and deep. It was a warm afternoon. Fat white clouds drifted over, and their shadows slid across the grass and off again. A breeze came in off the water, turning the leaves silver side up, and somewhere in the oaks a bird was singing as if nothing in the world was about to happen.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne came out of the pines with the heavy, rolling pace of her kind and stopped where the shade ended. White fur, the dark blue-grey of her face, the scythe of her horn catching the sun as she turned her head. She looked at the lake for a long moment, the way she might look at something that had insulted her, and set her broad paws on the dry grass, well back from the sand.

*Dry ground. Stay on it.*

— Ripples —

Ripples came up out of the shallows without a splash, sleek and low, orange fur flattened to her body and running with water. She walked up the sand on two webbed feet, shook herself from ears to tail-tips, and stood there dripping in the sun, her bare cream throat pale, her twin tails beginning to turn lazily behind her. The blue fins on her forearms opened and closed once.

Her black eyes went over the Absol from horn to sickle tail, then to the careful paws on the grass, and the marks on her cheeks twitched.

*Doesn't like the water. Good.*

## beat 2
— Nocturne —

Nocturne did not waste the sun on staring. She came across the grass in four long strides, low, and her right forepaw came round flat and fast, claws spread and edged with darkness, raking for the Buizel's left shoulder.

— Ripples —

The claws took her across the point of the shoulder and dragged down, and the dark in them bit deep. It jarred into her chest and snapped up into her throat, where the cream fur was thinnest, and for one moment her body simply would not move: her head jerked back from it on its own and stayed there, eyes squeezed shut, the blow ringing through her.

*Move. Move!*

She couldn't. Not yet. The flinch held her a heartbeat too long, and she knew the Absol had seen it.

## beat 3
— Nocturne —

She had. While the Buizel stood locked in her flinch, Nocturne's horn swung, and a pale blade of force sheared off its edge across the gap between them.

— Ripples —

It caught her in the chest and ran up over the shoulder that was already burning and on into her neck. The left shoulder went from a sharp sting to something hot and wide that spread down her arm, and she staggered a step back on the sand before she caught herself, gasping, her left forearm fin clamped flat.

*Twice. She's hit me twice and I haven't touched her.*

## beat 4
— Ripples —

That was enough. Ripples dropped low, and water burst out of her: it wrapped her whole body in one roaring sheath, and her tails spun, and she went across the clearing like a thrown spear, straight at the white chest.

— Nocturne —

It hit her square in the chest and did not stop. The water closed round her too, and she was carried with it, a dozen strides across the grass, the jet scouring at her ribs, her shoulder, her throat the whole way, cold and hammering and impossible to get out of. Then the old oak came up behind her.

She hit it back first. The bark slammed into her back and her right shoulder and her left thigh all at once, and then the Buizel's weight came after it, driving into her chest and crushing her against the trunk. The breath went out of her. One knee touched down in the roots.

*Not down. Not for this.*

She got the knee back under her. She stayed on her feet, but her whole body rang, and for a moment she could only stand against the oak, soaked to the skin, the world tilting.

## beat 5
— Nocturne —

Nocturne straightened off the trunk, water running out of her white fur, and looked up past the leaves. The sun was already warm. She made it more than warm. Something in her called to it, and the light came down hard and white over the clearing, the heat of it pressing on her back, steam beginning to rise off her soaked coat.

*Fight in that, river rat.*

— Ripples —

The glare hit Ripples' eyes like a slap. The shallows flashed white; the sand turned hot under her feet. She felt the water on her own fur start to go, and something in her chest tightened. Her water would be weaker in this. She could feel it.

## beat 6
— Ripples —

So she took the sun away. She tipped her head back, cheek marks flaring, and called to the lake behind her, to the clouds, and they came: pulled in off the water, piling up grey over the treetops, swallowing the glare. The first fat drops hit the sand. Then it was raining properly, warm rain hissing on the lake and pattering through the leaves.

*Better. Much better.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne felt the rain land on her back and flatten the steam out of her fur, and her lip peeled back from one fang. The Buizel was standing in it with her face turned up like it was a gift.

## beat 7
— Nocturne —

She went in again through the rain. Her head jinked high, as if for the face, a quick, sharp feint, and then she dropped and went low, jaws open, for the arm.

— Ripples —

Ripples didn't flinch at the feint. She had watched those red eyes and they had never left her arm. But reading it and getting out of it were different things. The jaws closed on her right upper arm, hard, teeth through wet fur, and the bite wrenched her forearm fin sideways and jarred up into her shoulder.

She yelped, short and furious, and tore the arm free. It throbbed all the way down to her webbed paw, and when she flexed the fingers the fin quivered.

*Saw it coming. Still let her bite me. Stupid.*

## beat 8
— Ripples —

She answered with a jet: Water Gun, point-blank, straight into the Absol's face, the rain making it heavier.

— Nocturne —

It hit her in the muzzle and smashed her head back, and it was in her mouth before she could close it, up her nose, down her throat. Nocturne coughed, choked, coughed again, spitting water, her eyes streaming. Every breath she tried to drag in caught on the water still in her and turned into another cough. Her neck ached where the jet had driven her head back, and her face stung.

*Hate. Water.*

## beat 9
— Nocturne —

Still coughing, she spun. The sickle tail came round low and then up, hardened like steel, and caught the Buizel under the jaw and kept going.

— Ripples —

The jaw took it, her head snapped back, and the ground let go of her. She was in the air, her feet kicking at nothing, the rain and the sky and the treetops wheeling, and she had time to know she could do absolutely nothing about whatever came next.

— Nocturne —

What came next was Nocturne's claws. She was already moving under the falling Buizel, and her forepaw raked up across the cream stomach while it hung there: no ground under it, nothing to push from, nothing to twist with. She felt it go in, all of it.

— Ripples —

The claws went through her stomach and up into her chest, and the force of it turned her over in the air and smashed her down. She landed on her back in the wet grass, hard, her upper back and her shoulder taking it, and the air went out of her in one flat, cracked gasp and would not come back.

She lay there with her mouth open, trying to breathe and not breathing, the rain falling on her face.

*Can't— can't get—*

Her stomach was a single burning line. Her arms had come in over it on their own.

## beat 10
— Ripples —

She did the only thing she could from flat on her back. She threw up a shell of light round herself, shimmering and hard, and under it she rolled onto her front and got her paws on the fallen trunk and hauled. The first try folded. The second held, and she was up, swaying, one paw braced on the moss, her stomach pulled in tight.

— Both —

The rain eased off over the clearing as she stood, the clouds thinning, and the sun came back through them, warm and ordinary again.

## beat 11
— Nocturne —

Nocturne saw the Buizel up and lunged for the throat anyway, jaws wide.

— Ripples —

Her teeth hit the barrier a hand's width from Ripples' neck and stopped dead. The shell rang like a struck bell and broke into nothing, and the Absol was left with her jaws closing on air.

*Ha.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne pulled back, ears flat, the sting of it all the way up her muzzle.

## beat 12
— Ripples —

Ripples went for the foreleg, Ice Fang, frost spreading over her teeth as she lunged.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne twisted out of its line and let the freezing bite pass her shoulder, close enough to feel the cold of it, and as the Buizel came past she struck back: a short, hard swipe across the orange left arm. It was nothing like the bite; it was quick and mean and done.

— Both —

A gust came off the lake as they broke apart, a stronger one, flattening the grass and shoving at the Buizel's back, and she staggered half a step with it and caught herself.

## beat 13
— Nocturne —

Nocturne went to take hold of her, jaws for the right arm again, meaning to drag her in close and work on her there.

— Ripples —

Ripples sprang back, and the jaws closed on nothing. Out of the same movement she came back in and drove the heel of her paw into the Absol's nose.

— Nocturne —

It was a small blow, and it hurt far out of proportion: the nose was soft and the paw was hard, and Nocturne's eyes watered at once. She shook her head, snarling.

## beat 14
— Ripples —

Ripples backed off across the grass, breathing hard, and let a sheen spread over her fur, a cool, mirror-bright film that caught the sun.

*Go on. Hit me with something.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne saw the shine and did not understand it. She only saw a wounded river weasel standing still.

## beat 15
— Nocturne —

She opened her jaws and let the dark out: a ring of it, rolling across the grass, catching the Buizel in the head and the chest and the stomach that was already torn.

— Ripples —

It hurt. It hit her in the head and in the chest, and on the stomach it found the line the claws had left and pressed straight into it, and she folded round it with the air knocked clean out of her. But the sheen took the force as it came, and threw it back.

— Nocturne —

The dark came back at her, bent and brighter, and slammed into her ribs and her neck. Nocturne grunted and stepped sideways into it, the left ribs flaring hot where the oak had already bruised them.

*She sent it back. She sent my own—*

— Ripples —

Ripples was bent over, wheezing, unable to get a full breath, but her eyes were on the Absol, and they were bright.

## beat 16
— Nocturne —

Nocturne did not like being made a fool of, and she answered it with the tail. It came round low through the wet grass and took the Buizel's left knee clean out from under her.

— Ripples —

The knee buckled sideways and the ground came up, and she landed on her back again, hard, the impact jarring into her shoulders and her hip. The knee throbbed. Her stomach, still caught in its spasm from the dark, pulled her knees up toward it.

## beat 17
— Nocturne —

She was down, and Nocturne was on her before she could even roll: standing over her, forepaw raking down, once across the stomach, then the thigh, then the knee, then the knee again.

She did not stop.

Somewhere past the fourth blow it stopped being a choice. It was a rhythm, her own breath coming hard in time with it, the claws going back to the right knee and the right thigh because that was where they had already been, the same places, again, again.

— Ripples —

The first rake across her stomach tore a cry out of her. The next ones on her right leg came too fast to be separate. Thigh, knee, knee, thigh, thigh, each one landing on the last, until the leg was one hot, hammered ache from hip to foot and she was curled away from it with her arms over her head, gasping, her breath knocked out of her again.

*Stop. Stop stop stop—*

— Nocturne —

When the frenzy broke, Nocturne stepped back, panting, and watched the Buizel crawl. She watched which leg she wouldn't put weight on. The right one. She marked it.

— Ripples —

Ripples got to the boulder at the edge of the beach and climbed it with her hands, one ugly pull at a time, and on the third try she was standing, leaning on the warm grey stone, her right leg trembling under her.

## beat 18
— Nocturne —

Nocturne gave her no time to steady. The tail came again, this time high, and hammered across the Buizel's right forearm.

— Ripples —

The blow caught the fin edge-on and bent it back, and her whole right arm went numb to the shoulder. She sat down hard on the sand, legs gone from under her, the forearm fin throbbing so hard she could feel her pulse in it.

## beat 19
— Nocturne —

Sitting was not down enough. Nocturne seized the Buizel by the arm and hauled, dragging her bodily down the soft sand and into the warm shallows, until the water was round them both.

— Ripples —

The sand scraped her back and her hip as she went, and then the water closed over her, and she was pitched flat on her face in it, the lake in her eyes and her nose. She came up with her chin on the sand, coughing, soaked to the skin, her arms shaking under her.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne stood in the shallows to her hocks, hating every cold inch of it, and looked down at what she had dragged there.

## beat 20
— Nocturne —

She raked the Buizel's chest as she pushed herself up, a long cut through the cream fur that ran up into the neck and back over the right shoulder.

— Ripples —

It hurt in a new place and an old one at once. She did not go down. She crawled out of the water to the boulder again and pushed herself straight up against it on the third try, and as she stood, a dead branch cracked loose from high in an oak and dropped, glancing off her muzzle and her bad shoulder on its way to the grass.

She stared at it. Then she laughed, a cracked, breathless sound, because the alternative was something else.

## beat 21
— Ripples —

She went for the Absol's neck with Crunch, jaws wide, hurling herself off the rock.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne sprang back, and the teeth snapped shut a whisker from her ruff.

## beat 22
— Nocturne —

As the Buizel landed off-balance on the sand, Nocturne saw it: the right thigh, the one she had already hammered six times, turned square toward her, the weight on it, nothing guarding it. She did not think. Her claws went into the exact place they had been before.

It did not feel like the other blows. The others had met muscle. This one met nothing at all, went in, and kept going.

*That one went all the way.*

— Ripples —

She knew it was wrong before it finished landing. It went past the bruising and the torn-up fur and found the deepest, rawest place underneath, the place every one of those six blows had been building, and arrived there with everything behind it.

The cry was out before she could stop it, sharp and high. Her leg went. She didn't fall, quite: she caught herself on her hands and her good knee, and stayed there with the right leg stuck out behind her, shaking, a hard, fast tremor running from the thigh right up into her hip and the base of her tails.

*Something's wrong. Something's really wrong with my leg.*

Her body flinched away from it so hard that for a moment she could not move anything else at all.

— Ripples —

She stayed there, crouched on the sand, and it did not stop when the claws left. It kept arriving: a first hot wave, then a second, deeper, then a third that seemed to come from inside the bone. The thigh felt too big for her body, hot and wrong under her paw. She breathed through her teeth, small breaths, because the big ones made her whole leg jump. A thin whine leaked out between them, and she clamped her jaw on it and it leaked out anyway.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne watched her. She watched the leg stuck out behind, the tremor that would not stop, the paw pressed flat on the sand so hard the webbing spread. She watched the Buizel try to swallow the sound and fail. She stood very still in the sun and let her own breath come back.

*That's where you break. Right there.*

## beat 23
— Nocturne —

She let her breathe, because she needed to breathe herself. Her ribs were sore from the oak and her nose still ached, and her energy was down to almost nothing. She stood on the dry grass with her flanks heaving and kept her eyes on the Buizel the whole time.

— Ripples —

Ripples got her leg under her, slowly, testing it with a little weight and then a little more. It held. Barely.

## beat 24
— Nocturne —

Nocturne sent the blade from her horn across the gap again: Psycho Cut, slicing into the chest and the shoulder and the neck.

— Ripples —

It caught her across the chest and the left shoulder, which was already ruined, and up into her neck, and this time she couldn't keep upright. She doubled over round it, arms crossed over her chest, head down, the air hissing out through her teeth. For a long moment she could not straighten.

## beat 25
— Ripples —

She came up out of the fold with water: Water Pulse, a ring of it straight into the white face.

— Nocturne —

It burst over her head and neck and into her mouth again, and she was coughing again, spitting lake, her eyes streaming. Her head rang. Her ruff was plastered flat.

— Ripples —

Ripples kept her right leg turned away and her weight on the left, guarding it without thinking about it, and her left side was open for it.

## beat 26
— Nocturne —

Nocturne stood and coughed until the water was out. She took the beat to breathe, head low, watching the Buizel favour the leg.

## beat 27
— Ripples —

Ripples flung a spray of stars: Swift, glittering across the clearing, catching the Absol in the head, the chest and the right shoulder.

— Nocturne —

They stung more than they had any right to. Her head was beginning to throb in a slow, heavy rhythm now, the water and the stars and the nose all adding up.

## beat 28
— Nocturne —

She breathed again. She hated it. Every beat she spent catching her breath was one the Buizel spent catching hers.

## beat 29
— Nocturne —

Nocturne went in low and raked upward, and her claws caught the Buizel across the face: the nose, the jaw, the throat.

— Ripples —

The nose took the worst of it, and her eyes went white with tears at once. She reeled back, her paw going up to her face, her jaw aching, her throat raw.

*The face. She's going for the face now.*

## beat 30
— Ripples —

She answered with the one thing the Absol's kind feared: a fighting chop, Brick Break, the edge of her paw driven straight down onto the top of the white head.

— Nocturne —

It landed like a dropped stone. The force of it went straight through her skull and down into her horn and her throat, and the world went white and ringing for a heartbeat. It was the kind of blow her kind had always had to fear, and her body knew it before she did. She staggered, her head low, and for a moment she could not tell where the ground was.

*Brick Break. Of course. Of course she has that.*

## beat 31
— Nocturne —

Through the ringing, Nocturne found her. She hooked a hind leg behind the Buizel's good knee and swept it, and as Ripples went down she went with her, driving her forward and down into the shallows face-first. A forepaw found the back of the slim neck and pressed it under. Her full weight came down across the Buizel's upper back.

*Breathe that.*

— Ripples —

The water closed over her face. Sand in her mouth, lake up her nose, the weight on her back crushing her flat into the soft bottom, the paw on her neck holding her head under.

## beat 32
— Nocturne —

Nocturne bore down. She felt the neck under her paw, the back under her weight, the small body rigid beneath her, and she held it there, letting her up a finger's width for one gasp and pressing her under again.

— Ripples —

She got one breath, half water. Then she bucked: everything she had, her back arching up under the weight, her legs driving at the sand, her good hind foot lashing back and catching the Absol hard on the hind paw.

— Nocturne —

The paw jolted out from under her. Her weight slid, and the Buizel came up out of the shallows in an explosion of water, out from under her and away.

*No. No, I had her.*

She kept her feet. She stood in the shallows, dripping, her hind paw throbbing, and watched the Buizel crawl up the sand.

— Ripples —

Ripples lay on the sand at the waterline, retching up lake, every breath a scrape. She tried to get up and her arms wouldn't take it. Not yet.

## beat 33
— Nocturne —

Nocturne stood back and breathed, because she had nothing left to throw for a moment.

— Ripples —

Ripples got to the nearest pine and pushed herself straight up it, her back to the bark, her right leg held clear of the ground. On the third try she was standing.

## beat 34
— Nocturne —

Nocturne did something she rarely did. She stopped attacking. She set her paws wide, lowered her head, and waited, her weight back, every muscle wound and quiet.

*Come on. Come and hit me.*

— Ripples —

Ripples saw the stillness and didn't trust it. But the Absol was hurt, and so was she, and waiting was something she couldn't afford.

## beat 35
— Ripples —

She came off the pine with Brick Break, the edge of her paw driving down into the Absol's ribs, and felt it land deep.

— Nocturne —

She took it. She let it hit her: the ribs on her left side, already bruised from the oak and the mirror, took a chop that bent her sideways and drove the wind out of her, and then she sent it back. Everything that had gone into her came out again, doubled, in one savage swipe that caught the Buizel across the face.

— Ripples —

The cheek split hot, and the ear above it took the rest, and her head snapped sideways so hard the world tilted. She hadn't even seen the counter coming. She stood there with her ear ringing and her cheek burning, both, and stared.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne was bent round her ribs, unable to get a full breath, but there was a grim satisfaction in it.

## beat 36
— Ripples —

Ripples ducked in, low and quick, and wrapped her twin tails round the Absol's left foreleg, and cinched them like a rope round a post.

— Nocturne —

The tails closed tight round her upper foreleg. Not a blow; a grip, steady, squeezing, the leg going numb and tingling under it. She tried to pull it free and the tails only tightened.

## beat 37
— Ripples —

With the foreleg held, Ripples went for the neck. Crunch, all of it, her teeth sinking into the white ruff and the throat beneath it.

— Nocturne —

The bite went deep, and the shock of it made her flinch so hard her head jerked away on its own, and she couldn't make it come back. Her neck was on fire. The foreleg throbbed in the tails' grip. She braced against the bind, all four legs, and it held.

## beat 38
— Ripples —

Ripples bit again, lower this time, into the right foreleg, the shoulder, the paw, and her tails stayed wound round the left.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne clawed at the tails with her free forepaw, and they held. She could feel the bound leg going cold and heavy, slow to answer her. Her shoulder ached where the bite had been.

*Get off. Get off me.*

## beat 39
— Nocturne —

She charged. Bound leg and all, Nocturne put her shoulder down and drove forward, a reckless full-body ram, and took the Buizel off her feet and carried her across the clearing and into a tall pine.

— Ripples —

The ram took her square in the chest. Then the pine came up behind her, and her back and her ruined left shoulder and her hip slammed into the trunk all at once, and the Absol's whole weight crushed her chest against it. Something in her chest seemed to fold in.

— Nocturne —

The force of it jarred back into her own throat and ribs as she hit; the charge cost her too. But the Buizel was crumpling, sliding down the trunk, and Nocturne stepped back and watched her go.

— Ripples —

She slid down the pine to the roots and lay on her back, and her tails tore loose from the Absol's foreleg as she went. Then something lit inside her: a hot, bright surge that came up from somewhere she hadn't known was there, her heart pounding, her breath coming back, her eyes clearing.

*Not yet. NOT YET.*

## beat 40
— Ripples —

Still on her back, she opened her mouth and let the lake come out of her: Hydro Pump, a pillar of water.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne met it with the dark, a ring of it slammed straight into the oncoming water. The two met in the air between them, spray and shadow exploding outward, and for one moment they were both leaning into it.

Then the water won. It tore through the dark and hit her in the head, the chest, the belly, and held there: three long, brutal pulses, battering her head back, filling her mouth, driving her chest in. She doubled over round it, drenched, retching.

— Ripples —

Ripples got up after it. One ugly heave against the pine, and she was standing.

## beat 41
— Ripples —

She hit the Absol with another Water Pulse while she was still bent over: a ring of water into the face, the head, the neck.

— Nocturne —

Her head was already ringing. This made it worse. The world swam and doubled; she couldn't tell which of the two Buizels was the real one. She was coughing water up again. Her thoughts kept slipping off each other.

*Where. Where is she.*

## beat 42
— Nocturne —

She stood still and breathed, because there was nothing else she could do. Her head was a slow, heavy drum. Her neck burned. She kept her head low and turned slightly away, shielding it without deciding to.

## beat 43
— Ripples —

The surge was fading. Ripples could feel it going, and she knew there would not be another. So she spent all of it.

She flung Swift with everything she had left, a storm of stars, and they hit the Absol in the head and the chest and the right shoulder, harder than anything she had thrown all fight.

— Nocturne —

Every star felt like a stone. Her head had been hurt and hurt again and this hit it on top of everything, and the force of it rocked her back on her haunches. The air went out of her. She had nothing in her chest to get it back with.

— Ripples —

Then Ripples was empty. Her arms dropped. She swayed on the sand, breathing in harsh, dragging gulps, and there was nothing behind them at all.

## beat 44
— Nocturne —

Nocturne breathed. Short breaths, all she could get. Her head pounded so hard her vision pulsed with it.

## beat 45
— Ripples —

Ripples dragged herself forward and brought her paw down on the white head again, Brick Break, the same spot, the edge of her hand onto the place she'd hit before.

— Nocturne —

It landed exactly where the last one had, and the pain was so enormous it went quiet for a moment, and then roared. Her ear rang. Her throat burned. She should have gone down.

Something answered instead. Deep in her, under all of it, something lit, and the surge came up through her, hot and hard, clearing her eyes.

— Ripples —

Ripples had put too much into it. She overbalanced on the follow-through and stumbled, nearly falling, her bad leg buckling under her, and had to catch herself on her hands.

*Get up. Get up, get up—*

## beat 46
— Nocturne —

She was stumbling. Off balance, soaked to the bone, wide open. Nocturne let the lightning go: everything stored in her, released all at once, a white-blue bolt that hit the soaked Buizel and ran through every wet inch of her.

— Ripples —

The bolt went through her like she was made of water, which she nearly was. Every muscle seized at once. Her jaw locked. Her fins stood straight out. When it let go she was still standing, somehow, but the clearing had gone far away and blurry, and the sounds came late, as if from underwater.

## beat 47
— Nocturne —

The surge was still in her. She feinted high, a jerk of the head as if for the face.

— Ripples —

Ripples flinched from it with her whole dazed body, arms up, and the real blow came in low.

— Nocturne —

The forepaw drove into the Buizel's stomach, the ruined centre of it, from below, while she was turned the wrong way with her arms up and nothing guarding it.

— Ripples —

There was no rolling with it. It went in and stayed in, and the air went out of her and did not come back, and she folded round it with a sound she hadn't made before, high and thin and broken off.

*Fell for it. Fell for it.*

## beat 48
— Ripples —

She stood bent over, her arms locked round her middle, and breathed in small, desperate sips, because a real breath was impossible. She was so far past spent there was no word for it. She stayed on her feet only because falling seemed to need more than she had.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne watched her try to breathe. The surge in her was fading too. She could feel it going.

## beat 49
— Ripples —

From the fold, Ripples opened her mouth and breathed out cold: Ice Beam, held, a freezing white line into the Absol's head and chest and shoulder.

— Nocturne —

It found her head, the one place that had nothing left, and held there: two pulses of cold that drove into the bruised skull and froze the fur on her chest and stiffened her shoulder. Her vision swam and slid again. Her breath came short and shallow, and the frost in her ruff crackled every time she drew one.

*Too much. Too much. She was finished, she was supposed to be—*

## beat 50
— Nocturne —

Nocturne had almost nothing left. She knew it. So she put all of it into one thing.

The sickle tail came round low and hard, harder than anything she had swung all fight, and took the Buizel's right knee, the one she had hammered, the one that was already ruined.

— Ripples —

The knee went. She was down, on her back in the grass, her shoulder and her back slamming into the ground, her leg a white sheet of pain from hip to foot.

— Nocturne —

And then Nocturne was empty too. She stood over her, swaying, and every scrap of strength she'd had a moment ago was gone, poured out into the swing. Her legs trembled. She did not trust them.

## beat 51
— Nocturne —

She stood and breathed. That was all.

— Ripples —

Ripples got as far as sitting up, her hands braced in the grass behind her, her leg stuck out in front of her. Further than that it would not go.

## beat 52
— Both —

Neither of them moved for a while. The sun came through a gap in the clouds and lay warm across the clearing, and the lake lapped at the sand as if nothing had happened, and somewhere in the oaks a bird started singing again.

— Ripples —

Ripples sat in the grass and tried three times to get her leg under her, and three times it would not hold.

## beat 53
— Ripples —

This time she used the pine. She rolled onto her front and climbed the trunk with her hands, her face pressed against the rough bark, and on the third try she was up, barely, on one leg.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne watched her climb and did not stop her. She was still getting her breath back, and she wanted the Buizel up for what came next.

## beat 54
— Nocturne —

She breathed, and breathed, and felt a little of it come back into her legs.

## beat 55
— Nocturne —

Then she went in, and bit. Her jaws closed on the Buizel's stomach, on the place that had taken everything, and she bit down and twisted and let go.

— Ripples —

She was guarding it with both arms and it didn't matter. The teeth went past her arms into the ruined centre of her, and her whole body flinched round it so hard she couldn't move at all.

## beat 56
— Nocturne —

She waited out the flinch and breathed, her jaws aching, the taste of the lake and of wet fur in her mouth.

## beat 57
— Nocturne —

When the Buizel moved again, Nocturne was on her. She caught her across the hips with her shoulder and threw her, a heave that lifted her off her one good leg and dropped her flat on her back in the grass. Before she could move, Nocturne was over her: a forepaw ground into the ruined stomach, a hind paw pinning her thigh, her chest and ruff pressing down on the cream chest.

*Stay down. Stay down now.*

— Ripples —

The forepaw on her stomach was the worst thing that had ever happened to her. It didn't strike; it just pressed, and pressed, and every breath she took pushed her stomach up into it.

## beat 58
— Nocturne —

Nocturne bore down and held it. She could feel every breath under her paw. She could feel the small body trying to gather itself.

— Ripples —

Ripples tried to slide out sideways, out from under the weight, and got nowhere; the paw on her chest came down harder for it, and her chest flared.

## beat 59
— Ripples —

She tried again. She tried to slide, to twist, anything. The hind paw on her thigh ground down and pinned her leg harder, and her thigh lit up under it.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne watched the struggle, every twitch of it, and felt it weaken. Her own legs were shaking with the effort of holding her, but she held.

*Less each time. Less each time.*

## beat 60
— Ripples —

She bucked once, with everything that was left. It was not much. The weight barely moved, and the thigh under the hind paw paid for it.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne felt the buck and rode it, and felt how weak it was, and knew.

## beat 61
— Ripples —

She didn't try again. She lay under the weight and looked up past the white shape over her at the sky, at the clouds sliding by, at the sun coming and going. Her breath came shallow and quick. Every one of them pushed her stomach up into the paw, and every one was a little shorter than the last.

*Can't. Can't anymore.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne looked down at her and saw the eyes going unfocused. She did not let up.

## beat 62
— Ripples —

The edges of the sky went grey and soft. Her breath went small, then smaller. The pain in her stomach went from everything to something far away, and her paws, which had been pushing at the forepaw on her belly, slid off it and lay still in the grass. Her eyes closed. She was still breathing, slow and shallow, when the rest of her went quiet.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne felt her go slack under her paws. She held a moment longer, to be sure. Then she lifted her weight off, slowly, and stepped back, and stood over the still orange shape in the grass with her head low and her flanks heaving and her own legs trembling under her.

The bird in the oaks was still singing. The lake lapped at the sand.

*Done. It's done.*

She did not feel like she had won. She felt like she had survived. She turned her head away from the Buizel and looked at the water for a long time.
