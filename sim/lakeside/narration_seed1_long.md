## opening
— Both —

The clearing lay open to the lake like a held breath. Old pines and broad oaks stood round three sides of it, their roots humped up through the grass where the shade began, and along the treeline a fallen trunk lay half sunk in moss, soft and green and warm to the touch where the sun reached it. On the fourth side the grass thinned and paled into a beach of fine sand that ran down gently into the water. The shallows there were clear and warm, the colour of weak tea over the sand; a few lengths out, the lake went dark all at once, cool and deep.

It was the middle of a warm afternoon. Fat white clouds drifted over from the far shore, and their shadows slid across the clearing one after another, dimming the grass and letting it go. A breeze came in off the water, never strong, just enough to turn the oak leaves silver side up and set the long grass at the edge of the sand nodding. Pollen hung gold in the shafts of light under the trees. Somewhere high in an oak a bird was singing, the same bright phrase over and over, as if nothing in the world was about to happen.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne came out of the pines with the heavy, rolling pace of her kind and stopped at the edge of the shade. The sun found her at once: white fur gone almost too bright to look at, the dark blue-grey of her face, the scythe of her horn throwing a thin line of light as she turned her head. Her red eyes went over the clearing piece by piece, the way they always did: the trees, the trunk, the boulder at the edge of the beach, the long open stretch of grass, and last of all the lake.

She looked at the lake for a long time.

*Water. Of course it would be water.*

Her lip lifted off one fang and settled again. She set her broad paws down on the dry grass, well back from where it turned to sand, and felt the ground under her: firm, warm, a little springy with roots. Good ground. She meant to keep it. The sickle of her tail swung once behind her and went still.

— Ripples —

Ripples came up out of the shallows without a splash. One moment the water was flat and bright; the next there was a sleek orange head in it, then shoulders, then the whole slim length of her walking up the sand on two webbed feet with the lake running off her in sheets. She shook herself at the waterline from ears to tail-tips, a hard, whole-body shake that threw a spray of drops glittering into the sun, and then stood there dripping, her fur flattened dark to her body, her bare cream throat pale in the light.

The blue fins on her forearms opened and closed once. Behind her, her twin tails began to turn, lazily, the cream tips drawing slow circles in the air.

Her small black eyes went over the Absol from horn to sickle tail. They lingered on the horn, then the jaws, then dropped to the four careful paws planted on the dry grass, a long body-length back from the sand, and the marks on her cheeks twitched.

*Doesn't want her feet wet. Good. That's good.*

The bird in the oak sang its phrase again and went quiet.

## beat 2
— Nocturne —

Nocturne did not spend the quiet. She came off the grass in four long, low strides, faster than her size had any right to be, and the last stride carried her inside the Buizel's reach before the orange head had finished coming up. Her right forepaw came round flat and wide. The three claws were spread, and the dark came up along them as she swung, a thin smoke of it clinging to each edge like the shadow of a blade.

She aimed for the shoulder. Not the throat, not yet. The shoulder, to see how this one took a hurt.

— Ripples —

The claws took her across the point of the left shoulder and dragged down, and the dark in them bit in a way claws alone never had. It went through the wet fur as if it weren't there and found the meat under it, and the force of the rake turned her half round and jarred straight across into her chest and up into her throat, where the cream fur was thinnest.

And then she couldn't move.

It wasn't a choice. Her head jerked back from the blow on its own and stayed back, eyes squeezed shut, jaw locked, her whole body caught on that one moment of impact like a fish on a hook. She knew she had to move. She told her legs to move. They didn't answer. For one long heartbeat she simply stood there in the sun with her shoulder on fire, rigid, open.

*Move. MOVE.*

Her left fin had clamped flat against her forearm. The shoulder throbbed, a deep, spreading heat that ran down the outside of her arm and into her chest with every breath, and her throat ached where the edge of the blow had caught it. She could feel the Absol's eyes on her the whole time she stood there frozen. She hated that more than the claws.

## beat 3
— Nocturne —

She had seen it. A fighter who locked up like that after one rake was a fighter who would lock up again, and Nocturne did not waste it. While the Buizel stood caught in her flinch, Nocturne's head swung sharply to one side and the horn cut the air, and a pale, thin blade of force peeled off its edge and went across the space between them, humming.

— Ripples —

It caught her square in the chest, sliced up over the shoulder that was already burning, and ran on into the side of her neck. The chest took it like a slap; the shoulder took it like a brand. Whatever had been sharp there before went wide and hot and deep, and the heat spread down her arm to the elbow.

She staggered a step back on the sand, then another, and her heel sank and nearly took her over before she caught herself, arms flung out, gasping. Her neck stung where the blade had grazed it.

*Twice. She's hit me twice and I haven't touched her.*

Her left arm didn't want to come up. She made it come up anyway, held it across her body in front of the hurt shoulder, and set her feet in the sand, and looked at the Absol over it.

— Nocturne —

The Buizel was holding the arm in close. Nocturne noted it, and noted the eyes over it, black and hard and not frightened at all.

## beat 4
— Ripples —

That was enough of being looked at. Ripples dropped low, her weight settling into her legs, her twin tails winding up behind her into a blur, and the water came out of her all at once. It wrapped round her from nose to tail in a single roaring sheath, cold and bright, and she went across the sand and up onto the grass like a spear thrown off the lake, straight at the white chest.

— Nocturne —

She saw it coming and it didn't matter. It hit her square in the chest and simply did not stop.

The water closed round her too. That was the worst of it: not the blow but the being carried, her paws torn off the good ground, her whole body swallowed into the jet and borne across the clearing with it, a dozen strides, more, the water hammering and scouring at her chest and her ribs and her left shoulder and her throat the whole way, cold, relentless, impossible to get a foot under. She could not see. She could not breathe. Somewhere in it the Buizel's body was driving into her, and the water poured round them both.

Then the old oak came up behind her.

Her back hit the trunk first, then her right shoulder, then her left thigh, all in one crack against the bark, and in the same instant the Buizel's weight arrived behind the water and crushed her chest against the tree. Every scrap of air went out of her. Something in her ribs flared white. One knee buckled and touched down in the roots, and for a moment the whole clearing tilted.

*Not down. Not for this. Not for her.*

She got the knee back under her. She pushed off the trunk with her shoulder and stood, swaying, water pouring out of her fur, her chest heaving for air that came in short and shocked, the bark's print still burning across her back. She stayed on her feet. But her head was ringing and her legs were not quite her own, and she knew she could not have closed on anything just then if her life depended on it.

— Ripples —

Ripples bounced off and landed in a crouch on the grass, the water draining out of her sheath and into the roots. She looked at the Absol leaning on the oak, soaked white fur plastered to her ribs, and felt a hard, hot, savage satisfaction.

## beat 5
— Nocturne —

Nocturne straightened off the oak and stood in the dapple under its leaves with the lake running out of her fur. She looked up through the branches. The sun was already warm, high and white beyond the clouds.

She made it more than warm.

Something in her reached for it, and the light answered. The clouds thinned and drew back from the sun as if it had pushed them, and it came down on the clearing hard and white and merciless, flattening every shadow, pressing on her back like a hand. Steam began to rise off her soaked coat in faint wisps. The grass smelled suddenly hot.

*Let's see you fight in that, river rat.*

— Ripples —

The glare hit her eyes like a slap. The shallows behind her flashed blinding white; the sand under her feet went from warm to hot in moments. She felt the lake water on her own fur start to go, drying stiff at the tips, and her breath came shorter in the heat.

And under that, something worse. She could feel it, the way she could feel weather coming: in this light her water would come weaker. Every jet, every pulse, thinner and slower to bite. Her cheek marks twitched. She squinted across the white glare at the Absol steaming under the oak and knew exactly what she had done.

## beat 6
— Ripples —

So she took the sun away.

She tipped her head back, eyes narrowed against the glare, and reached the other way: past the trees, to the lake, to the water lying warm and heavy under the surface and the clouds banked low over the far shore. It came. Clouds pulled in off the water and piled up over the treetops, grey and swelling, and the white light died to a soft, damp grey. The breeze turned cool. The first fat drop struck the sand by her foot and left a dark coin. Then another, and another, and then it was raining properly, warm rain hissing on the lake and pattering through the oak leaves and running down her face.

She stood in it with her face turned up and her fins spread, and the stiffness went out of her fur.

*Better. Much better.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne felt the rain land on her back and flatten the steam out of her coat, and the sun she had called was gone as if it had never been. Water ran into her eyes. Her lip peeled back from her fangs. The Buizel was standing out there in it with her face tipped up to the sky as if it were a gift, and Nocturne understood very clearly that it was.

## beat 7
— Nocturne —

She went in through the rain, low and fast. Halfway there her head jinked high and left, a sharp, sudden snap toward the orange face as if she meant to take it, and then she dropped and went low, jaws already open, for the arm.

— Ripples —

Ripples didn't flinch at the feint. She hadn't been watching the head. She'd been watching those red eyes, and the eyes had never once left her right arm, and she knew the fake for what it was the moment it came.

Knowing wasn't the same as getting out of the way. The jaws closed on her right upper arm, hard, teeth driving through wet fur into the muscle, and the bite wrenched her forearm fin sideways and jarred up into her shoulder. She yelped, short and furious, and tore the arm free, and the teeth raked her on the way out.

The arm throbbed from shoulder to fingers. When she flexed her webbed paw, the fin on her forearm quivered and wouldn't lie flat.

*Saw it coming. Still let her bite me. Stupid. Stupid.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne backed off two paces with the taste of wet fur and lake in her mouth. The Buizel hadn't fallen for the feint; she had seen that too. This one watched eyes, not paws. She filed it away.

## beat 8
— Ripples —

Ripples answered with a jet. She brought her head round and spat Water Gun point-blank across the closing gap, and the rain fed it; it came out of her thick and hard as a thrown rope, straight into the Absol's face.

— Nocturne —

It smashed into her muzzle and snapped her head back on her neck, and it was in her mouth before she could close it. Up her nose. Down her throat. Her chest convulsed. She coughed, and coughed again, and spat a mouthful of lake onto the grass, and tried to draw a breath and got more water with it and coughed so hard her eyes streamed.

Her face stung from the force of the jet. Her neck ached where it had whipped her head back. And every breath she dragged in caught on the water still sitting at the back of her throat and turned into another wet, tearing cough.

*Hate. Water.*

She stood with her head low and her sides heaving, coughing, the rain running off her horn, and she did not take her eyes off the Buizel for one moment of it.

## beat 9
— Nocturne —

Still coughing, she spun. Her body went round all at once, low to the grass, and the sickle tail came with it, hardening in the swing until it rang like a struck blade. It came round low and then rose, sweeping up under the Buizel's chin, and caught her under the jaw, and kept going.

— Ripples —

The jaw took it with a crack that went up through her skull, her head snapped back, and the ground let go of her.

There was nothing under her feet. That was the strangest thing: one moment the wet grass, the next nothing at all, her legs kicking at air, the rain and the grey sky and the dark tops of the pines wheeling past. Her ear rang where the edge of the tail had clipped it. Her throat burned. And she had time, hanging there, to understand that she could do absolutely nothing about whatever came next.

— Nocturne —

What came next was Nocturne.

She was already moving under the falling Buizel, already rising onto her hind legs, and her right forepaw came up and across in one long, dark-edged rake through the cream of the stomach while it hung there in the air. No ground under it. Nothing to push off from. Nothing to twist away with. She felt the claws go in and drag through, all of it, every scrap of force in her shoulder going where she sent it.

— Ripples —

The claws went through her stomach and up into her chest, and the force of it flipped her over in the air and drove her down. She hit the grass on her back, hard, her shoulders and the hurt left shoulder taking the landing and her right thigh slapping down after them, and the air went out of her in one flat, cracked gasp.

It did not come back.

She lay in the wet grass with her mouth open, trying to breathe and not breathing. Her chest heaved and nothing went into it. Rain fell on her face, into her open eyes. Her stomach was a single burning line, hot from one side to the other, and her arms had come in across it on their own, pressing, as if she could hold it shut.

*Can't— can't get—*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne stood over her in the rain with her own breath still rasping on the last of the water in her throat, and watched.

She watched the arms come in over the stomach. She watched the chest heave and catch, heave and catch, and the mouth open on nothing, the cream throat working. She watched the small black eyes stare straight up into the rain, unfocused, and then find her, and narrow. Every twitch told her something. The stomach was the one. The Buizel had covered it before anything else, before her head, before her throat. She would cover it again.

*There. That's where you hurt.*

The breath came back to the Buizel at last, a thin, whistling gasp, and Nocturne heard every bit of it.

## beat 10
— Ripples —

She could not get up yet. She knew it, flat on her back in the wet grass with her stomach on fire and her breath coming in thin sips. So she did the one thing she could do from there. She threw a shell up round herself: light, hard and shimmering, closing over her like the inside of a bubble, rain beading and running off its curve.

Under it, she rolled. It hurt; everything in her stomach pulled as she went over onto her front. She got her paws under her and found the fallen trunk with them, soft moss and rotten wood, and hauled. Her arms shook. The first heave folded and dropped her back onto her chest. The second held, and she came up off the grass with both hands braced on the trunk and her stomach pulled in tight, swaying, her legs slow to take her weight.

— Both —

As she stood, the rain thinned and stopped. The clouds she had called drifted apart over the lake, and the sun came back through them, warm and ordinary again, lighting the drops on every blade of grass. The shell round her shimmered in it, holding.

## beat 11
— Nocturne —

Nocturne saw the Buizel up and leaning on the trunk, still bent round her stomach, and went for the throat. She did not slow for the shimmer. She lunged with her jaws wide.

— Ripples —

The teeth hit the barrier a hand's width from her neck and stopped dead.

The shell rang like a struck bell, a clear, high note that went out across the lake, and broke into nothing round her, falling away in sparks of light. The jaws snapped shut on air an inch from her fur. She saw the red eyes, very close, go wide.

*Ha. Not this time.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne pulled back with her ears flat and the sting of the barrier tingling all the way up her muzzle into her skull. Her teeth ached. The Buizel was right there, unhurt by it, and Nocturne had thrown her whole weight into a bite at nothing.

She did not like that at all.

## beat 12
— Ripples —

Ripples went for the foreleg before the Absol could reset. Frost spread over her teeth as she lunged, white and crackling, and she aimed low, under the white chest, for the leg Nocturne was leaning on.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne twisted out of its line. Her body turned on itself like a cat's, and the freezing jaws passed so close to her shoulder that she felt the cold of them through her fur. And as the Buizel came past, overreaching, Nocturne struck back without even setting herself: a short, hard swipe of her forepaw across the orange left arm.

— Ripples —

It was nothing like the bite. It was quick, mean, and over before she knew it had landed: a hot stripe across her upper arm, deep enough to make her hiss through her teeth. She stumbled on past and turned.

— Both —

A gust came in off the lake at that moment, stronger than the breeze had been all afternoon. It flattened the grass in a long silver wave and shoved at the Buizel's back as she turned, and she staggered half a step with it, her tails flaring to catch her balance, before she found her feet.

— Ripples —

She stood with her new stripe throbbing on top of the old shoulder, and the stomach under both of them, and counted. Shoulder. Chest. Arm. Stomach. Arm again. And the Absol had a sore nose from nothing, because she hadn't laid a single thing on her that stuck.

*She's faster than she looks. Remember that.*

## beat 13
— Nocturne —

Nocturne went to take hold of her: jaws for the right arm again, the one already bitten, to drag her in close where there was no room for jets and shells, and work on her there.

— Ripples —

Ripples sprang back. Her legs, which had been so slow on the trunk, found something from somewhere, and the jaws closed on air where her arm had been. Out of the same spring, before she had even landed properly, she came back in and drove the heel of her paw straight into the Absol's nose.

— Nocturne —

It was a small blow. It hurt out of all proportion. The nose was soft and the paw was hard and wet and moving fast, and pain went straight up between Nocturne's eyes into her skull. Her eyes watered at once. She shook her head hard, snarling, and the snarl came out thick.

## beat 14
— Ripples —

Ripples backed off across the grass, breathing hard through her mouth, her stomach pulled in. She did not run. She stood, and let a sheen spread over her fur from her ears to her tail-tips: a cool, slick, mirror-bright film that caught the sun and threw it back in a hundred small points of light.

*Go on. Hit me with something. Anything that flies.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne saw the shine and did not understand it. Whatever the Buizel was doing, she was standing still to do it, wounded, breathing hard, holding her stomach. That was all Nocturne needed to understand.

## beat 15
— Nocturne —

She opened her jaws and let the dark out of her. It came as a ring, rolling out from her across the grass, warping the light as it went, flattening the wet blades under it. It broke over the Buizel in one wave: the head, the chest, and the torn stomach under the crossed arms.

— Ripples —

It hurt. She had known it would. It cracked across her head and hammered her chest, and on the stomach it found the long line the claws had left and pressed straight down into it, and she folded round it with the air punched clean out of her.

But the sheen took it.

She felt it happen: the force sinking into the film over her fur, held there for one strange instant, and then thrown back out the way it had come, bent and brighter and harder than it had arrived.

— Nocturne —

The dark came back at her. It slammed into her left side, into the ribs that had hit the oak, and into the neck that the jet had already whipped, and it was her own strength doing it, every scrap of it, returned with something extra. She grunted and staggered sideways into it, and her ribs lit up hot under her fur.

*She sent it back. She sent my own—*

— Ripples —

Ripples stayed folded where she stood. Her stomach would not unclench; every muscle round it had locked up tight over the hurt and would not let go, and her breath came in short, shallow pulls that stopped halfway. The cut there throbbed in time with her heart, hot and slow, and spread out under her arms into her sides. She could taste copper. Her head rang from the ring.

She tried to straighten and couldn't. She tried again and got halfway, her arms still crossed over herself, her tails hanging limp behind her and dragging in the wet grass. A thin sound came out of her on the out-breath, and she clamped her jaw on it.

*Breathe. Small ones. Small ones are fine.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne watched her through the throb in her own ribs. Bent nearly double, arms locked over the stomach, the tails gone dead behind her: the Buizel had stopped spinning them for the first time all fight. Her breath came short and caught. That sound she had tried to swallow had got out anyway, small and high, and Nocturne had heard it.

But the black eyes were still up. Still on her. Still bright.

*Clever. You'll pay for clever.*

## beat 16
— Nocturne —

Nocturne did not like being made a fool of, and she answered it the way she answered most things. Her body turned, the sickle tail came round low and flat through the wet grass, hardening as it swung, and it took the Buizel's left knee clean out from under her.

— Ripples —

The knee went sideways with a jolt that ran all the way up into her hip, and the foot went with it, and the ground came up. She landed on her back again, hard, her shoulders slapping into the grass, the hurt left shoulder grinding, her hip taking the rest. Her knee throbbed and her foot tingled. Her stomach, still caught in its spasm from the ring, pulled her knees up toward it, curling her on her side.

## beat 17
— Nocturne —

She was down, and Nocturne was over her before she could even roll. One forepaw came down across the cream stomach, a short, hard rake from above. Then the right thigh. Then the knee. Then the knee again.

She did not stop.

Somewhere past the fourth blow it stopped being a choice. It became a rhythm: her own breath coming hard and harsh in time with it, her shoulder rolling, the claws going back and back to the right leg because that was where they had already been, the same knee, the same thigh, the same places, again, again. She was hardly aiming any more. She did not need to.

— Ripples —

The first rake across her stomach tore a cry out of her. After that they came too fast to be separate things. Thigh, knee, knee, thigh, thigh, each landing on top of the last before the last had finished hurting, until the whole right leg was one hot, hammered ache from hip to foot and she was curled away from it on her side with her arms over her head, gasping, the breath knocked out of her again and again.

*Stop. Stop stop stop—*

— Nocturne —

When it broke, it broke all at once. Nocturne stepped back, panting hard, her forepaw stinging, and stood in the grass looking down.

The Buizel was crawling. Not up yet, just away, pulling herself across the grass with her hands toward the edge of the beach. Nocturne watched which leg she pushed with. The left. The right one dragged behind her, the knee held stiff, the foot not touching down. When it bumped a root, the whole orange body jerked and a high, thin sound got out through the gritted teeth. The tails were dragging too.

*Right leg. Right knee. Remember.*

She did not follow. Not yet. She stood and breathed and watched every twitch of it.

— Ripples —

The boulder at the edge of the beach was warm and grey and smooth, and she climbed it with her hands, one ugly pull at a time. The first time her right leg took weight it folded. The second time she got as far as her good knee. The third time she was standing, her whole body leaning against the stone, her right leg held off the ground and trembling, her breath coming in short, dragged sips that never got all the way down.

## beat 18
— Nocturne —

Nocturne gave her no time to steady. She came off the grass as the Buizel straightened against the rock, and the tail came round again, high this time, chest height, hardened and humming, and hammered across the orange right forearm where it was braced on the stone.

— Ripples —

The blow caught the blue fin edge-on and bent it back against the bone, and the whole right arm went numb from the shoulder down. Her paw slid off the boulder. Her legs, already shaking, went from under her, and she sat down hard on the sand at the foot of the rock with a jolt that went up her spine into her teeth.

— Ripples —

For a while she just sat there.

The fin was the worst of it, and the strangest. It didn't hurt the way a cut hurt. It throbbed, deep and slow and hot, and she could feel her pulse in it, every beat of her heart pushing against the bent spines, and when she tried to lift the arm it came up late and heavy, as if it belonged to someone who was only half listening. Her paw hung open at the end of it. She looked at it. She told the fingers to close, and they curled, slowly, and stopped halfway.

Under the arm, everything else was still there. The stomach, a long, low burn she had stopped being able to separate from her own breathing. The shoulder. The leg stuck out in front of her in the sand, trembling on its own, the knee hot and swollen. Her breath came in quick, shallow pulls through her mouth.

A small sound kept trying to come up her throat. She swallowed it down. It came up again, a thin whine on the out-breath, and she pressed her muzzle into her own good shoulder until it stopped.

*Fine. Fine. I'm fine. Get up.*

She didn't get up.

## beat 19
— Nocturne —

Sitting was not down enough. Nocturne closed on her, seized the bitten right arm in her jaws, and hauled. She dragged the Buizel bodily off the foot of the boulder and down the soft slope of the beach, sand ploughing up under the orange back, and on into the warm shallows, until the water was round her own hocks and the Buizel was in it to the neck.

— Ripples —

The sand scraped up her back and her hip as she went, gritty and hot, and then the water closed over her and she was pitched forward and flat on her face in it. The lake filled her eyes and her nose. She came up with her chin on the sandy bottom and her mouth just out of the water, coughing, soaked to the skin all over again, her arms shaking where they were braced in the soft sand under her.

The water was warm. That was almost the worst thing about it. It was her water, and she was lying in it like something dropped.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne let go of the arm and stood in the shallows, hating every warm, wet inch of it round her legs, and looked down at what she had dragged there. Face down. In the water. Exactly where she wanted her.

The Buizel was still lying flat. Nocturne could feel her own breath coming hard from the drag, though, and her ribs ached. She waited.

## beat 20
— Nocturne —

The Buizel began to push herself up in the shallows, and Nocturne raked her as she came: a long cut down across the cream chest, the claws catching the side of the neck on the way in and the right shoulder on the way out.

— Ripples —

It hurt in a new place and an old one at the same time, the chest and the shoulder both lighting up, and she nearly went back down into the water. She didn't. She crawled out of the shallows to the boulder again, dripping, and put both hands on it and pushed herself straight up the stone, and on the third try she was standing.

— Both —

As she straightened, something cracked high in the oaks behind her. A dead branch broke loose and came down through the leaves, glancing off the top of her muzzle and her ruined left shoulder on its way to the grass, where it bounced once and lay still.

— Ripples —

She stared at it. Then she laughed, a short, cracked, breathless sound with no air behind it, because the alternative was something else.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne watched her laugh at a stick with her muzzle stinging and her shoulder dragging and every hurt part of her on show. Chest. Shoulder. Stomach. Knee. The arm. The laugh was the only thing on her that wasn't hurt, and it was thin. Nocturne's ears turned toward it and stayed there.

She did not think it was funny. But she noted that the Buizel could still laugh, and that this was a thing that would have to be taken from her too.

## beat 21
— Ripples —

Ripples pushed off the rock and hurled herself at the white neck with Crunch, jaws wide, putting the whole length of her body behind it.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne sprang back. The teeth snapped shut a whisker from her ruff, close enough that she felt the wind of the jaws closing, and the Buizel landed short on the sand, stumbling, her right leg buckling under her as it took the weight.

## beat 22
— Nocturne —

And there it was.

The Buizel had landed with her weight on the right leg, the leg that had taken six blows and a fall, and it was turned square to Nocturne with nothing in front of it: no arm, no tail, nothing. Off balance. Still stumbling. Nocturne did not think about it. Her claws went into the exact place they had been before, the dark smoking off their edges, the outside of the right thigh, where it was already torn and swollen and soft.

It did not feel like the other blows. The others had met muscle and fur, something that pushed back. This one met nothing at all. It went in, and in, and kept going, and she felt the leg give round her claws like wet sand.

*That one. That went all the way in.*

She was almost startled. Then something cold and hard and satisfied came up through her chest, and she held the claws there half a heartbeat longer than she needed to.

— Ripples —

She knew it was wrong before it finished landing.

The thigh had been hurt before; she knew how it hurt. This was not that. This went past the bruising and the torn fur and found the deepest, rawest place underneath, the place every one of those six blows had been building, and arrived there with everything behind it. The cry was out before she could stop it, sharp and high, a sound that went out over the lake and sent two birds clattering up out of the oaks. She didn't fall, quite. She caught herself on her hands and her good knee, and stayed there on all fours with the right leg stuck out stiff behind her, and the leg began to shake. A hard, fast tremor ran from the thigh up into her hip and the base of her tails and would not stop.

Her body flinched away from it so hard and so completely that for a moment she could not move anything else at all.

*Something's wrong. Something's really wrong with my leg.*

— Ripples —

It did not stop when the claws left.

It kept arriving. A first hot wave, then a second under it, deeper, then a third that seemed to come up from inside the bone itself and spread out through her hip and down to her foot. The thigh felt too big for her body, swollen tight and hot and wrong, and when she pressed her paw to it she could feel her heartbeat there, thudding against her palm.

She breathed through her teeth. Small breaths, because the big ones made the whole leg jump. A thin whine leaked out between them, and she clamped her jaw on it, and it leaked out anyway, a high, steady thread of sound she could hear and could not stop. Her cheek marks were twitching. Cold sweat had come up under her fur, though nothing about her was cold.

She tried to pull the leg in under her and it would not come. It lay there behind her, trembling, and every tremor was a fresh small jolt all the way up into her spine.

*Okay. Okay. That— that was bad. That was really bad.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne stood over her and watched.

She watched the leg stuck out behind, the tremor that would not stop and the way it climbed into the hip and the root of the tails. She watched the webbed paw pressed so hard against the thigh that the webbing spread white. She watched the Buizel try to swallow the sound and fail, and try again and fail again, the cream throat working round it. She watched the black eyes, which had been bright with something all fight, go wide and fixed on nothing at all.

Her own breath came back slowly in the sun. She did not hurry it.

*That's where you break. Right there. I'll remember.*

## beat 23
— Nocturne —

She let the Buizel breathe, because she needed to breathe herself. Her ribs ached deep from the oak and the mirror, her nose throbbed, and when she reached inside herself for the next blow there was almost nothing there; she had spent it all. She stood on the dry grass with her flanks heaving and her head low, and she kept her eyes on the Buizel the whole time.

— Ripples —

The Buizel was getting the leg under her. Slowly. A little weight, then a little more, her whole body held rigid while she tested it. Her mouth was open. Her tails hung dead behind her in the sand. The leg held, just, with the knee locked and every muscle above it shaking.

## beat 24
— Nocturne —

Nocturne swung her head and sent the blade off her horn across the gap again. Pale and humming, it took the Buizel across the chest, up over the ruined left shoulder, and into the side of the neck.

— Ripples —

The chest took it, the shoulder took it badly, and the neck flared. She couldn't stay upright. She doubled over round it with her arms crossed hard over her chest and her head down, air hissing out through her teeth, and for a long moment she could not straighten at all. — Ripples —

She stood like that, bent over, staring at the sand between her feet. Everything had started to run together. The chest had been one place; now it was joined to the shoulder, and the shoulder to the neck, one long band of heat from her throat to her arm, and the stomach under it, and the leg under that. When she breathed, all of it moved at once.

*Don't look at her. Just breathe. Then look at her.*

She breathed. It came short, and caught, and came again. Then she looked at her.

## beat 25
— Ripples —

She came up out of the fold with water. Water Pulse, a ring of it flung from her open mouth, straight into the white face.

— Nocturne —

It burst over her head and neck and the thick ruff on her chest, and it went into her mouth again, and up her nose again, and she was coughing again, spitting lake onto the grass. Her head rang where the ring had struck it. Her ruff was plastered flat and heavy against her chest.

— Ripples —

Ripples straightened as far as she could, which wasn't far, and watched the Absol cough. She had her weight on the left leg and the right one turned away behind her without ever deciding to, guarding it. She knew it left her left side open. She couldn't make herself turn back.

She watched the ribs heave on the left side, the side the oak had hit, the side the mirror had hit, and she saw that the Absol was not breathing evenly any more either. Short on that side. Catching.

*There. She's hurt there. She's hurt too.*

It was the first time she had let herself believe it.

## beat 26
— Nocturne —

Nocturne stood and coughed until the water was out of her. It took a while. When she could breathe again she kept her head low and her mouth open, panting, and took the moment to let her strength come back into her legs, because it had to come back from somewhere.

She watched the Buizel favour the leg while she did it. The weight all on the left. The right foot barely touching. The left side turned toward her without any guard on it at all.

## beat 27
— Ripples —

Ripples flung a spray of stars across the clearing: Swift, a glittering burst of them, bright even in the sun, curving in at the Absol from three sides at once.

— Nocturne —

They caught her in the head, the chest and the right shoulder, and they stung far more than anything that small had a right to. The one on her head was the worst; it landed on the place the water had already hammered, and a slow, heavy throb started up behind her eyes and did not go away.

— Nocturne —

She stood with her head low and felt it settle in.

Her head was the trouble now. Not the sharp sting of the nose any more, not the slap of the jet: something deeper, a pressure building behind her eyes in time with her heartbeat, slow and heavy, as if her skull had become a drum. Every time her heart beat, the clearing seemed to pulse very slightly brighter and then dim. The water, the ring, the stars; one blow at a time they had added up.

Her chest stung where the stars had hit. Her right shoulder ached. Her ribs on the left side still caught every time she drew a full breath, so she drew half breaths, and her own panting sounded too loud in her ears.

*Nothing. It's nothing. She's worse. Look at her.*

She looked at her. The Buizel was worse. That helped less than it should have.

## beat 28
— Nocturne —

She breathed again, and hated it. Every moment she spent catching her breath was a moment the Buizel spent catching hers, and the Buizel had the lake right there behind her, cool and close. Nocturne shook her head once, hard, to clear the drumming, and it did not clear.

## beat 29
— Nocturne —

She went in low anyway and raked upward, and her claws caught the Buizel across the face: across the nose, along the jaw, down into the throat.

— Ripples —

The nose took the worst of it. Her eyes went white and blind with tears at once, and she reeled back with her paw flying up to her face, her jaw aching where the claws had dragged along it, her throat raw and burning where they had finished. She tasted the lake and something else.

*The face. She's going for the face now.*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne watched her stagger back with both paws over her muzzle. The Buizel was still guarding the leg; even blinded, she turned it away. Good. Let her guard the leg. There was a great deal of her that was not leg.

## beat 30
— Ripples —

Ripples answered with the one thing the Absol's kind had always had reason to fear. She blinked the tears away, set her good foot, and brought the edge of her paw straight down onto the top of the white head: a chop with all her weight dropped behind it, Brick Break, aimed at the place the stars had found.

— Nocturne —

It landed like a dropped stone.

The force went straight down through her skull and on into her horn and her throat, and the world went white and ringing. It was the kind of blow her kind had always had to fear, and her body knew it before she did. Her legs went soft under her. She staggered sideways, her head hanging, and for one long moment she could not tell where the ground was, or which of the bright shapes in front of her was the Buizel.

— Nocturne —

The drumming in her head had become a roar.

She stood with her legs braced wide and her head down, and the pain came in waves, each one a little worse than the last. Her horn ached at the root, a deep, strange ache she had never felt in it before, as if the whole curved blade of it were a tooth that had been struck. Her throat burned. Her ears were full of a high, thin ringing that drowned out the lake and the birds and her own breath.

A sound came up out of her chest, a low, cracked growl, and she let it, because the other sound waiting behind it was worse.

*Brick Break. Of course. Of course she has that.*

She blinked, and the two Buizels in front of her slid slowly together into one.

## beat 31
— Nocturne —

Through the ringing, she found her. The Buizel had come in close behind the chop, too close, her weight on the good left leg and the bad right one trailing.

Nocturne hooked a hind leg behind the good knee and swept it.

The Buizel went over, and Nocturne went with her, driving her forward and down the last stride of the beach and into the shallows face-first with her whole weight. Water exploded up round them both. A forepaw found the back of the slim neck and pressed it down, under the surface, into the soft sand of the bottom. The rest of her came down across the Buizel's upper back, heavy and wet and immovable.

*Breathe that. Breathe your water.*

— Ripples —

The lake closed over her face. Sand in her mouth, water up her nose, the warm weight on her back crushing her flat into the soft bottom so that she couldn't get her arms under her, and the paw on the back of her neck, holding her head down, steady, patient. She could hear nothing but water. She could see nothing but brown light.

She held the one breath she'd snatched on the way down, and it began, immediately, to burn.

## beat 32
— Nocturne —

Nocturne bore down. She could feel the neck under her paw, the small back under her weight, the whole body rigid beneath her. She held her there, and when she felt the first frantic jerk, she let her up a finger's width, just enough for one gasp, and pressed her under again.

*Not yet. Not yet. I decide when you breathe.*

— Ripples —

She got one breath, half of it water, and coughed it out into the lake. Her chest was on fire. Her neck ached under the paw. And somewhere under all of it, under the panic and the burning, a cold, small part of her worked out where the weight was: across her back, high, toward her shoulders. Not on her hips.

She bucked.

Everything she had left went into it: her back arching up under the weight, her legs driving at the soft sand, the good hind foot lashing back as hard as it would go. It caught something hard and furred: the Absol's hind paw, square on.

— Nocturne —

The paw jolted out from under her, and her balance went with it. Her weight slid sideways off the narrow back, her forepaw skidded off the wet neck, and the Buizel came up out of the shallows under her in an explosion of water and spray, out from under her and away.

*No. No, I had her. I HAD her.*

She kept her feet, just. She stood in the shallows with the water pouring off her and her hind paw throbbing where the foot had caught it, and watched the Buizel crawl up the sand on her belly.

— Ripples —

She got as far as the waterline and stopped there, on her front, coughing up lake into the sand. Every breath was a scrape. Her neck felt bruised all the way round. She tried to get her arms under her, and they shook, and folded, and she lay with her cheek on the wet sand and her eyes half shut and simply breathed, because breathing was suddenly the best thing she had ever done.

## beat 33
— Nocturne —

Nocturne stood back from her and breathed too, because she had nothing left to throw for the moment. Her head was still roaring. Her hind paw ached. She had had the Buizel pinned in the water, face down, done, and the Buizel had kicked her off.

— Ripples —

Ripples crawled to the nearest pine and put her back to it. She pushed herself up it with her hands and her good leg, the bark scraping her shoulders, her right leg held clear of the ground. The first try failed. The second slid her back down. On the third she was standing, her back flat to the trunk, the sun in her eyes, her chest heaving.

## beat 34
— Nocturne —

Nocturne did something she almost never did. She stopped attacking.

She set her four paws wide on the grass and lowered her head, and let her weight sink back onto her haunches, and waited. Every muscle in her wound tight and went quiet. Her ears turned forward. Her red eyes fixed on the Buizel against the pine and did not blink.

*Come on, then. Come and hit me.*

— Ripples —

Ripples saw the stillness and didn't trust it. A fighter that size did not just stand there. But the Absol was hurt, she could see that now, the uneven breathing, the head held low and a little to one side, and she was hurt worse herself, and waiting was a thing she could not afford. Every moment she spent leaning on a tree, her leg got stiffer.

She pushed off the pine.

## beat 35
— Ripples —

She came in on one good leg and two good arms, and brought the edge of her paw down with all her weight behind it, not on the head this time but into the ribs on the left side, where she had seen the Absol's breath catch. Brick Break. She felt it land deep.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne let it. She let the chop come and took it full in the ribs, the ribs the oak had cracked against and the mirror had hit, and it bent her sideways and drove the wind out of her and lit the whole left side of her body up white. And then she sent it back.

Everything that had gone into her came out of her again, doubled, in one savage swipe of her forepaw that caught the Buizel full across the face.

— Ripples —

She never saw it coming. One instant she was finishing the chop, the next her left cheek split hot and her right ear exploded with pain, and her head snapped sideways so hard her whole body turned with it. She hadn't even seen the paw move.

— Ripples —

She stood where she had stopped, her head still turned, and for a moment she didn't understand what had happened to her.

Then it came in. The cheek first, a hot, wet sting along the black marks. Then the ear, and the ear was worse: not a sting but a deep, ringing ache that went in through the side of her skull and filled it, so that the clearing seemed to tilt to one side and stay there. Sound came through it wrong. The lake was too loud on one side and too quiet on the other.

Her own chop had come back to her. She understood that slowly, the way you understand a fall after you have landed. She had hit the Absol as hard as she could, and the Absol had given every bit of it back to her, plus more, in the face.

*She waited for it. She just stood there and waited for me to—*

She put her paw up to her ear, and took it away, and put it back.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne was bent round her ribs, unable to get more than a sip of air past them. But she had seen the Buizel's head snap round. That had been worth it.

## beat 36
— Ripples —

Ripples ducked in, low and quick, under the Absol's guard while she was still bent round her ribs. She did not strike. She whipped her twin tails round the white left foreleg, once, twice, and cinched them tight, like a rope thrown round a post.

— Nocturne —

The tails closed round her upper foreleg and held. It was not a blow. It was a grip, steady and squeezing, and almost at once the leg began to go numb and tingling below it. She tried to pull the leg free and the tails only tightened.

— Ripples —

Ripples held on and watched what it did. She watched the Absol pull, and stop, and pull again, harder, and the tails hold. She watched the foreleg's paw open and close on nothing. She watched the red eyes come round to her, narrow, and then flick down to the tails and back. And she saw, for the first time all fight, something in them that wasn't contempt.

*Got you. Got something.*

## beat 37
— Ripples —

With the leg held, she went for the neck. Crunch, all of it, her whole body behind it, her teeth sinking deep into the thick white ruff and the throat beneath it.

— Nocturne —

The bite went in deep, and the shock of it was so sudden and so close to her throat that her head jerked away on its own and she couldn't make it come back. For a heartbeat she was frozen with her head turned aside and the teeth in her neck, and she couldn't do a thing about it.

Her neck was on fire. Her throat closed round the pain. And all the while the tails kept squeezing her foreleg, which had gone cold now, and heavy, and tingling all the way to the claws. She braced against the bind with all four legs, pulling back from it with her whole weight, and the twin tails held as they were.

— Nocturne —

She stood braced and breathing with the teeth gone from her neck and the tails still on her leg, and took stock.

The neck was bad. It throbbed and burned in a ring from her jaw down into her chest, and her throat was raw inside and out, so that every swallow was something she had to decide to do. Her head was still drumming. Her ribs on the left were a solid ache. And the leg, the leg in the tails, had stopped feeling like part of her at all. It was a cold, heavy, prickling thing hanging from her shoulder.

*Get it off. Get her off.*

She had never been held by anything she couldn't throw off. She did not like how it felt.

## beat 38
— Ripples —

Ripples bit again, lower this time: into the white right foreleg, up near the shoulder, and dragged her teeth down toward the paw. And all the while her tails stayed wound round the left.

— Nocturne —

The bite tore into her other foreleg. Now both of them were wrong: one bitten, one bound. She clawed at the tails with her free forepaw, raking at the orange coils, and they held.

— Ripples —

Ripples hung on with her tails and watched the Absol claw at them. The claws scraped her tail fur and didn't get through. She watched the white body twist and pull, and twist again, the bound leg dragging, the bitten one held off the ground now, the head low. She saw the Absol's breath come short and harsh.

She saw something else, too: the Absol had stopped looking at her face. She was looking at the tails. All of her attention was on the leg.

*Good. Look at the leg. Keep looking at the leg.*

## beat 39
— Nocturne —

She charged.

Bound leg and all, bitten leg and all, Nocturne put her shoulder down and drove forward, a reckless, full-body ram with every scrap of her weight behind it. The tails round her foreleg dragged and slowed her, and she went anyway. She took the Buizel off her feet and carried her across the clearing, a dozen strides, into the trunk of a tall pine.

— Ripples —

The ram took her square in the chest and the right arm and shoulder, and lifted her. Then the pine came up behind her, and her back and her ruined left shoulder and her hip all slammed into the trunk at once, and the Absol's whole weight crushed her chest against the bark. Something in her chest seemed to fold in on itself.

— Nocturne —

The force of it jarred back into her own body as she hit, up through her throat and into her ribs; the charge cost her as much as it cost anything. But the Buizel was crumpling against the trunk, sliding down it, and Nocturne stepped back and let her go.

— Ripples —

She slid down the pine to the roots and lay there on her back, and as she went down her tails tore loose from the Absol's foreleg and fell slack in the grass.

The pine went up and up above her into the blue. Her chest wasn't working. It moved, but only a little, and every movement was a wide, flat, crushing ache, as if the Absol's weight were still on it. Her shoulder was a white noise. Her hip throbbed. She lay in the roots and couldn't move and stared up through the branches.

Then something lit inside her.

It came from somewhere deep, somewhere she hadn't known was there: a hot, bright surge that rose up through her chest and her limbs all at once. Her heart started to pound. The breath came back into her, ragged but real. Her eyes cleared, and the pine was sharp again against the sky.

*Not yet. Not yet. NOT YET.*

## beat 40
— Ripples —

Still on her back in the roots, she opened her mouth and let the lake come out of her: Hydro Pump, a pillar of water thick as her own body, aimed straight up at the white shape over her.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne saw it coming and met it head-on. She opened her jaws and threw the dark at it, a ring of it straight into the oncoming water. The two met in the air between them with a sound like surf breaking on rock, spray and shadow tearing out sideways, and for one long moment they were both leaning into it with everything they had.

Then the water won.

It tore straight through the dark and hit her in the head, the chest, the belly, and it did not stop. It held there: three long, brutal pulses of it, battering her head back on her neck, filling her mouth, driving into her chest until she couldn't breathe, soaking her through. She doubled over round her belly in the middle of it, coughing.

— Ripples —

Ripples got up after it. One ugly heave against the pine trunk, both hands on the bark, and she was standing, the surge still burning in her chest.

— Ripples —

She stood against the pine and watched the Absol come apart.

The white head hung low, water streaming off the horn. The body was bent round the belly. The ribs heaved on both sides now, short and uneven, and each heave ended in a cough. The bound foreleg, free now, was held off the ground. The Absol's eyes were half shut, and when they opened they didn't find her straight away.

Ripples watched every bit of it, and something hot and fierce came up in her that wasn't the surge.

*Look at you. Look what I did.*

## beat 41
— Ripples —

She hit the Absol with Water Pulse while she was still bent over: a ring of water straight into the white face, the head, the neck, the ruff.

— Nocturne —

Her head was already roaring. This made it worse than worse. The ring burst over her skull and the world swam and doubled and tripled, so that there were two pines and two lakes and two Buizels, and none of them would hold still. She was coughing water up again. Her thoughts kept slipping off each other. She could not have said, for a moment, which way the lake was.

— Nocturne —

She stood with her legs splayed, swaying, and tried to find the Buizel and couldn't.

Every time she fixed on one of the orange shapes it slid sideways and became two. Sound came late. The lake lapped, and then, half a heartbeat after, she heard it lap. Her head was a single huge pulse, and in the middle of each beat the light flared and went grey at the edges and came back. Her neck burned. Her ribs burned. Water dripped off her chin.

Somewhere far down under all of it, she was frightened, and she hated that more than all the rest together.

*Where. Where is she. Where—*

She shook her head, and the two Buizels swung away from each other and back, and neither of them was any more real than the other.

## beat 42
— Nocturne —

She stood still and breathed, because there was nothing else in the world she could do. Her head hung low and a little to one side, the side away from the Buizel, shielding it without her ever deciding to. The drumming went on and on. Slowly, very slowly, the two lakes slid back into one.

— Ripples —

Across the grass, Ripples felt the surge beginning to ebb. Not gone. Going. The heat in her chest was cooling a little with every breath, and the pain was coming back up underneath it like water rising through sand.

## beat 43
— Ripples —

She knew there would not be another surge. So she spent all of it.

She drew herself up on her good leg, set her paws, and flung Swift with everything she had left: not a spray this time but a storm of stars, a blinding glittering mass of them, hurled at the white head and chest and shoulder with every scrap of strength in her body.

— Nocturne —

Every star felt like a stone. They struck her head, the head that had already been hammered and chopped and drowned, and her chest, and her right shoulder, and the force of them rocked her back onto her haunches. The air went out of her in a rush, and her chest was so bruised that she could not get it back. She sat there on the grass with her mouth open, gulping at nothing.

— Nocturne —

She sat on her haunches in the grass like a pup that had been knocked over, and could not get up.

— Ripples —

Ripples watched her sit there and gulp at the air, and saw the head hang, and heard the sound coming out of the Absol, low and ragged and broken, and knew she had put everything into it and it had gone in.

The stars were gone, and the surge was gone, and there was nothing behind them at all: no strength, no breath, just the hurt, all of it, all at once, coming up through the place where the surge had been. *That's it. That's everything. Please let it be enough.*

## beat 44
— Nocturne —

Nocturne breathed. Short breaths, all her chest would allow. Her head pounded so hard her vision pulsed with it, bright, dim, bright. She stayed sitting on her haunches, and the Buizel stood swaying, and for a long moment neither of them could do anything to the other at all.

## beat 45
— Ripples —

Ripples saw her sitting there with her head down, and something in her that wasn't strength, only stubbornness, dragged her forward. One step on the bad leg, a lurch, another. She raised her paw and brought it down on the white head again, Brick Break, the same spot, the edge of her hand onto the place she'd hit before.

— Nocturne —

It landed exactly where the last one had, and the pain was so enormous that for a moment it went completely quiet. Then it roared. Her ear rang. Her throat burned all the way down. She should have gone down. Her body was already going.

— Ripples —

Ripples had put too much into it. There had been nothing left to put into it, and she had found some anyway, and now it was gone and her balance went with it. She overbalanced on the follow-through and stumbled past the Absol, her bad leg buckling under her, and had to throw her hands down to catch herself on the grass. *Get up. Get up. Get up—*

## beat 46
— Nocturne —

The Buizel was stumbling. Off balance, soaked to the bone, wide open, half on her hands. Nocturne let the lightning go.

It had been stored in her the whole fight: a hot, tight, crawling thing under her fur, waiting. She released it all at once. A white-blue bolt leapt from her to the soaked Buizel with a crack that echoed off the far shore of the lake, and ran through every wet inch of her.

— Ripples —

## beat 47
— Nocturne —

The surge was still in her. Nocturne jerked her head high and to the left, a sudden snap toward the orange face.

— Ripples —

Ripples flinched from it with her whole dazed body. Her arms flew up over her head.

— Nocturne —

— Ripples —

*Fell for it. I fell for it. I watched her eyes and I—*

— Nocturne —

Nocturne stepped back and watched her fold.

Her arms came down from over her head and crossed over her stomach, too late, much too late, and pressed. Her mouth was open and nothing was going into it. The cream throat worked and worked. Her good leg was trembling now too, both legs, and the tails lay flat in the grass behind her like dead things.

Nocturne watched her try to breathe. She watched the first breath come, a thin, rattling sip, and catch, and come again.

*That's the one. That's the one I wanted.*

The surge was still hot in her. She did not move. She wanted to see all of it.

## beat 48
— Ripples —

She stood bent over with her arms locked round her middle and breathed in tiny, desperate sips, because a real breath was impossible. Each one went in a little way, caught on the hurt, and stopped. She was so far past spent there was no word for it. She stayed on her feet only because falling seemed to need more than she had.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne watched her try to breathe, and felt her own surge begin to fade, the way the Buizel's had. The heat was draining out of her legs. The drumming in her head was starting to come back up underneath it.

## beat 49
— Ripples —

Bent over, without straightening, Ripples opened her mouth and breathed out cold: Ice Beam, a freezing white line held steady into the Absol's head, chest and shoulder.

— Nocturne —

It found her head, the one place on her that had nothing left, and held there. Two long pulses of cold drove into the bruised, hammered skull until it felt like ice had been packed inside it. Frost spread over her chest and stiffened the fur in her ruff, and her left shoulder went rigid with cold.

— Nocturne —

When it stopped, she could not see properly.

The clearing had gone grey and swimming. Her head was not a drum any more; it was a single vast block of pain with a cold core, and every heartbeat cracked it a little further. The frost in her ruff crackled every time she drew a breath, and she could draw only short ones, half-ones, her chest locked tight with cold and bruising. Her left shoulder would not move at all.

She had been certain. When the Buizel folded over her paw, she had been certain.

*Too much. Too much. She was finished. She was supposed to be finished—*

Somewhere in the grey, the Buizel was still standing.

## beat 50
— Nocturne —

Nocturne had almost nothing left. She knew it, standing there in the grey with her head splitting. So she put all of it into one thing.

The sickle tail came round low and hard, harder than anything she had swung all fight, every scrap of strength in her body poured into the swing, and it took the Buizel's right knee: the knee she had hammered, the knee that was already ruined.

— Ripples —

The knee went. Not buckled: went. She was on her back on the grass before she understood she had fallen, her shoulders and her ruined left shoulder slamming into the ground, her right leg a white sheet of pain from hip to foot.

— Ripples —

She lay on her back and could not do anything.

The leg was the whole world. The knee had become a hot, swollen, pounding thing, and the thigh above it, and the foot below it tingled and burned. She could not move it. She did not want to move it. She lay with her arms crossed over her stomach, guarding it from nothing, and stared straight up into the blue, and the sky was very bright and very far away.

A sound was coming out of her. Not a cry. A long, low, shaking breath on every exhale that sounded like it belonged to someone else. Her fins were trembling. Her whole body was trembling, a deep, steady shudder from the knee outward that she could not stop.

*Can't feel— can't— it's too much. It's too much.*

— Nocturne —

And then Nocturne was empty too.

She stood over the Buizel and swayed, and every scrap of strength she had had a moment ago was simply gone, poured out into the swing. Her legs trembled under her. She did not trust them. She locked them and stood there over the shaking orange body in the grass and watched it shake.

She watched the leg, and the way the Buizel would not even look at it. She watched the arms locked over the stomach, the trembling fins, the black eyes staring past her into the sky. She watched the breath go in and out, long and shaking.

She had nothing left to finish it with. She knew that too. She stood and watched and waited for her legs to come back.

## beat 51
— Nocturne —

She stood and breathed. That was all she could do, and all she did. Her head throbbed. Her shoulder was stiff with cold. The surge had left nothing behind it but a shaking weakness in her legs and a great dull weight in her chest.

— Ripples —

Ripples got as far as sitting up. She pushed herself up on her hands, slowly, with her bad leg stuck straight out in front of her in the grass, and stayed there, braced, her head hanging. Further than that it would not go.

## beat 52
— Both —

For a while neither of them moved.

The sun came through a gap in the clouds and lay warm across the clearing. The lake lapped at the sand as if nothing had happened at all. The breeze moved the grass in slow silver waves. Somewhere in the oaks, the bird that had fallen quiet when the lightning cracked started singing again, the same bright phrase, over and over.

— Ripples —

Ripples sat in the grass and tried three times to get her leg under her. Each time it took a little weight and then folded, and she sat back down, and breathed, and looked at the Absol through the hair in her eyes.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne stood and watched her try. She did not move to stop it. Her own legs were only just beginning to feel like legs again.

## beat 53
— Ripples —

This time she used the pine. She rolled onto her front, which hurt everything, and crawled the last body-length to the trunk on her elbows, and climbed it with her hands, her face pressed against the rough, warm bark, her good leg pushing. The first try slid her back down. The second got her halfway. On the third, she was up, barely, on one leg, clinging to the tree.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne watched her climb and did not stop her. She was still getting her breath back. And she wanted the Buizel up for what came next.

## beat 54
— Nocturne —

She breathed, and breathed, and felt a little of it come back: into her legs first, then her chest, then, very slowly, a small cold steadiness behind the drumming in her head.

— Ripples —

Ripples hung on the pine with her cheek against the bark and watched her do it. She knew what it meant. She could not do anything about it.

## beat 55
— Nocturne —

Then Nocturne went in, and bit.

She crossed the grass in three slow strides, and the Buizel saw her come and could do nothing but cross her arms over her stomach. Nocturne's jaws went past the arms. They closed on the stomach, on the place that had taken everything, and she bit down, and twisted, and let go.

— Ripples —

The arms didn't matter. The teeth went past them into the ruined centre of her, and her whole body flinched round the bite so hard and so completely that she couldn't move anything at all. She hung on the pine by one hand, curled round herself, rigid, her mouth open on a sound that wouldn't come.

## beat 56
— Nocturne —

Nocturne waited out the flinch. She stood back a pace and breathed, her jaws aching, the taste of the lake and of wet orange fur in her mouth, and watched the Buizel hang there frozen against the tree.

— Ripples —

Slowly, the flinch let go of her. She could move her head again. She could move her hand. She did not move her legs.

## beat 57
— Nocturne —

The moment the Buizel moved again, Nocturne was on her.

She caught her across the hips with her shoulder, lifted her off her one good leg, and threw her. It was a heave with her whole body behind it, and it dropped the Buizel flat on her back in the grass at the foot of the pine. Before she could do anything at all, Nocturne was over her. A forepaw ground down into the ruined stomach. A hind paw pinned the left thigh. Her own chest and the thick white ruff pressed down on the cream chest, heavy and immovable.

*Stay down. Stay down now.*

— Ripples —

The forepaw on her stomach was the worst thing that had ever happened to her.

It did not strike. It did not need to. It simply pressed, steady and heavy, down into the place where all of it had gone, and every breath she took pushed her stomach up into it. So she breathed less. And less.

## beat 58
— Nocturne —

Nocturne bore down and held it. She could feel every breath under her paw: the rise, the catch, the shudder as it hit the weight. She could feel the small body under her trying to gather itself, every muscle drawing tight.

— Ripples —

She tried to slide out sideways, out from under the weight, twisting her hips toward the trunk. She got nowhere. The paw on her chest came down harder for it, and her chest flared hot under the white ruff.

## beat 59
— Ripples —

She tried again. She tried to slide, to twist, anything at all. The hind paw on her thigh ground down and pinned the leg flatter, and her thigh lit up under it.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne watched the struggle, every twitch of it, and felt it weaken under her.

The first try had had strength in it. This one was mostly will. She felt the hips twist and fail, felt the thigh jerk under her hind paw and go still, and through her paw on the belly she felt the breath come in shorter afterwards, and shorter. She watched the black eyes squeeze shut against it. She watched the jaw clench and the cheek marks twitch, and a high, thin sound slip out between the teeth and stop.

Her own legs were shaking with the effort of holding. Her head was a slow, heavy drum. She held.

*Less each time. Less each time. Just hold.*

## beat 60
— Ripples —

She bucked once, with everything that was left. It was not much. Her back arched a finger's width off the grass, and the weight barely moved, and the thigh under the hind paw paid for it with a bright, hot jolt that ran all the way up into her hip.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne felt the buck and rode it. She felt how weak it was, how little was in it, and knew.

## beat 61
— Ripples —

She didn't try again.

She lay under the weight and looked up past the white shape over her, past the horn, past the dark pine needles, at the sky. A cloud was sliding across it, slow and white, its edge lit gold. The sun came out from behind it and lay on her face, warm. Her breath came shallow and quick, and every breath pushed her stomach up into the paw, and every one was a little shorter than the last.

The pain had stopped being a place. It was simply the weather now: everywhere, steady, the air she was lying in. Her leg, her stomach, her chest under the ruff, her shoulder, her face; they had all run together into one long, low hum. She could hear the lake. She could hear the bird.

*Can't. Can't anymore. I'm sorry. I can't.*

She did not know who she was saying it to.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne looked down at her and saw the black eyes going unfocused, drifting off her face to somewhere over her shoulder. She did not let up.

## beat 62
— Ripples —

The edges of the sky went grey and soft.

Her breath went small, then smaller. The pain in her stomach went from everything to something a long way off, like a sound across water. Her paws, which had been pressing up weakly against the forepaw on her belly, slid off it and lay open in the grass. The bird sang its phrase. Her eyes closed. She was still breathing, slow and shallow, when the rest of her went quiet.

— Nocturne —

Nocturne felt her go slack under her paws.

She held a moment longer, to be sure, her weight still on the small, still body, her own breath rasping. Nothing moved. Then she lifted her forepaw off the Buizel's stomach, slowly, and her hind paw off the thigh, and stepped back, and stood over the still orange shape in the grass with her head low and her flanks heaving and her legs trembling under her.

Her head was splitting. Her neck burned, her ribs ached, her shoulder was stiff, and the foreleg the tails had held still tingled. The fur on her chest was matted with lake water and frost-melt. She stood in the sun at the foot of the pine and let all of it arrive.

The bird in the oaks was still singing. The lake lapped at the sand.

*Done. It's done.*

She did not feel as if she had won. She felt as if she had survived something. She turned her head away from the Buizel, slowly, because her neck would not let her do it fast, and looked out at the water for a long time.
